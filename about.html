About

Sam Mozaffari

I am an Experience Designer in Sydney. My background is product design, first physical products and then digital ones, and for the last two years I have worked on how services operate inside a large restaurant network: how people report a hazard, how a manager rosters a shift, how a new team member gets hired and trained.

The part of the job I like most is the middle. Running the interviews, sitting in the restaurant, drawing the blueprint, and then getting the safety team, the training team and the operators into a room to agree what to fix first. I build small tools when the research needs somewhere to live, and I use AI in that work every day.

How I work

Evidence first, then the argument

Research designed to answer a question

In the safety-reporting work I chose restaurants to test whether location explained why some reported more than others. It did not, and that finding moved the recommendations from system fixes to manager routines. A sample that can surprise you is worth more than a big one.

Blueprints that people argue over

A blueprint earns its keep in the workshop, when the operations lead and the safety lead look at the same step and disagree about who owns it. I draw them to make that conversation happen.

AI in the workflow, with a human gate

I use AI to transcribe, extract and classify, and I built an internal tool around that pipeline. Every insight in it was reviewed and mapped by me before it was used. The question I am working on now is how to design that review step for other people, so generated work is inspected before it becomes a plan.

What I want next

Work where the research, the blueprint and the decision sit with the same person, in a business that runs on real operations: service design, experience design of AI-enabled services, transformation consulting, or applied-AI adoption. I lead projects and stakeholders, and I want to keep doing that with a bigger remit.

On the side

A book and some tooling

I wrote The Agentic Service Designer to work out, in detail, what service design methods look like when agents do the first draft and humans hold the gates. The open tooling behind it is in the Library. Both are personal projects built with AI assistance; the worked example in the book is a fictional ferry operator.
