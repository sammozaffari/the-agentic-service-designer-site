Learning Objectives

  1. Theoretical Foundations: Argue the demographic fallacy from first principles and from the literature — why age/income/postcode segments mislead service design and behavioral signatures don't.
  2. Agent Topology & Contracts: Run behavioural-personas (synthesis) and customer-typology-mapping (measurement) live, reading their real contracts and acceptance rules.
  3. Architecture & Schemas: Author a persona-set whose every persona cites ≥2 content-addressed evidence records and whose goal field is behavioral.
  4. Case Study Execution: Apply the Outside In customer-understanding discipline (vol. 14, ch. 4) and its decision-grade persona bar.
  5. Adversarial Verification: Watch the contract refuse demographic definitions — forbid-field-values on description, plus the critic gate rejecting demographic goals.
  6. Hands-on Practice: Produce a three-persona set from the Fernwood corpus with triangulated citations.

1. Theoretical Grounding & Literature Synthesis

The demographic fallacy, stated precisely

A demographic segment tells you who someone is; a service encounter depends on what they are trying to do, under what constraints, with what prior experiences. The fallacy is the inference from the first to the second. Martin & Hanington (vol. 18, ch. 5) put it flatly: effective design personas are "grounded in empirical behavioral patterns, mental models, goals, and constraints — not in fictional demographic trivia (age, stock photos, marital status) that often reinforces subconscious designer bias."

Manning & Bodine's Outside In (vol. 14, ch. 4, "Customer Understanding") sets the quality bar this course adopts: the decision-grade persona. A persona is decision-grade only if a product manager, an engineer, and a frontline worker can each use it to resolve a real trade-off. Their Fidelity Investments case is the canonical demonstration: shifting from demographic segments (age, income) to the behavioral "Transition to Retirement" journey exposed that pre-retirees' acute anxiety over tax sequencing and healthcare costs was invisible to age-based segmentation — and redesigning around the behavior produced integrated retirement transition services that demographics never implied.

The mechanism behind the fallacy is worth naming: demographic attributes are cheap proxies that correlate with behavior only inside stable contexts. Services break contexts (a channel fails, a promise breaks, a life event hits), and under broken contexts behavior reorganizes around goals and constraints, not around age. The 62-year-old who photographs every mould patch and the 24-year-old who does the same are the same persona for repair-portal design purposes — the Vigilant Archivist — and designing for their "generations" instead of their documentation behavior produces two wrong services instead of one right one.

Behavioral signatures

A behavioral signature is an observable, repeatable pattern of action under a defined context: self-documenting after data loss, exit-to-private after six weeks of silence, repeat-calling when records don't travel. Signatures are what survive aggregation: they are falsifiable (you can count them), segmentable (people sort by them), and actionable (each implies a design response). A persona in this course is a named cluster of signatures with a goal — nothing more, and crucially nothing less.

2. Agentic Topology & Role Contracts

Two registered methods produce persona sets in this toolchain; their contracts differ in phase and intent, and both were retrieved live:

Method Phase Owner What it is for Critic gate (verbatim from contract)
behavioural-personas synthesis synthesizer Cluster participants by observed behaviour and goal from interview/diary transcripts, on top of a prior insight-set "Reject any persona whose defining goal is demographic rather than behavioural."
customer-typology-mapping measurement measurer Cluster customers by observed behaviour or value in transactional/behavioral data "Reject any type with no cited behavioural evidence, since an assumed type is a stereotype, not a typology."

Per-role specifics:

  • Synthesizer (behavioural-personas steps 1–2): clusters by behavior and goal — never by demographic attribute alone — then drafts one persona per cluster, citing the evidence for its defining goal and its main frustration.
  • Measurer (customer-typology-mapping step 1): works the same discipline against transactional data; both methods hand their persona-set to the ideator after the critic passes.
  • Critic: the gate text is itself the anti-demographic enforcement — and it is backed by a deterministic acceptance rule (below).
  • Human designer: scope-confirmation before run; handoff-approval before the set moves to ideation.

3. Schemas & Verified Execution

The persona-set schema (from asd schema-get --kind persona-set)

// node fields — fieldPolicy: "closed"; edges forbidden
{
  name: string        // required
  goal?: string       // behavioral goal — the load-bearing field
  role?: string; context?: string; description?: string
  needs?: string[]; barrier?: string[]; tags?: string[]
  age?: number        // present but optional — and irrelevant to acceptance
}

The behavioural-personas contract — anti-demographic by construction

Retrieved live with asd method-run --methodId behavioural-personas (it fails closed until a prior insight-set exists — the personas must be built on synthesized insight, not raw hunch):

{
  "acceptance": [
    {"rule": "min-nodes", "min": 2},
    {"rule": "min-evidence-per-node", "min": 2},
    {"rule": "field-non-empty", "field": "goal"},
    {"rule": "forbid-field-values", "field": "description", "deny": ["demographic"]}
  ],
  "inputs": [
    {"kind": "evidence-set", "min": 5, "note": "interview or diary-study transcripts"},
    {"kind": "artifact", "min": 1, "artifactKinds": ["insight-set"], "note": "a prior insight set"}
  ],
  "outputKind": "persona-set"
}

Read the acceptance list as the demographic fallacy, encoded: every persona must cite at least two evidence records; every persona must state a goal; and the description field literally cannot contain the value demographic.

4. Case Study Lens: Decision-Grade Personas at Fidelity (vol. 14, ch. 4)

Manning & Bodine's discipline is the lens: personas earn their keep only when they settle trade-offs. Fidelity's move from age/income segments to the behavioral "Transition to Retirement" journey found the anxiety signature (tax sequencing, healthcare cost fear) that demographics structurally could not see. The Fernwood set above is built to the same bar: "The Quiet Deserter" (exit-to-private after broken promises) tells a housing officer exactly which trade-off to make — visible outcome history before new channel features — while "women, 45–54" tells them nothing.

The synthetic program articles apply the same discipline: Northgate (Articles 02/12) organized its telehealth redesign around behavioral onboarding archetypes rather than member demographics, which is why its completion fixes targeted behavior (form abandonment, no-show patterns) rather than age bands.

# Deliberate fail-closed demos:
# (a) one evidence ref per persona → accepted:false, draft retained with check.failures
# (b) description set to the string "demographic" → write refused by forbid-field-values

Verification checklist

  • No persona's defining goal references a demographic attribute (the critic gate's exact test).
  • Every persona cites ≥2 evidence ids that resolve in the project store.
  • context carries situation and constraints; age is absent or incidental.
  • A description of "demographic" is rejected by forbid-field-values (run it).

Key Takeaways

  • The demographic fallacy is a mechanical risk, so the fix is mechanical: behavioural-personas requires evidence-cited behavioral goals and forbids demographic description values at write time.
  • Behavioral signatures — observable action patterns under context — are the persona's unit of construction, and each maps to a design response.
  • Personas are built on insight, not hunch: the method fails closed until a prior insight-set artifact exists.
  • Decision-grade is the bar (Manning & Bodine): if a persona cannot settle a trade-off for a frontline worker, it is decoration.
