Module 08.1: Grönroos Quality Management & The Five Service Gaps

Course: Service Quality, Good Services Audits & Continuous Operations · Level: Advanced · Duration: 60 min · Format: Theory, Architecture, Verified CLI & Hands-on Lab

🎯 Learning Objectives

By completing this module, you will be able to:

  1. Explain Grönroos's Total Perceived Service Quality model — expected vs. experienced service, technical vs. functional quality, and the image filter — and how it relates to the Parasuraman–Zeithaml–Berry gap tradition.
  2. Map each of the five service gaps to a specific, executable ASD gate: the evidence gate, the method acceptance gate, the critic review gate, the deterministic factcheck, and the human approval gate.
  3. Run the service-quality-diagnostic method (owner measurer, review gate critic, output kind experience-audit) against a real project and read its method contract.
  4. Write an experience-audit artifact that passes the closed kind schema (fields.issue required) and the method's acceptance rules, and survive a critic veto.
  5. Verify the result with asd factcheck and explain exactly what each check proves.

📖 1. Theoretical Grounding: Perceived Quality and the Gap Model

1.1 Grönroos: quality is a comparison, not a specification

In Service Management and Marketing, Ch. 2 (books/19-service-management-and-marketing/02-perceived-service-quality.md), Christian Grönroos makes the move that defines the Nordic school: service quality cannot be an engineered property of an output, because the customer co-produces the service. Quality is a subjective judgment produced by comparing expected service with experienced service:

  • Experienced > Expected → exceptionally high perceived quality
  • Experienced = Expected → satisfactory
  • Experienced < Expected → defective; dissatisfaction

The experienced side decomposes into technical quality (the what: the repaired meter, the executed transfer — relatively objective, the baseline ticket to compete) and functional quality (the how: attitude, accessibility, communication clarity — the usual differentiator, because competitors match the what). Both are filtered through corporate image: a strong image cushions minor failures; a fragile image amplifies them. Grönroos operationalizes this into six criteria of good perceived quality: professionalism & skills, attitudes & behavior, accessibility & flexibility, reliability & trustworthiness, service recovery, and reputation & credibility.

The expectation side matters just as much: expectations are shaped by explicit marketing, implicit cues (price, facilities), word of mouth, image, and personal needs. Overpromising is therefore an operational defect, not a marketing success.

1.2 The five gaps

The American school (Parasuraman, Zeithaml & Berry; SERVQUAL) instrumented the same structure as five measurable gaps. Where the schools disagree is psychometrics (Grönroos rejects difference scores as fragile); where they agree is the causal chain that produces disappointment:

# Gap Definition Typical root cause
1 Knowledge gap Customer expectation vs. management's perception of that expectation Decisions made on anecdote; research that never reaches designers
2 Standards gap Management perception vs. service quality specifications Vague standards ("respond quickly") that cannot be audited
3 Delivery gap Specification vs. actual delivery Scripts, systems and incentives that diverge from the spec
4 Communication gap Delivery vs. what external communications promise Marketing commitments operations cannot fulfill
5 Service gap Expected service vs. perceived service The accumulation of gaps 1–4, experienced by the customer

SERVQUAL scores gap 5 across five dimensions — reliability, responsiveness, assurance, empathy, tangibles — which is exactly the dimension set the ASD service-quality-diagnostic method instructs the researcher to score ("Score the gap between expected and perceived service quality for each dimension (reliability, responsiveness, assurance, empathy, tangibles), citing verbatim evidence").

1.3 Why the gap model demands gates, not workshops

Every gap in the model is a place where an assertion can drift from evidence: management asserts what customers expect (gap 1), asserts a standard (gap 2), asserts compliance (gap 3), asserts a promise (gap 4). A workshop can record these assertions; it cannot keep them honest. The agentic toolchain exists precisely to put a mechanical gate on each gap — the subject of the next section.

🤖 2. Agentic Topology: One ASD Gate per Gap

The five gaps are not a metaphor for the toolchain — each one is closed by a distinct, executable mechanism. This is the load-bearing mapping of this module:

Gap ASD gate Mechanism (verified)
1 Knowledge Evidence gate sd_evidence_add mints content-addressed records (id = SHA-256 of [source,kind,body,origin]); sd_method_run fails closed when the method's input minimum is unmet: method "service-quality-diagnostic" requires at least 3 evidence records; project has 2. Management cannot assert customer expectations the project has no evidence for.
2 Standards Acceptance gate Every method carries machine-checkable acceptance rules in its contract. For service-quality-diagnostic: min-nodes: 5, field-non-empty: severity, min-evidence-per-node: 1. A score without a severity or without cited evidence is not a standard — the artifact is retained as draft with accepted:false.
3 Delivery Critic review gate sd_review with reviewGate: critic. A pass verdict needs at least one evidence-cited row and no defect rows (verified error: a pass verdict cannot carry defect rows — record them as proposals or veto). A veto needs every defect row to carry a fix. The artifact stays draft until the gate clears.
4 Communication Deterministic factcheck sd_factcheck re-verifies every artifact's evidence integrity (dangling refs), closed kind schema, acceptance, and review verdict — exit 0 only when all pass. What the team claims about the artifact must match what the store proves.
5 Service Human approval gate sd_phase_advance fails closed without an approvalReceipt and refuses to advance while any design artifact is draft or vetoed. The final judgment of expected-vs-perceived is a human's, recorded as a receipt.

💻 3. Schemas & Verified Execution

3.1 The artifact contract (@asd/core)

// suite/packages/core/src/types.ts (abridged)
export type Artifact = {
  readonly id: string
  readonly kind: ArtifactKind            // "experience-audit" here; 32 kinds registered
  readonly projectId: string
  readonly title: string
  readonly schemaVersion: number         // 1
  readonly methodId?: string             // "service-quality-diagnostic"
  readonly nodes: readonly ArtifactNode[]  // { id, label, evidence: EvidenceRef[], fields }
  readonly edges?: readonly ArtifactEdge[] // { id, from, to, relation, fields }
  readonly status: 'draft' | 'final'
  readonly check?: ArtifactCheck         // acceptance failures while draft
  readonly review?: ArtifactReview       // { verdict: 'pass' | 'veto', logArtifactId }
}

The experience-audit kind has a closed field policy (asd schema-get --kind experience-audit): issue (string, required), plus optional severity, score, touchpoint, opportunity, owner. A node carrying a field outside this set is rejected at write time.

3.2 Verified terminal session

Run against the standalone runtime (node suite/packages/runtime/lib/cli.mjs) in a disposable workspace. Project: kestrel-quality-ops-1e9bfb41 — your ids will differ; the CLI mints them.

export ASD_WORKSPACE=/tmp/asd-lab
CLI="node suite/packages/runtime/lib/cli.mjs"

# Method contract (trimmed): note inputs, acceptance, reviewGate, humanCheckpoints
$CLI method-run --projectId kestrel-quality-ops-1e9bfb41 --methodId service-quality-diagnostic
# { "outputKind": "experience-audit", "reviewGate": "critic", "owner": "measurer",
#   "inputs": [{ "kind": "evidence-set", "min": 3,
#                "note": "customer feedback, complaints or survey verbatims" }],
#   "acceptance": [ {"rule":"min-nodes","min":5},
#                   {"rule":"field-non-empty","field":"severity"},
#                   {"rule":"min-evidence-per-node","min":1} ],
#   "availableEvidence": 5 }

# Write the artifact: one node per SERVQUAL dimension, issue required, severity per acceptance
$CLI artifact-write --projectId kestrel-quality-ops-1e9bfb41 \
  --methodId service-quality-diagnostic --title "Outage comms quality gaps (SERVQUAL)" \
  --nodes '[
   {"id":"d1","label":"Reliability gap","evidence":[{"evidenceId":"c18a3f85…"}],
    "fields":{"issue":"No proactive status update during outages","severity":"critical","touchpoint":"status page / SMS"}},
   {"id":"d2","label":"Responsiveness gap","evidence":[{"evidenceId":"98bc3f2e…"}],
    "fields":{"issue":"11m20s average handle time for outage calls","severity":"high","touchpoint":"contact centre"}},
   {"id":"d3","label":"Assurance gap","evidence":[{"evidenceId":"e7d429ba…"}],
    "fields":{"issue":"Agent cannot see prior report; customer repeats reference number","severity":"high"}},
   {"id":"d4","label":"Empathy gap","evidence":[{"evidenceId":"6bde4454…"}],
    "fields":{"issue":"Resolution learned via monthly bill, not from the service","severity":"medium"}},
   {"id":"d5","label":"Tangibles gap","evidence":[{"evidenceId":"c19efcc2…"}],
    "fields":{"issue":"Outage page ambiguous about whether a report was logged","severity":"medium"}}]'
# → artifact b2b147d09984418aa67065983e0007a6, "status": "draft", "failures": []

# Critic veto (defect row MUST carry a fix)
$CLI review --projectId kestrel-quality-ops-1e9bfb41 --artifactId b2b147d0… \
  --verdict veto --rows '[{"rowKind":"defect","severity":"high","rule":"evidence-citation",
   "message":"Reliability gap cites the complaint log but the draft does not state the remediation owner.",
   "fix":"Add fields.owner to the d1 node and re-submit for review.",
   "evidenceId":"c18a3f85…"}]'
# → "verdict": "veto", artifact stays "draft", log artifact ee878c0ae47a432c8b3d656978a3f2c0

# After remediation: pass (needs >=1 evidence-cited row, zero defect rows)
$CLI review --projectId kestrel-quality-ops-1e9bfb41 --artifactId b2b147d0… \
  --verdict pass --rows '[{"rowKind":"proposal","severity":"medium",
   "message":"Five SERVQUAL dimensions scored with verbatim evidence.",
   "evidenceId":"c18a3f85…"}]'
# → "verdict": "pass", "status": "final"

$CLI factcheck --projectId kestrel-quality-ops-1e9bfb41   # exit 0: all artifacts pass

📊 4. Case Study Lens: Kestrel Energy (Article 22) and Lighthouse Transit (Article 31)

Synthetic illustration — not a production log. Both articles are illustrative composites from this repo's articles/ corpus; their metrics and transcripts are fictional.

  • Article 22 — Service Quality Management at Kestrel Energy (articles/22/README.md) is the scenario the verified session above mirrors: an energy retailer whose outage-communication journey exhibits a classic gap-5 collapse — the technical quality (meters get fixed) is fine, but functional quality (silence, repeated reference numbers, learning about resolution from the bill) destroys perceived quality. The five-dimension experience-audit is the instrument that makes the gap legible.
  • Article 31 — Riding the Current (articles/31/README.md) runs the same discipline in public transport: 18 weeks, failure demand 38%→18% (illustrative), and the critic's evidence-cited verdicts on every gated artifact. Its §3 documents the honest platform boundary: 29 tools over CLI/MCP, with host-pack installation unverified — the same boundary this course keeps.

The lesson from both: the gap model tells you where quality leaks; the gates tell you whether your diagnosis is evidence-backed enough to act on.

🔬 5. Hands-On Lab

In a disposable workspace (export ASD_WORKSPACE=$(mktemp -d)):

  1. Create the project and add two evidence records (one quote, one telemetry).
  2. Run method-run --methodId service-quality-diagnostic and observe the fail-closed error: requires at least 3 evidence records; project has 2. This is the gap-1 gate working.
  3. Add a third evidence record; re-run method-run and read the contract (acceptance, reviewGate, humanCheckpoints).
  4. Write a 5-node experience-audit. Deliberately omit severity on one node — observe accepted:false and the retained failures list (gap-2 gate).
  5. Write a node citing "evidenceId":"000…0" — observe the write-time rejection cites evidence "000…" which is not in the project evidence store.
  6. Fix the nodes, run the veto/pass sequence from §3.2, then factcheck and confirm exit 0.

Verification checklist

  • Method contract read from live method-run output (not from memory)
  • All five SERVQUAL dimensions present, each with issue, severity, and ≥1 real evidence id
  • Veto log artifact id captured; artifact observed in draft between veto and pass
  • factcheck exit code 0 after pass; non-zero while the defective draft existed

🚀 Key Takeaways

  • Perceived quality = experienced (technical + functional, image-filtered) vs. expected; overpromising is an operational defect.
  • The five gaps are five places assertions drift from evidence; ASD closes each with a distinct fail-closed gate — evidence, acceptance, critic review, factcheck, human approval.
  • service-quality-diagnostic → experience-audit: min 5 nodes, issue required by the closed kind schema, severity non-empty by acceptance, evidence per node.
  • Critic pass = evidence-cited rows and no defects; veto = defects, each with a fix; the veto log is a first-class persisted artifact.
