Module 08.2: Three-Tier KPI Trees — From Operational Telemetry to Financial Outcome

Course: Service Quality, Good Services Audits & Continuous Operations · Level: Advanced · Duration: 55 min · Format: Theory, Architecture, Verified CLI & Hands-on Lab

Learning Objectives

  1. Explain the Service Profit Chain's causal logic and its lead/lag measurement discipline, and the Outside In three-tier measurement architecture (relationship, journey, operational).
  2. Construct a kpi-tree artifact in which every KPI traces upward to a business outcome through typed edges, and every KPI cites evidence.
  3. Operate the two measurement methods that produce kpi-tree artifacts — kpi-tree-design and three-tier-measurement — and read their differing acceptance rules.
  4. Use the closed kpi-tree kind schema (metric required; tier, parent, target, baseline optional) correctly.
  5. Avoid the single-metric trap by proving financial linkage with baseline/target pairs.

1. Theoretical Grounding

1.1 The Service Profit Chain: causality you must instrument

Heskett, Sasser & Schlesinger's chain (books/20-the-service-profit-chain/05-measuring-the-chain.md) runs: internal service quality → employee satisfaction & retention → productivity → external service value → customer satisfaction → loyalty → revenue growth & profitability. Chapter 5 turns it from slogan to discipline with three rules that drive everything in this module:

  • Measure at the operating-unit level (store, route, contact-centre queue) where employees and customers actually interact — corporate aggregates are misleading averages.
  • Distinguish lead from lag indicators. Leading indicators live upstream (tool uptime, training completion, first-contact resolution, frontline turnover); lagging indicators are financial outcomes (net revenue retention, churn, margin). "Managing an organization purely by lag indicators is akin to driving a vehicle by looking only in the rearview mirror."
  • Respect time-lagged causality. A tooling fix may take 30 days to lift employee satisfaction, 90 to lift retention, 180 to reach the income statement. A KPI tree with no tier structure cannot express this — it collapses lead and lag into one flat scoreboard.

1.2 Outside In: the three-tier measurement architecture

Manning & Bodine (books/14-outside-in/06-measurement.md) supply the tier structure itself. Their argument: CX programs get defunded because they measure perceptions without proving economic linkage. The fix is a multi-tiered framework that measures customer perceptions across three levels and statistically joins them to money:

Tier Granularity Example metrics Audience
Relationship / financial Whole relationship, annual cadence CXi, NPS, retention, cost-to-serve Executives
Journey / experience After critical journey milestones 3-question diagnostic (meets needs / easy / enjoyable), CES, repeat-contact rate Journey owners
Operational Touchpoint telemetry, continuous Queue time, FCR, proactive-update coverage, error rates Frontline teams

The single-metric trap: a top-level NPS tells leadership how the brand is perceived but where nothing failed and what nobody must fix. Their proof technique is financial linkage — join survey respondent ids to transaction records and compare revenue/churn across perception cohorts (Sprint: billing-dispute FCR re-engineering saved $1.7B).

1.3 Why a tree

A tier list is a table; a KPI tree is a causal claim: operational drivers drive experience KPIs, which roll up to financial outcomes. That is why the ASD kpi-tree artifact kind has edges with a relation field and a parent node field — the structure is the argument. A KPI that cannot name its parent outcome is exactly the vanity metric the critic is instructed to reject ("Reject any KPI whose tier is missing or a placeholder, since an untiered KPI cannot be rolled up to the outcome it serves").

2. Agentic Topology & Method Contracts

Two registered methods produce kpi-tree artifacts, both in the measurement phase, both owned by the measurer role with reviewGate: critic:

kpi-tree-design three-tier-measurement
Sources Service Profit Chain ch.5; TISD ch.7 Service Mgmt & Marketing ch.2; Outside In ch.6
Input ≥1 artifact: service-blueprint or journey-map ("defining the outcomes to measure") ≥1 artifact: service-blueprint or kpi-tree
Steps synthesizer derives tiered tree → critic gate researcher finds ≥1 data point per tier per metric → critic gate
Acceptance tier non-empty; tier ∉ {tbd, unscored}; min-nodes 4 min-nodes 3; tier non-empty; min-evidence-per-node 1
Handoff → presenter → presenter

Read them as a pair: kpi-tree-design enforces structure (every KPI tiered, no placeholder tiers), three-tier-measurement enforces evidence (every tier claim cites a data point). The critic instructions differ accordingly — an untiered KPI and an uncorroborated tier claim are different failure modes.

3. Schemas & Verified Execution

3.1 The kpi-tree kind schema

asd schema-get --kind kpi-tree (verified): fieldPolicy: closed; node members id, label, evidence, fields; fields:

{ "tier":     { "type": "string", "required": false },
  "parent":   { "type": "string", "required": false },
  "metric":   { "type": "string", "required": true  },
  "target":   { "type": "number", "required": false },
  "baseline": { "type": "number", "required": false },
  "measure":  { "type": "string", "required": false } }

Omitting metric fails at write time (verified): artifact.nodes[0].fields.metric is required. Edges use the core ArtifactEdge shape — {id, from, to, relation, fields}; an edge field named label is rejected (edges[0]: unknown field "label").

Worked example: a three-tier tree for the follow-up backlog

Fixture. A telemetry export for Northfield's day-surgery follow-up journey:

metric,value,window
first_followup_miss_rate_pct,18,Q2
median_callback_latency_h,62,Q2
portal_duplicate_entries_per_patient,3,Q2
nurse_gp_chase_min_per_patient,40,Q2
followup_completed_within_14d_pct,61,Q2
followup_cost_per_episode_usd,74,Q2

Application. Build bottom-up: operational telemetry drives experience KPIs, which roll up to the financial outcome — one edge per causal claim.

Node Tier Metric Baseline Target Parent
n1 operational portal duplicate entries per patient 3 1 n3
n2 operational nurse GP-chase minutes per patient 40 10 n3
n3 experience median callback latency (h) 62 24 n4
n4 experience follow-ups completed within 14 days (%) 61 90 n5
n5 financial follow-up cost per episode ($) 74 38 —

Expected artifact (kpi-tree via kpi-tree-design; each node cites the telemetry evidence record 5d71a3e8…):

{ "kind": "kpi-tree",
  "nodes": [ { "id": "n3", "label": "Median callback latency",
               "evidence": [{ "evidenceId": "5d71a3e8c06b42f9a13d7e25b98c40f16d2a7c53e91b84f06c3d5a29e71f48b2" }],
               "fields": { "tier": "experience", "metric": "median callback latency h",
                           "baseline": 62, "target": 24 } } ],
  "edges": [ { "id": "e2", "from": "n2", "to": "n3", "relation": "drives", "fields": {} },
             { "id": "e4", "from": "n4", "to": "n5", "relation": "rolls-up", "fields": {} } ] }

Interpretation. The tree earns its keep on the n2 → n3 edge: the causal claim is that removing duplicate data entry frees the nurse hours that currently produce the 62h latency — a lead-to-lag chain with a built-in time delay that a flat scoreboard cannot express. The baseline/target pair on every node is what turns the financial claim at n5 from rhetoric into something the critic can test.

4. Case Study Lens: Servitizing a Capital-Equipment Vendor (Article 32)

Synthetic illustration — not a production log. Article 32 (articles/32/README.md, "Air in the Cylinders") is an illustrative composite.

A capital-equipment vendor shifting from selling compressors to selling uptime is the purest service-profit-chain scenario there is: the product's financial outcome (contract margin on uptime SLAs) is mechanically driven by operational telemetry (mean time between failures, first-time-fix rate) through experience KPIs (perceived reliability, effort). The article's KPI work is exactly the two-method pattern of this module: design the tiered tree from the blueprint's outcomes, then force every tier claim to cite a data point before the critic will let it through. The transferable lesson: a KPI tree is the contract between the service promise and the income statement — write it like one, with baselines, targets, and evidence.

5. Hands-On Lab

  1. On a fresh project, attempt method-run --methodId kpi-tree-design with no artifacts — record the fail-closed input-gate error.
  2. Produce a journey-map (see module 08.1 or course 05) and re-run; read the contract's acceptance block.
  3. Write a 4-node tree where one node has "tier":"tbd" — observe the forbid-field-values acceptance failure (tier ∈ {tbd, unscored} denied) and the retained draft.
  4. Fix the tier, add edges with relation values drives / rolls-up, pass the critic, and export-artifact the SVG. Open it: every node label and edge comes from your JSON.
  5. Run three-tier-measurement and attempt a node with no evidence — observe min-evidence-per-node fail.

Verification checklist

  • Every node has metric, a real tier, and ≥1 evidence id
  • Every operational/experience node is the from of at least one edge; the tree has a financial root
  • Baseline and target present on each node (financial linkage is provable)
  • SVG render opens and matches the JSON structure

Key Takeaways

  • Lead indicators (operational) drive experience KPIs, which roll up to lagging financial outcomes; time-lagged causality means flat scoreboards hide the mechanism.
  • kpi-tree-design enforces structure (tiered, no placeholders, ≥4 nodes, needs a blueprint/journey input); three-tier-measurement enforces evidence (≥1 data point per node).
  • The closed kind schema requires metric per node; edges are {id, from, to, relation, fields}.
  • A KPI without a parent outcome is a vanity metric — the critic gate exists to reject it.
