Service Quality, Good Services Audits & Continuous Operations

Module 08.3: Lou Downe's 15 Principles — Decoded into a Machine-Auditable Scorecard

Course: Service Quality, Good Services Audits & Continuous Operations · Level: Advanced · Duration: 60 min · Format: Theory, Architecture, Verified CLI & Hands-on Lab

Learning Objectives

  1. Explain the inductive origin of Downe's 15 principles, the principles-vs-rules distinction, and the three functional clusters.
  2. Score a live service against all fifteen principles using the Good Services Scale (pass / partial / fail), citing observable evidence for every below-target score.
  3. Operate good-services-audit-15 (owner measurer, gate critic, output kind insight-set) and satisfy its acceptance rules: all fifteen principles, every one evidence-cited.
  4. Work within the insight-set closed field policy — where scores legally live when score is not an allowed field.
  5. Turn audit output into a remediation backlog rather than a compliance trophy.

1. Theoretical Grounding

1.1 Principles from failure, not theory

Lou Downe's Good Services (books/05-good-services/02-the-fifteen-principles.md) derived the 15 principles inductively — from years at the UK Government Digital Service watching how services actually fail. The empirical claim: services vary infinitely in subject matter, but the ways they fail are remarkably consistent. Users cannot find them, cannot tell what they are for, get stranded mid-journey, face contradictory rules across channels, or are excluded by the technology.

That origin matters for auditing: each principle is a failure-mode inversion. Principle 7 ("agnostic of organisational structures") inverts Conway's Law — the organisation shipping its org chart to the customer. Principle 10 ("no dead ends") inverts the rejection-screen pathology. When you audit against principle N, you are really asking "does failure mode N exist here, and where is the evidence?"

1.2 Principles, not rules

Downe is explicit that services are too heterogeneous for prescriptive rules ("all forms must use radio buttons"). Principles describe necessary user outcomes ("a good service sets a user's expectations upfront") and leave the solution to the team. This is exactly why the audit must be evidence-based: you cannot verify a principle by inspecting the interface for a checkbox pattern; you verify it by observing whether users achieve the outcome — which is why the method contract demands cited post-launch evidence and why the critic rejects "any principle with a below-target score and no cited evidence."

1.3 The fifteen, in three clusters

Cluster # Principle (canonical, book 05 ch.2)
Usability & discoverability 1 Is easy to find
2 Clearly explains its purpose
3 Sets a user's expectations
4 Enables each user to complete the outcome they set out to do
5 Works in a way that is familiar
6 Requires no prior knowledge to use
Structural integrity & trust 7 Is agnostic of organisational structures
8 Requires the minimum possible steps to complete
9 Is consistent throughout
10 Has no dead ends
Control, inclusion & adaptability 11 Is usable by everyone, equally
12 Responds to change quickly
13 Clearly explains why a decision has been made
14 Makes it easy to get human assistance
15 Requires the right knowledge from the user

The Good Services Scale scores each pass / partial / fail on observable evidence. One genuine fail degrades the whole service — the principles form a chain, not a menu.

2. Agentic Topology & Method Contract

asd method-run --methodId good-services-audit-15 (verified, trimmed):

{ "outputKind": "insight-set", "owner": "measurer", "reviewGate": "critic",
  "inputs": [ { "kind": "artifact", "min": 1,
                "note": "a live or shipped service blueprint or journey map",
                "artifactKinds": ["service-blueprint", "journey-map"] },
              { "kind": "evidence-set", "min": 2,
                "note": "post-launch user feedback or support logs" } ],
  "steps": [ { "role": "researcher",
               "instruction": "Score the live service against each of the fifteen Good Services principles, citing evidence for every score below target." },
             { "role": "critic", "gate": true,
               "instruction": "Reject any principle with a below-target score and no cited evidence, and require all fifteen principles to be scored, not a sample." } ],
  "acceptance": [ { "rule": "min-nodes", "min": 15 },
                  { "rule": "field-non-empty", "field": "theme" },
                  { "rule": "field-non-empty", "field": "source" },
                  { "rule": "min-evidence-per-node", "min": 1 } ] }

Three contract details do the theoretical work:

  • min-nodes: 15 — sampling is prohibited. Downe's chain property (one fail degrades the whole) is enforced mechanically: you may not audit the convenient principles.
  • min-evidence-per-node: 1 — the principles-vs-rules point. A score is an evidence claim, not an interface opinion.
  • Output kind is insight-set, not experience-audit. The audit's product is a set of findings (insight per principle), which the handoff passes to the mapper role — the scorecard is a starting point for remediation design, not an end report. (experience-audit and service-quality-diagnostic produce the experience-audit kind — the gap-instrument from 08.1 and the continuous-audit instrument of 08.4. Do not conflate them.)

The insight-set kind schema is closed: insight (required), confidence, source, theme, opportunity, description. There is no score field — verified at write time: artifact.nodes[0].fields.score is not allowed by the closed kind schema. Encode the verdict in description ("verdict: fail") and the strength of evidence in confidence.

  "nodes": [
    { "id": "p07", "label": "Is agnostic of organisational structures",
      "evidence": [{ "evidenceId": "a64c2f81d35e09b74c18f6a2d95e7301b84c6f2a5d39e107c62b8f45a91d73e6" }],
      "fields": { "insight": "Patients traverse three portals that mirror three internal departments",
                  "theme": "org-agnostic", "source": "good-services-audit-15",
                  "confidence": "high", "description": "verdict: fail" } } ] }

Interpretation. The chain property shows immediately: the single pass (purpose) cannot rescue the journey, because principle 7's fail poisons every downstream step with re-entry work. Sequence remediation from the structural cluster — kill the triple re-entry (principles 7 and 8) before polishing expectation-setting, or the communications fix will be built on a journey that still strands users.

4. Case Study Lens: The Virtual Ward at Capacity (Article 33)

Synthetic illustration — not a production log. Article 33 (articles/33/README.md) is an illustrative composite of a telehealth scale-up "with the ASD Critic on every gate."

A virtual ward is a service where the control cluster (11–15) is existential: patients who cannot get human assistance or an explained decision are not inconvenienced — they are unsafe. The article's audit pattern matches this module: the full fifteen scored on post-launch evidence, no sampling, and the critic refusing to let a "mostly good" scorecard through when individual principles fail on evidence. The transferable lesson: the scorecard's value is the remediation sequence it forces — in the Kestrel run above, ten fails ordered by severity, starting with the dead-end that strands users after job closure.

5. Hands-On Lab

  1. Satisfy the inputs: a journey-map artifact and ≥2 post-launch evidence records (quote from support verbatims, document from a complaint log).
  2. Reproduce the fail-closed demo: write 3 nodes, observe accepted:false + failures:[min-nodes], and confirm the draft is retained (not deleted) via artifact-list.
  3. Attempt a node with "fields":{"score":2,...} — record the closed-schema rejection.
  4. Write all 15 canonical principles with verdicts in description; pass the critic with an evidence-cited proposal row.
  5. Run factcheck twice: once while the FAIL DEMO draft exists in the project (exit 1) and once in a clean project (exit 0). This is the CI behavior module 08.4 builds on.

Verification checklist

  • 15 nodes, labels matching the canonical principles, each with insight, theme, source, and ≥1 evidence id
  • Verdicts encoded inside the closed schema (description), not in an invented field
  • Critic pass recorded with an evidence-cited row
  • Both factcheck exit codes observed and explained

Key Takeaways

  • The 15 principles are inductive failure-mode inversions; audit = "does failure mode N exist, cite the evidence."
  • good-services-audit-15 → insight-set: all fifteen nodes mandatory, theme/source non-empty, evidence per node; handoff to mapper for remediation.
  • The insight-set kind is closed-field: no score; encode verdicts in description, evidence strength in confidence.
  • Partial audits are retained as drafts with explicit failures — the machine will not let you audit the convenient principles.
