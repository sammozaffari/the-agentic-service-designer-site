1. Theoretical Grounding & Literature Synthesis

From task statements to towers to spaces

Indi Young's construction recipe (canonized in Kalbach, Mapping Experiences ch. 6) is a disciplined climb up a cognitive ladder:

  1. Task-focused interviews ask about goals, reasoning, and reactions — never about the interface.
  2. Atomic task statements are extracted as granular, active-verb phrases: "check whether the last bus already left", "decide if the walk home feels safe".
  3. Affinity clustering stacks tasks that serve the same intent into a vertical tower.
  4. Tower grouping arranges towers along the horizontal axis into mental spaces — the broad purposes (Anticipate, Verify, Recover) that anchor the skyline from module 05.1.

The ladder metaphor matters because each rung has a different evidentiary burden. A task statement needs one verbatim line. A tower needs its tasks to cohere around an intent. A mental space is a hypothesis about human purpose — the most durable layer and the one most worth getting right, because (per Kalbach) purposes change far more slowly than features.

The three alignment states

Once the skyline exists, organizational capability is audited beneath it, and every tower–offering pair resolves to one of three states:

State Meaning Reading
Support The offering genuinely serves the tower's intent Keep, and protect the evidence link
Half-way The offering gestures at the intent but breaks the reasoning chain Highest-leverage redesign target
Counter-productive The offering actively works against the user's model Remove or invert

Half-way support is where services bleed. Kalbach's ch. 6 examples show the pattern: a feature exists, so the organization checks the box — but the user's tower is about certainty, and the feature delivers a possibility. The Meridian night bus illustrates it: the website posts stop changes (an offering under the Verify tower), yet driver route sheets lag by 4 days, so the rider's lived information channel — other passengers — contradicts the official one. The tower is half-supported, and the gap is invisible until both sides of the exchange are mapped.

Two mental models, one process

Outside-In (Manning & Bodine, ch. 4–5) adds the organizational half: frontline staff hold their own mental model of the same process, and divergence between the two models is where experience breaks. A rider thinks "the helpline will rebook me"; the agent's model is "I am authorized to read the website aloud." Both are locally rational. The failure lives in the seam — which is why the mental-model-alignment method builds two parallel journeys for the same process, stage by stage, and marks divergence with a quote from each side.

2. Agentic Topology & Contracts

The mental-model-alignment method (synthesis phase), verified live via asd method-run:

Contract member Verified value
outputKind journey-map (the two-sided map uses the journey substrate)
owner / ownerRole mapper
reviewGate critic
inputs evidence-set, min 3 — "stakeholder and customer interviews on the same process"
sources article 15; 09-mapping-experiences ch. 4; 14-outside-in ch. 5
handoff to ideator after critic pass

Step roles (verbatim from the contract):

  1. mapper — "Build two parallel journeys for the same process: the customer's and the frontline staff's, stage by stage."
  2. synthesizer — "Mark every stage where the two mental models diverge, citing the quote from each side."
  3. critic (gate) — "Reject any divergence marked without a quote from both sides."

The acceptance rule that changes everything: min-evidence-per-node: 2 (plus min-nodes: 3, field-non-empty: emotion, field-non-empty: touchpoint). A divergence claim must cite at least two evidence records — one from each mental model. A single quote can describe one side's experience; it cannot, by construction, evidence a divergence. This is the two-sidedness of alignment compiled into the deterministic gate.

Evidence-gated emotions. Both journeys carry an emotion field per stage, and the method instruction is binding: "Give each stage an emotion drawn from the quotes, never inferred." The mapper may not write "frustrated" because a stage feels frustrating; the word must be traceable to a cited line ("I felt like screaming at the phone"). Module 05.4 builds the sentiment curve on exactly this discipline.

3. Schemas & Verified Execution

Two-sided divergence as journey-map nodes

The map rides the journey-map kind schema (fields: stage, emotion required, touchpoint, actor, pain, gain, opportunity, description, notes, tags — closed policy). The alignment encoding convention:

{
  "id": "s2",
  "label": "Rider learns stop moved from a stranger",
  "evidence": [
    {"evidenceId": "cf7764fd…", "quote": "I found out from another passenger, not from Meridian."},
    {"evidenceId": "d2de7128…", "quote": "stop-change notices posted to the website 4 days before drivers receive updated route sheets"}
  ],
  "fields": {
    "stage": "journey",
    "actor": "rider + driver",
    "emotion": "confused",
    "touchpoint": "bus stop",
    "pain": "customer model: 'official channels tell me'; staff model: 'my route sheet is current' — divergence",
    "tags": ["divergence", "alignment:half-way"]
  }
}

Note what the acceptance engine forces: two evidenceIds — the rider quote and the operations telemetry. One without the other fails min-evidence-per-node: 2 and the write is retained as a failing draft.

4. Case Study Lens: Meridian Move Night Buses (Article 15)

Synthetic illustration — not a production log. Meridian Move is a fictional composite client from articles/15/; figures are illustrative.

Meridian Move ran AI-facilitated co-design workshops with night-bus riders — shift workers, carers, and students whose mental model of "getting home after dark" centered on certainty and safety, not on routes and timetables. The authority's operating model centered on vehicle-hours and coverage. Both models were coherent; the service failed in the seams between them.

The alignment read:

  • Support: community stop placement matched the rider tower "be dropped where people can see me" — workshops confirmed it with quotes from both riders and drivers.
  • Half-way: the cancellation SMS existed, but arrived ~11 minutes after scheduled departure — an offering under the Anticipate tower that delivered information too late to reason with.
  • Counter-productive: the helpline answered cancellation calls by re-reading the website, actively confirming the rider belief that "official channels don't know either."

The co-design output — a service specification turned into an implementation plan — reads as a tower-by-tower remediation of exactly these states. That is the point of the method: alignment states are not commentary, they are the backlog.

5. Hands-On Lab

export ASD_WORKSPACE=/tmp/asd-lab-05-02
CLI="node suite/packages/runtime/lib/cli.mjs"

# 1. Evidence from BOTH sides of one process (≥3 records; ≥2 per divergence node)
$CLI evidence-add --projectId <pid> --source cust-01 --kind quote --body "<rider verbatim>"
$CLI evidence-add --projectId <pid> --source staff-01 --kind quote --body "<agent verbatim>"
$CLI evidence-add --projectId <pid> --source ops-01 --kind telemetry --body "<operational log>"

# 2. Contract check — confirm min-evidence-per-node: 2
$CLI method-run --projectId <pid> --methodId mental-model-alignment

# 3. Write the two-sided map: divergence nodes cite one quote from EACH side
$CLI artifact-write --projectId <pid> --methodId mental-model-alignment \
  --title "Two-Sided Alignment Map" --nodes '<≥3 nodes, divergence nodes carry 2 evidence refs>'

# 4. Fail-closed demo: write a divergence node with ONE evidence ref
#    → {"accepted":false,"failures":[{"rule":"min-evidence-per-node", …}]}, retained as draft

# 5. Gate, factcheck
$CLI review --projectId <pid> --artifactId <aid> --verdict pass --rows '<≥1 evidence-cited row>'
$CLI factcheck --projectId <pid>

Verification checklist

  • Every divergence node cites ≥2 evidence records from different sides of the process.
  • Every stage emotion is a word traceable to a cited quote — none inferred.
  • The one-sided write was retained as a failing draft, and factcheck exited 1 while it existed.
  • The remediated map passed the critic gate and factcheck exited 0.

Key Takeaways

  • Towers are climbed, not declared: task statements → towers → mental spaces, each rung with its own evidentiary burden.
  • Every tower–offering pair resolves to support, half-way, or counter-productive — and half-way support is the highest-leverage redesign target.
  • Divergence is a two-sided claim: min-evidence-per-node: 2 makes a one-sided divergence structurally unwritable.
  • Emotions are evidence-gated: drawn from quotes, never inferred — the discipline that makes module 05.4's sentiment curve honest.
