Learning Objectives

By completing this module, you will master:

  1. Theoretical Foundations: John Seddon's failure-demand thesis (Vanguard school) as carried by the corpus — demand caused by a failure to do something, or do something right, for the customer — versus value demand, and why the distinction reorganizes whole services.
  2. Agent Topology & Contracts: The failure-demand-analysis method (research phase, researcher owner) producing an insight-set, with the critic rule that a "customer error" root cause needs at least two coded contacts.
  3. Architecture & Schemas: The closed insight-set kind schema and the min-evidence-per-node: 2 acceptance rule that forces every insight onto convergent evidence — quote and telemetry.
  4. Case Study Execution: Article 11 — Fernwood Council's housing repairs, where 41% of open jobs attracted a status-chase call: ~3,100 failure-demand calls a month created by silence, not residents.
  5. Adversarial Verification: A real run coding repeat-call telemetry into three evidence-backed insights.
  6. Hands-on Practice: Run the analysis, hit the gates, and feed the insights back into the journey map.

1. Theoretical Grounding & Literature Synthesis

Value demand vs failure demand

Seddon's distinction (carried in the corpus by Service Design for Business, ch. 2 and ch. 7, and Grönroos's service-recovery treatment in Service Management and Marketing, ch. 6):

  • Value demand — contacts the service exists to serve: "please fix my heating." You want more of it, handled well.
  • Failure demand — "inbound contacts generated because an earlier step was delayed, ambiguous, inaccurate, or broken" (SDfB ch. 2): "where is my repair?", "you said you'd call back", "your text told me nothing." The scale is not marginal: SDfB reports 40–60% of contact-center and branch capacity in legacy service firms consumed by failure demand.

Three properties make failure demand strategically decisive:

  1. It is manufactured upstream. The caller did not create the demand; the service did — through silence, latency, or error. Treating failure demand as a call-handling problem optimizes the symptom and locks in the cause.
  2. It is measurable as money. SDfB ch. 7's evaluation recipe: multiply failure-demand volume by unit channel cost to get total avoidable operational waste, then track the volume reduction post-launch as direct bottom-line savings. Eliminating failure demand unlocks capacity without headcount increases (SDfB ch. 2).
  3. It recurs until the root cause dies. Grönroos (ch. 6) closes the loop: recovery is incomplete if it ends at appeasement — every recovery incident must feed root-cause data back into operations. His recovery-paradox caveat matters here too: recovery can increase loyalty only for minor, first-time failures; repeated systemic failure (the definition of failure demand) destroys trust, and no apology script fixes it.

Why coding beats counting

The unit of analysis is not the call but the coded contact: each contact labeled value or failure, each failure tagged with its upstream cause. Only then can contacts be grouped by root cause rather than by channel — the move that turns a noise metric into a design backlog. And a crucial guardrail from the method itself: "customer error" is the root cause lazy analyses reach for, so the critic gate rejects it unless at least two coded contacts back it.

2. Agentic Topology & Contracts

failure-demand-analysis — verified live from asd method-run. Note first: it is a research-phase method, and its outputKind is insight-set, not a map. Its job is to arm the mapper.

Contract member Verified value
phase research (method-phase enum — not the discover/define/develop/deliver project enum)
outputKind insight-set
owner / ownerRole researcher
reviewGate critic
inputs evidence-set, min 1 — "contact logs or call reasons coded as value demand or failure demand"
handoff to mapper, artifact kind insight-set — the insights land where journeys are drawn

Step roles (verbatim):

  1. researcher — "Code a sample of contacts as value demand or failure demand, noting the upstream cause behind each failure."
  2. synthesizer — "Group failure-demand contacts into insights by root cause, not by channel."
  3. critic (gate) — "Reject a root cause of 'customer error' that is not backed by at least two coded contacts."

Acceptance rules: min-nodes: 3, min-evidence-per-node: 2, field-non-empty: insight. The middle rule is the method's conscience: an insight resting on a single contact is an anecdote; the gate demands convergence — typically a telemetry log plus a verbatim quote describing the same failure from the lived side.

3. Schemas & Verified Execution

The insight-set kind schema (verbatim from asd schema-get --kind insight-set)

Closed field policy: insight (required), confidence, source, theme, opportunity, description, notes, tags. Edges are forbidden ("edge": {"policy": "forbidden"}) — an insight-set is a set of defensible claims, not a graph; relationships between insights belong in maps that reference them. A verified consequence: fields outside the list are hard-rejected — writing demand, upstreamCause, or volume as node fields fails with "…is not allowed by the closed kind schema" (exit 1). Upstream cause and volume live inside the insight/description prose, anchored by the cited telemetry.

Verified terminal session (real ids, disposable workspace 2026-08-22)

Every insight cites two records — one telemetry, one lived-experience quote. That is min-evidence-per-node: 2 doing its job: the number says how much, the quote says what it feels like, and neither alone would have passed.

Demand-coding sequence diagram

Worked example: a failure-demand register with a declared severity rule

Fixture — three telemetry rows (Meridian evening operations, minted via sd_evidence_add --kind telemetry):

[{"id":"f91c67f7…","body":"repeat-call log: 41 of 60 evening helpline calls are re-calls after a cancellation SMS with no rebooking offer"},
 {"id":"1d6120dc…","body":"cancellation SMS sent on average 11 minutes after scheduled departure"},
 {"id":"d2de7128…","body":"website stop-change notices published 4 days before driver route sheets update"}]

Step 1 — declare the severity rule before scoring. severity = volume(0–4) + cost(0–3) + recurrence(0–3), where volume bands monthly contacts (<50=1, 50–200=2, 200–600=3, >600=4), cost is unit channel cost × handling time relative to a £5 baseline call, and recurrence counts how many consecutive months the cause has appeared (capped at 3). Scale 0–10; declared up front so any reviewer can recompute and falsify.

Step 2 — the register. Each row links one telemetry record to its upstream cause, the journey hotspot it feeds, and its scored severity:

telemetry id upstream cause friction hotspot (journey node) volume / cost / recurrence severity
f91c67f7… helpline lacks rebooking authority n3 "Call helpline after cancellation" 4 / 3 / 3 10
1d6120dc… alert latency past departure time n2 "Learn stop moved from a stranger" 3 / 2 / 2 7
d2de7128… backstage notice/route-sheet lag n2 (same hotspot, second cause) 2 / 1 / 3 6

Step 3 — expected artifact. The register's top row becomes an insight-set node whose evidence converges telemetry with the lived quote, and whose journey map cites the set via references: derived-from:

{"id":"i3","label":"Helpline cannot rebook, only re-read",
 "evidence":[{"evidenceId":"f91c67f7…"},{"evidenceId":"433237cb…"}],
 "fields":{"insight":"Severity 10/10: agents repeat website content because they lack rebooking authority, generating 41 of 60 evening calls",
           "theme":"failure demand","description":"Upstream cause: missing frontline authority. Rule: volume4+cost3+recurrence3."}}

Interpretation. The register's value is the ordering, not the arithmetic: a declared rule turns "the phones are busy" into a ranked backlog where the top severity names its kill point. Note also that one hotspot collects two causes — severity is scored per cause, because each cause needs its own fix even when the pain lands on the same stage.

4. Case Study Lens: Fernwood Council Housing Repairs (Article 11)

Synthetic illustration — not a production log. Fernwood Council is a fictional composite client from articles/11/; figures are illustrative.

Fernwood handled 28,000 repair requests a year across ~19,000 council homes — and 41% of open jobs attracted at least one status-chase contact, about 3,100 failure-demand calls a month, because no system, letter, or person could tell a resident what was happening. The service was spending more energy administering repairs than fixing homes.

The failure-demand lens reorganized the engagement:

  • The calls were not the problem. Each status-chase was manufactured by an upstream step — a letter that said nothing, a scheduler that never confirmed, an operative visit nobody logged. Coding contacts by root cause (not by channel) turned a "call volume crisis" into a list of backstage design faults.
  • The blueprint became the diagnostic instrument. Once the resident journey spine was validated, fail points were annotated where the backstage broke the frontstage promise — the same "kill points" in the concept diagram above.
  • Savings were stated in SDfB ch. 7 terms: failure-demand volume × unit channel cost = avoidable waste; the pilot tracked volume reduction as the bottom-line result. That is the measurement contract failure demand deserves — and why the insight-set hands off to the mapper, not to a call-center script.

5. Hands-On Lab

export ASD_WORKSPACE=/tmp/asd-lab-05-05
CLI="node suite/packages/runtime/lib/cli.mjs"

# 1. Evidence: at least one coded contact log (telemetry) + caller verbatims (quote)
$CLI evidence-add --projectId <pid> --source tel-01 --kind telemetry --body "<coded repeat-call log>"
$CLI evidence-add --projectId <pid> --source int-01 --kind quote --body "<caller verbatim>"

# 2. Contract: confirm phase research, outputKind insight-set, min-evidence-per-node 2
$CLI method-run --projectId <pid> --methodId failure-demand-analysis

# 3. Write ≥3 insights, each citing ≥2 records (one telemetry + one quote)
$CLI artifact-write --projectId <pid> --methodId failure-demand-analysis \
  --title "Failure Demand Insights" --nodes '<json>'

# 4. Fail-closed demos:
#    a) an insight citing ONE record → accepted:false,
#       failures[rule=min-evidence-per-node, "cites 1 evidence refs, needs 2"]
#    b) a field outside the closed schema (e.g. "upstreamCause") → exit 1 hard rejection

# 5. Gate, then hand off: reference the insight-set from your journey-map
#    (journey-map references allow derived-from → insight-set, provenance required)
$CLI review --projectId <pid> --artifactId <aid> --verdict pass --rows '<evidence-cited row>'
$CLI factcheck --projectId <pid>

Verification checklist

  • Every insight groups contacts by root cause, not by channel.
  • Every insight cites ≥2 evidence records — convergent telemetry + quote.
  • A one-record insight produced a retained failing draft with the exact min-evidence-per-node failure.
  • No insight claims "customer error" without two coded contacts behind it.

Key Takeaways

  • Failure demand is manufactured upstream: silence, latency, and error in earlier steps — 40–60% of contact capacity in legacy services.
  • Code contacts as value vs failure with the upstream cause; group insights by root cause, never by channel.
  • The insight-set is convergence-gated: min-evidence-per-node: 2 makes anecdote insights unwritable, and edges are forbidden — claims, not graphs.
  • Failure demand is the journey map's fuel: the insight-set hands off to the mapper, and the remediated journey (05.6) is where the demand goes to die.
