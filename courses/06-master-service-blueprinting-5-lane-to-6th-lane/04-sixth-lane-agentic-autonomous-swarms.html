Module 06.4: The Sixth Lane Done Right — Orchestration Plans & Human Gates

Learning Objectives

By completing this module, you will master:

  1. Theoretical Foundations: experience orchestration theory (Risdon & Quattlebaum, books/08) and the module 06.2 test — a lane earns its existence only if it has a distinct governor.
  2. What the Sixth Lane Actually Is: a plane of orchestration records — plans, dispatches, retries, typed handoffs, human gates — not a fantasy lane of autonomous agent swarms.
  3. Agent Topology & Contracts: the four real orchestration tools (sd_orchestration_plan, sd_orchestration_status, sd_orchestration_dispatch, sd_orchestration_handoff) and their exact parameter shapes.
  4. Honesty Boundaries: the runtime is record-only; host-agent-spawn is recorded as explicitly unsupported, never simulated.
  5. Case Study Lens: how Article 11's pilot coordination reads as an orchestration plan with human gates.
  6. Hands-on Practice: create a plan, record a failed-retryable dispatch, retry it, and complete a typed handoff — live.

1. Theoretical Grounding & Literature Synthesis

Orchestration is a discipline, not a swarm

Orchestrating Experiences (Risdon & Quattlebaum; books/08-orchestrating-experiences/02-what-is-orchestration.md) defines experience orchestration as the deliberate alignment and real-time coordination of organizational actors, channels, environments, processes, and technology systems around a shared understanding of the customer's journey. Its governing metaphor is the conductor: the conductor produces no sound directly — they interpret the score, set tempo, cue sections, and keep individual virtuosos harmonized. The customer journey is the score; the specialist teams (and, now, software agents) are the sections; orchestration is the discipline that keeps them coherent.

Two tenets from that tradition discipline everything in this module:

  • Creating conditions vs. mandating outcomes (books/08/02): you cannot directly control a customer's experience; you design the conditions — information availability, timing, data persistence, frontline discretion — that make the desired experience probable. An orchestration lane therefore records who is cued to do what, under which condition, with which artifact, not a fiction of commanded outcomes.
  • Sense-and-respond loops (books/08/03, the orchestration canvas): an organization that cannot sense signals cannot orchestrate responses. In toolchain terms: dispatch records and status reads are the sensing; retries and handoffs are the responding.

Does a "sixth lane" survive the module 06.2 test?

Module 06.2 set the bar: a lane exists only if its boundary has a distinct governor. "AI agents" drawn as a sixth swimlane of boxes making autonomous decisions fails that test — it is just the support lane with new nouns, and its governor is still system ownership. But there is a sixth demarcation with a genuinely different governor: orchestration authority — who sequences the work, what happens on failure, when a human must approve. That plane has its own vocabulary (plans, attempts, retries, handoffs, gates), its own failure modes (stalled steps, exhausted retries, unowned handoffs), and its own accountability (the human checkpoint authority). Drawn honestly, the sixth lane is the orchestration lane: the record of how all the other lanes' work is coordinated.

This is what the standalone runtime actually implements. There are no swarm primitives in the tool registry — no agent-spawning, no autonomous decision loops. There are four orchestration tools, and their own descriptions state the honesty boundary in plain text: "The plan records work; it does not claim that this host can spawn agents."

2. Agentic Topology & Contracts — The Four Real Tools

Probed live against asd help and executed in this course's verification run:

sd_orchestration_plan — declare the work

Parameters: projectId, idempotencyKey, title, steps (JSON array, required) — each step {id, role, instruction, maxRetries?} where role must be one of the ten registered practice roles (orchestrator, researcher, synthesizer, mapper, ideator, prototyper, measurer, critic, presenter, librarian). The persisted plan is stamped scheduler: "host-independent" — the record is portable across host packs.

sd_orchestration_dispatch — record an attempt

Parameters: projectId, planId, stepId, idempotencyKey, capability (record-only | host-agent-spawn), outcome (succeeded | failed; omit to leave the step running), error (failure detail), retryable (boolean — whether a later dispatch may retry), retryOf (prior dispatch id when this is a retry). Replaying an idempotency key returns the original record instead of duplicating it.

sd_orchestration_status — sense the state

Parameters: projectId, optional planId. Returns {plans, dispatches, handoffs} — step statuses (queued | running | succeeded | failed | blocked), attempt counts, retry chains, and handoff records. This is the sense-and-respond loop's sensing surface.

sd_orchestration_handoff — move work between roles, typed

Parameters: projectId, planId, idempotencyKey, from and to (practice roles), condition (what the receiving role requires), and an optional typed artifact pointer: artifactId + artifactKind. The tool validates the pointer against the actual artifact — wrong kind or missing artifact fails closed. artifactKind without artifactId is rejected outright.

3. Verified Execution — The Real Orchestration Session

Everything below is real output from this course's disposable-workspace run (project fernwood-repairs-blueprint-sprint-7cee33e4).

CLI="node suite/packages/runtime/lib/cli.mjs"
PID=fernwood-repairs-blueprint-sprint-7cee33e4

# 1. Plan — steps is a JSON array of {id, role, instruction, maxRetries?}
$CLI orchestration-plan --projectId $PID --idempotencyKey bp-plan-1 \
  --title "Blueprint production plan" --steps '[
   {"id":"s1-lay-lanes","role":"mapper","instruction":"Lay out the five blueprint lanes from evidence","maxRetries":2},
   {"id":"s2-annotate-lines","role":"presenter","instruction":"Annotate line of visibility and wait points","maxRetries":1},
   {"id":"s3-gate","role":"critic","instruction":"Reject placeholder lanes and untraceable fail points","maxRetries":0}]'
# → {"plan":{"kind":"orchestration-plan","id":"4f5a8d97ca194e24969c9a35f364354b",
#    "scheduler":"host-independent","steps":[{"id":"s1-lay-lanes","status":"queued","retries":0}, …]}}

# 2. Dispatch — record-only success
$CLI orchestration-dispatch --projectId $PID --planId 4f5a8d97… --stepId s1-lay-lanes \
  --idempotencyKey d1 --capability record-only --outcome succeeded
# → {"dispatch":{"capability":"record-only","status":"succeeded","attempt":1},"unsupported":false}

# 3. Dispatch — failure WITH retry semantics
$CLI orchestration-dispatch --projectId $PID --planId 4f5a8d97… --stepId s2-annotate-lines \
  --idempotencyKey d2 --capability record-only --outcome failed \
  --error "presenter host timed out annotating wait points" --retryable true
# → {"dispatch":{"status":"failed","retryable":true,"error":"presenter host timed out…"}}

# 4. Retry — a new dispatch chained with retryOf
$CLI orchestration-dispatch --projectId $PID --planId 4f5a8d97… --stepId s2-annotate-lines \
  --idempotencyKey d3 --capability record-only --outcome succeeded --retryOf 02e1e16edae0487bad42e30fbf6137a6
# → {"dispatch":{"stepId":"s2-annotate-lines","status":"succeeded","attempt":2,
#    "retryOf":"02e1e16edae0487bad42e30fbf6137a6"}}

# 5. The honesty boundary, verbatim — host-agent-spawn is NOT simulated
$CLI orchestration-dispatch --projectId $PID --planId 4f5a8d97… --stepId s3-gate \
  --idempotencyKey d4 --capability host-agent-spawn
# → {"dispatch":{"status":"unsupported", …}, "unsupported":true,
#    "error":"host-agent-spawn capability is not supplied by this host; only record-only scheduling is supported"}

# 6. Typed handoff — work moves between roles with an artifact pointer
$CLI orchestration-handoff --projectId $PID --planId 4f5a8d97… --idempotencyKey h1 \
  --from mapper --to critic \
  --condition "Five lanes populated; gate review required before ideator handoff" \
  --artifactId 4d64fbd3395440efa44c17363e5b8f10 --artifactKind service-blueprint
# → {"handoff":{"id":"9687048958f64c6b8937d20191e4c243","from":"mapper","to":"critic",
#    "artifactKind":"service-blueprint","status":"recorded", …}}

# 6b. Typed handoff fails closed on a kind lie:
$CLI orchestration-handoff … --artifactId 4d64fbd3… --artifactKind roadmap
# → asd orchestration-handoff: artifact kind mismatch: 4d64fbd3… is service-blueprint, not roadmap

Notice what this session is: an auditable record of who was cued to do what, what happened, what was retried, and what changed hands — with humans holding the gates. That is the sixth lane done right. Notice also what it is not: nothing in this transcript claims an agent was spawned, decided, or acted autonomously. Where a host cannot spawn agents, the record says unsupported — explicitly, in writing, forever.

Worked example: One orchestration run — retry, block, handoff

This exercise drives a three-step plan through the three outcomes the ledger can record: a retryable failure that recovers, a non-retryable failure that blocks, and a typed handoff of finished work.

Fixture — the plan (orchestration-plan, steps as a JSON array):

[
  {"id":"s1-lay-lanes","role":"mapper","instruction":"Lay the five lanes from evidence","maxRetries":2},
  {"id":"s2-annotate","role":"presenter","instruction":"Mark lines and wait points","maxRetries":1},
  {"id":"s3-gate","role":"critic","instruction":"Gate review of the draft","maxRetries":0}
]

Step-by-step run. Step s1 succeeds first attempt. Step s2 fails retryable — the presenter host times out — then a second dispatch chained with retryOf succeeds at attempt 2. Step s3 fails non-retryable — no critic reviewer is available this week — and with maxRetries: 0 the step goes straight to blocked. The finished draft is then handed from mapper to critic with a typed artifact pointer, so the blocked step has a concrete object waiting for a human to re-plan it.

$CLI orchestration-plan --projectId $PID --idempotencyKey wx-plan-1 \
  --title "Blueprint run with failure drill" --steps '<fixture above>'
# → {"plan":{"id":"a1c2e8…","scheduler":"host-independent","steps":[…"status":"queued"…]}}

$CLI orchestration-dispatch --projectId $PID --planId a1c2e8… --stepId s1-lay-lanes \
  --idempotencyKey wx-d1 --capability record-only --outcome succeeded
# → {"dispatch":{"status":"succeeded","attempt":1}}

$CLI orchestration-dispatch --projectId $PID --planId a1c2e8… --stepId s2-annotate \
  --idempotencyKey wx-d2 --capability record-only --outcome failed \
  --error "presenter host timed out annotating wait points" --retryable true
# → {"dispatch":{"status":"failed","retryable":true,"attempt":1}}

$CLI orchestration-dispatch --projectId $PID --planId a1c2e8… --stepId s2-annotate \
  --idempotencyKey wx-d3 --capability record-only --outcome succeeded --retryOf <wx-d2 dispatch id>
# → {"dispatch":{"status":"succeeded","attempt":2,"retryOf":"<wx-d2 dispatch id>"}}

$CLI orchestration-dispatch --projectId $PID --planId a1c2e8… --stepId s3-gate \
  --idempotencyKey wx-d4 --capability record-only --outcome failed \
  --error "no critic reviewer available before Friday" --retryable false
# → {"dispatch":{"status":"failed","retryable":false}}

$CLI orchestration-handoff --projectId $PID --planId a1c2e8… --idempotencyKey wx-h1 \
  --from mapper --to critic --condition "Draft lanes complete; gate review pending" \
  --artifactId 4d64fbd3395440efa44c17363e5b8f10 --artifactKind service-blueprint
# → {"handoff":{"from":"mapper","to":"critic","artifactKind":"service-blueprint","status":"recorded"}}

Expected result — orchestration-status reads the whole ledger back:

{"plans":[{"id":"a1c2e8…","steps":[
   {"id":"s1-lay-lanes","status":"succeeded","retries":0},
   {"id":"s2-annotate","status":"succeeded","retries":1},
   {"id":"s3-gate","status":"blocked","retries":0}]}],
 "dispatches":[
   {"stepId":"s2-annotate","attempt":1,"status":"failed","error":"presenter host timed out…","retryable":true},
   {"stepId":"s2-annotate","attempt":2,"status":"succeeded","retryOf":"<wx-d2 dispatch id>"},
   {"stepId":"s3-gate","attempt":1,"status":"failed","retryable":false}],
 "handoffs":[{"from":"mapper","to":"critic","artifactId":"4d64fbd3…","artifactKind":"service-blueprint"}]}

Interpretation. The retry chain (attempt: 2 with retryOf) and the terminal blocked state are different by design: a retry says the machine may try again, a block says the machine declines to decide and the question passes to a human — with the handoff record telling that human exactly which artifact is waiting. Nothing here is simulated autonomy; the ledger records who was cued, what happened, and where judgment is now required.

4. Case Study Lens: Article 11's Pilot as an Orchestration Plan

Synthetic illustration — not a production log.

Article 11's Phase 4 pilot (two repairs teams, weeks 11–20) reads naturally as an orchestration plan rather than a project memo: steps for retraining the contact centre (role: presenter), reconfiguring the scheduler (role: prototyper), weekly failure review (role: critic), each with retry limits and a typed handoff of the future-state service-blueprint between roles. The article's "weekly steering gate" is precisely the human handoff-approval checkpoint — authority human-designer — that the method contract declares. Read this way, the sixth lane adds no new mysticism to the engagement; it adds the ledger of coordination that a five-lane blueprint has nowhere to put.

5. Hands-On Lab

export ASD_WORKSPACE=/tmp/asd-lab06
CLI="node suite/packages/runtime/lib/cli.mjs"

# 1. Plan your own blueprint production (steps MUST be a JSON array)
$CLI orchestration-plan --projectId <pid> --idempotencyKey my-plan-1 --title "My blueprint plan" --steps '[
  {"id":"lay","role":"mapper","instruction":"Lay five lanes","maxRetries":2},
  {"id":"annotate","role":"presenter","instruction":"Mark lines and waits","maxRetries":1},
  {"id":"gate","role":"critic","instruction":"Gate review","maxRetries":0}]'

# 2. Record a failed, retryable dispatch; then retry it with retryOf
$CLI orchestration-dispatch --projectId <pid> --planId <planId> --stepId annotate \
  --idempotencyKey a1 --capability record-only --outcome failed --error "timeout" --retryable true
$CLI orchestration-dispatch --projectId <pid> --planId <planId> --stepId annotate \
  --idempotencyKey a2 --capability record-only --outcome succeeded --retryOf <dispatch-id-from-a1>

# 3. Confirm the honesty boundary yourself
$CLI orchestration-dispatch --projectId <pid> --planId <planId> --stepId gate \
  --idempotencyKey a3 --capability host-agent-spawn
# EXPECT: {"unsupported":true,"error":"host-agent-spawn capability is not supplied by this host…"}

# 4. Hand off your blueprint to the critic with a typed pointer; read state with status
$CLI orchestration-handoff --projectId <pid> --planId <planId> --idempotencyKey h1 \
  --from mapper --to critic --condition "lanes complete" --artifactId <aid> --artifactKind service-blueprint
$CLI orchestration-status --projectId <pid> --planId <planId>

Verification checklist

  • Your plan shows scheduler: "host-independent" and all steps queued.
  • The retry dispatch shows attempt: 2 and a retryOf pointer to the failed attempt.
  • The host-agent-spawn dispatch returned unsupported: true — not a simulation.
  • A handoff with a wrong artifactKind was rejected with the kind-mismatch error.
  • orchestration-status reads back plans, dispatches, and handoffs as one coherent ledger.

Key Takeaways

  • The sixth lane earns its demarcation because its governor is orchestration authority — sequencing, failure policy, and human gates — not because "AI" needs its own swimlane of boxes.
  • The real tools are a ledger: orchestration_plan (steps + retry limits), orchestration_dispatch (capability/outcome/retryable/retryOf), orchestration_status (the sensing surface), orchestration_handoff (typed role-to-role artifact movement).
  • Honesty is mechanical: host-agent-spawn is recorded as unsupported and returned explicitly. This runtime never simulates autonomy it does not have.
  • Humans hold the gates: scope-confirmation before the run, handoff-approval before work moves on, phase advance only with a signed receipt. Orchestration plans coordinate; people authorize.
