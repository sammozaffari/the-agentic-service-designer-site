📖 1. Theoretical Grounding & Literature Synthesis

Shostack's original argument: services are processes, and processes can be engineered

G. Lynn Shostack introduced the service blueprint in "How to Design a Service" (European Journal of Marketing, 1982) and "Designing Services That Deliver" (Harvard Business Review, 1984). Her move was radical for a marketing audience: she insisted that a service is not an atmosphere or a brand promise but a process rendered against time, and that anything processual can be documented, measured, and engineered with the same rigour as a manufacturing line (see books/02-this-is-service-design-thinking/01-what-is-service-design.md for the 1982/1984 publication lineage, and books/07-designing-for-service-key-issues/02-service-design-and-management.md for the managerial framing).

Her canonical demonstration was a retail discount brokerage (books/02/01, books/07/02). Management believed customer satisfaction turned on broker demeanor. The blueprint proved otherwise: satisfaction depended on backstage check-clearing speed and error-free monthly statement generation — processes the customer never saw. By drawing the line of visibility and separating what the customer experiences from what the organization executes, Shostack could attach explicit time standards to backstage steps and locate failure points that no amount of frontline charm could compensate for. Three durable claims follow:

  • A service's failure modes live below the waterline. The customer-visible surface is the tip of a socio-technical iceberg; redesigning the interface while leaving backstage feeds broken makes things worse, not better (books/01-this-is-service-design-doing/01-why-service-design.md recounts the [illustrative] billing-redesign case where unaddressed backstage billing-engine errors raised call-centre volume 30% after launch).
  • A blueprint is a specification you can compute over. Once steps, owners, and crossings are explicit, you can calculate cycle times, count handoffs, set standards, and price failure — which is exactly what Grönroos's service-process chapter (books/19-service-management-and-marketing/07-designing-and-managing-the-service-process.md) means by moving "from customer perceptions to operational engineering."
  • Documentation beats demeanor. An undrawn service cannot be managed, because nobody can see the whole of it. The blueprint is the shared object that lets finance, operations, IT, and frontline staff argue about the same service.

Bitner, Ostrom & Morgan (2008): the codified five-component form

Mary Jo Bitner, Amy Ostrom, and Felicia Morgan formally codified the practice in "Service Blueprinting: A Practical Technique for Service Innovation" (California Management Review, 2008). James Kalbach's chapter-length treatment (books/09-mapping-experiences/05-service-blueprints.md — one of the service-blueprinting method's registered sources) summarizes their formulation: a blueprint is an operational process diagram that depicts the service system so all stakeholders can understand it objectively, aligning five components — physical evidence, customer actions, onstage/frontstage contact, backstage contact, and support processes — along a shared chronological spine, divided by three canonical lines (interaction, visibility, internal interaction). Their express-parcel case (books/09/05) is the classic proof of mechanism: a 15-minute backstage baggage delay at a sorting hub cascaded across lanes into a failed frontstage delivery commitment. Satisfaction is an emergent property of backstage synchronization — which is why a diagram that only shows the customer's view cannot find that class of failure.

Blueprint ≠ journey map — the distinction this course enforces

Journey map Service blueprint
Question answered What is it like to be the customer? How does the organization produce that experience?
Perspective Subjective, first-person, emotional Objective, third-person, operational
Primary content Stages, touchpoints, emotions, quotes Lanes, actors, systems, lines, fail/wait points
Failure it detects Pain, confusion, dead ends Bottlenecks, unowned handoffs, missing standards
Kalbach's summary "illuminates subjective human experience" "details how the organization delivers it" (books/09/05)

A journey map that showed an unexplained two-hour discharge wait could not say why; the blueprint revealed the wait spanned four disjointed backstage departments — pharmacy, ward sign-off, billing, transport — and a single new "Discharge Coordinator" role eliminated the handoff friction (books/09/05, hospital case). Read the two artifacts as a pair: the journey map supplies the symptom, the blueprint supplies the diagnosis. This is why the @asd schema lets a service-blueprint carry a typed derived-from reference to a journey-map — the toolchain hard-wires the theory's division of labour into the data model.

Why "architecture" is the right word — and why the tooling exists

Architecture is the set of load-bearing decisions that are expensive to change later. A blueprint earns the word because it fixes the contracts that everything else depends on: which actor owns which step, what the customer may see, where system boundaries sit, what happens when a step fails. Traditional blueprinting died in workshops because the artifact was a static poster: it drifted from reality within weeks and nobody could prove which claim rested on which observation. The agentic toolchain exists to make the blueprint executable architecture: typed nodes with mandatory lane fields, every node citing content-addressed evidence, an adversarial critic gate on the write path, and a deterministic compiler that renders the same SVG from the same JSON every time. The theory's invariants become the schema's acceptance rules.

Methodological invariants

  • Referential integrity: every blueprint node must resolve to a SHA-256 evidence record in the project's evidence store (per-project JSON under ASD_WORKSPACE, default ~/.asd/projects). A node without evidence is not an observation; it is an opinion, and the acceptance rules reject it.
  • Typing over chat memory: no design handoff lives in a conversation. State is serialized to structured JSON on disk — artifacts, review verdicts, veto logs, orchestration records.
  • Adversarial falsification: the critic role sits on the write path and tries to disprove the blueprint before any client sees it. Shostack wanted engineering rigour; the gate is how rigour survives contact with deadline pressure.

🤖 2. Agentic Topology & Role Contracts

The service-blueprinting method (phase synthesis, owner mapper) declares exactly three execution steps, and each maps to real tools:

Role Contract step (verbatim) Tools the role calls Output
mapper (owner) "Lay out physical evidence, customer actions, frontstage, backstage and support processes as five lanes." sd_evidence_query, sd_method_run, sd_artifact_write Draft service-blueprint with lane-typed nodes
presenter "Annotate the line of visibility and mark every wait point observed." sd_artifact_get, sd_artifact_write (edge labels, marker: "W" fields) Line/wait annotations on the same draft
critic (gate) "Reject any lane left as a placeholder rather than a named process step." sd_review (veto/pass rows), veto persists as review-veto-log Verdict; draft blocked until pass

The contract also fixes reviewGate: "critic", evidenceRules: {traceability: "required", provenance: "required"}, a handoff to the ideator role with artifactKind: "service-blueprint", and two human checkpoints: scope-confirmation (before-run) and handoff-approval (before-handoff), both with authority: "human-designer". Agents prepare; humans authorize.

💻 3. Schemas & Verified Execution

TypeScript contract (@asd/core)

/** Real types from @asd/core — suite/packages/core/src/types.ts */
export type Artifact = {
  readonly id: string
  readonly kind: ArtifactKind        // "service-blueprint" for this course
  readonly projectId: string
  readonly title: string
  readonly schemaVersion: number     // 1
  readonly methodId?: string         // "service-blueprinting"
  readonly sources: readonly SourceRef[]
  readonly nodes: readonly ArtifactNode[]   // { id, label, evidence: EvidenceRef[], fields }
  readonly edges?: readonly ArtifactEdge[]  // { id, from, to, relation, fields }
  readonly references?: readonly ArtifactReference[]  // typed cross-artifact links, provenance required
  readonly status: 'draft' | 'final' // final = schema + acceptance + review all passed
  readonly check?: ArtifactCheck     // acceptance failures while draft
  readonly review?: ArtifactReview   // { verdict: 'pass' | 'veto', logArtifactId }
}

export type ArtifactReference = {
  readonly artifactId: string
  readonly kind: ArtifactKind
  readonly relation: string          // e.g. "derived-from" (journey-map → blueprint)
  readonly nodeId?: string
  readonly provenance: ArtifactReferenceProvenance  // { source: 'method'|'human'|'import', methodId?, note? }
}

The service-blueprint kind schema (verify yourself: asd schema-get --kind service-blueprint) declares a closed field policy — fields.lane is required, and the optional vocabulary is stage, touchpoint, emotion, actor, frontstage, backstage, support, marker, description, notes, tags[]. References allow exactly two relations: derived-from (targets: journey-map, ecosystem-map, value-proposition-canvas, service-profit-chain) and supports (targets: service-standard-set, change-plan, roadmap, kpi-tree) — both with provenance required. The schema is the theory, serialized.

Verified terminal session (real run, disposable workspace)

Every id below was minted by the CLI in a throwaway ASD_WORKSPACE during course verification; yours will differ because evidence ids are SHA-256 content hashes of your records.

export ASD_WORKSPACE=/tmp/asd-lab
CLI="node suite/packages/runtime/lib/cli.mjs"

$CLI project-create --name "Fernwood Repairs Blueprint Sprint" --client "Fernwood Council" \
  --sector government --service "housing repairs reporting"
# → {"project":{"projectId":"fernwood-repairs-blueprint-sprint-7cee33e4","phase":"discover",...}}

$CLI evidence-add --projectId fernwood-repairs-blueprint-sprint-7cee33e4 \
  --source int-01 --kind quote \
  --body "I reported the leaking tap online and got a text back within an hour with a booking reference."
# → id "1515c366a19faab7…" (sha256 of [source,kind,body,origin])

$CLI method-run --projectId fernwood-repairs-blueprint-sprint-7cee33e4 --methodId service-blueprinting
# → the method CONTRACT (trimmed):
#   {"method":{"owner":"mapper","reviewGate":"critic",
#              "evidenceRules":{"traceability":"required","provenance":"required"}},
#    "steps":[{"role":"mapper","instruction":"Lay out physical evidence, customer actions, …"},
#             {"role":"presenter","instruction":"Annotate the line of visibility and mark every wait point observed."},
#             {"role":"critic","instruction":"Reject any lane left as a placeholder…","gate":true}],
#    "acceptance":[{"rule":"min-nodes","min":4},
#                  {"rule":"min-evidence-per-node","min":1},
#                  {"rule":"field-non-empty","field":"lane"},
#                  {"rule":"forbid-field-values","field":"lane","deny":["unknown","tbd","n/a"]}],
#    "inputs":[{"kind":"evidence-set","min":4}], "outputKind":"service-blueprint"}

Note the fail-closed behaviour: with fewer than 4 evidence records, method-run refuses — "requires at least 4 evidence records; project has N." The contract is enforced before any agent writes a word.

📊 4. Case Study Lens: Article 11 — Fernwood Council Housing Repairs

Synthetic illustration — not a production log. Article 11 (Service Blueprints as Operational Diagnostics: Fernwood Council's Housing Repairs Service) is a synthetic composite case study from this repo's articles/ corpus. Its counts and transcripts are illustrative, not measured production data.

Read the article through this module's lens:

  • Phase 1 (Discover) produced the journey spine — report → wait → visit → complete → invoice — exactly the symptom view. Tenants' anxiety at the wait stage is journey-map content.
  • Phase 2 (Define) drew the current-state blueprint as a diagnostic: the wait symptom decomposed into a contact-centre re-keying step (backstage), a depot scheduling batch (backstage), and a ticketing system with no status API (support). That is the Shostack move — the failure was never in the visible encounter.
  • The fail-point register (one row per F or W mark) is Bitner's notation operationalized; in the toolchain each mark is a node's marker field plus an edge labelled with the crossing it violates, and every row must cite evidence or the critic vetoes it.
  • Phase 3–4 used the future-state blueprint to sequence a pilot with two repairs teams — a blueprint earning the word "architecture" because changing it changed budgets, rosters, and system procurement.

The full 5-lane Fernwood blueprint is authored live in module 06.6 and diagnosed in 06.7; this module established why that artifact and not a prettier journey map.

🔬 5. Hands-On Lab

Work in courses/labs/lab-06-blueprinting-6lane/ — the verifier script there drives exactly this spine.

export ASD_WORKSPACE=/tmp/asd-lab06
CLI="node suite/packages/runtime/lib/cli.mjs"

# 1. Project + four evidence records (method input gate is min 4)
$CLI project-create --name "My Repairs Blueprint" --client "Fernwood Council" --sector government --service "repairs reporting"
$CLI evidence-add --projectId <pid> --source int-01 --kind quote --body "<verbatim tenant quote>"
# …add 3 more; kinds allowed: quote | telemetry | document

# 2. Read the contract before writing anything
$CLI method-run --projectId <pid> --methodId service-blueprinting

# 3. Deliberate fail-closed demo: cite an evidence id that does not exist
$CLI artifact-write --projectId <pid> --methodId service-blueprinting --title "Should fail" \
  --nodes '[{"id":"x1","label":"Invented step","evidence":[{"evidenceId":"0000…deadbeef"}],"fields":{"lane":"backstage-actions"}}]'
# EXPECT: 'cites evidence "0000…" which is not in the project evidence store'

# 4. Verify a real write is retained with acceptance status
$CLI artifact-write --projectId <pid> --methodId service-blueprinting --title "Current state" --nodes '<4+ nodes, each with fields.lane and one real evidenceId>'

Verification checklist

  • method-run printed the contract with owner: "mapper", reviewGate: "critic", and the four acceptance rules.
  • The dangling-evidence write was rejected at write time (not at review time).
  • Your real write returned {"accepted":true,"status":"draft"} — acceptance met, gate not yet passed.
  • You can explain, in one sentence each, what the journey map cannot tell you that the blueprint can, and vice versa.

🚀 Key Takeaways

  • ✅ Shostack's insight (1982/1984): services are processes; processes can be engineered. The line of visibility turned "improve the service" from a vibes exercise into cycle-time and failure-mode arithmetic.
  • ✅ Bitner/Ostrom/Morgan (2008) codified the five components and three lines. The express-parcel and hospital-discharge cases show satisfaction is backstage synchronization made visible.
  • ✅ A journey map shows the symptom; a blueprint shows the mechanism. The toolchain hard-wires this with the typed derived-from reference (provenance required).
  • ✅ Architecture means load-bearing contracts with enforcement. In @asd the enforcement is mechanical: closed schema, required lane field, per-node evidence, critic gate, deterministic factcheck.
