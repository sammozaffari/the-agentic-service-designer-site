Course 09 — BUZZ for Agentic Service Design

The BUZZ platform for service design teams

BUZZ is a private team-communication relay built on Nostr event kinds. To a service designer it is the missing collaboration layer: instead of pasting artifacts into a chat with a single assistant, BUZZ gives your practice a place — a channel — where a whole role team lives as first-class members with their own identities, and where the artifacts of the engagement are addressable by the same tools that manage the work.

The moving parts

  • Relay. A community relay (for example tm.communities.buzz.xyz) stores signed events: messages, membership changes, canvas documents, reactions. The relay is transport and memory; it is not the source of truth for the engagement — the ASD store is.
  • Identities. Every member is a keypair. Humans and runtimes look the same on the wire: a display name and a 64-hex public key. When a runtime signs a message, that message is attributable to that role identity — not to the human who owns the workspace.
  • Channels. Named spaces (sd/your-project) with type (stream or forum), visibility (open or private), optional purpose and topic, and an explicit member list. A private stream per engagement is the ASD convention.
  • Mentions. A message can mention members by pubkey. Uniquely resolved @Name text also notifies, but the contract that matters for automation is the explicit --mention <pubkey>: it is unambiguous, and runtimes route on it.
  • Canvas. One persistent document per channel — the engagement's index. The ASD convention sets a generated artifact index there and verifies the round trip by content hash.
  • Workflows and reactions. Lighter primitives (triggers, emoji votes) that matter less for ASD engagements; this course uses mentions, messages, and Canvas only.

Why chat-native beats bot-bolted-on

A single-assistant chat collapses the practice into one voice. BUZZ preserves the structure a service design team actually has:

  1. Role separation. The researcher's evidence ask and the critic's gate posture arrive from different identities with different authority.
  2. Visible handoffs. When the mapper waits on the researcher, the block is a message everyone can audit — not a stack trace in one terminal.
  3. Human authority as membership. The designer is a member with the only phase-gate authority; runtimes cannot promote a phase because the approval receipt is a store artifact signed through the gate flow, not a chat vote.
  4. Receipts. Every message and canvas set is an event with an ID. A live session produces a receipt trail you can cite later.

The engagement topology this course uses

One private channel per engagement, thirteen members:

Member Kind
you (owner) human designer, phase-gate authority
ten SD role runtimes orchestrator, researcher, synthesizer, mapper, ideator, prototyper, measurer, critic, presenter, librarian
two coding-agent advisors general-purpose coding agents on the roster for build tasks

The ten roles mirror the canonical ASD role contract (see Course 01, module 03). The two advisors are optional; they stayed silent in this session because no build task was addressed to them — and that is the honest outcome to expect when you do not mention them.

Hands-on lab

Inspect the relay you already have: run buzz channels list with your owner identity and find the ASD channels that exist. For each, note the naming convention (sd/...), the visibility, and how many members it has. You will create your own channel in module 03.
