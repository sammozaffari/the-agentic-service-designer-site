Course 08: Service Quality, Good Services Audits & Continuous Operations

Grönroos Perceived Quality, Lou Downe's 15 Principles Machine Audit, Service Recovery SLAs & Continuous Blueprints

The capstone course for enterprise service leaders. Implement Grönroos Perceived Quality, Parasuraman's 5 GAPs, and 3-tier KPI trees linking operational telemetry to financial ROI. Run Lou Downe's 15 Principles of Good Services as a machine-checkable experience-audit artifact. Engineer recovery playbooks that capitalize on the Service Recovery Paradox. Transition to continuous service architecture: evidence reopens loops, artifacts version, and phase gates stay human-owned.

Module 08.1 03 / 10

Service Quality Management: Grönroos Technical/Functional Quality & The 5 GAP Model

  • Grönroos: technical quality (what was delivered) and functional quality (how) fail independently — measure both.
  • The 5 GAP model locates failure between expectations, specifications, delivery, and communication.
  • service-quality-diagnostic operationalizes both lenses as an auditable method.
  • Each gap is a measurable distance between two artifacts — not a sentiment.
Expectations→ Specification→ Delivery→ Perception — measure each gap
"What you delivered and how you delivered it fail independently; measure both."
Module 08.2 04 / 10

Building 3-Tier KPI Trees: Operational → Experiential → Financial Traceability

  • kpi-tree-design builds kpi-tree artifacts with three tiers: operational → experiential → financial.
  • three-tier-measurement proves the ROI path from friction removal to revenue.
  • Every KPI node cites the telemetry evidence that feeds it.
  • A metric with no path to the financial tier is instrumentation, not insight.
Tier 1
Operational: queue time, error rate
Tier 2
Experiential: effort, satisfaction
Tier 3
Financial: retention, LTV, cost-to-serve
"A KPI without a tier is a number without a job."
Module 08.3 05 / 10

Lou Downe's 15 Principles of Good Services: Decoded into Machine-Auditable Heuristics

  • Lou Downe's 15 principles of good services decode into machine-checkable heuristics.
  • Each principle becomes typed review rows: rowKind, severity, message, rule, fix.
  • good-services-audit encodes the standard as a method any agent can run.
  • A principle that cannot be checked remains a poster; decoded, it becomes a gate.
15 principles→ Machine heuristics→ Typed review rows→ Auditable standard
"A principle that cannot be checked is a poster; decoded, it becomes a gate."
Module 08.4 06 / 10

Automated Standards Auditing (good-services-audit-15) & Public Service Transformation

  • good-services-audit-15 runs the full 15-principle audit on live portals and services.
  • Output is a defect scorecard: exact, severity-ranked remediation tickets.
  • experience-audit extends the same rigor to end-to-end experience reviews.
  • Public service transformation starts with an audit that produces tickets, not adjectives.

Module 08.5 07 / 10

Service Recovery Engineering: The Recovery Paradox & Automated Compensation SLAs

  • The recovery paradox: a well-recovered failure can build more loyalty than flawless service.
  • service-recovery-design produces recovery-playbook artifacts with compensation SLAs.
  • Recovery triggers are wired to telemetry — proactive, not apologetic after the fact.
  • Every playbook step is tested like a standard: authored, versioned, audited.
Failure signal (telemetry)→ recovery-playbook→ Compensation SLA→ Advocacy (paradox)
"Recover well and the failure becomes the story the customer tells in your favor."

  • Living blueprints synchronize with production telemetry; drift between model and operation is detected, not assumed away.
