Agentic Service Design Fundamentals

Module 01.4: The Autonomy Spectrum & The Sovereign Human Decision Gate

Course: Agentic Service Design Fundamentals · Level: Foundation · Duration: 45 min · Format: Theory, Architecture, Verified CLI Run & Hands-on Lab

Learning Objectives

By completing this module, you will be able to:

  1. Place any service design task on the autonomy spectrum and justify the placement from the theory of accountable decision-making.
  2. Distinguish the three enforcement layers the runtime provides: the critic reviewGate (machine adversary), humanCheckpoints (human authorization inside a method), and sd_phase_advance --approvalReceipt (human authorization between project phases).
  3. Never conflate the two phase enums — project phases discover|define|develop|deliver vs. method phases research|synthesis|ideation|prototyping|delivery|measurement — and explain why they are different axes.
  4. Demonstrate fail-closed governance — run a phase advance without a receipt and watch it refuse.

1. Theoretical Grounding: Why Some Decisions Cannot Be Delegated

The service design canon is unambiguous that design is a normative act, not just an analytic one. Stickdorn et al. (This is Service Design Doing, Ch. 2, books/01-this-is-service-design-doing/02-principles-of-service-design.md) put "human-centered" first among the principles: services are judged by the people who live them, so decisions that trade one group's burden against another's convenience are ethical decisions. Kumar (101 Design Methods, books/17-101-design-methods/01-innovation-as-a-process.md) adds the organizational half: innovation is a managed discipline, and management means accountable owners for each mode transition — his "non-linear looping" is a governance structure, not a free-for-all.

This yields the autonomy spectrum's logic. Automate what is reversible and verifiable: evidence capture, clustering, draft generation, schema validation, citation checking. These fail safely — a bad draft is deleted. Reserve for humans what is irreversible or normative: publishing a claim about real people, advancing a project phase, spending client money on a recommendation. These fail badly — a published falsehood about a tenant's experience cannot be unpublished.

The platform encodes this as three concentric gates, each stricter than the last:

  1. The critic review gate (reviewGate: "critic") — a machine adversary on every artifact. Fast, tireless, automatic: the verification half of autonomy.
  2. Method humanCheckpoints — embedded in the method contract itself. journey-from-transcripts carries scope-confirmation (before-run: "Confirm the scope, inputs, consent and intended decision") and handoff-approval (before-handoff: "Approve the evidence-backed journey-map and its open gaps"). The method cannot be honestly executed without a named human answering these.
  3. The phase gate (sd_phase_advance --approvalReceipt) — the sovereign gate. Moving a project from discover to define requires a signed JSON receipt with receiptId, approvedBy, decision, rationale, and approvedAt. No receipt, no advance. Agents prepare; humans authorize.

2. The Two Phase Enums — Do Not Conflate Them

The runtime has two different phase vocabularies on two different axes, and mixing them up produces meaningless sentences like "advance the project to synthesis":

A project in discover typically runs research- and synthesis-phase methods (like journey-from-transcripts, whose contract declares "phase":"synthesis"). A project in deliver runs measurement-phase methods (like service-profit-chain-mapping). The mapping is a convention, not a constraint — the hard rule is only that project-phase transitions need a human receipt, whatever methods were run inside the phase.

3. Verified Execution: Fail-Closed Governance (run today, 2026-08-22)

The fail-closed refusal — no receipt, no advance, exit 1:

$CLI phase-advance --projectId fernwood-repairs-sprint-f0d60fa1 --toPhase define --idempotencyKey gate-1
# → asd phase-advance: missing required flag "--approvalReceipt"   (exit 1)

There is a second fail-closed layer: even with a receipt, the gate refuses while any design artifact is still a draft — phase advance requires all design artifacts to be final; drafts: <id>. Autonomy can produce; only finalized, reviewed work can justify a phase transition.

The authorized advance — a signed receipt, and the runtime records the full evaluation context:

$CLI phase-advance --projectId fernwood-repairs-sprint-f0d60fa1 --toPhase define --idempotencyKey gate-1 \
  --approvalReceipt '{"receiptId":"RCP-2026-08-22-01","approvedBy":"Sam Mozaffari","decision":"approve","rationale":"Journey map final, evidence-backed, ready to frame opportunities.","approvedAt":"2026-08-22T11:38:00Z"}'

{"advanced":true,
 "decision":{"kind":"phase-gate-decision","id":"e16f658814864488affb5d6d8a04fa41",
  "fromPhase":"discover","toPhase":"define","idempotencyKey":"gate-1",
  "receipt":{"receiptId":"RCP-2026-08-22-01","approvedBy":"Sam Mozaffari","decision":"approve",
   "rationale":"Journey map final, evidence-backed, ready to frame opportunities.","approvedAt":"2026-08-22T11:38:00Z"},
  "evaluation":{"artifactCount":1,"finalArtifactIds":["2baab028b1fb4cc997d5f8d977a049f5"],
   "draftArtifactIds":[],"vetoArtifactIds":[],"factcheckPass":true},
  "outcome":"advanced"}}

Notice what the decision record preserves: not just who approved, but the state of the world at approval time — how many artifacts existed, which were final, whether factcheck passed. This is the audit trail a workshop can never produce: the decision and its evidential basis are one immutable record. The idempotencyKey means a retried submission replays the same decision instead of double-advancing.

Worked Example A: A Checkpoint Transcript — Rejected Receipt, Corrected Receipt

The fixture. A real gate conversation (disposable run, 2026-08-23). Callorna rescheduling project; one journey-map (1528c1c4c25b4902a46d5b0df0b71135) final, factcheck exit 0. The human approver is Dana Okafor, service owner.

orchestrator: All artifacts final, factcheck green. Requesting discover → define.
dana:         Approved.
orchestrator: I need that as a receipt, not a sentence. Submitting what I have…

$CLI phase-advance --toPhase define --idempotencyKey gate-1 \
  --approvalReceipt '{"receiptId":"RCP-CLN-01","approvedBy":"Dana Okafor",
    "decision":"approve","rationale":"","approvedAt":"2026-08-23T09:14:00Z"}'
→ asd phase-advance: approvalReceipt.rationale must be a non-empty string   (exit 1)

orchestrator: Refused. The rationale field is empty — "approved" is a decision,
              not a reason. Why is the evidence base sufficient?
dana:         Because the journey map is final, every stage is cited, and we can
              now frame opportunities. Resubmit it like that.

Step 1 — resubmit under a new key. The refusal is pinned to gate-1; reusing the key replays the recorded refusal even with a perfect receipt (verified: the same key with a corrected receipt returns the original error). Remediation means a new idempotency key:

$CLI phase-advance --toPhase define --idempotencyKey gate-2 \
  --approvalReceipt '{"receiptId":"RCP-CLN-01","approvedBy":"Dana Okafor","decision":"approve",
    "rationale":"Journey map final and fully cited; rescheduling evidence base is sufficient to frame opportunities in define.",
    "approvedAt":"2026-08-23T09:21:00Z"}'

Step 2 — read the stored receipt. The decision record persists both the receipt and the world-state at approval time:

{"advanced":true,"decision":{"kind":"phase-gate-decision","id":"acbd899f1f344b64976a6639760a3c3b",
 "fromPhase":"discover","toPhase":"define","idempotencyKey":"gate-2",
 "receipt":{"receiptId":"RCP-CLN-01","approvedBy":"Dana Okafor","decision":"approve",
  "rationale":"Journey map final and fully cited; rescheduling evidence base is sufficient to frame opportunities in define.",
  "approvedAt":"2026-08-23T09:21:00Z"},
 "evaluation":{"artifactCount":1,"finalArtifactIds":["1528c1c4c25b4902a46d5b0df0b71135"],
  "draftArtifactIds":[],"vetoArtifactIds":[],"factcheckPass":true,"factcheckFailures":[]},
 "outcome":"advanced"}}

Interpretation. The gate refused a contentless approval, not a missing signature — the five receipt fields are all mandatory because each answers a different audit question: who (approvedBy), what (decision), why (rationale), when (approvedAt), and which decision instance (receiptId). The evaluation block is the part no meeting minute ever captures: the exact set of final artifacts the approver was looking at. And the key-pinning behavior teaches the operational rule — a gate attempt is an immutable event, so you never "edit" a refusal, you supersede it with a new keyed attempt.

Worked Example B: Reliability vs. Validity, Then Abduction — One Ambiguous Telemetry Dip

The fixture. Week 7 telemetry from the Northfield Regional Health day-surgery follow-up portal: completed follow-up confirmations drop from 82% to 64% in one week. One number, one week, no explanation. (This is the same service where 18% of patients miss the first follow-up at baseline.)

Step 1 — reliability first: is the signal repeatable? Before any interpretation, ask whether the same measurement procedure yields the same result. Recompute the rate per day (not per week): the dip is concentrated on Wednesday–Friday. Recompute excluding one portal node: the dip survives. Check the ingestion log: no deploy, no schema change, no missing-day gaps. Conclusion: the signal is reliable — a real, recomputable drop, not an instrumentation artifact.

Step 2 — validity second: does the metric measure what we think? "Completed confirmation" is a proxy for patients being followed up, but a valid dip in the proxy is not automatically a valid dip in the construct. The SMS-to-portal handoff changed copy the same week (found in the release notes): the proxy now undercounts confirmations completed by phone. Part of the dip is measurement, not behavior.

Step 3 — abduce hypotheses and design discriminating probes. Inference to the best explanation means generating rival explanations and finding the cheapest observation that separates them:

# abductive hypothesis discriminating next probe
H1 Copy change confused patients; behavior genuinely worsened Cohort compare: patients who received old vs. new SMS copy, same week — if old-copy cohort is flat, H1 weakens
H2 Channel shift: confirmations moved to phone, construct is fine Join portal logs to call-centre disposition codes for the dip window — phone confirmations should rise by the missing ~18 points
H3 Ward capacity: fewer nurses chasing GP confirmations (~40 min/patient at baseline), so follow-ups genuinely slipped Correlate daily dip depth with ward staffing rota — H3 predicts dip tracks understaffed days, not the copy-change date

Interpretation. Reliability and validity answer different questions and must be answered in order: an unreliable signal needs no explanation, and a reliable signal may still measure the wrong thing. Abduction then disciplines the jump from data to story — each hypothesis must name the observation that would demote it. Notice the artifact consequences: H2's probe becomes a telemetry evidence record, H1's becomes an experiment documented in a test-report, and the surviving hypothesis earns its place in the insight register only with its probe's evidence id attached.

Synthetic illustration — not a production log.

Article 04 (articles/04/README.md) is the right lens for autonomy boundaries: insurance claims are a domain where a wrong automated assertion has regulatory and human cost. As you read it, classify every agent action in the article onto the spectrum above — you will find the pattern is always the same: agents ingest, cluster, draft, and check; humans sign the gate documents. Verification checklist

  • Exit 1 without --approvalReceipt; exit 1 with receipt while drafts exist; success only when all artifacts final + receipt present.
  • The recorded decision includes evaluation.finalArtifactIds and factcheckPass:true.
  • You can list both enums from memory and give a sentence that correctly uses both.
  • You can name the two humanCheckpoints on journey-from-transcripts and their when values.

Key Takeaways

  • Autonomy is allocated by reversibility. Agents own reversible, machine-verifiable work; humans own irreversible, normative decisions.
  • Three gates, ascending authority: critic (machine adversary per artifact) → method humanCheckpoints (named human per method) → sd_phase_advance --approvalReceipt (sovereign human per phase).
  • Fail-closed is the default. Missing receipt, lingering drafts, failing factcheck — each independently blocks the advance with exit 1.
  • Two phase enums, two axes. Project phases (discover|define|develop|deliver) locate the engagement; method phases (research|synthesis|ideation|prototyping|delivery|measurement) classify the thinking. Never use one where the other belongs.
