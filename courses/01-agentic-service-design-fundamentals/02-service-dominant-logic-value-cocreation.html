Module 01.2: Service-Dominant Logic & Value Co-Creation in the Age of AI

Course: Agentic Service Design Fundamentals · Level: Foundation · Duration: 50 min · Format: Theory, Architecture, Verified CLI Run & Hands-on Lab

🎯 Learning Objectives

By completing this module, you will be able to:

  1. State the five S-D Logic axioms (Vargo & Lusch 2016) and explain what each implies for how a service design project must collect and treat evidence.
  2. Distinguish co-creation from co-production, value-in-use from value-in-exchange, and explain value co-destruction as a design risk.
  3. Dogfood the theory — treat every sd_evidence_add quote as a co-creation record (FP10: value is phenomenologically determined by the beneficiary) and map the Service Profit Chain method (service-profit-chain-mapping → service-profit-chain kind) as SDL-aware measurement.
  4. Position AI agents as operant resources — capabilities applied for benefit, not products delivered — and say precisely where that analogy breaks.

📖 1. Theoretical Grounding: Vargo & Lusch, Properly

1.1 The axioms and why each one is a design constraint

Service-Dominant Logic: Premises, Perspectives, Possibilities (Lusch & Vargo 2014; the corpus summary lives at books/12-service-dominant-logic/02-the-foundational-premises.md) consolidates eleven foundational premises into five axioms. Read them as acceptance criteria for your research practice, not as slogans:

  • Axiom 1 (FP1): Service is the fundamental basis of exchange. What is exchanged is applied capability. A "repairs portal" is not the service; a home returned to habitability is. Design artifacts that describe features instead of applied capability violate FP1.
  • Axiom 2 (FP6): Value is co-created by multiple actors, always including the beneficiary. The council cannot deliver a repaired-home experience; the tenant must integrate the offer into their life (FP7: actors can only offer value propositions). This is why unevidenced journey stages are not "gaps" but fabrications — you guessed at the beneficiary's integration work.
  • Axiom 3 (FP9): All actors are resource integrators. Tenants integrate private resources (time, phone credit, literacy), public resources (tenant rights, language support), and market-facing resources (the portal, the call centre). A journey map that only models the market-facing resources misses two-thirds of the value equation.
  • Axiom 4 (FP10): Value is uniquely and phenomenologically determined by the beneficiary. "Did I feel heard?" is answered by the tenant, not by your SLA dashboard. This axiom is the theoretical license for the platform's hardest rule: emotions on a journey map must be drawn from quotes, never inferred (see the journey-from-transcripts contract, step 2).
  • Axiom 5 (FP11): Co-creation is coordinated through institutions. Policies, scripts, and norms shape what value can be realized — which is why document-kind evidence (like Repairs Policy v3.2) is a first-class record, not appendix material.

1.2 Co-creation ≠ co-production, and the co-destruction risk

Chapter 3 (books/12-service-dominant-logic/03-value-co-creation.md) draws the distinction practitioners constantly blur. Co-production is optional: the tenant joining a co-design workshop, or assembling IKEA furniture. Co-creation is invariant: even a tenant who never touches a workshop still co-creates the value of the repair by integrating the appointment, the access, and the outcome into their household. Confusing the two leads organizations to push co-production labor onto users (complex self-service forms) while delivering no additional co-created value — the chapter's Co-Creation vs. Co-Production Alignment Audit.

The same chapter names the failure mode: value co-destruction (Plé & Chumpitaz Cáceres; Echeverri & Skålén). When a beneficiary lacks the resources to integrate the offer — no phone credit to wait on hold, no written English to read the confirmation letter — the interaction destroys well-being. Our verified evidence corpus contains a live example: 41% of status-check sessions end on the 'no update available' page within 20 seconds — telemetry of integration failure, and the interview quote nobody ever told me what happened next is its phenomenological twin. Telemetry says how often; the quote says what it felt like. FP10 requires both.

🤖 2. Agentic Topology: Dogfooding SDL in the Toolchain

The platform's evidence model is FP10 operationalized. When the researcher role calls sd_evidence_add --kind quote --body "<verbatim>", the stored record is a co-creation record: the beneficiary's own account of what value (or its destruction) felt like in context. The SHA-256 content id means the phenomenological account cannot be edited after an artifact cites it — the beneficiary's determination of value stays the beneficiary's words.

The measurer role then tests causal claims about the ecosystem with the service-profit-chain-mapping method (owner measurer, gate critic, outputKind service-profit-chain) — a direct operationalization of Axiom 2's multi-actor causality: employee capability → service value → customer loyalty → growth. Its contract (captured live below) refuses to run with fewer than 2 evidence records and its acceptance rules require every chain link to carry a metric and at least one citation — no uncited causal arrows, which is precisely the discipline S-D Logic imposes on value narratives.

Role SDL function Tools
researcher Captures phenomenological value determinations (quotes), integration-failure rates (telemetry), institutional rules (documents). sd_evidence_add, sd_evidence_query
mapper Models the beneficiary's resource-integration journey; emotions only from quotes (FP10). sd_method_run (journey-from-transcripts), sd_artifact_write
measurer Maps multi-actor causal chains with cited metrics. sd_method_run (service-profit-chain-mapping)
critic Rejects uncorroborated value claims — institutionalized skepticism about inferred value. sd_review, sd_factcheck

Where the "AI agents as operant resources" analogy holds: agents apply knowledge and skill for the beneficiary's benefit (FP4), and they are worthless until integrated into the designer's practice. Where it breaks: agents are not beneficiaries; they determine no value. FP10 keeps the human — tenant and designer — as the sole arbiter. That is why every handoff in the method contract carries a humanCheckpoints entry with authority: "human-designer".

🔬 5. Hands-On Lab

export ASD_WORKSPACE=/tmp/asd-lab-02
CLI="node suite/packages/runtime/lib/cli.mjs"

# 1. New project
$CLI project-create --name "SDL Value Map" --client "Forge-style client" --sector manufacturing --service "equipment maintenance"

# 2. Capture one record per FP9 resource pool
$CLI evidence-add --projectId <pid> --source crew-int --kind quote --body "<beneficiary's own words about value in use>"
$CLI evidence-add --projectId <pid> --source ops-tel --kind telemetry --body "<integration success/failure rate>"
$CLI evidence-add --projectId <pid> --source policy --kind document --body "<institutional rule shaping co-creation>"

# 3. Read the SDL measurement contract; find the critic's gate instruction
$CLI method-run --projectId <pid> --methodId service-profit-chain-mapping

# 4. Inspect the artifact kind schema the method produces
$CLI schema-get --kind service-profit-chain

Verification checklist

  • You can name all five axioms and point to the tool behavior each one motivates.
  • Each evidence record is labeled with the resource pool (private/public/market-facing) it documents.
  • Contract shows owner: "measurer", acceptance field-non-empty: metric, and inputs[0].min: 2.
  • schema-get --kind service-profit-chain shows node field metric required and edge fields weight/label optional.
  • You can state one way value co-destruction could appear in your project's telemetry.

🚀 Key Takeaways

  • Value-in-use, not value-in-exchange. The platform's quote-first evidence model is FP10 made operational: the beneficiary determines value, verbatim and uneditable after citation.
  • Co-creation is invariant; co-production is a choice. Audit where your service forces user labor without adding integrated value — that is co-destruction risk, and telemetry will show it first.
  • Causal value claims need cited metrics. service-profit-chain-mapping encodes this: minimum 4 chain links, metric non-empty, ≥1 evidence per node, critic rejects unsupported links.
  • Agents are operant resources, never beneficiaries. They apply capability; humans integrate it and arbitrate value — hence human-designer authority on every checkpoint.
