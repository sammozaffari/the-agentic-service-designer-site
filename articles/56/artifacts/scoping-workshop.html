Method

A full-day facilitated workshop on a timed agenda. Current-state mapping ran one swim lane per system across the complete learner lifecycle — user creation, login, navigation, enrolment, launch and completion, reporting — with every hand-off between systems treated as a potential fail point. The signature exercise was target-state visioning: if the legacy front-end disappeared tomorrow… Requirements were captured in three groups — learners; managers and administrators; platform and compliance — rather than one per system, which kept the artifact whole. The workshop ran under an explicit norm of brutal honesty, and every output landed on one spreadsheet by end of day: the spreadsheet, not the room, was the single source of truth.

The facilitator prompts that did the work:

Where does this decision actually happen? Is that enforced, or just hidden? What breaks if this step fails?

Requirements — three groups

Requirements and user stories were structured by user group, not by system — the collision between learner and compliance needs is the design problem, not a data problem:

GroupRepresentative requirements
LearnersMobile-first; only assigned focus areas visible; due and overdue indicators; "show completed" toggle; completion dates
Managers & administratorsTeam view for all managers; advanced reporting for leaders; proxy access; notifications ("only 14 days to complete")
Platform & complianceAge gates; expiry tracking for legislative certifications; prerequisite badges; audit trail; cross-portal completion marking

Output contract

Every sticky lands on exactly one spreadsheet sheet by end of day — as-is flows, to-be flows, risks, assumptions, requirements, owners and due dates. The workshop closed with named owners and next-two-weeks actions, and the blueprint was rehearsed with zero open gaps before any build began.
