The brief

A global quick-service restaurant chain asked for one thing: retire the legacy learning platform and make a single, mobile-first platform the one learning system for tens of thousands of crew members and restaurant managers. The analysis refused the tool-swap framing. The legacy front-end was a door onto an uncontrolled warehouse: the failure lived in the ecosystem — six systems hand-shaking beneath the learner — not in any single screen. Three failure clusters, each stated in the business case: incorrect training completions (people completing training they should not, missing training they need, nothing restricted by age); poor user experience (not mobile-friendly, complex navigation, an unclear learning journey); complex training management (no workflow control or pacing, everything available on day one, duplicated standards, no analytics).

Success criteria

0support tickets /month during pilot (vs ~155 baseline)
100%completion-marking accuracy
1,000+training completions in pilot
100%of surveyed learners self-directed (agree)

Scope

IN: the learner journey from account creation through certification — every action, decision and system required to deliver role-based learning with proof: the support ecosystem, the six-system hand-offs, the compliance gates, and the certification lifecycle. OUT: the learning standards themselves — the redesign changed the experience, never what defines a certified team member at any station — plus pay and benefits, which route through other workstreams and are never absorbed into a platform study.

Constraints

No new licence cost in the model. The back-end platform licence was already paid, so the business case was purely a support-burden and compliance-risk release — unusually clean economics. Roughly twelve months to land before the annual recertification cycle — the period when the old platform’s failure modes did the most damage. Franchisee autonomy: a centrally imposed platform still had to be sold to the network, not dictated. Age data was not fed into any system — true age-based restriction needed a data-level fix, so an interim self-confirmation gate was the design answer. A peak mid-year promotional trading period sat across the pilot window, forcing a phased, market-by-market start. External dependencies: the platform vendor’s product team, and a sandbox whose administrator access was initially too limited to build in independently.

Decision requested

Four decisions at the governance gate: (1) the replacement option — do nothing, a minor upgrade, or full replacement; (2) the outcomes and measures the platform must improve; (3) the functions and owners required across the ecosystem; (4) the restaurants and data needed for the pilot.
