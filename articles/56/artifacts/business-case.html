The support-cost model

Built from the ticketing-system export, trailing twelve months: ~155 tickets a month, ~50 support hours a month, roughly $2,150 a month. Annualized: ~1,860 tickets, ~600 hours, ~$25,800 a year. The basis is stated in the model — hours at ~0.32 per ticket, cost at roughly $43 an hour — and every figure traces to a ticket count.

Stated exclusions

The model deliberately understates: every exclusion pushes the burden lower, never higher — and it survived the funding gate because it did.

Excluded from the modelWhy it is excluded
Manager time spent logging and chasing ticketsNot captured in the ticketing system export
Escalation time to the global support teamSits outside the base hourly resolution rate
Manager cost inside the hourly rateThe rate is a support-desk rate, not a management rate

Three independent signals confirmed the monthly model: the 18-month run corroborates the rate, the escalation share shows how much burden leaked past the local desk, and a full-time role absorbed by tickets priced the problem in people, not just dollars.

Compliance-risk register

The six-row risk table is the reason this was a safety project, not an upgrade:

RiskWhat the legacy platform didImpact
Irreversible badgesModules could be removed, but a certification badge, once awarded, could never be revokedCertification integrity undermined
No audit trailNo trace of post-incident recertificationLegal and reputational exposure
No expiry trackingLegislative certifications (food safety, first aid) not tracked to expiryLegislative gap

Restricted reportingAdvanced reporting limited to a single person per restaurantNo oversight; concentrated control

Registers

Two registers completed the picture. The feature-loss register named twelve capabilities stripped from the last migration:

proxy access franchisee admin access all-management reporting view own training assessment security prerequisite locks training-to-complete view notifications cross-portal completion marking evidence upload comment visibility self-serve reports

The desired-capability register named thirteen capabilities the network needed to be a leading quick-service learning experience:

proxy access manager sign-off content pacing and spacing collaborative learning hosted and tracked live sessions age-restricted blocking eligibility-change notifications mobile-first courses detailed progression analytics AI search AI curation and problem identification augmented-reality training language translation

Every claim in both registers was validated line-by-line with the platform vendor’s product owner — so feasibility was agreed before the design depended on it.

Full replacement was approved on the economics: the platform licence was already paid, so the release was pure cost reduction plus risk reduction.

Human designer judgment

The model was built to understate. Stating the exclusions made the ~$25,800 figure defensible under challenge — an understated burden is more credible than an overstated one — and the compliance register carried the safety argument that no cost model alone could make.
