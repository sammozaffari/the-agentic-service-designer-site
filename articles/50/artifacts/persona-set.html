Three personas, one chain

METhe new starter
Crew · weeks 0–12

First job or first role in the chain; friends already work there; wants independence and pocket money.

Goals
  • Learn the stations and feel part of the team
  • Predictable hours that build skills
  • A visible next step
Pains
  • Only a minority get a structured plan or check-ins
  • Independence still low at week 12
  • Recognition is the largest unmet expectation
Source: Composite of onboarding wave + 2/6/12-week cohorts
SQThe squeezed middle
Assistant manager

Delivers the shift, coaches the crew, fills roster gaps — often without the authority or recognition to match.

Goals
  • A clear role with real boundaries
  • Coaching from the restaurant manager
  • Recognition and a visible next step
Pains
  • Roughly a quarter intend to stay under a year
  • Stress and recognition among the worst of any role
  • 25% doubt survey action will happen
Source: Composite of ~780 assistant-manager responses
CMThe committed overloaded
Restaurant manager

Highly committed — the vast majority intend to stay — but carrying workload, equipment and staffing failures.

Goals
  • A sustainable operating model, not another engagement push
  • Working equipment and enough capable people
  • Reliable support from the area coach
Pains
  • Only half say stress is manageable
  • Workload is the top unmet expectation
  • Recognition trails the weight of the role
Source: Composite of restaurant-manager responses + resource deep-dives

Design response

The personas are not marketing archetypes — each maps to a blueprint lane and a set of measures. The new starter defines the first-12-weeks journey; the squeezed middle defines the coaching and recognition lanes; the committed overloaded defines the above-store support and escalation lanes.
