The brief

A global quick-service restaurant chain asked for three separate things: a holistic view of the crew-member experience, a single definition of the restaurant-manager role (five unconnected frameworks existed), and relief on record turnover. The analysis refused to treat them as three problems. They are one connected chain: unprepared start → inconsistent hours and skill mix → pressured shifts → inconsistent support and recognition → limited progress → avoidable attrition.

Success criteria

20,000+survey responses, ~54% participation
~37%of crew intend to leave within a year
25–35ptmanager-vs-crew perception gap on the same behaviours
3blueprint options tabled for a single decision gate

Scope

IN: the first twelve weeks of a crew member, end to end — every action, decision and system required from assistant managers, restaurant managers and above-store teams to deliver it. OUT: pay architecture, benefits design, and the serious-conduct pathway (harassment, bullying, retaliation), which route to confidential case management and are never absorbed into a general blueprint study.

Constraints

Survey results are read as strongly-agree only — the favourable bar the business itself set. The first blueprint must be bounded enough to pilot across a balanced store sample. Exit records are not linked to survey responses, so causation is argued from a consistent evidence set, never claimed.

Decision requested

Four decisions at the governance gate: (1) the first blueprint scope, (2) the outcomes and measures it must improve, (3) the functions and owners required, (4) the restaurants and data needed for discovery and piloting.
