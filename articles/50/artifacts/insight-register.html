Seven insights, each graded

I-01 Readiness fails before day one — Uniforms, user IDs and training schedules are the top missing items; only ~41% of new starters strongly agree they had everything needed on day one.
Root cause: Preboarding is a service, not an admin step · Evidence: 31 items · survey + open text · Implication: Day-1 readiness standard with five named gates
I-02 The first 12 weeks flatline — Welcome moves 47→50%, exceeded-expectations falls, independence reaches only ~29% by week 12.
Root cause: Tenure accrues faster than experience improves · Evidence: Onboarding KPI wave · Implication: Structured check-ins at 2/6/12 weeks
I-03 Hours are the root cause, not the symptom — Crew want more hours while managers report understaffed shifts — the roster allocates hours without the right skill mix.
Root cause: Roster allocation and capability, not labour volume · Evidence: Survey + roster data · Implication: Availability-matched rostering with skill-mix reviews
I-04 The assistant manager is the squeezed middle — ~24% intend to stay under a year; recognition and stress score among the worst of any role; ~25% doubt survey action will happen.
Root cause: Responsibility without authority, coaching or recognition · Evidence: ARM cut (~780 responses) · Implication: Role boundaries, RGM coaching routine, recognition
I-05 The restaurant manager is committed but overloaded — ~90% intend to stay, yet only ~51% say stress is manageable; workload is the top unmet expectation.
Root cause: The operating model, not commitment, is the problem · Evidence: RGM cut + resource deep-dives · Implication: Sustainable operating model, working equipment, reliable support
I-06 Recognition is the largest controllable gap — Recognition is the second most-cited unmet expectation after pay — and the most cited improvement theme in open text.
Root cause: Effort is unseen because the shift is under pressure · Evidence: 3,152 pay / 2,446 recognition mentions · Implication: Recognition during learning, not only after mastery
I-07 The career path is invisible — Only ~15% strongly agree they can see a path at week 12.
Root cause: Progress is never made visible · Evidence: 12-week wave · Implication: Named next step at 12 weeks, visible skills ladder

Reading rule

Every insight must change what gets designed — an insight that does not alter a decision is a theme, not an insight. Each card carries its evidence count and its design implication, and every implication traces into the blueprint (see the service blueprint artifact).
