Installation is uniform; adoption is not. The same machine and the same menu produced wildly different shares — usage follows customer flow and floor support, not the presence of the hardware.

Store-level clusters: adoption is a design outcome, not luck

Store-level adoption tracking split same-format stores into clusters. In steady-flow clusters, kiosks stayed below ~15% of front-counter sales; in high-flow clusters they reached 30–60%+ of front-counter sales. The difference was not the machine: it was zone design, menu-board decisions, and whether staff were trained and rostered to support new users.

Channel mix 2023

In-store channel mix at the end of the period: drive-thru ~56%, delivery ~10%, front counter ~34%. Digital growth raised the bar for the front counter: the training and strength of team members on the front counter must run parallel with that of those on the drive-thru.

What this means for the design

Deploy per format with differentiated expectations; target coaching at low-adoption formats rather than treating one standard as uniform. The floor-role standard, the deployment cards, and the governance timeline all flow from this single chart.
