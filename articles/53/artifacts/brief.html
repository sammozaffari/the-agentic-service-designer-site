The brief as stated

A global quick-service restaurant chain asked for the training gap to be closed: “There is currently no process in place to help train and upskill our teams on kiosks once they are installed into their restaurant. Troubleshooting and development documents exist; however, they are not sent to restaurants proactively or collated.” The learning platform carried only IT troubleshooting content; the onboarding courses for order-taking and register work had zero self-service content; and no channel training existed for any ordering channel.

The channel was growing without an operating model: self-service orders doubled every year for three consecutive years and reached roughly half the network — while training, rollout, rostering, and recovery did not exist. The working group reframed the brief from “how do we train people on the machine?” to “how do we design the human half of a self-service channel?”

The reframe: four interlocking gaps

GapWhat was actually true
No channel trainingOnboarding courses for order-taking and register work had zero self-service content; channel training existed for no ordering channel; training was only built where 80%+ of restaurants had the technology — and kiosks fell below that bar for years.
No rollout processNo defined process for installing kiosks and bringing a restaurant team up to speed; the user-guide distribution had lapsed entirely.
No floor roleThe foyer role was not formalised — the time & attendance platform could not roster anyone to stand in the foyer; the floor-service guidance inside the user guide was outdated and stuck in the IT backlog.
No design for channel limitationsThe kiosk is not a replica of the register or the app: no cash, express-kiosk exclusions on specific items, menu availability mismatches, and no team script for machine failure.

The training gap was a symptom. Closing it alone would have produced trained teams standing in a system that could not roster them, install machines, or recover failures. The four gaps are one missing operating model.

Constraints

Labour was the hardest constraint: the foyer host is a floating role competing with back-of-house demand, and lower-volume stores could not staff one at all. Cash was a policy constraint: kiosks take no cash by design, cash-capable machines cost 3–4× a standard kiosk with an unproven business case, and team members handling cash at the machine were prohibited as unsafe. Technology added fragility — POS integration, nightly menu refreshes, pin-pad timeouts, all-kiosk outages. Data was siloed: channel sales existed but no customer-level linkage, so lifetime-value claims could not yet be confirmed. Accessibility and menu-board regulations constrained layouts, and franchise heterogeneity meant any standard would need local translation.

Scope — IN and OUT

IN: channel strategy (penetration and adoption), the floor-role standard, the training ecosystem, labour-deployment modelling, journey mapping, and audit-linked governance. OUT: hardware procurement and POS replacement, the ordering app, vendor contracts, and pay and terms — all deliberately outside the working group's remit.

Decision requested

The gate decision the package was built to support: publish and enforce — stand up an interim rollout process before the next wave of installations, tie the standard to the ops audit, and re-read the feedback wide sweep quarterly to confirm the channel stays invisible when the floor role works.
