Executive read

A self-service channel that doubled every year for three years had no channel training, no rollout process, no floor role, and no design for its limitations. Four evidence streams — channel-mix data, guest-feedback mining at two scales, cross-market benchmarking, and pilot measurement — converged on one concept: the human is the channel's load-bearing wall. The gate decision: publish and enforce.

The feedback shift: absence is the success signal

ReadSampleKiosk-mention rateReading
Deep dive~50 self-service-related comments~90% (~45 of ~50)The channel dominates feedback when service fails
Wide sweep~6,700 comments across the store population~0.09% (6 of ~6,700)When the floor role works, guests narrate the whole visit, not the machine

Feedback volume is not the KPI — mine for absence. The fail cluster that does surface (waiting, cleanliness, cash, tech) is the action list; the silence is the success signal. The wide-sweep count is re-read quarterly to confirm the channel stays invisible.

What the evidence cannot say

No customer-level linkage exists yet, so lifetime-value claims are not confirmed; ticket results vary with customer mix and placement; and the absence signal is read quarterly, never assumed. The decision rests on the triangulated set — channel performance, feedback, benchmarking, and pilots — not on any single number.
