Why benchmark

The benchmarking interviews found no single corporate answer: four franchisee groups running floor-role programs had designed four different humans, with four different theories of where the human belongs in a self-service channel. The standards program's job was to converge them into one floor-role standard without flattening what made each work.

Convergence: what the four designs share

Across the four archetypes, five behaviours recurred:

  • Greet within seconds at the door — all four
  • Assist without judgment at the kiosk — all four
  • Direct cash and specific-piece requests to the counter — three of four
  • Clean: foyer and high-touch surfaces as a secondary duty — three of four
  • Roster to lunch and dinner peaks — all four

These five shared behaviours became the floor-role standard: greet, assist, direct, clean, and roster — with the role required during peak hours for the first six months of any kiosk installation, or until adoption reaches 80% of in-store sales.

A post-implementation phone check called three sampled restaurants: none had implemented — or heard of — the flagship foyer program. A great role design is worthless without rollout, rostering, and coaching. That finding, not the successes, drove the governance design: every standard paired with an interim rollout process and audit-linked enforcement.
