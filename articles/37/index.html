01 · Executive summary

Executive summary

Metrovale's emergency response is technically well supplied but experienced as a collection of disconnected services. During a severe-weather event, a resident with a mobility constraint may receive a transport cancellation from one agency, a shelter message from another, and a welfare check from a third—with no shared view of what has already happened. OneRecovery is a fictional twelve-week service-design engagement to make that handoff visible, safe, and recoverable. BUZZ is used for the human-and-agent conversation; the ASD workspace and MCP interface persist evidence and artifacts; ACP records typed handoffs but does not spawn an external agent.

02 · The brief

The brief

Metrovale coordinates buses, paratransit, public shelters, a contact centre, volunteer transport, and welfare checks. The fictional service handles approximately 18,000 journey requests during a large event. A resident does not experience these as six systems. They experience one question: “Can I get somewhere safe, and will somebody know if the plan changes?”

The current service has three visible failure-demand patterns:

  1. residents repeat their address and access needs to each agency;
  2. call-centre staff cannot tell whether a transport promise is still active; and
  3. shelter staff receive a person without the context needed to welcome them safely.

The design brief is to create a shared recovery journey with explicit consent, accessible channels, and a handoff that can be audited after the event. The design must not create a centralised dossier, infer a resident's health condition, or make an automated safety decision without a human owner.

Success criteria for the fictional pilot are:

  • reduce repeated resident explanations from a median of three to two;
  • reduce confirmed safe-reroute time from 52 to 32 minutes;
  • make every cross-agency handoff resolve to an owner, next action, and timestamp; and
  • obtain a minimum 4.2/5 accessibility and clarity rating from a representative pilot group.

The constraints are substantial: legacy dispatch systems cannot be replaced in twelve weeks; the service must work by voice, SMS, web, and assisted in-person contact; data minimisation is mandatory; and the duty manager retains final authority during an active event. Service Design is appropriate because the problem is not one screen. It is the relationship between people, policy, channels, frontline work, backstage systems, and recovery promises (see books/01-this-is-service-design-doing/03-the-service-design-process.md, books/08-orchestrating-experiences/03-the-orchestration-canvas.md, books/09-mapping-experiences/04-customer-journey-maps.md, and books/09-mapping-experiences/05-service-blueprints.md).

03 · Approach

Approach: the AI-first Service Design process

3.1 What BUZZ is—and what it is not

BUZZ is the collaboration surface: a channel where a human designer can convene specialist ASD runtimes, inspect replies, ask for critique, and make the work visible to colleagues. A BUZZ mention is a request in a social workflow. It is not, by itself, proof that an artifact was persisted or that a tool ran successfully.

The case uses three deliberately separate boundaries:

Boundary Responsibility Evidence required
BUZZ Human/agent channel messages, membership, threads, and Canvas Authenticated channel/member/runtime receipts and screenshots
ASD MCP/CLI Project, evidence, artifacts, reviews, factcheck, exports Pack-local command/MCP receipts with resolvable IDs
ACP Typed handoff record between roles A stored handoff receipt; external spawn is unsupported/record-only

The current foundation counts are 29 tools, 225 resources, and 10 prompts. These are release evidence counts, not a promise that every host or every desktop client can execute them. Six emitted MCP bundles have fresh live stdio evidence. Host execution must be labelled verified only when a host-specific receipt exists.

The ten ASD role definitions/personas are: orchestrator, researcher, synthesizer, mapper, ideator, prototyper, measurer, critic, presenter, and librarian. A role definition is not automatically an available runtime. Before mentioning a role in BUZZ, verify its runtime public identity, health, membership, and reply author. This distinction is the central operational lesson of the case.

3.2 Process map

The engagement maps the Design Council Double Diamond (Discover, Define, Develop, Deliver) to the TISDD loop (Research, Ideation, Prototyping, Implementation) and Kumar's seven modes. The designer owns framing, consent, prioritisation, and gates. Agents accelerate bounded synthesis and artifact production, but they cannot silently authorise a safety decision. The illustrative schedule is four 21-day stages, exactly 84 days, with a human gate between each stage.

04 · The work, phase by phase

Phase-by-phase narrative

4.1 Discover — make the service system visible

Goal. Understand what residents, frontline workers, and partner agencies actually do during disruption, including workarounds that never appear in a system log.

Method and SOP. The researcher uses contextual inquiry and the methods toolbox (01/04 and 02/04): establish consent, capture exact words and observed actions, label source and sensitivity, separate interpretation from evidence, and return the map to participants for correction.

AI execution. In BUZZ, the designer asks @researcher to collect gaps and @librarian to check provenance. In the ASD workspace, sd_evidence_add stores synthetic records and sd_method_list returns the available method contracts. MCP is the persistence interface; it is not evidence that a BUZZ message was authored by a runtime. The planned host delegation is explicit and remains PENDING HOST EVIDENCE: Claude Code would inspect the evidence register and draft a source map; OpenCode would run a deterministic CSV normalisation check; Codex would perform an adversarial provenance review; GitHub would hold the issue and PR that gate the generated evidence register. The hosts are not claimed to have run in BUZZ.

NOTE
@researcher Extract only observed actions from evidence IDs <E1..E12>.
Return source, quote, sensitivity, and an unresolved-question list.

The delegation is split because extraction, deterministic data checking, and adversarial review have different failure modes. A planned GitHub workflow would run the article consistency, secret, and Mermaid checks on the PR; it requires a fresh host receipt before it can be called live.

Human judgment. The designer chooses which residents must be represented, refuses to infer medical or emotional traits, and decides whether an operational record can be used for the design question. The output is the evidence register and stakeholder map.

4.2 Define — frame the failure demand

Goal. Turn scattered observations into a shared problem statement without jumping to a product solution.

Method and SOP. The synthesizer runs journey maps and affinity synthesis (09/04 and 01/04): cluster verbatim evidence, name moments, mark contradictions, trace every insight to source, and write opportunity statements with a measurable outcome.

AI execution. The designer asks @synthesizer to cluster only the evidence IDs in the project, then asks @mapper for a current-state journey. The CLI/MCP store holds the artifact; sd_artifact_write must reject a node with no resolvable evidence. The critic reviews claims before the human accepts the framing. Planned host delegation is PENDING HOST EVIDENCE: Claude Code would author the first journey draft from the returned IDs, OpenCode would run a schema/CSV consistency check, Codex would challenge unsupported causal language, and GitHub would record a Define issue, PR, and Actions receipt. None is represented as a completed BUZZ run.

NOTE
@synthesizer Cluster <EVIDENCE_IDS> into moments and tensions.
@mapper Draft a current journey; every node must cite an evidenceId or be labelled gap.
@critic reject any node whose source cannot be resolved.

Human judgment. “No shared recovery promise” is selected over “build one super-app” because the former respects channel diversity and legacy constraints. The output is the current journey and opportunity matrix.

4.3 Develop — design and challenge the future service

Goal. Explore concepts that work across voice, SMS, web, and assisted contact, then make their operational consequences explicit.

Method and SOP. The mapper uses service blueprinting and the orchestration canvas (09/05 and 08/03): define frontstage and backstage lanes, assign owners, mark dependencies, identify failure points, and test the design with a falsifiable prototype plan.

AI execution. @ideator proposes alternatives, @prototyper makes a test card, and @critic checks evidence, safety, and ownership. A typed ACP handoff can record that the mapper handed a draft to the critic; it cannot start a Claude or Codex process. The same artifact ID is retained when the designer remediates a veto. Planned host work is PENDING HOST EVIDENCE: Claude Code would render the blueprint and prompt the role team, OpenCode would validate the Mermaid and artifact JSON, Codex would run an adversarial safety review, and GitHub Actions would attach the generated render and test receipt to the Develop PR. These are planned responsibilities, not claims about a live host.

NOTE
@ideator Propose three concepts against accessibility, minimum-data, ownership,
and human-override principles. @critic cite an evidenceId for every veto or pass.

Human judgment. The designer rejects the first blueprint because its backstage handoff lane is unnamed: ownership and escalation cannot be reviewed. The designer names the case-state and escalation support process, keeps the escalation owner visible in every recovery handoff, and asks the accessibility lead to review the script. The output is the future blueprint, concept brief, and prototype card.

4.4 Deliver — pilot, measure, and govern

Goal. Run a bounded service test and make the decision to continue, change, or stop visible to the people accountable for safety.

Method and SOP. The prototyper defines a pilot, the measurer builds a KPI tree, and the team audits Good Services principles (03/06, 20/05, and 05/02). The presenter prepares an evidence-linked readout and the librarian checks the handoff pack. sd_factcheck verifies integrity, acceptance, and review state before an export is treated as final.

AI execution. The agent team drafts test cases, measurement definitions, and a readout. The CLI/MCP receipts provide persistent IDs; BUZZ keeps the decision thread visible. The duty manager—not a model—reviews risk, expiry, owner, and escalation. Planned host delegation remains PENDING HOST EVIDENCE: Claude Code would assemble the readout, OpenCode would run export/reproducibility checks, Codex would review the factcheck and claims, and GitHub Actions would run the Deliver gate on a PR. The current verified evidence is the pack-local disposable CLI/MCP receipt, not a host execution receipt.

NOTE
@measurer Build a KPI tree from <EVIDENCE_IDS>; mark each target illustrative.
@presenter prepare a handoff whose claims all resolve to artifact or evidence IDs.
@librarian report dangling links and stop if any source is missing.

Human judgment. The designer decides whether the fictional pilot's evidence is strong enough to recommend a next step and records the conditions of approval. The output is the pilot plan, KPI tree, factcheck report, and human gate.

Step 6 — Review, remediate, and pass

The review loop has four states: draft, veto, remediation, and evidence-cited pass. The pass must include a review row with a resolvable evidenceId; an empty rows array is not a passing review. The critic can identify a defect, but the human owns the decision to accept the corrected design.

NOTE
asd review --projectId <PROJECT_ID> --artifactId <BLUEPRINT_ID> \
  --verdict veto --rows '[{"rowKind":"defect","severity":"high",
  "rule":"field-non-empty",
  "message":"The backstage handoff lane is unnamed, so ownership cannot be reviewed",
  "fix":"Name the case-state and escalation support process, then re-run acceptance",
  "evidenceId":"<EVIDENCE_ID>"}]'

After the same artifact is repaired, a human designer asks the critic to re-review. The pass row cites the acceptance evidence. The presenter does not export until factcheck confirms that references resolve and the review state is valid.

06 · Use cases

Use cases / case studies

Use case 1 — The cancelled accessible journey

Mina receives an SMS cancellation, calls the contact centre, and chooses voice assistance. The contact worker sees the recovery reference, confirms her preferred communication channel, and names the next update time. The dispatch desk accepts the handoff, and the shelter receives only the information Mina consented to share.

The designer asks researcher to retrieve the original evidence, mapper to update the journey, and critic to check that the flow does not infer a medical trait. The outcome target is 32 minutes to a confirmed safe reroute and two or fewer explanations. Both numbers are illustrative until a pilot is run.

AI working-session summary. The planned BUZZ sequence is @researcher → @mapper → @critic, with MCP/CLI receipts after each artifact write. A representative prompt is: “Trace Mina's preferred channel from <EVIDENCE_IDS> through the journey; flag every handoff that lacks an owner or update time.” A real host invocation and BUZZ reply are PENDING LIVE EVIDENCE. The outcome boundary is important: a successful artifact review can show that the design is coherent; it cannot show that a resident actually reached a shelter or that the 32-minute target was achieved.

Use case 2 — The stale transport promise

An earlier route is delayed beyond its time box. The backstage service marks the plan stale, alerts the contact worker, and requires a human decision before a new promise is made. The critic checks that “automatically rerouted” is not presented as a completed action when only a recommendation exists. The duty manager chooses the safe option.

This use case demonstrates that a good service is also a controlled failure path. A Canvas link to the stale handoff should resolve to its review and remediation records; if it does not, the case study must report the failure and not hide it.

AI working-session summary. The planned prompt is: “@critic inspect the route promise expiry rule; veto any automatically rerouted claim unless an evidence row, owner, timestamp, and human escalation are present.” OpenCode and GitHub Actions may mechanically test the expiry fixture if their receipts are obtained; Claude Code and Codex host execution remain PENDING HOST EVIDENCE. The outcome boundary is a verified failure-handling rule, not a claim that a live dispatch system changed state. Illustrative pilot outcomes are: stale promises detected within five minutes in at least 95% of test cases, and an escalation owner present in 100% of accepted handoffs. These are targets to measure, not observed results.

Use case 3 — Shelter arrival and recovery

Asha receives a concise, consented welcome handoff. She does not receive an inferred diagnosis, a full conversation transcript, or an expired route promise. After the event, the librarian links the outcome to the original evidence and the measurer adds it to the KPI review. The human team decides which learning belongs in the next service standard. The illustrative outcome target is 100% minimum-field completeness, an acknowledgement from the shelter team within ten minutes, and an accessibility clarity score of at least 4.2/5 in the pilot sample. No target is reported as achieved.

AI working-session summary. The planned sequence is @librarian to assemble the consented handoff, @measurer to attach the KPI, and @critic to check for stale or over-broad data. Representative prompt: “Return only the minimum context Asha needs to welcome the resident; cite every field and mark missing evidence.” A Canvas link, MCP artifact, and human gate are PENDING LIVE EVIDENCE for this fictional channel. The outcome boundary is a testable handoff design; it is not evidence of a real shelter interaction.

07 · SOPs for AI

SOPs for AI and guardrails

Each role follows the same encoded pattern: method, inputs, step list, output schema, acceptance criteria, review gate, and human checkpoint. For example, the blueprint SOP requires evidence IDs on nodes, explicit owners, and a review before promotion.

NOTE
method: evidence-gated-blueprint
inputs: evidence register, current journey, opportunity matrix
steps: map lanes; cite evidence; name owner; mark assumptions; request critic review
output: service-blueprint with resolvable references
acceptance: no dangling refs; no unsupported traits; every handoff has an owner
human_checkpoint: duty manager approves safety and operating constraints

The meta-SOP is research → synthesize → map → ideate → prototype → measure → deliver. The main failure modes are stale identity membership, a role claim without a runtime, an artifact that exists only in chat, a review that cites no evidence, and a Canvas link that points nowhere. The mitigations are exact identity audits, persisted IDs, strict review rows, content hashes, and reload tests.

10 · Lessons learned

Lessons learned and pitfalls

  • A polished transcript can be owner-authored and still look like an agent exchange.
  • A role definition, persona slug, and runtime public key are different objects.
  • A passing review without an evidence row is not a passing review.
  • A Canvas is useful only when every link is resolvable and survives reload.
  • A shared MCP count does not prove BUZZ or host execution.
  • ACP records coordination intent; it does not grant spawn authority.
  • Fictional metrics make a method concrete, but they must never be reported as pilot results.
  • Human approval remains necessary for safety, consent, and policy decisions.
