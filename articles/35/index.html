35 — The Butler's Memory: Staging Luxury with the ASD Critic on the Write Path

0. Confidentiality & provenance note

Sable & Slate is a fictional composite of luxury boutique hotel groups; no real property, guest, or financial data appears. This article is a runlog of the Agentic Service Designer (ASD) in Claude Code — the plugin's Critic (kind 38 verdicts) is the star, and the same adversarial review contract ships to AGY (platform/packs/agy/review-session.md) for the heaviest gates. Consent is a designer-and-privacy-officer convention recorded in gate decisions (kind 37), plain and auditable.

1. Executive summary

A nine-property luxury group with €680 ADR digitized its way to impersonality: the app could do everything, so nobody did anything, and reviews said "the technology remembers nothing about me, the staff remember everything busy." One designer rebuilt the stay around a guest-context memory that serves the cast, not the screen, in 17 weeks on the ASD plugin. Check-in 14→4 min, first-touch resolution 61%, turnover 34%→21%, repeat 38%→52%, NPS 41→68, pilot RevPAR +7%.

What you will learn

  • The full plugin surface of article 31 applied to experience staging — and how a privacy convention becomes a gate-artifact rule.
  • How the Critic (veto) is the load-bearing mechanic: it caught an attribution error, a no-trait-inference rule, and a brand-damaging feature — all in files.
  • Why "automation must end where human attention begins" (books 13, 20) is a principle the platform can carry as gates, not vibes.

2. The brief

Sable & Slate: nine boutique properties, €680 ADR, "we remember you." A three-year digital program removed human touchpoints — and with them the memory. Guests re-told preferences at every property; staff had no continuity between shifts or visits; turnover at 34% drained the expertise luxury sells.

Success criteria (illustrative): check-in ≤ 5 min; first-touch resolution ≥ 60%; repeat ≥ 50%; turnover ≤ 20%; NPS ≥ +60; pilot RevPAR +5%. Constraints: no renovation; a unionized nights/concierge workforce to design with; GDPR-grade consent; the brand's non-negotiables (no visible tracking, no "algorithmic" service language, human final authority). Why service design: the canonical experience-economy case (book 13) — the offering is the staged event, the cast is the product (book 20, literal).

3. The plugin, at a glance

Same surface as article 31 §3 — install, ten agents, twelve skills, eleven tools, draft→final, kind-37/38 gates, fact-check, §3.6 honest boundary. This engagement's gate conventions: (1) consent — no personalization artifact passes its gate without a kind-37 decision referencing the consent spec (data fields enumerated, opt-in per purpose, retention, opt-out, read audit) signed by the privacy officer; (2) the Critic sits on every review-gated artifact — and because five of the twelve skills are critic-gated, the Critic is genuinely on the write path. The AGY review contract (platform/packs/agy/review-session.md) is the same adversarial posture, invoked for the heaviest gates.

4. Phase-by-phase session log (Claude Code + ASD, Critic-led)

4.1 Session 1 — Discover (weeks 1–5)

Drive.

/asd plan-from-brief
@researcher run contextual-inquiry for sable-slate: 28 guest interviews, 5
shadow days across desk/concierge/housekeeping/lobby, a 32-person staff
diary, and 24 months of review text mined via transcript-mining.

What the plugin did. The register reached 1,847 content-addressed rows. Transcript-mining flagged needs/frustrations across 18,000 classified review phrases — the engine's real behavior, nothing more.

Team analysis (outside the engine, marked). The critical correction — the "impersonal" reviews were about the loss of a human reference point (staff who no longer knew the guest), not about the app per se — came from the designer's analysis of the classified phrases. It changed the brief from "undo automation" to "re-point automation."

Gate G1. Register in place. Critic over the research plan: one HIGH finding (the attribution error) — adopted before design locked.

Human judgment. The designer ran shadow days across all shifts (incl. the 3am night audit), chose staff diaries over a survey, and framed privacy as a brand asset in front of leadership first.

4.2 Session 2 — Define (weeks 6–8)

Drive.

@mapper run journey-from-transcripts -> the six-moment stay (book, arrive,
dwell, recover, depart, return). @ideator sketch the context-memory concept
as a concept-brief (kind 18). @critic review the memory concept; privacy
officer co-signs the consent gate.

What the plugin did. Six journey-map artifacts wrote through the evidence gate (a "recover" node with zero rows was rejected on first write). The context-memory concept — a guest brief, staff-facing, guest-invisible, consent-bound — came back from the Critic with a veto:

{ "kind": "review-veto-log", "role": "critic", "target": "context-memory",
  "rows": [
    { "type": "defect", "rule": "evidence-sufficiency",
      "message": "auto-filled emotional inferences (guest seems anxious) have
      a dossier feel and zero supporting rows",
      "evidence": [] },
    { "type": "proposal", "rule": "respect",
      "message": "memory stores facts and requests only; never trait judgments;
      every guest-visible surface stays a mirror, never a dossier",
      "evidence": [] }
  ], "verdict": "veto" }

The concept was reworked: facts, requests, boundaries — no trait inferences. The consent spec was drafted as an artifact and the privacy officer signed the gate decision.

Human judgment. The designer named the design object from book 13 — the butler's memory: staff-facing, guest-invisible, read in 30 seconds — and locked the consent default (opt-in, granular, revocable in-app and in-person) with the privacy officer at this phase.

4.3 Session 3 — Develop (weeks 9–13)

Drive.

@mapper run orchestration-canvas (kind 15 — the Mapper owns it) and
service-blueprinting, then evidence-gated-blueprint for pre-arrival, express
arrival, first-touch recovery, and shift handoff.
@prototyper storyboard the four designs (storyboard-walkthrough, kind 23),
each with assumption, fidelity, subjects, and pass/fail criteria.
@critic review each; staff co-design the recovery script.

What the plugin did. The blueprint's five lanes and gates held staging honest: a backstage row that silently reverted to the old 5-day request queue was rejected and corrected. Prototype discipline made every storyboard falsifiable.

Team analysis (outside the engine, marked). The shift-handoff timing check — the "60-second brief" that degrades to 3 minutes under short staffing, fixed as a two-tier brief (minimum/complete) — was the designer's operational analysis with the front-desk cast. The platform did not simulate.

Human judgment. The designer set the staging rule (book 13): the system cues, the staff perform; no script substitutes for judgment. The front-desk co-design veto on "we will make it right" boilerplate became verbatim in the final script.

A second critic veto — the brand call. An old analytics roadmap item — a guest "emotion score" — re-appeared in a concept review. The Critic vetoed it under the same no-trait-inference rule it had enforced in Define; the item was dropped and the boundary recorded in the veto log. The platform's own gate, not the designer, killed a feature that would have broken the brand.

4.4 Session 4 — Deliver (weeks 14–17)

Drive.

@measurer build the kpi-set (kind 26) from the four success criteria.
@presenter assemble the steering packs from final artifacts.
@librarian produce the handover pack.
/asd phase-gate <G4>; asd factcheck --projectId <id>

What the plugin did. Deterministic fact-check across 27 artifacts (per-artifact integrity + acceptance replay) with two privacy-signed gate decisions: { "pass": true, "rows": […] }.

Team analysis (outside the engine, marked). The property rollout rule (week-2 dip at property 7: first-touch 51% vs 60% — adopted the script, not the triage table) was the designer's operational governance; the fix went through the co-design channel.

Human judgment. The designer chose the two pilot properties (one city, one resort — different cadences), ran the union consultation that shaped the night-shift variant, and personally handled the single opt-out complaint in pilot — handled well, and logged as a design success, not a leak.

6. Use cases (routed to the right agents)

6.1 The anniversary return. Pre-arrival with the window seat remembered; a note composed by the ambassador, not an algorithm template (the critic's template-vs-human review kept it human). Repeat-booking interval −21%. 6.2 The business regular's layover. Express arrival, keys by name, invoice sorted in one conversation. City NPS +14 for business stays; a critic-reviewed auto-checkout concept was vetoed (guest control) — the human stayed close. 6.3 The night-shift handoff. A 2am request crossing concierge to night audit, carried by the tier-1 brief. Handoff loss 12%→4%; the two-tier split was the team's operational analysis (marked).

7. SOPs for AI — the adversarial write path

Same role contract and skills as article 31 §7. The Critic is the headline mechanic here, and its own instructions.md names the order: schema → referential integrity → acceptance rules → judgment, then falsification (absence of contrary evidence is not confirmation), placeholder hunt, and the number ledger. A veto without a fix suggestion is incomplete; a pass without a proposal is complacency. Consent is a kind-37 gate convention shared with the privacy officer. Failure modes observed: (1) the attribution error (app vs lost human reference) — analyst-corrected; (2) a persona with an invented anniversary detail — integrity rejected; (3) the emotion-score roadmap item — the critic killed it by applying its own Define-era rule; the veto log records the boundary holding.

9. AI tool playbook

Claude Code + ASD plugin — the engagement; the Critic was the star and produced two gate-changing vetoes. Failed at: staging judgment — the book-13 rule (cues, not scripts) stayed the designer's. AGY (platform/packs/agy/review-session.md) — the heaviest gates ran as external adversarial sessions; same posture, separate machine. asd CLI / MCP — deterministic cross-checks and fact-check. GitHub — PR artifact gates, privacy review on consent changes. Other hosts ship the same tree (OpenCode, Grok, OMP/Pi, Codex).

10. Lessons learned & pitfalls

  1. The "impersonal" signal misattributed to the app was really the lost human reference; attribution checks change briefs.
  2. Privacy is a brand asset in luxury; the consent-spec gate bought more trust than any marketing line.
  3. Stage the system to cue, staff to perform (book 13); every script needs a human-judgment escape.
  4. Two-tier briefs survive short-staffing; single-tier promises fail at 3am.
  5. Staff co-design is not consultation; the front-desk veto measurably improved the recovery script.
  6. Opt-out is a design success, not a leak; handling it well turned one complaint into praise.

13. Tutorial — reproduce this engagement (20 minutes)

export ASD_WORKSPACE=$HOME/consult-sable-slate
asd() { platform/packs/cli/asd "$@"; }
OUT=$(asd project-create --name sable-slate --client "Fictional composite" --sector hospitality --service "luxury stays")
ID=$(echo "$OUT" | grep -oE 'sable-slate-[a-f0-9]{8}' | head -1)
E1=$(asd evidence-add --projectId "$ID" --source review-class-0412 --kind quote --body "The technology remembers nothing about me" | grep -oE '"[a-f0-9]{64}"' | tr -d '"' | head -1)
E2=$(asd evidence-add --projectId "$ID" --source frontdesk-02 --kind quote --body "There is no memory between shifts" | grep -oE '"[a-f0-9]{64}"' | tr -d '"' | head -1)
NODES=$(printf '[{"id":"arrive","label":"Arrive","kind":"step","fields":{"emotion":"2","touchpoint":"desk"},"evidence":[{"evidenceId":"%s"},{"evidenceId":"%s"}]},{"id":"checkin","label":"Check-in","kind":"step","fields":{"emotion":"1","touchpoint":"desk"},"evidence":[{"evidenceId":"%s"}]},{"id":"dwell","label":"Dwell","kind":"pain","fields":{"emotion":"2","touchpoint":"room"},"evidence":[{"evidenceId":"%s"},{"evidenceId":"%s"}]}]' "$E1" "$E2" "$E1" "$E1" "$E2")
asd artifact-write --projectId "$ID" --methodId journey-from-transcripts --title "Remembered stay" --nodes "$NODES"
asd method-run --methodId good-services-audit --projectId "$ID"
asd factcheck --projectId "$ID"

What you should see: {"accepted": true}; the good-services contract; factcheck → {"pass": true}. Add the consent convention before a real gate: a signed kind-37 decision referencing the consent spec, and a kind-38 critic verdict on every review-gated artifact — that's how the emotion-score feature died on the platform, not in a meeting.

Try-it checklist. (1) Install (§3); (2) @researcher → two evidence rows; (3) write a journey and rebut the gate rejection; (4) draft a context-memory concept with a trait inference in it and let the critic veto it; (5) run asd factcheck at the gate — then, for the heaviest pass, run the AGY review contract against your own milestone diff.
