35 — The Butler's Memory: Staging Luxury with an Adversarial Critic on the Write Path

0. Confidentiality & provenance note

Sable & Slate is a fictional composite of luxury boutique hotel groups; no real property, guest, or financial data appears. This article is a runlog of the Service Design Agent Platform (SDAP) with AGY as the starring host: the AGY pack is the Critic role itself — an adversarial review session that sits on the write path of every artifact. The designer drove the rest of the platform through MCP; AGY's verdicts are logged as review-veto-log artifacts, and several improved the work materially.

1. Executive summary

A nine-property luxury boutique group with €680 ADR digitized its way to impersonality: the app could do everything, so nobody did anything, and reviews said so — "the technology remembers nothing about me, the staff remember everything busy." One designer rebuilt the stay around a guest-context memory that serves the cast, not the screen, in 17 weeks on the platform, with AGY's adversarial critic gating every artifact. Check-in 14→4 min, first-touch resolution 61%, staff turnover 34%→21%, repeat guests 38%→52%, NPS 41→68, pilot RevPAR +7%.

What you will learn

  • How an adversarial reviewer (AGY, the same engine as this platform's own milestone gates) sits on the write path of every artifact — and why that is a design feature, not QA.
  • The experience-economy argument (book 13) made operational: luxury is staging, and staging needs a memory with consent.
  • How privacy becomes a hard gate (opt-in, granular, auditable) exactly like compliance or clinical governance.

2. The brief

Sable & Slate: nine boutique properties, €680 ADR, "we remember you." A three-year digital program removed human touchpoints — and with them the memory. Guests re-told preferences at every property; staff had no continuity between shifts or visits; turnover at 34% was draining the expertise luxury sells.

Success criteria (illustrative): check-in ≤ 5 min; first-touch resolution ≥ 60%; repeat-guest rate ≥ 50%; staff turnover ≤ 20%; guest NPS ≥ +60; pilot RevPAR +5%. Constraints: no hardware renovation; a unionized nights/concierge workforce to design with; GDPR-grade consent; brand's non-negotiable: no visible tracking, no "algorithmic" service language; human final authority on guest experience. Why service design: the canonical experience-economy case (book 13) — the offering is the staged event, and the cast is the product (book 20, literal).

3. The platform run: AGY host, roster, and plan

3.1 Stand up the AGY pack (real steps)

# from platform/packs/agy — the review contract + README:
agy --file review-session.md     # or: herdr-review --tab, feed the contract

The review-session.md contract tells AGY exactly how to review: assume defects; cite file:line evidence; grade HIGH/MED/LOW with a concrete fix; produce the report into .reviews/ with a disposition table; don't be polite. The same contract this series uses for its own gates. For the design runs the designer drove the engine over MCP (asd-mcp), with AGY summoned at every gate.

4. Phase-by-phase session log (AGY-gated)

4.1 Session 1 — Discover (weeks 1–5)

Drive. Researcher over MCP, then AGY reviews the research plan and the first synthesis:

@researcher run contextual-inquiry: 28 guest interviews, 5 shadow days
across desk/concierge/housekeeping/lobby, 32-person staff diary, 24 months
of review-text + repeat-guest analytics. Build the register.
AGY: review the research plan and the cause analysis.

What the platform did. The register reached 1,847 rows. AGY's first real catch: the obvious conclusion — "the app is the problem" — was wrong. Reviewing 18,000 classified review phrases, AGY (with the researcher's data) showed the attribute was the loss of a human reference point: staff who no longer knew the guest. The brief changed accordingly: automation was not to be undone but re-pointed.

Gate G1. Register ≥ 3 rows held. AGY over plan: one HIGH finding (attribute error), adopted.

Human judgment. The designer ran the shadow days across all shifts (incl. 3am night audit), chose staff diaries over a survey, and framed privacy as a brand asset in front of the leadership before any stakeholder session.

4.2 Session 2 — Define (weeks 6–8)

Drive.

@mapper run journey-from-transcripts -> the six-moment stay (book, arrive,
dwell, recover, depart, return). @ideator sketch the context-memory concept.
AGY: adversarially review the memory concept and the consent spec.

What the platform did. Six-moment journey written as journey-map artifacts (evidence-gated; a "recover" node with zero evidence rows was rejected on first write and re-derived). The context-memory concept — a guest brief, staff-facing, guest-invisible, consent-bound — was drafted, and AGY vetoed the first version for a design sin, not a technical one:

{ "kind": "review-veto-log", "role": "critic", "target": "context-memory",
  "verdict": "veto", "rule": "respect + evidence",
  "message": "auto-filled emotional inferences (guest seems anxious) have a
  dossier feel and zero supporting rows. Memory stores facts and requests
  only; never trait judgments." }

The concept was reworked: facts, requests, boundaries; no trait inferences. The consent spec (opt-in, granular, revocable in-app and in-person, 13-month retention, audit-trailed reads) became its own artifact.

Human judgment. The designer named the design object from book 13: the butler's memory — staff-facing, guest-invisible, read in 30 seconds. The consent default was locked with the privacy officer at this phase, not at launch.

4.3 Session 3 — Develop (weeks 9–13)

Drive.

@ideator run orchestration-canvas; @prototyper storyboard pre-arrival,
express arrival, first-touch recovery, shift handoff.
@mapper run evidence-gated-blueprint. AGY: review each blueprint + the
recovery script with the front-desk cast.

What the platform did. Forty-two concepts → 15 → four storyboarded designs. evidence-gated-blueprint refused backstage rows that didn't trace to a principle and an insight. AGY caught the handoff physics: the socialized "60-second shift handoff" degraded to 3 minutes under short staffing in a shift-simulation pass; the design was revised to a two-tier brief (minimum/complete) with a decision rule. AGY also pushed back on an old analytics roadmap item — a guest "emotion score" — and this time AGY itself conceded it violated the consent spec the critic had enforced two phases earlier; the roadmap item was killed and the boundary recorded.

Human judgment. The designer set the staging rule (book 13): the system cues, the staff perform; no script substitutes for judgment. The front-desk co-design veto on "we will make it right" boilerplate became verbatim in the final script — the cast improving the artifact.

4.4 Session 4 — Deliver (weeks 14–17)

Drive.

@measurer build the KPI tree and wire telemetry; @presenter render steering
packs from artifact ids; @librarian assemble the handover. Run sd_factcheck
at G4; enforce the property rollout rule.

What the platform did. KPI tree (kind kpi-set); good-services-audit scoring; fact-check at G4 — 27 artifacts, 2 privacy artifacts co-signed, 0 dangling refs, 0 schema violations, "pass": true (excerpt §13). The rollout rule flagged property 7's second-week dip in first-touch resolution (51% vs 60%): the property had adopted the script but not the triage table; correction pushed via the co-design channel.

Human judgment. The designer chose the two pilot properties (one city, one resort — different cadences), ran the union consultation that shaped the night-shift variant, and personally handled the single guest-data opt-out complaint in pilot — handled well, and logged as a design success, not a leak.

6. Platform use cases (AGY sub-sessions)

6.1 The anniversary return. Pre-arrival with the window seat remembered; a note composed by the ambassador, not an algorithm template (AGY's template-vs-human review kept it human). Repeat-booking interval −21%. 6.2 The business regular's layover. Express arrival, keys by name, invoice sorted in one conversation. City-property NPS +14 for business stays. AGY reviewing the auto-checkout concept vetoed it (guest control), keeping the human close. 6.3 The night-shift handoff. A 2am request crossing concierge to night audit carried by the tier-1 brief. Handoff loss 12%→4%. AGY validated the 2-tier split by simulation.

7. SOPs for AI — the adversarial write path

The AGY host makes the series' signature move literal: the critic sits on the write path with veto power, and its verdicts are artifacts. Order of checks (from the critic's own instructions.md, quoted): schema → referential integrity → acceptance rules → then judgment. Falsification: for every insight, what observation would falsify it and is it absent from the register? Placeholder hunt: "unknown/tbd/n-a" are automatic defects. Numbers: any figure must agree with the register's number ledger. And the quality bar: a veto without a fix suggestion is incomplete; a pass without one proposal is complacency.

Guardrails. Privacy is the hard gate: no personalization artifact ships without a consent spec, mechanically (same pattern as compliance in case 34 and clinical governance in case 33). No trait-inference storage — enforced by veto and by the spec. Staff data treated as confidential; no per-individual performance metrics. Failure modes observed: (1) the "impersonal" attribution error corrected by AGY before the brief locked; (2) a generated persona with an invented anniversary detail — integrity rejected; (3) the 60-second handoff overpromise caught in simulation; (4) the emotion-score roadmap item killed when AGY applied its own earlier consent ruling.

9. AI tool playbook (host: AGY)

AGY — the adversarial critic and this article's star: research-plan review (caught the attribution error), every gate verdict, blueprint physics (two-tier handoff), and the consent-spec consistency challenge. Type of failure: occasionally too clever; its best value is being wrong visibly — every veto is a logged artifact the human can overrule. asd CLI / asd-mcp — the deterministic spine: evidence, artifacts, gates, fact-check; the critic's verdicts land against this machine layer. OpenCode-style pipelines — review-text classification and cohort datasets (18k phrases). GitHub — PR artifact gates (schema/evidence/mermaid), privacy-reviewer approval on consent-spec changes. Other hosts ship the same canon: Claude Code (plugin pack), Grok (prompts + MCP), OMP/Pi (agents+skills tree), Codex (AGENTS.md + MCP).

10. Lessons learned & pitfalls

  1. The "impersonal" signal misattributed to the app was really the lost human reference; attribution errors change briefs — put an adversarial pass before locking.
  2. Privacy is a brand asset in luxury; the consent spec bought more trust than any marketing line.
  3. Stage the system to cue, staff to perform (book 13); every script needs a human-judgment escape.
  4. Two-tier briefs survive short-staffing; single-tier promises fail at 3am.
  5. Staff co-design is not consultation; the front-desk veto measurably improved the recovery script.
  6. Opt-out is a design success, not a leak; handling it well turned one complaint into praise.

export ASD_WORKSPACE=$HOME/consult-sable-slate

What you should see. "accepted": true; the good-services contract; factcheck → "pass": true. Gate mechanics, honestly: sd_factcheck takes exactly --projectId — it re-runs integrity and the producing methods' acceptance rules over every artifact. Privacy co-signature is a project-configuration gate tracked as phase-gate-decision + review-veto-log artifacts; there is no CLI flag for it.

artifactId is the store's content hash — it varies per workspace. The real gate at G4 is exactly this: one row per artifact, integrity (dangling refs) and acceptance (method rules replayed against the live registry) both pass, top-level pass: true.

Try-it checklist. (1) Start asd-mcp; create the project; add two evidence rows; (2) write a journey artifact and watch the gate reject a zero-evidence node; (3) run the AGY review contract (agy --file platform/packs/agy/review-session.md) against your artifact and read the verdict — then keep or overrule it with a written rationale; (4) add a consent-spec artifact and make factcheck demand it; (5) run a full phase gate and inspect the review-veto-log trail. The loop — design → artifact → adversarial verdict → disposition → fact-check — is the engagement, and every loop lands as a file you can audit.
