Three journeys, one funnel

JLThe first-job school leaver
Crew candidate · under legal working age

Context. First ever job; the family is in the loop on the paperwork. No bank account, tax number, or photo identification yet.

Goals. A job close to home; a clear next step; parents satisfied the process is legitimate.

Pains. The form asks for information the family does not have yet; identity validation becomes difficult and lengthy without photo ID; the parental-consent form expires after seven days; no human anywhere in the loop.

Design response. Minimal-ask conversation that works on a phone; guided document capture; a human review lane; the parental-consent flow runs before anything else proceeds.

RCThe returning crew member
Re-hire · transfer across the network

Context. Worked at the chain before, left, and is coming back — sometimes to the same franchisee group, sometimes to a different one.

Goals. Pick up where they left off; training and credentials carry over; no repeated paperwork.

Pains. Treated like a brand-new applicant; the work-rights check and onboarding form repeated pointlessly; records never off-boarded, so the system does not even know they left.

Design response. A re-hire branch at the application stage: same franchisee group skips the work-rights check and onboarding and transfers the record; everyone else runs the full workflow with new records.

AMThe manager hiring inside a full day
Assistant restaurant manager · hiring as one more duty

Context. Carries recruitment inside a day of shifts, paperwork, and customer peaks.

Goals. Fill shifts with good people, fast; admin in minutes, not hours; see candidate status without chasing.

Pains. Re-enters candidate data across systems; texts applicants to arrange interviews; discovers wrong documents only at pay time; unreachable candidates are never closed off.

Design response. A review queue plus an auto-scheduled interview calendar; automated offers, reminders, and employee setup; unreachable candidates dispositioned after 10 days of no action.

How the personas behave in the design

Each persona maps to a blueprint lane and a set of measures — a persona that does not change a design decision is cut.

Evidence discipline

Twelve-plus personas in total, each composite and grounded in interview, observation, and survey evidence. The three above are the journeys the engagement follows end to end: the core candidate, the re-hire, and the manager who carries hiring inside an already full day.
