Triangulated evidence

Fieldwork stayed human — consent, rapport, and observing a real interview are not delegable. Everything after the recording was agent-amplified: transcripts pseudonymized and processed by an extraction team with verbatim quote verification, survey open-text triaged, and the funnel pipeline normalized into a single leakage view.

Application completed18,500−10% · 45% cumulative drop-off

The funnel reads like an autopsy: the biggest losses sit in the middle of the form — start to details to upload — where candidates hit unnecessary asks and repeated requests. “Effort to complete the application” was logged as the leading volume-loss driver. The design implication was minimal-ask: every field had to survive the question does this change a decision?

The two no-data stages are the point. The chain knew how many people applied and how many got offers; it could not tell you how many new hires actually showed up for a first shift, or who was still there at 90 days. “Anecdotal evidence runs supreme in this business because the data is just too hard to get your hands on.” Making the missing data visible, with owners attached, was a first-class deliverable.

The top of the pipeline eroded as the bottom leaked: the six-plus-year tenure path from crew to restaurant manager compressed, and only about a quarter of crew expressed interest in stepping up to shift supervisor. The manager competency framework was the response.
