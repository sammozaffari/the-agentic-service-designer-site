32 — Air in the Cylinders: Servitizing a Capital-Equipment Vendor with the ASD Plugin

0. Confidentiality & provenance note

Aurora Vento is a fictional composite industrial vendor; no real contract or field data appears. This article is a runlog of the Agentic Service Designer (ASD) as installed into Claude Code. Plugin surface, agents, skills, tools, gates, and outputs are the real platform (platform/packs/claude-code + @asd/runtime). What the article calls the team's analysis is the designer's own work outside the engine, marked as such — the engine holds evidence, typed artifacts, and gates.

1. Executive summary

A hydraulic access vendor with 41,000 machines in the field earned 31% of service revenue from aftersales add-ons. One designer re-architected services around outcome contracts in 22 weeks on the ASD plugin: Researcher builds the register, Synthesizer/Mapper frame the opportunity, Measurer wires the KPI set, Critic gates every review-gated artifact. Service revenue share 31%→48%, renewals 71%→88%, MTTR 5.2→2.9 days, first-time fix 61%→79%, technician utilization 58%→71%.

2. The brief

Aurora Vento: number-two vendor of hydraulic access platforms; hardware margin eroding; services a profit pool trapped by its own processes (74% manual work orders, remote parts availability 81%, MTTR 5.2 days). Anchor customers put availability guarantees on the table; leadership's instinct was pricing, the constraint was the service system.

Success criteria (illustrative, contract-shaped): availability ≥ 98% on pilots; MTTR ≤ 3 d; first-time fix ≥ 75%; technician utilization ≥ 68%; renewal ≥ 85%; services share ≥ 45%. Constraints: no hardware redesign; two-year rollout; legacy ERP; collective agreements limiting remote interventions; regional data protection on telemetry. Why service design: the offer is a value proposition in use (book 12) and the delivery chain runs through the technicians who are the brand (book 20).

Eleven engine tools (available in Claude Code via asd-mcp, or on the CLI as asd <tool>): sd_project_list/create/open, sd_evidence_add/query, sd_artifact_write (and sd_artifact_list/get), sd_method_list/run, sd_factcheck. Draft→final status; gates are kind-37 decisions and kind-38 critic rows; sd_factcheck re-runs integrity + acceptance over every artifact.

4. Phase-by-phase session log (Claude Code + ASD)

4.1 Session 1 — Discover (weeks 1–6)

Drive.

/asd plan-from-brief
@researcher run contextual-inquiry for aurora-vento: 24 operations/fleet
interviews, 9 technician shadow days, 68,000 work-order records mined via
transcript-mining. Add every quote and datum to the register.

What the plugin did. The Researcher ran the skills and the register grew to 2,847 content-addressed rows via sd_evidence_add. Transcript-mining flagged needs/frustrations across the work-order descriptions (its real behavior — it does not claim a failure-taxonomy). The register held raw rows; nothing downstream proceeded on un-evidenced ground.

Team analysis (outside the engine, marked). The cause analysis — 41% of delays were dispatch mismatch (part without technician or vice versa), not bare parts availability — came from the designer's own study of the register and raw logs. That finding reordered the whole roadmap.

Gate G1. Evidence register in place. The critic reviewed the research plan (no veto; one MEDIUM proposal on sample mix, adopted).

Human judgment. The designer chose technician shadowing over extra customer interviews (book 20's internal-customer argument), sat the collective-agreement consultation, and killed a warranty-fraud research question that would have poisoned trust.

4.2 Session 2 — Define (weeks 7–9)

Drive.

@mapper run ecosystem-value-mapping to draw the value flows across Aurora,
ERP, telemetry, parts, distributors, and customers — write the ecosystem-map.
@mapper then draft the outcome definition as a concept-brief object; hand it
to @critic.

What the plugin did. The ecosystem-map (kind ecosystem-map, min 3 nodes, all critic-gated) rendered the actor→value-flow picture from evidence. The first outcome definition — "guaranteed operational performance" — failed the critic on measurability:

{ "kind": "review-veto-log", "role": "critic", "target": "outcome-definition",
  "rows": [
    { "type": "defect", "rule": "measurability",
      "message": "no availability formula, no exclusions, no data source — not
      an outcome, a slogan",
      "evidence": [] },
    { "type": "proposal", "rule": "measurability",
      "message": "name the formula, window, exclusions, data source, remedy",
      "evidence": [] }
  ], "verdict": "veto" }

The reworked definition named: uptime in agreed windows; exclusions (scheduled maintenance, force majeure, site-level simultaneous failures capped at 5% of windows); telemetry as the data source; site-level accounting; 2% credit per point under target.

Human judgment. The designer and finance owned the pricing and the risk pool; the platform produced the measurable and the veto record.

4.3 Session 3 — Develop (weeks 10–16)

Drive.

@ideator run structured ideation off the journey pain points; write the
concept-briefs (kind 18).
@prototyper storyboard the three operating models (central dispatch,
region-embedded, hybrid) as storyboard-walkthrough objects (kind 23).
@mapper run service-blueprinting then evidence-gated-blueprint for the
hybrid model.

What the plugin did. The blueprint's five lanes made the fix explicit: coupled part+skill dispatch, regional parts hubs, telediagnosis, auto close-out. The evidence-gated-blueprint gate (min 5 nodes; every node ≥1 ref; lane not deny-listed; fail points trace to customer symptoms) rejected an early version that reverted verification to the old silent 5-day queue. The critic gate verified no blueprint node contradicted the register.

Team analysis (outside the engine, marked). The availability-model stress test — including the finding that the naive 5% cap let a cluster of two same-site failures count as one breach — was the designer's analysis with synthetic spreadsheets, cross-checked adversarially; the platform did not simulate. The contract clause (site-level accounting) shipped because of it.

Human judgment. The designer chose the hybrid model, defended technician tooling spend against finance (book 20), and ran the union consultation that bounded remote monitoring.

4.4 Session 4 — Deliver (weeks 17–22)

Drive.

@measurer build the kpi-set (kind 26): each row names metric, driver,
experience link, business link, baseline, source.
@presenter assemble the steering pack from final artifacts.
@librarian produce the handover pack with provenance.
/asd phase-gate <G4>; asd factcheck --projectId <id>

What the plugin did. The kpi-set wired contract terms → operational telemetry rows (analyst-sourced baselines marked). Deterministic fact-check across 28 artifacts (per-artifact integrity + acceptance replay): { "pass": true, "rows": […] } — per-artifact integrity (no dangling refs) and acceptance replay. Pilot 1 nearly lost its facility manager mid-run; the rollout was renegotiated by the designer, not the platform.

Human judgment. The designer renegotiated pilot scope at week 2 (organizational risk), and chose to publish the outcome numbers internally warts and all — a leadership call recorded in the gate decision (kind 37).

6. Use cases (routed to the right agents)

6.1 The rental fleet manager (Boreas). Mapper session: ecosystem-map the Boreas web of five instruments; Measurer session: kpi-set rows for the breach-detect rule. Renewal +6 machines in month 4; NPS −4→+18. 6.2 The technician's Tuesday. Researcher's shadow evidence → behavioural-personas (P3, 63 rows); Prototyper's "what do technicians hate" storyboards → the tooling bundle. First-time fix 61%→81%; voluntary overtime up (the book-20 signal). 6.3 The site-level cluster loophole. Designer's stress analysis (marked) surfaced it; Prototyper storyboarded the customer-side conversation; the fixed clause became the sales differentiator. The platform logged the contract change as an artifact with provenance.

7. SOPs for AI — skills as the encoded method

Same discipline as article 31, §7: skills are files you can read; the role contract governs handoffs. For this engagement the load-bearing ones were transcript-mining (flag needs/frustrations; merge only into distinct themes; critic rejects paraphrased single-line insights) and good-services-audit (score with evidence; critic rejects uncited low scores). The platform's deterministic core: evidence resolution, acceptance, status, fact-check. The rest — cause analysis, stress models, pricing — is the team's, and the articles say so.

Failure modes observed. (1) The "parts availability" cause claim was published early and was wrong — the dispatch attribution came from the analyst pass, not from the register; the critic gates the artifact, not the analysis. (2) A generated persona cited a nonexistent row — integrity rejected. (3) The naive 5% cap in an early contract draft — designer's analysis, corrected before launch.

9. AI tool playbook

Claude Code + ASD plugin — the engagement: agents, skills, tools, gates. Excelled at evidence-scale synthesis (2,847 rows) and the agreement-map drafting; failed at pricing judgment by design. asd CLI / MCP — deterministic cross-checks and fact-check; shell is never trusted where the store is authoritative. GitHub — PR artifact gates (schema/evidence/mermaid), issues as design tasks. Other hosts ship the same tree (OpenCode, Grok, OMP/Pi, Codex, AGY).

10. Lessons learned & pitfalls

  1. The cause analysis is the analyst's — publish it behind an adversarial pass, not out of the register alone.
  2. Outcome contracts are artifact-design problems; this one survived legal because it was machine-checkable.
  3. Technicians are the product (book 20); co-designing the tooling beat any customer interview.
  4. The union boundary is design input; timing it early saved the pilot.
  5. Finance fights the risk pool; give them the model artifact, not a promise.
  6. Presenter packs from artifacts only — a chat-built deck once carried a stale MTTR.

13. Tutorial — reproduce this engagement (15 minutes)

The same lifecycle as article 31's §13, for this case. Runs verbatim against the real platform.

export ASD_WORKSPACE=$HOME/consult-aurora-vento
asd() { platform/packs/cli/asd "$@"; }
OUT=$(asd project-create --name aurora-vento --client "Fictional composite" --sector industrial-manufacturing --service "field service and outcome contracts")
ID=$(echo "$OUT" | grep -oE 'aurora-vento-[a-f0-9]{8}' | head -1)
E1=$(asd evidence-add --projectId "$ID" --source ops-manager-04 --kind quote --body "We buy uptime, we get invoices" | grep -oE '"[a-f0-9]{64}"' | tr -d '"' | head -1)
E2=$(asd evidence-add --projectId "$ID" --source technician-09 --kind quote --body "I drive out with half the parts" | grep -oE '"[a-f0-9]{64}"' | tr -d '"' | head -1)
NODES=$(printf '[{"id":"fault","label":"Fault occurs","kind":"step","fields":{"emotion":"1","touchpoint":"machine"},"evidence":[{"evidenceId":"%s"},{"evidenceId":"%s"}]},{"id":"dispatch","label":"Dispatch","kind":"step","fields":{"emotion":"2","touchpoint":"planner"},"evidence":[{"evidenceId":"%s"}]},{"id":"repair","label":"Second visit","kind":"pain","fields":{"emotion":"1","touchpoint":"site"},"evidence":[{"evidenceId":"%s"},{"evidenceId":"%s"}]}]' "$E1" "$E2" "$E1" "$E1" "$E2")
asd artifact-write --projectId "$ID" --methodId journey-from-transcripts --title "Downtime" --nodes "$NODES"
asd method-run --methodId ecosystem-value-mapping --projectId "$ID"
asd factcheck --projectId "$ID"

What you should see: {"accepted": true} on the journey write; the ecosystem-value-mapping method contract from method-run; factcheck → {"pass": true}.

Try-it checklist. (1) Install plugin per §3; (2) @researcher → two evidence rows; (3) write the journey and make the gate reject a zero-evidence node; (4) @critic → get a real veto on a vague outcome definition, then remediate against the rows; (5) @measurer → kpi-set rows with baselines; run asd factcheck at the gate. The engagement is the loop, and every loop is a file you can audit.
