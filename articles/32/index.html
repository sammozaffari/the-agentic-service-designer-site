32 — Air in the Cylinders: Servitization Driven through the Grok Pack

0. Confidentiality & provenance note

Aurora Vento is a fictional composite industrial vendor; no real contract or field data appears. This article is a runlog of the Service Design Agent Platform (SDAP) as consumed by Grok through the MCP server, with the ten role prompts loaded from the Grok pack. Every command shown was run against the real platform; every figure is machine-checked by the fact-check layer.

1. Executive summary

A hydraulic access vendor with 41,000 machines in the field earned 31% of revenue from services sold as aftersales add-ons. One designer re-architected services around outcome contracts by driving the platform from Grok: load the role prompt for the phase, let that role run the matching skill through the asd-mcp server, gate with fact-check, and keep the human in the pricing chair. Service revenue share 31%→48%, renewals 71%→88%, MTTR 5.2→2.9 days, first-time fix 61%→79%, technician utilization 58%→71%.

What you will learn

  • How Grok consumes the platform: start the MCP server, load the role prompt, drive the phase — no plugin install needed.
  • Why the availability contract is a machine-checkable artifact, and how the outcome definition survives legal review because it is.
  • How S-D logic (book 12) and internal service quality (book 20) show up as platform kinds, not slogans.

2. The brief

Aurora Vento: number-two vendor of hydraulic access platforms, eroding hardware margin, and a services profit pool trapped by its own processes — 74% manual work orders, parts availability 81% at remote regions, MTTR 5.2 days. Anchor customers put availability guarantees on the table. Leadership's instinct was pricing; the design evidence showed the constraint was the service system itself.

Success criteria (illustrative, contract-shaped): availability ≥ 98% on pilot sites; MTTR ≤ 3 days; first-time fix ≥ 75%; technician utilization ≥ 68%; renewal ≥ 85%; services share ≥ 45%. Constraints: no hardware redesign; two-year rollout; legacy ERP; collective agreements limiting remote interventions; regional data protection on telemetry. Why service design: the offer is a value proposition in use (book 12), and the delivery chain runs through the human technicians who are the brand (book 20).

3. The platform run: Grok host, roster, and plan

3.1 Wire Grok to the platform (real steps)

cd <repo>/suite && pnpm install && pnpm --filter @asd/runtime build
ln -s "$PWD/packages/runtime/lib/mcp.mjs" /usr/local/bin/asd-mcp
# register asd-mcp.json in your MCP client; ASD_WORKSPACE defaults to ~/.asd/projects

Grok consumes the platform through the MCP server; platform/packs/grok/prompts/ holds the ten role system prompts as shareable text. Working pattern: for each phase, paste the role prompt (orchestrator → researcher → synthesizer → mapper → ideator → prototyper → measurer → critic → presenter → librarian), then instruct the model to drive sd_* tools. The canon is identical to every other host — same engine, same tool definitions, same gates.

4. Phase-by-phase session log (Grok + MCP)

4.1 Session 1 — Discover (weeks 1–6)

Drive. Load the researcher prompt, then:

You are the platform researcher. Run sd_project_create for aurora-vento,
then execute the contextual-inquiry skill: shadow 9 technicians across 3
regions and 24 customer operations/fleet interviews. Normalize the 68,000
work-order records via transcript-mining and write every claim as an
evidence-add row. Call sd_evidence_query to show the register is sound.

What the platform did. Every row landed with a sha-256 id, project-scoped; the register reached 2,847 rows. Mid-phase the designer ran an adversarial sweep ("critic prompt: attack the cause analysis") and caught the driver error: 41% of delays were dispatch mismatch (truck + part, no technician, or vice versa), not bare parts availability. The finding was corrected before synthesis locked.

Gate G1. Register ≥ 3 rows; held at week 5. Critic over the research plan: no veto.

Human judgment. The designer chose technician shadowing over more customer interviews (book 20's internal-customer argument), sat the collective-agreement consultation, and killed a warranty-fraud research question that would have poisoned trust.

4.2 Session 2 — Define (weeks 7–9)

Drive.

Load the synthesizer prompt, then the mapper. Run affinity-diagramming and
ecosystem-value-mapping; produce the agreement map artifact showing the 5
overlapping SLAs per major customer. Then draft the outcome definition and
submit it for critique.

What the platform did. Nineteen insight statements; the agreement map artifact consolidated 5 instruments per customer (some contradictory) into one contract object; a first outcome definition — "guaranteed operational performance" — was vetoed by the critic as unmeasurable:

{ "kind": "review-veto-log", "role": "critic", "target": "outcome-definition",
  "verdict": "veto", "rule": "measurability",
  "message": "no availability formula, no exclusions, no data source — not an outcome, a slogan." }

The definition was reworkable: formula (uptime in agreed windows), exclusions (scheduled maintenance, force majeure, site-level simultaneous failures capped at 5% of windows), data source (telemetry), accounting level (site), remedy (2% credit per point under target).

Human judgment. The designer and finance owned the risk pool and the pricing; the platform produced the measurable and the evidence. The veto log stayed as the record.

4.3 Session 3 — Develop (weeks 10–16)

Drive.

Load ideator → prototyper → mapper. Run orchestration-canvas, then
service-blueprinting with evidence-gated-blueprint for the target operation
under the finalized outcome definition. Stress-test the availability model.

What the platform did. Forty-eight concepts, NUF to 14, three storyboarded operating models. The blueprint named the fix: coincident part+skill dispatch, regional parts hubs, telediagnosis, auto close-out. An adversarial stress-test (100k simulated failures, Codex-style pass) exposed a cluster-failure loophole: two machines failing at one site counted only one breach under the naive cap; the contract was revised to site-level accounting before launch, not after.

Human judgment. The designer chose the hybrid operating model, defended technician tooling spend against finance (book 20), and ran the union consultation that bounded remote-monitoring.

4.4 Session 4 — Deliver (weeks 17–22)

Drive.

Load measurer → presenter → librarian. Build the KPI tree from the contract
terms; run good-services-audit; render both pilot-region steering packs from
artifact ids; assemble the handover pack. Run sd_factcheck at gate G4.

What the platform did. KPI tree wired contract terms → operational telemetry; 28 artifacts, zero dangling refs, zero schema violations at G4; fact-check "pass": true (excerpt §13). Pilot 1 nearly lost its facility manager mid-run; the rollout rule held and the designer reset the client-side point person.

Human judgment. The designer renegotiated pilot scope at week 2 (organizational risk, not design risk), and chose to publish the outcome numbers internally warts and all — an explicit leadership decision recorded in a phase-gate-decision artifact.

6. Platform use cases (Grok sub-sessions)

6.1 The rental fleet manager (Boreas). Mapper session: consolidate Boreas's five instruments into one contract; measurer session: wire the breach-detect rule so availability credits compute and issue. Outcome: renewal +6 machines in month 4; NPS −4→+18.

6.2 The technician's Tuesday. Researcher's shadow analysis → behavioural-personas; ideator's "what do technicians hate" run produced the tooling bundle; measurer tracked first-time fix 61%→81% and voluntary overtime up (the internal-quality signal, book 20).

6.3 The site-level cluster loophole. Adversarial stress-test found it; prototyper storyboarded the customer-side discussion; the fixed clause became the sales differentiator. The platform logged the contract change as an artifact with provenance through legal.

7. SOPs for AI — skills as the encoded method

The Grok host never sees the skills as files; it sees them as instructions the role prompt loads and the MCP server enforces. contextual-inquiry demands evidence-set (min 3) and emits insight-set; transcript-mining tags failure vs value demand with the book-19 taxonomy; evidence-gated-blueprint refuses changes that don't trace to a principle and an insight; good-services-audit scores 15 principles with evidence on every below-target cell.

Guardrails. Referential integrity is absolute (dangling refs hard-error). Numbers: the fact-check layer keeps the ledger — MTTR, 41%, 98% never drift between artifact and dataset. Pricing: no auto-approval; the human and finance owned the risk pool and the platform recorded the boundary. Failure modes observed: (1) a generated persona cited a nonexistent row — rejected; (2) the naive cap crept into an early draft — the stress test and the veto log caught it; (3) Codex-style passes sometimes over-instrumented against the collective agreement — the designer enforced the boundary at the phase gate.

9. AI tool playbook (host: Grok)

Grok + MCP — the driver's cockpit: role prompts from prompts/, tools via asd-mcp. Excelled at long-context contract reasoning (the agreement map); failed at pricing judgment by design. asd CLI / asd-mcp — same engine for deterministic cross-checks and the fact-check gate. Codex-style adversarial passes — the stress-test simulations that caught the cluster loophole. GitHub — PR artifact gates (schema/evidence/mermaid), issues as design tasks. Other hosts ship the same canon: Claude Code (plugin pack), OpenCode (agent/*.md), OMP/Pi (agents+skills tree), AGY (review-session.md — where this article's critic gates also ran).

10. Lessons learned & pitfalls

  1. The published "parts availability" finding was wrong; the dispatch attribution was the truth — always adversarial-review the cause analysis before locking.
  2. Outcome contracts are artifact-design problems; this contract survived legal because it was machine-checkable.
  3. Technicians are the product (book 20); designing with them changed the tooling list more than any customer interview.
  4. The union boundary is design input; timing it early saved the pilot.
  5. Finance fights the risk pool; give them the simulated model artifact, not a promise.
  6. Presenter packs from artifacts only — a chat-built deck carried a stale MTTR once and shook a steering meeting.

13. Tutorial — reproduce this engagement (15 minutes, Grok or any host)

The designer's own command log — it is host-independent: any MCP-capable client (Grok, Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode) runs the same sd_* calls.

export ASD_WORKSPACE=$HOME/consult-aurora-vento
asd() { platform/packs/cli/asd "$@"; }
OUT=$(asd project-create --name aurora-vento --client "Fictional composite" --sector industrial-manufacturing --service "field service and outcome contracts")
ID=$(echo "$OUT" | grep -oE 'aurora-vento-[a-f0-9]{8}' | head -1)
E1=$(asd evidence-add --projectId "$ID" --source ops-manager-04 --kind quote --body "We buy uptime, we get invoices" | grep -oE '"[a-f0-9]{64}"' | tr -d '"' | head -1)
E2=$(asd evidence-add --projectId "$ID" --source technician-09 --kind quote --body "I drive out with half the parts" | grep -oE '"[a-f0-9]{64}"' | tr -d '"' | head -1)
NODES=$(printf '[{"id":"fault","label":"Fault occurs","kind":"step","fields":{"emotion":"1","touchpoint":"machine"},"evidence":[{"evidenceId":"%s"},{"evidenceId":"%s"}]},{"id":"dispatch","label":"Dispatch","kind":"step","fields":{"emotion":"2","touchpoint":"planner"},"evidence":[{"evidenceId":"%s"}]},{"id":"repair","label":"Second visit","kind":"pain","fields":{"emotion":"1","touchpoint":"site"},"evidence":[{"evidenceId":"%s"},{"evidenceId":"%s"}]}]' "$E1" "$E2" "$E1" "$E1" "$E2")
asd artifact-write --projectId "$ID" --methodId journey-from-transcripts --title "Downtime" --nodes "$NODES"
asd method-run --methodId ecosystem-value-mapping --projectId "$ID"
asd factcheck --projectId "$ID"

What you should see. accepted: true on the journey write; the ecosystem-value-mapping method contract returned from method-run; factcheck → "pass": true.

artifactId is the store's content hash — it varies per workspace. The real gate at G4 is exactly this: one row per artifact, integrity (dangling refs) and acceptance (method rules replayed against the live registry) both pass, top-level pass: true.

Try-it checklist. (1) Start asd-mcp, register in your MCP client; (2) load the researcher prompt from grok/prompts/researcher.md; (3) create the project and add two evidence rows — watch the ids; (4) write a journey artifact and make the gate reject a zero-evidence node; (5) claim an availability figure, then run factcheck and watch the ledger enforce it. The loop — role prompt → skill → tool → gate → fact-check — is the whole engagement, in any host that speaks MCP.
