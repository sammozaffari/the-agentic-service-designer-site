34 — The Street of Small Business: Banking That Learns the Business, Driven by Codex

0. Confidentiality & provenance note

Vela Bank is a fictional composite digital SME bank; no real customer, account, or credit data appears. This article is a runlog of the Service Design Agent Platform (SDAP) hosted on Codex: the repo is grounded by the pack's AGENTS.md, Codex drives the same sd_* tools over MCP, and compliance sits on the design path as a hard gate. Every command shown ran against the real platform; all figures are machine-checked.

1. Executive summary

A digital SME bank with 120,000 customers was winning on price and losing on friction: 9-day onboarding with 62% abandonment, 11-day loan decisions with 27% applications erroring before underwriting, and "relationship" promised but absent for 91% of customers. One designer rebuilt the SME lifecycle in 19 weeks on the Codex pack: assisted onboarding that does the complying for the customer, an evidence-based lending fast-path, and cash-flow foresight. Onboarding 9→2.1 days, abandonment 62%→31%, loan decisions 11→4.5 days, NPS 12→44, monthly churn 4.8%→2.1%.

What you will learn

  • How Codex consumes the platform: a grounding AGENTS.md plus MCP tool access, no plugin magic.
  • Why compliance (KYC, credit, fair lending) becomes a service-design seam instead of a form-forest, per book 19.
  • How fair-lending evidence is a design constraint, not paperwork — and the platform enforces it mechanically.

2. The brief

Vela is a digital-native SME bank with a strong price story and a weak service story. Onboarding: 14 fields, 3 document uploads, 5-day verification queue. Lending: 13 documents for a £15k loan, 11-day decisions. Support: 4.1 contacts per customer/year, 38% progress-chasing. The strategic shift: from "cheap banking" to "the bank that knows my business," with compliance as the non-negotiable spine.

Success criteria (illustrative): onboarding ≤ 3 days, abandonment ≤ 35%; loan decision ≤ 5 days, application error ≤ 12%; NPS ≥ +40; churn ≤ 2.5%/mo; cash-flow-foresight adoption ≥ 50%. Constraints: no change to regulatory obligations; read-only legacy core; risk-committee lending appetite; fair-lending rules the design must not route around; data-protection limits on transaction-derived products. Why service design: SME banking is high-context, high-consequence service (books 03, 19), and the economics run through retention and deepening (book 20); the product is commoditized, so the staged experience is the differentiation (book 13).

4. Phase-by-phase session log (Codex)

4.1 Session 1 — Discover (weeks 1–5)

Drive. In Codex, with AGENTS.md loaded:

Plan the discovery with the orchestrator prompt. Then as researcher: run
sd_project_create for vela-bank; execute contextual-inquiry for the SME week
(18 diary studies, 34 interviews); normalize the 2.1M interaction records
(transcript-mining); write every claim as an evidence-add row. Adversarially
review the journey model before we lock it.

What the platform did. The register reached 2,214 rows. The adversarial review of the journey model found the key misread: onboarding did not end at account open but at first transaction — a third of customers opened an account, stalled at the 14-field top-up/payee setup, and quietly never became active. That single correction roughly doubled the real scope and reordered the roadmap.

Gate G1. Register ≥ 3 rows, held. Critic over plan: no veto.

Human judgment. The designer held the diary-study timetable against product pressure, excluded two hyper-successful customers as unrepresentative, and framed the lending problem as an evidence problem, not a risk problem — which is what bought the redesign room inside the risk committee.

4.2 Session 2 — Define (weeks 6–8)

Drive.

As synthesizer: affinity-diagramming -> insight-set. As mapper:
journey-from-transcripts + behavioural-personas -> four journeys, four
personas. Draft the fast-path concepts; hand them to the critic.

What the platform did. Eighteen insight statements; four journeys (open, borrow, run, recover) and four personas. The lener... the critic vetoed the fast-path concept on fair-lending grounds before it ever reached the risk committee:

{ "kind": "review-veto-log", "role": "critic", "target": "lending-fast-path",
  "verdict": "veto", "rule": "fair-lending-evidence",
  "message": "auto-approval on transaction data alone has no human-understandable
  decision rule. Requires assisted underwriting with an explainability layer." }

The design pivoted to assisted underwriting with an explainability layer — a mechanism the risk committee later approved.

Human judgment. The designer's framing did the heavy lifting here: "do the complying for the customer" — the bank assembles the evidence (open banking, accounting integrations) and asks only for what genuinely must come from the customer. The compliance officer co-signed the framing as a design principle before any build.

4.3 Session 3 — Develop (weeks 9–14)

Drive.

As ideator: orchestration-canvas, 8 HMWs, NUF filter. As prototyper:
storyboard assisted onboarding, the lending fast-path, and cash-flow
foresight. As mapper: evidence-gated-blueprint for all three. Stress-test
the fast-path with a synthetic credit corpus before the compliance gate.

What the platform did. Thirty-nine concepts → 14 → three storyboarded designs. The evidence-gated-blueprint runs refused backstage steps that silently reverted to the old 5-day manual verification queue. The stress test of the lending fast-path against 100,000 synthetic applications (mixed-income businesses with invoices + cash) showed the error-rate model was optimistic; thresholds were revised before launch. sd_artifact_write gates caught the slips: nodes without evidence refs returned min-evidence-per-node, re-derived clean.

Human judgment. The designer chose assisted onboarding over pure automation (warmth at the hard moment, book 13), insisted cash-flow foresight default to "no data leaves Vela," and chaired the compliance reviews that negotiated the 13-document rule down to four with an evidence log.

4.4 Session 4 — Deliver (weeks 15–19)

Drive.

As measurer: build the KPI tree from the success criteria and wire telemetry.
As presenter + librarian: render steering packs from artifact ids and assemble
the handover (incl. the risk-committee pack). Run sd_factcheck at G4 and
enforce the segment rollout rule.

What the platform did. KPI tree (kind kpi-set); fact-check at G4 — 30 artifacts, 0 dangling refs, 0 schema violations, "pass": true (excerpt §13). The rollout rule caught the micro-SME segment at week 2 (onboarding 3.4 days vs 3.0 target): assisted-chat coverage was understaffed on that segment; corrected before full launch.

Human judgment. The designer ran the risk-committee presentation (the fast-path's evidence logic), made the "no auto-approval" call stick against product pressure, and chose the two pilot segments deliberately (micro + freelance: the least and most complex credit files).

6. Platform use cases (Codex sub-sessions)

6.1 The solo founder's Monday. Assisted onbarding + the design's friction-point detection. Cohort abandonment 31%, median 2.1 days, funded Tuesday morning. 6.2 The seasonal trades loan. Invoice-heavy, seasonal pattern failed the first fast-path threshold; assisted underwriting with explainability approved in 4.5 days with understandable terms. The mixed-income stress test produced the revised threshold. 6.3 The cash-flow Tuesday. P3 opened foresight in week 6; "what changes if we pay VAT early" scenarios; adoption 61% of actives by pilot end.

7. SOPs for AI — skills as the encoded method

On Codex, the discipline is enforced by AGENTS.md (grounding) + the skills (encoded) + the MCP gates (mechanical). The four rules that did most of the work, verbatim from the pack: artifact types from platform/templates; acceptance machine-checked on every write; the critic on the write path (review-gated artifacts need a passing verdict); no fabricated evidence — if missing, say so and ask.

Guardrails. Compliance gates are hard: a lending artifact without a compliance artifact cannot pass its phase gate. Fair-lending evidence: every fast-path decision rule ships its evidence lineage. Data privacy: transaction-derived insights default to "no data leaves Vela"; consent matrix is a versioned artifact. Failure modes observed: (1) the decision-time model was optimistic for mixed-income businesses — the stress test corrected it before launch; (2) a generated persona lacked variance evidence — referential integrity re-gated it; (3) product pressure for auto-approval was resisted by the fair-lending evidence artifact; the veto log records it.

9. AI tool playbook (host: Codex)

Codex — the driver: grounded by AGENTS.md, tools over MCP. Excelled at the evidence-scale journey reconstruction and at long synthetic-corpus stress tests (the mixed-income correction). Failed at: regulatory judgment — the designer and compliance owned every gate. asd CLI / asd-mcp — deterministic cross-checks and the fact-check gate; used from the same shell as Codex. GitHub — PR artifact gates (schema/evidence/mermaid), compliance artifacts required before merge in the lending workspace. Other hosts ship the same canon: Claude Code (plugin pack), Grok (prompts + MCP), OMP/Pi (agents+skills tree), AGY (review-session.md — where the fair-lending veto was also adversarially stress-tested).

10. Lessons learned & pitfalls

  1. Onboarding ends at first transaction, not account open; a third of customers opened and stalled unseen.
  2. Compliance staffed as design partners produced the fast path; staffed as reviewers it would have reneged into a 13-document form.
  3. Fair-lending evidence is a design constraint; the explainability layer shipped because of it.
  4. Assisted chat is a cast, not a fallback (book 13); understaffing it produced the only pilot scare.
  5. Synthetic stress tests beat optimism; the mixed-income threshold revision is the artifact of record.
  6. Present the risk committee the evidence model, not the mock — they approved the faster path.

13. Tutorial — reproduce this engagement (15 minutes, Codex or any MCP host)

export ASD_WORKSPACE=$HOME/consult-vela-bank
asd() { platform/packs/cli/asd "$@"; }
OUT=$(asd project-create --name vela-bank --client "Fictional composite" --sector fintech --service "SME banking")
ID=$(echo "$OUT" | grep -oE 'vela-bank-[a-f0-9]{8}' | head -1)
E1=$(asd evidence-add --projectId "$ID" --source diary-007 --kind quote --body "I have 4 hours a week for banking" | grep -oE '"[a-f0-9]{64}"' | tr -d '"' | head -1)
E2=$(asd evidence-add --projectId "$ID" --source funnel-2203 --kind quote --body "320 of 1000 abandon at 14 fields" | grep -oE '"[a-f0-9]{64}"' | tr -d '"' | head -1)
NODES=$(printf '[{"id":"open","label":"Open","kind":"step","fields":{"emotion":"2","touchpoint":"app"},"evidence":[{"evidenceId":"%s"},{"evidenceId":"%s"}]},{"id":"verify","label":"Verification","kind":"step","fields":{"emotion":"1","touchpoint":"app"},"evidence":[{"evidenceId":"%s"}]},{"id":"first","label":"First use","kind":"pain","fields":{"emotion":"1","touchpoint":"app"},"evidence":[{"evidenceId":"%s"},{"evidenceId":"%s"}]}]' "$E1" "$E2" "$E1" "$E1" "$E2")
asd artifact-write --projectId "$ID" --methodId journey-from-transcripts --title "Onboarding" --nodes "$NODES"
asd method-run --methodId good-services-audit --projectId "$ID"
asd factcheck --projectId "$ID"

What you should see. "accepted": true; the good-services contract; factcheck → "pass": true. Gate mechanics, honestly: sd_factcheck takes exactly --projectId — it re-runs integrity and the producing methods' acceptance rules over every artifact. Compliance co-signature is a project-configuration gate tracked as phase-gate-decision + review-veto-log artifacts; there is no CLI flag for it.

Deterministic fact-check (real output shape, from this tutorial's own run):

artifactId is the store's content hash — it varies per workspace. The real gate at G4 is exactly this: one row per artifact, integrity (dangling refs) and acceptance (method rules replayed against the live registry) both pass, top-level pass: true.

Try-it checklist. (1) Drop AGENTS.md into a scratch project and merge the MCP block; (2) create the project and add two evidence rows; (3) write a journey artifact and watch the gate reject a zero-evidence node; (4) write a fast-path concept and ask the critic to review it — then read the fair-lending veto; (5) run factcheck at a gate. The Codex loop — grounding file → role prompt → skill → MCP tool → gate → fact-check — is the whole engagement.
