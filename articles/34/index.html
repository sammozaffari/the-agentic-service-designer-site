34 — The Street of Small Business: Banking That Learns the Business, on the ASD Plugin

0. Confidentiality & provenance note

Vela Bank is a fictional composite digital SME bank; no real customer, account, or credit data appears. This article is a runlog of the Agentic Service Designer (ASD) in Claude Code. Plugin surface and behavior are the real platform; compliance co-signing is implemented as gate decisions (kind 37) + critic rows (kind 38) + human sign-off — stated plainly, because each is real.

1. Executive summary

A digital SME bank with 120,000 customers was winning on price and losing on friction: 9-day onboarding with 62% abandonment, 11-day loan decisions with 27% applications erroring before underwriting, "relationship" promised but absent for 91% of customers. One designer rebuilt the SME lifecycle in 19 weeks on the ASD plugin. Onboarding 9→2.1 days, abandonment 62%→31%, loan decisions 11→4.5 days, NPS 12→44, monthly churn 4.8%→2.1%.

2. The brief

Vela is a digital-native SME bank with a strong price story and a weak service story. Onboarding: 14 fields, 3 document uploads, 5-day verification queue. Lending: 13 documents for a £15k loan, 11-day decisions. Support: 4.1 contacts/customer/year, 38% progress-chasing. Strategic shift: from "cheap banking" to "the bank that knows my business," with compliance as the non-negotiable spine.

Success criteria (illustrative): onboarding ≤ 3d, abandonment ≤ 35%; loan decision ≤ 5d, error ≤ 12%; NPS ≥ +40; churn ≤ 2.5%/mo; cash-flow-foresight adoption ≥ 50%. Constraints: no change to regulatory obligations; read-only legacy core; risk-committee lending appetite; fair-lending rules the design must not route around; data-protection limits on transaction-derived products. Why service design: SME banking is high-context, high-consequence service (books 03, 19); economics run through retention and deepening (book 20); the staged experience is the differentiation (book 13).

3. The plugin, at a glance

Same surface as article 31 §3 — install, ten agents, twelve skills, eleven tools, draft→final, kind-37 gate decisions and kind-38 critic rows, fact-check, and the §3.6 honest boundary. The compliance twist: this engagement's gate convention adds "a lending-change artifact does not pass its gate without a signed kind-37 decision naming the regulatory obligation and the fair-lending evidence" — a human-and-artifact rule, exactly like the governance convention in article 33.

4. Phase-by-phase session log (Claude Code + ASD)

4.1 Session 1 — Discover (weeks 1–5)

Drive.

/asd plan-from-brief
@researcher run contextual-inquiry for vela-bank: 18 diary studies (SMEs
logging a week of banking), 34 interviews, and the 12-month interaction
dataset mined via transcript-mining. Register everything.

What the plugin did. The register reached 2,214 content-addressed rows; every claim in the project now had to name a row.

Team analysis (outside the engine, marked). The key finding — onboarding did not end at account open but at first transaction; a third of customers opened and quietly stalled at top-up/payee setup — came from the designer's analysis of the register and raw funnel data, adversarial-checked. It roughly doubled the real scope.

Gate G1. Register in place; critic over plan: no veto.

Human judgment. The designer held the diary-study timetable against product pressure, excluded two hyper-successful customers, and framed the lending problem as an evidence problem — which is what bought redesign room inside the risk committee.

4.2 Session 2 — Define (weeks 6–8)

Drive.

@mapper run journey-from-transcripts -> four journeys (open, borrow, run,
recover) and behavioural-personas -> four personas. @critic review the
lending fast-path concept before the risk committee sees it.

What the plugin did. Journeys and personas wrote through the evidence gates (personas carried ≥3 distinct refs each). The fast-path concept — auto-approval on transaction data alone — was vetoed by the critic on fair-lending grounds before it ever reached the risk committee:

{ "kind": "review-veto-log", "role": "critic", "target": "lending-fast-path",
  "rows": [
    { "type": "defect", "rule": "fair-lending-evidence",
      "message": "auto-approval on transaction data alone has no
      human-understandable decision rule",
      "evidence": [] },
    { "type": "proposal", "rule": "fair-lending-evidence",
      "message": "assisted underwriting with an explainability layer",
      "evidence": [] }
  ], "verdict": "veto" }

The design pivoted to assisted underwriting with an explainability layer — a mechanism the risk committee later approved, with the veto in evidence.

Human judgment. The designer's frame — "do the complying for the customer": the bank assembles the evidence, asks only for what must come from the customer — was co-signed by compliance as a design principle before any build.

4.3 Session 3 — Develop (weeks 9–14)

Drive.

@mapper run orchestration-canvas (kind 15 — Mapper owns it) and
service-blueprinting, then evidence-gated-blueprint for assisted onboarding,
the lending fast-path, and cash-flow foresight.
@prototyper storyboard all three as storyboard-walkthrough objects (kind 23).
@critic review each; compliance co-signs the gate.

What the plugin did. The evidence-gated-blueprint gate refused backstage steps that silently reverted to the old 5-day manual verification queue — re-derived, then clean. Prototype discipline held: every storyboard named its assumption, fidelity, subjects, and pass/fail criteria.

Team analysis (outside the engine, marked). The lending error-rate model and the mixed-income correction (invoices+cash) came from the designer's quantitative analysis with a synthetic credit corpus — analyst work, marked; the platform did not run it. The thresholds shipped because of it.

Human judgment. The designer chose assisted onboarding over pure automation (warmth at the hard moment, book 13), insisted cash-flow foresight default to "no data leaves Vela," and chaired the compliance reviews that cut the 13-document rule to four with an evidence log.

4.4 Session 4 — Deliver (weeks 15–19)

Drive.

@measurer build the kpi-set (kind 26) from the four success criteria.
@presenter assemble the steering + risk-committee packs from final artifacts.
@librarian produce the handover pack.
/asd phase-gate <G4>; asd factcheck --projectId <id>

What the plugin did. The kpi-set named driver/baseline/source per row. Deterministic fact-check across 30 artifacts (per-artifact integrity + acceptance replay) with two compliance-signed gate decisions: { "pass": true, "rows": […] }.

Team analysis (outside the engine, marked). The segment rollout rule (micro-SME launched early, week-2 onboarding 3.4d vs 3.0d target, assisted-chat understaffed) was the designer's operational governance; corrected before full launch.

Human judgment. The designer ran the risk-committee presentation (the fast-path's evidence logic), made the "no auto-approval" call stick, and chose the two pilot segments deliberately (micro + freelance: the least and most complex credit files).

6. Use cases (routed to the right agents)

6.1 The solo founder's Monday. Assisted onboarding + friction-point detection. Cohort abandonment 31%, median 2.1d, funded Tuesday morning. 6.2 The seasonal trades loan. Invoice-heavy seasonal pattern failed the first fast-path threshold; assisted underwriting with explainability approved in 4.5d. The threshold correction was the team's quantitative analysis (marked). 6.3 The cash-flow Tuesday. P3 opened foresight in week 6; "what changes if we pay VAT early" scenarios. Adoption 61% of actives by pilot end.

7. SOPs for AI — skills as the encoded method

Identical discipline to article 31 §7. For financial services the load-bearing rules: referential integrity is absolute (a lending claim must cite a row); every KPI names its baseline and source; the critic's veto logs defect/proposal rows with rule names — and the compliance gate is a signed kind-37 decision, not an engine feature. Failure modes observed: (1) the decision-time model was optimistic for mixed-income businesses — analyst-corrected before launch; (2) a persona lacked variance evidence — integrity re-gated it; (3) product pressure for auto-approval — held by the fair-lending veto; the row is in evidence.

9. AI tool playbook

Claude Code + ASD plugin — the engagement. Excelled at evidence-scale journey reconstruction and artifact authoring under acceptance; failed at regulatory judgment by design. asd CLI / MCP — deterministic cross-checks and fact-check. GitHub — PR artifact gates; compliance sign-off required before merge in the lending workspace. Other hosts ship the same tree (OpenCode, Grok, OMP/Pi, Codex, AGY).

10. Lessons learned & pitfalls

  1. Onboarding ends at first transaction, not account open — the scope doubled because someone read the register.
  2. Compliance staffed as design partners produced the fast path; as reviewers it would have reverted to the 13-document form.
  3. Fair-lending evidence is a design constraint; the explainability layer shipped because of it.
  4. Assisted chat is a cast, not a fallback (book 13); understaffing it produced the only pilot scare.
  5. Quantitative checks beat optimism; the mixed-income correction is the artifact of record.
  6. Present the risk committee the evidence model, not the mock.

13. Tutorial — reproduce this engagement (15 minutes)

export ASD_WORKSPACE=$HOME/consult-vela-bank
asd() { platform/packs/cli/asd "$@"; }
OUT=$(asd project-create --name vela-bank --client "Fictional composite" --sector fintech --service "SME banking")
ID=$(echo "$OUT" | grep -oE 'vela-bank-[a-f0-9]{8}' | head -1)
E1=$(asd evidence-add --projectId "$ID" --source diary-007 --kind quote --body "I have 4 hours a week for banking" | grep -oE '"[a-f0-9]{64}"' | tr -d '"' | head -1)
E2=$(asd evidence-add --projectId "$ID" --source funnel-2203 --kind quote --body "320 of 1000 abandon at 14 fields" | grep -oE '"[a-f0-9]{64}"' | tr -d '"' | head -1)
NODES=$(printf '[{"id":"open","label":"Open","kind":"step","fields":{"emotion":"2","touchpoint":"app"},"evidence":[{"evidenceId":"%s"},{"evidenceId":"%s"}]},{"id":"verify","label":"Verification","kind":"step","fields":{"emotion":"1","touchpoint":"app"},"evidence":[{"evidenceId":"%s"}]},{"id":"first","label":"First use","kind":"pain","fields":{"emotion":"1","touchpoint":"app"},"evidence":[{"evidenceId":"%s"},{"evidenceId":"%s"}]}]' "$E1" "$E2" "$E1" "$E1" "$E2")
asd artifact-write --projectId "$ID" --methodId journey-from-transcripts --title "Onboarding" --nodes "$NODES"
asd method-run --methodId good-services-audit --projectId "$ID"
asd factcheck --projectId "$ID"

What you should see: {"accepted": true}; the good-services contract; factcheck → {"pass": true}. Add the compliance convention before a real gate: a signed kind-37 decision naming the obligation and fair-lending evidence, plus a kind-38 critic verdict on the fast-path.

Try-it checklist. (1) Install (§3); (2) @researcher → two evidence rows; (3) write a journey and rebut the gate rejection; (4) @critic → veto an auto-approval concept and remediate against the rows; (5) run asd factcheck.
