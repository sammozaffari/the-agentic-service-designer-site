34 — The Street of Small Business: Banking That Learns the Business

0. Confidentiality & provenance note

Vela Bank is a fictional composite digital bank for small businesses; no real SME customer, account, or credit data appears here. All figures are illustrative and were checked by the platform fact-check layer. The engagement was AI-first, with regulatory-compliance gates (KYC, credit risk, vulnerable-customer care) sitting on the design path as hard gates, exactly as a clinical-governance lead sits on healthcare work.

1. Executive summary

A digital SME bank with 120,000 customers was winning on price and losing on friction: account onboarding took a median 9 days with 62% abandonment, small-business loans took 11 days to decision with 27% of applications erroring out before underwriting, and the "relationship" the brand promised did not exist for the 91% of customers with no named banker. A 19-week AI-first engagement rebuilt the SME lifecycle around three platform-executed designs—a synchronously-assisted onboarding that does the complying for the customer, an evidence-based loan fast-path, and a cash-flow-foresight service that makes the bank useful between transactions. Onboarding fell to 2.1 days and 31% abandonment, loan decisions to 4.5 days, application error rate to 9%, NPS rose 12→44, and monthly SME churn fell from 4.8% to 2.1%.

2. The brief

Vela is a digital-native SME bank: instant business accounts, transparent pricing, no branch. Market position: challenger with a strong price story and a weak service story. The complaint base was consistent: "the app is fast, the bank is slow." Onboarding required 14 fields, 3 document uploads, and a 5-day manual verification queue. Underwriting wanted 13 documents for a £15k loan. Support handled 4.1 contacts per customer per year, 38% of them progress-chasing ("where is my application?").

The challenge: the promise had to shift from "cheap banking" to "the bank that knows my business," and every system (onboarding, lending, support, data) had to be re-understood as one journey with different entry points. Compliance—KYC, fraud, credit-risk, vulnerable-customer rules—was the non-negotiable spine.

Success criteria (illustrative): onboarding ≤ 3 days, abandonment ≤ 35%; loan decision ≤ 5 days, application error ≤ 12%; NPS ≥ +40; SME churn ≤ 2.5%/mo; cash-flow-foresight adoption ≥ 50% of active customers.

Constraints: no change to core regulatory obligations; a read-only old core-banking platform (integrations only); lending appetite governed by a risk committee; and fair-lending rules that the design must not route around; data-protection limits on using transaction data for insight products.

Why service design: SME banking is high-context, high-consequence service (books 03, 19); the owner's time is the scarce resource; and the economics run through retention and deepening, not price (book 20). This is also an experience-economy case (book 13): the product is commoditized, the staged experience is the differentiation.

4. Phase-by-phase narrative

4.1 Discover (weeks 1–5)

Goal. Understand the SME week: when banking happens, what interrupts it, and what "relationship" would actually mean to this segment.

Method & SOP. Mixed methods: 18 diary studies (SME owners logging every banking moment for a week), 34 interviews across micro, small, and mid-SME, and a 12-month quantitative analysis of onboarding, support, and lending data. SOP steps: plan → gather → tag evidence → store.

AI execution. Researcher executed contextual-inquiry + diary-study analysis. OpenCode agent-mode normalized 2.1 million interaction records into an evidence-ready dataset and quantified the progress-chasing share (38% of support contacts). The evidence register grew to 2,214 tagged rows. Codex adversarially reviewed the journey model and corrected a systematic misread: the "onboarding" journey did not end at account open but at first transaction—a third of customers opened an account and then stalled facing the 14-field top-up and payee set-up. That finding quietly doubled the real scope of the problem.

Human designer judgment. The designer held the diary study timetable against product pressure, excluded diary entries from two hyper-successful customers (unrepresentative of the 91% without a banker), and framed the lending problem as an evidence problem, not a risk problem—buying the redesign room in the risk committee.

Artifact produced. Research plan, evidence register (2,214 rows), interaction dataset, and the "SME week" synthesis (A7).

4.2 Define (weeks 6–8)

Goal. Define the customer journeys and the three fast paths that beat the legacy forms.

Method & SOP. Affinity synthesis and journey mapping from evidence (09/04; platform journey-from-transcripts), service-quality framing (book 19/03: perceived quality is the gap, not the feature list).

AI execution. Synthesizer produced 18 insight statements. Mapper ran journey-from-transcripts and behavioural-personas, producing four journeys (open, borrow, run, recover) and four personas. The Ideator sketched the fast-path concepts straight from the journeys. The Critic vetoed a lending fast-path that proposed auto-approval on transaction data without a human-understandable rule, citing fair-lending evidence requirements; the veto logged as a review-veto artifact, and the design pivoted to assisted underwriting with an explainability layer.

Human designer judgment. The designer framed "do the complying for the customer": instead of asking the customer to assemble 13 documents, Vela would assemble evidence (open banking, accounting integrations) and ask only for what genuinely must come from the customer. The compliance officer co-signed the framing as a design principle before any build.

Artifact produced. Insight set, four journeys (A1), personas (A5), fast-path concepts (A6).

4.3 Develop (weeks 9–14)

Goal. Prototype the three flagship designs to production-feasible blueprints.

Method & SOP. Structured ideation (book 17), service blueprinting (09/05), rapid prototyping in the live environment with staff-as-users (book 03: staged experiences need a cast; here the cast is in-app), and continuous compliance review as a hard gate.

AI execution. Ideator generated 39 concepts; NUF to 14. Prototyper storyboarded: (1) assisted onboarding with a live human available by chat at the moment of friction, (2) evidence-based lending fast-path, (3) cash-flow foresight with "what changes if" scenarios. Mapper ran service-blueprinting and evidence-gated-blueprint three times—each blueprint iteration went through the Critic, which flagged when backstage steps (document verification) reverted to the old manual 5-day queue without a mitigation. Codex stress-tested the lending fast-path against 10,000 synthetic applications and found the error-rate model was optimistic for mixed-income businesses (invoices + cash); the thresholds were revised. OpenCode built the synthetic datasets (A7).

Human designer judgment. The designer chose assisted onboarding over pure automation (human warmth at the hard moment, book 13), insisted the cash-flow product default to "no data leaves Vela" explanations, and chaired the compliance reviews where the 13-document rule was negotiated down to 4 with an evidence log.

Artifact produced. Three blueprints (A2), concept briefs, storyboards, traceability (A9).

4.4 Deliver (weeks 15–19)

Goal. Pilot on two customer segments, prove value, and build the expansion rule into the platform.

Method & SOP. Pilot + test-report (book 17), KPI tree (20/05), Good Services audit (05), rollout-by-measured-gate (same pattern as case 33). SOP steps: pilot → monitor → gate → expand.

AI execution. Measurer built the KPI tree from success criteria to telemetry; fact-check ran at every gate. Presenter rendered steering packs; Librarian assembled the handover (blueprints, KPI tree, risk-committee pack, runbooks). GitHub Actions validated artifact PRs. The rollout rule—expand a design when its segment holds its target 4 weeks—caught the micro-SME segment launch early (onboarding 3.4 days vs 3.0 target in week 2); investigation showed the assisted-chat coverage was understaffed on that segment; staffing was corrected before full launch.

Human designer judgment. The designer ran the risk-committee presentation (the lending fast-path's evidence logic), made the "no auto-approval" call stick when product pushed for speed, and decided the pilot segments themselves (micro + freelance, deliberately the least and most complex credit files).

Artifact produced. KPI tree, rollout report, review-veto-log (2 vetoes), handover pack.

6. Use cases

6.1 The solo founder's Monday. P1 started onboarding Sunday 10pm, hit the payee-setup wall, and the assisted chat surfaced a human in 2 minutes; the account funded Tuesday morning. Outcome: cohort abandoned rate 31%, median 2.1 days. AI session: Prototyper storyboarded the assist moment; OpenCode built the friction-detection dataset.

6.2 The seasonal trades loan. P4's application (invoices + seasonal cash pattern) failed the first fast-path threshold; the assisted underwriting path with a human explainability step approved it in 4.5 days with terms the owner understood. AI session: Codex's mixed-income stress test produced the revised threshold; the Measurer tracked decision-time reduction.

6.3 The cash-flow Tuesday. P3 (back-office angel) opened foresight in week 6: "what changes if we pay the VAT early" scenarios. Adoption reached 61% of actives by pilot end. AI session: Mapper's blueprint for the insight engine; compliance review of the data-consent defaults.

7. SOPs for AI

Encoding pattern. Same meta-SOP. Skills: contextual-inquiry, transcript-mining, affinity-diagramming, journey-from-transcripts, behavioural-personas, service-blueprinting, evidence-gated-blueprint, good-services-audit, ecosystem-value-mapping.

Guardrails. Compliance gates are hard: a lending design without a compliance artifact cannot pass its phase gate, mechanically. Fair-lending evidence: every fast-path decision-rule ships with its evidence lineage. Data privacy: transaction-derived insights default to "no data leaves Vela" and the consent matrix is an artifact with a version. Progress-chasing targets: KPI claims carry evidence refs and are re-gated by fact-check at each milestone.

Failure modes. (1) The decision-time model was optimistic for mixed-income businesses; Codex's synthetic stress test corrected it before launch, not after. (2) A generated persona ("P4 The Seasonal Trades") first appeared without variance evidence; the register had the rows but the artifact had not referenced them—referential integrity re-gated it. (3) Product pressure to auto-approve was resisted with the fair-lending evidence artifact; the veto log records it as a design decision.

9. AI tool playbook

Claude Code — agent team executing skills, artifact authoring, fact-check. Excelled at the evidence-scale journey reconstruction; the "bank learns the business" framing came from its synthesis of the diaries. Failed at: regulatory judgment—the designer and compliance owned every gate. OpenCode — data engineering: 2.1M-record normalization, friction telemetry, synthetic credit datasets. Excelled at volume; schema-gated. Codex — adversarial review: the open-but-inactive customer correction, mixed-income credit stress test, compliance-boundary suggestions (the auto-approval pushback was product pressure, which Codex did not help resist—evidence did). GitHub — issues/PRs/Actions gates; compliance artifacts required before merge in the lending workspace. SDAP — roles, kinds, evidence, fact-check: the mechanical conscience that kept 19 weeks repeatable.

10. Lessons learned & pitfalls

  1. The onboarding journey ends at first transaction, not account open; a third of customers were opening and stalling unseen.
  2. Compliance, staffed as design partners, produced the fast path; staffed as reviewers, it would have produced the 13-document form again.
  3. Fair-lending evidence is a design constraint, not paperwork; the explainability layer shipped because of it.
  4. Assisted chat is a cast, not a fallback (book 13); understaffing it on one segment produced the only pilot scare.
  5. Synthetic stress tests beat optimism; the mixed-income threshold correction is the artifact of record.
  6. Present the risk committee with the evidence model, not the mock; they approved the faster path.
