01 · Executive summary

Executive summary

The next frontier for service design is not faster artifact production. It is a shift in what the practice is for. In the first wave of the field, service designers made invisible systems visible. In the next wave, they must make live systems governable: versioned, evidence-linked, ethically reviewable, and continuously improvable. The series has already shown the component parts: article 01 established a service-design operating system with 100% traceability; article 11 proved blueprint-as-code for public services; article 20 showed that recovery quality depends on fairness and escalation design; article 21 showed scaling as design, not deployment; article 22 turned service quality into a standing operating rhythm. This final article combines those proof points with the future-facing literature to define the AI-native practice of 2027: a capability stack that blends service craft with prompt and code anatomy, evidence literacy, infrastructure judgment, and institutional ethics; a service-as-code lifecycle in which journeys, blueprints, standards, and SOPs become versioned artifacts with automated gates; and a continuous-design model in which the project ends and the service layer begins.

02 · The brief

The brief

Client portrait. Because this is a closing essay, the "client" is a fictional composite practice: a seven-person service-design capability working across three recurring conditions from the series — a mid-size insurer, a utility, and a local authority. In 2026 this practice looked familiar. It ran good research, facilitated strong workshops, and produced credible journeys and blueprints. It also carried the weaknesses of late-stage service design as a profession: too much method quality living in individual memory, too many artifacts dying after the steering committee, too little continuity between discovery and operations, and almost no consistent way to govern AI now entering frontline and backstage service delivery.

The challenge. The practice was caught between two failures. The old failure was manual drag: transcript coding, synthesis wall transcription, blueprint updates, evidence packs, and slide-deck translation consumed hundreds of designer hours per engagement. Article 01 quantified the cost of that drag in the operating system benchmark, where the point was never speed alone but the ability to preserve rigor at lower cost. The new failure was more dangerous: uncontrolled automation. Teams could now generate maps, scripts, and concepts quickly, but without explicit evidence binding, human fallback rules, and ethics gates they also generated plausible nonsense, coercive service patterns, and governance theatre. The discipline was therefore under pressure from both sides: to become more infrastructural and more accountable at the same time.

Success criteria. The reference transformation used five explicit tests:

  1. Reduce lead-designer hours per full-loop engagement by at least 50% while keeping or improving outcome quality.
  2. Move all critical service artifacts — journeys, blueprints, SOPs, service standards, review packs — into versioned repositories with review history.
  3. Cut blueprint staleness from multi-week lag to less than 24 hours for in-scope services.
  4. Establish a mandatory ethics gate for all high-stakes automated service changes, with named human sign-off.
  5. Appoint a single service owner for every live service layer, following the governance logic in See: 05-good-services/07-running-service-design.md.

Constraints. The practice had to work inside regulated, legacy-heavy organizations. It could not assume clean data, limitless engineering capacity, or universal executive literacy in service design. It had to handle GDPR-grade privacy concerns, audit-sensitive domains, and frontline teams already overloaded by operational metrics. It also had to resist the market fantasy that AI removes the need for fieldwork, facilitation, or political judgment. In reality, it raises the stakes of all three.

Why service design. The future question is not "how do designers use AI" but "how do services behave when software, policy, operations, and algorithmic decisions co-produce experience continuously?" That is still a service-design question. The future chapters are clear on this. Service design is moving from touchpoint optimization to ecosystem choreography and continuous discovery. See: 01-this-is-service-design-doing/10-the-future-of-service-design.md. The field's long-term maturity depends on institutionalization, in-house capability, and transdisciplinary education. See: 02-this-is-service-design-thinking/06-the-future-of-service-design.md. Smart, datafied services require explicit human override and governance design. See: 07-designing-for-service-key-issues/08-future-directions.md. The work increasingly sits at the level of platforms, infrastructure, and ecologies of change rather than isolated projects. See: 11-design-unbound/07-ecologies-of-change.md and 11-design-unbound/08-platforms-and-infrastructure.md. In short: the discipline does not disappear in the AI era. It either grows up into a governing practice, or it is reduced to visual decoration around automated systems designed by others.

03 · Approach

Approach: the AI-first service design process

The reference transformation used the UK Design Council's Double Diamond as the outer delivery arc. Inside each phase, the team ran a TISDD loop of Research, Ideation, Prototyping, and Implementation so the work stayed iterative even while governance and delivery became more operational. The outputs are different: instead of ending in a static recommendation, they establish a live service layer with ongoing evidence, versioning, and review.

The loop deliberately combines two traditions. Discover, Define, Develop, and Deliver come from the UK Design Council's Double Diamond. TISDD contributes the inner iteration rhythm: Research, Ideation, Prototyping, and Implementation inside each phase. The point is not to replace the Double Diamond with a dashboard. It is to make it permeable to live evidence and operational learning.

04 · The work, phase by phase

Phase-by-phase narrative

4.1 Discover — audit the practice as it actually behaves

Goal. Expose where the current service-design practice creates delay, decay, or ungoverned risk.

Method & SOP. The discover phase combines three moves. First, a capability audit in the institutionalization sense: which competencies are explicit, shared, and reviewable, and which live only in senior-designer craft memory? See: 02-this-is-service-design-thinking/06-the-future-of-service-design.md. Second, a temporal and weak-signal scan: where are the early signs that existing project rhythms cannot keep up with service reality, such as shadow spreadsheets, ad hoc prompt libraries, or field teams using unofficial tools? See: 11-design-unbound/06-design-and-time.md. Third, a governance audit drawn from Lou Downe's service-owner logic: who actually owns the service once the project team leaves? See: 05-good-services/07-running-service-design.md.

AI execution. Claude Code handled the first-pass diagnostic because the work was evidence-heavy and repetitive rather than politically ambiguous. The baseline prompt forced structure and confidence thresholds:

Illustrative Claude Code host command — emitted pack; live case execution unverified.

NOTE
claude "Read service review packs, journey files, blueprint files, PR history,
and weekly metrics for the last 12 months. Build a practice-baseline table with
these fields only: artifact_type, refresh_lag_days, evidence_coverage_pct,
owner_named, review_gate_present, ethics_gate_present, failure_mode. Flag only
items supported by file history or metric logs."

OpenCode then joined the repo history with the telemetry sample to produce one row per phase and one summary per engagement:

Illustrative OpenCode host command — emitted pack; live case execution unverified.

TERMINAL
opencode run --agent measurer "Read articles/30/data/practice_telemetry_sample.csv
plus repo PR metadata. Calculate per-phase human hours, AI hours, quality score,
and cycle-time trend. Emit dashboard-ready CSV and note any phase where quality
falls while AI hours rise."

GitHub was not just storage in this phase; it was evidence. Closed issues showed which decisions were made. Missing review threads showed where review had been assumed but never recorded. That distinction matters: in an AI-native practice, undocumented judgment effectively does not exist.

Human designer judgment. The human lead did two things agents could not do credibly. First, they named the practice-level problem in political language executives could hear: not "design needs better tooling" but "our service decisions lose chain of custody after each workshop." Second, they chose which weak signals mattered. A designer's private prompt notebook may be harmless. A frontline team using an unreviewed AI-generated eligibility script is not.

Artifact produced. A practice baseline with six dominant failure modes: static artifacts, evidence gaps, no named post-project owner, inconsistent exception design, ethics review after the fact, and local optimization by channel or department.

4.2 Define — specify the AI-native capability stack and governance model

Goal. Turn a vague ambition to "use AI in service design" into a concrete capability model, boundary of accountability, and ethics regime.

Method & SOP. This phase draws from three sources. From the older future chapter comes professionalization: the field needs explicit roles, validated competencies, and organizational placement. See: 02-this-is-service-design-thinking/06-the-future-of-service-design.md. From the newer future chapter comes the expanded object of design: platforms, multi-actor ecosystems, and AI-mediated service interactions. See: 01-this-is-service-design-doing/10-the-future-of-service-design.md. From Good Services comes the practical organizational rule that every live service needs a named owner and evidence-based governance. See: 05-good-services/07-running-service-design.md.

AI execution. Claude Code drafted the first-pass capability model, but Codex was deliberately used to challenge it. Capability models drift toward wishful thinking; adversarial review keeps them honest.

Illustrative Codex host command — emitted pack; live case execution unverified.

TERMINAL
codex exec --skip-git-repo-check "Read the draft capability stack, delegation
model, and ethics charter. Attack three assumptions: (1) where is a human still
named but lacks real decision rights, (2) where is AI trusted without a contest
path, (3) which capability labels are vague enough to avoid accountability.
Return only defects that change the operating model."

That challenge pass mattered. It forced three design decisions. One, "data literacy" was too weak a label; the stack was rewritten as evidence literacy, because the practice must distinguish raw telemetry, interpreted signals, and decision-grade evidence. Two, the service owner was moved from advisory participant to formal gate signer. Three, "ethics review" stopped being a workshop and became a board with mandatory triggers.

GitHub then turned the governance model into a living contract: one PR template for service-logic changes, one for evidence-model changes, and one for ethics-sensitive changes. Every template required risk classification, affected standards, and rollback note.

Human designer judgment. Only the human team could decide where the line hardens. The practice chose four areas that AI can support but never decide alone: service framing, vulnerability and dignity trade-offs, final escalation language in high-stakes cases, and any change that redistributes value between actors without clear consent. That is the real center of the AI-native service designer: not mastery of every tool, but disciplined judgment about which decisions must stay socially accountable.

Artifact produced. The AI-native capability stack, the operating-principles charter, the ethics decision board, and the delegation model.

4.3 Develop — build the service-as-code substrate

Goal. Make service design operate on versioned artifacts, automated quality gates, and reusable infrastructure rather than handcrafted project debris.

Method & SOP. This is the infrastructural turn. The practice used platform layer decomposition to separate invariants from extension points: shared schemas, standards, evidence contracts, and review gates became the invariant layer; journey specifics, prompts, scripts, and local channel adaptations remained extensible. See: 11-design-unbound/08-platforms-and-infrastructure.md. It also adopted the smart-service blueprinting logic from the future-directions chapter: every important service flow must show the sensing layer, algorithmic inference layer, human override, and governance layer. See: 07-designing-for-service-key-issues/08-future-directions.md.

AI execution. OpenCode built the first scaffolds because this phase involved schema work, data joins, and static generation.

Illustrative OpenCode host command — emitted pack; live case execution unverified.

TERMINAL
opencode run --agent orchestrator "Create a service-as-code scaffold with folders for
journeys, blueprints, standards, evidence, ethics, and telemetry. Add schemas
for versioned standards, review packs, and evidence links. Generate a nightly
job that updates blueprint freshness and opens an issue when any critical node
has no evidence refresh inside 24 hours."

Claude Code then authored the first living blueprint and standards files from the scaffold, while GitHub Actions enforced four non-negotiable checks: mermaid validity, schema validity, evidence-link presence, and high-risk-change gate presence. Article 11 had already shown the value of blueprint-as-code. Article 22 showed that service quality improves when standards become a standing review discipline. This phase fuses the two.

Human designer judgment. The practice refused one common anti-pattern: automating whatever was easy to detect. Instead it selected invariants based on what creates trust and reversibility. For example, a node without evidence is a release blocker. A stale style note is not. Similarly, the practice chose to version decision rationales, not only outputs. In an AI-native environment, rationale is part of the artifact.

Artifact produced. The service-as-code lifecycle, the continuous-design loop, the future-state blueprint of the practice, and the first set of automated quality gates.

4.4 Deliver — run design as a standing service layer

Goal. Move from transformation project to continuous service stewardship with live telemetry, review cadences, and institutional embedding.

Method & SOP. Delivery here does not mean handover. It means standing up a live operating rhythm. The logic comes from dual-track continuous service design, evidence-based governance, and institutional work. See: 01-this-is-service-design-doing/10-the-future-of-service-design.md, 05-good-services/07-running-service-design.md, and 12-service-dominant-logic/05-innovation-and-institutions.md. The practice adopted monthly service reviews, weekly evidence refresh, explicit ethics triggers, and quarterly ecology scans. The ecology frame matters because service changes propagate across channels, teams, vendors, regulations, and public expectations in nonlinear ways. See: 11-design-unbound/07-ecologies-of-change.md.

AI execution. Claude Code generated the weekly evidence digest and the monthly review pack; Codex ran the challenge pass before high-stakes releases; GitHub encoded the operating rhythm.

Illustrative GitHub CLI command — synthetic repository workflow; live execution unverified.

TERMINAL
gh issue create --title "Weekly service review pack" \
  --body "Refresh journey health, blueprint freshness, ethics exceptions,
  and standards drift for named services. Link evidence quotes and telemetry.
  Block release if any high-risk change lacks named human approver."

A representative Claude Code review prompt was equally strict:

Illustrative Claude Code host command — emitted pack; live case execution unverified.

NOTE
claude "Prepare the monthly service review for the claims, repairs, and billing
services. Use only committed telemetry and evidence files. Show: trend deltas,
new failure modes, ethics exceptions, open standards gaps, and the next adjacent
possible move for each service. No recommendations without evidence links."

Human designer judgment. The live practice depends on human stewardship in three places. Someone must chair the review and decide what matters now. Someone must hold relational trust with frontline teams and sponsors when telemetry contradicts the official story. Someone must carry the long horizon and prevent the service from being optimized into a narrow efficiency machine. That is design as temporal and institutional work, not output management.

Artifact produced. A continuous service-layer operating cadence, live issue flow, and a practice model that treats every service as a learnable, governable system.

A6 — Service design templates for AI-native practice

Template 1 — Ethical review SOP

NOTE
SOP ID: ETH-REV-001
Purpose: Review any service change that automates, prioritizes, scores, or materially nudges user behavior.

Inputs:
- Proposed change summary
- Affected journey step and blueprint node IDs
- Evidence links
- User segments affected
- Known failure modes

Steps:
1. Classify risk: low, medium, high.
2. Name the user value created and the value potentially extracted.
3. State the contest path available to users and staff.
4. Test dignity risk for vulnerable or low-agency users.
5. Verify human override and rollback path.
6. Record named approver, decision, and review date.

Output schema:
- risk_level
- affected_service
- contest_path_present
- dignity_risk
- override_owner
- decision
- reviewer_names

Acceptance:
- No high-risk release without named human approver.
- No automated decision without explanation and contest path.

Template 2 — Evidence-operating SOP

NOTE
SOP ID: EVID-OPS-004
Purpose: Keep journeys, blueprints, and standards synchronized with live service evidence.

Daily:
- Refresh telemetry joins.
- Flag nodes with evidence older than threshold.
- Open issues for unresolved drift.

Weekly:
- Publish journey-health digest by service.
- Summarize new weak signals from frontline and support.
- Review exception paths used more than threshold.

Monthly:
- Run service review with service owner.
- Approve blueprint edits, standard changes, and experiment priorities.

Output schema:
- node_id
- last_evidence_refresh
- quality_status
- drift_flag
- action_owner
- next_review_date

Template 3 — Stay-human checklist

NOTE
Use before release when the service touches vulnerability, appeals, debt, health,
claims, eligibility, or complaint recovery.

- Have we named the moment where empathy matters more than throughput?
- Can the user reach a human without repeating context?
- Is the explanation honest about uncertainty?
- Would we accept this treatment for a low-literacy or distressed user?
- Who is accountable if the automated step gets it wrong?

Use case 1 — A living blueprint maintained under continuous evidence

Scenario. A council-style repairs service and a utility-style billing service had both already proven the value of service blueprinting and quality governance in earlier work. Article 11 demonstrated blueprint-as-diagnostic in housing repairs. Article 22 demonstrated a living quality operating rhythm in utilities. The AI-native move was to connect them: blueprint nodes became monitored operational entities rather than workshop documentation. See: ../11/README.md and ../22/README.md.

What was designed. The practice created a living blueprint layer with 164 nodes across frontstage, backstage, and support actions. Each critical node carried an evidence source, freshness threshold, and standard owner.

Mini flow Description
Telemetry drift detected Nightly job sees repair-status node freshness exceed threshold
Issue opened GitHub opens blueprint-drift issue linked to node ID
Review pack updated Weekly digest shows resident pain, ops metric, and stale standard
Owner action Service owner approves script or process update

AI working session summary. Claude Code maintained node-level evidence links and assembled the weekly digest; OpenCode ran the nightly data join; Codex challenged whether a node really needed redesign or just better evidence. Representative prompt:

Illustrative Claude Code host command — emitted pack; live case execution unverified.

NOTE
claude "Update the living blueprint for repairs and billing. For any node with
freshness over 24 hours or quality below threshold, attach the latest evidence,
propose the smallest reversible change, and open an issue. Do not edit standards
without naming the owner and the affected KPI."

Outcome. Blueprint freshness fell from an average of 42 days to less than 24 hours. Stale-node count dropped from 37 to 6 within eight weeks. In the repairs service, status-chase demand fell a further 9 percentage points from the post-blueprint baseline; in billing, standards reviewed within 90 days rose from 28% to 96%.

Use case 2 — An ethics-gated recovery SOP for telecom complaints

Scenario. Article 20 showed that complaint recovery quality depends on procedural, interactional, and distributive justice. The AI-native extension was to make those principles enforceable at release time rather than discussed after a failure. See: ../20/README.md.

What was designed. The practice encoded a recovery SOP for complaint automation. Low-risk cases could receive AI-drafted acknowledgement and next-step messaging. Medium-risk and vulnerable-user cases triggered a named human review before dispatch.

Recovery step Rule
Billing error under €20 and no vulnerability flag AI may draft response, human samples QA
Vulnerable or repeated complaint Mandatory human review and explicit ownership
Compensation recommendation AI may suggest range, never send final decision
Ombudsman signposting Policy-controlled copy only

AI working session summary. Claude Code drafted playbooks and message variants, Codex challenged fairness language, and GitHub blocked merges if the ethics checklist was missing. Representative challenge prompt:

Illustrative Codex host command — emitted pack; live case execution unverified.

TERMINAL
codex exec --skip-git-repo-check "Read the complaint recovery SOP, copy variants,
and vulnerability rules. Find any message that apologizes without action,
hides a delay, or shifts burden back to the customer. Return only failures that
change fairness, compliance, or trust."

Outcome. Low-risk acknowledgement drafting time fell by 72%. No vulnerable-user case was auto-sent without review. Post-recovery fairness score improved from 2.4 to 4.1 in the pilot queue, while repeat complaints fell from 27% to 13%.

Use case 3 — A service operating system for a mid-size insurer

Scenario. Article 21 showed that scaling a redesigned claims service requires journey governance, wave logic, and manager enablement. The AI-native extension was to turn that rollout logic into a standing service operating system for a 900,000-policy insurer with legacy architecture and mixed channels, while also solving the legacy-corporate knowledge-funnel problem signposted for article 28 in the series map: how local workarounds become institutional learning instead of site folklore. See: ../21/README.md.

What was designed. The insurer's claims blueprint, standards, readiness checks, site-level telemetry, and workaround-learning loop were moved into one service layer. Local workarounds were no longer tolerated as invisible heroics; they were surfaced, classified, and either standardized or retired.

Operating object AI-native behavior
Site readiness Calculated weekly from adoption, quality, and standards drift
Leader standards Versioned cards with last-reviewed date
Exception paths Explicit and measurable rather than folklore
Scale decisions Gated through evidence packs, not anecdote

AI working session summary. OpenCode generated site-level readiness trackers, Claude Code assembled cross-site review packs, and GitHub held every runbook change as a PR linked to blueprint steps. Representative build command:

Illustrative OpenCode host command — emitted pack; live case execution unverified.

TERMINAL
opencode run --agent measurer "Join claims site telemetry, standards review dates,
and exception volumes. Output one operating-system pack per site with readiness,
owner, top failure mode, and the next adjacent-possible move. Flag any site that
improves speed while degrading fairness or rework."

Outcome. Weekly rollout-decision prep time fell from 10 days to 2 days. Sites with complete standards review coverage rose from 41% to 93%. New local exceptions were classified and either standardized or shut down within 48 hours instead of disappearing into site folklore.

07 · SOPs for AI

SOPs for AI — how service design SOPs are best used by agents

The AI-native practice does not rely on clever prompts alone. It relies on method encodings that make outputs reviewable. The base pattern is consistent across the practice:

Method -> step list -> input contract -> output schema -> acceptance criteria -> review gate

The meta-SOP for the AI-native practice

  1. Frame the service boundary. Name the verb-led service and the responsible owner.
  2. Bind evidence. Collect the minimum viable qualitative and quantitative evidence needed to support a claim.
  3. Map current reality. Build or refresh the journey, blueprint, standards, and risk view.
  4. Design the smallest next move. Prefer adjacent-possible probes to grand redesign theatre. See: 11-design-unbound/06-design-and-time.md.
  5. Run ethics and contestability review. Any change that scores, prioritizes, nudges, or denies needs an explicit gate.
  6. Commit the artifact. No consequential output lives outside version control.
  7. Run the service. Review live telemetry, frontline exceptions, and user evidence.
  8. Learn and institutionalize. Turn successful local practices into reusable standards and retire harmful workarounds.

Prompt template — evidence-bound mapping agent

NOTE
You are MAP-OPS.
Input:
- evidence register
- telemetry summary
- current journey and blueprint files
- service standards
Task:
1. Update only the nodes affected by new evidence.
2. Preserve node IDs and traceability links.
3. If evidence is weak or conflicting, open a review issue instead of inventing.
4. Output: changed nodes, rationale, affected KPIs, required human review.
Acceptance:
- No changed node without evidence link.
- No high-risk automation change without ethics trigger.

Prompt template — ethical review agent

NOTE
You are ETHICS-CHALLENGER.
Review the proposed service change for:
- coercion or unfair burden shifting
- missing contest path
- dignity risk for vulnerable users
- hidden data capture or surveillance creep
- explanation quality and uncertainty honesty
Return:
- pass or fail
- exact risk found
- required human decision
No generic advice.

Evidence-operating SOP

The practice's evidence-operating SOP is the operational heart of continuous design:

  • Daily: refresh telemetry joins, check node freshness, flag drift.
  • Weekly: issue digest, exception analysis, weak-signal capture from frontline teams.
  • Monthly: named service owner review; approve blueprint edits, standard changes, and experiments.
  • Quarterly: ecology scan across policy, vendor, channel, and public-trust shifts. See: 11-design-unbound/07-ecologies-of-change.md.

Failure modes observed and mitigations

Failure mode What it looks like Mitigation
AI summary without chain of custody Beautiful synthesis, no supporting quote trail Evidence-link enforcement and adversarial review
False precision in metrics Tiny samples treated as operational truth Confidence labels and minimum sample thresholds
Local optimization Faster channel, worse whole-service outcome Cross-channel KPI review and propagation hypothesis
Automation theatre AI inserted where there is no true leverage Value hypothesis required before workflow change
Human deskilling Staff follow generated scripts blindly Explicit human-decision points and rehearsal
Governance lag Review bodies meet slower than service changes ship Tiered gates and default issue escalation
Ethics as afterthought Risks logged only after harm Pre-release ethics board triggers

The stay-human checklist

The practice uses one rule of thumb: if the service moment involves vulnerability, blame, apology, denial, debt, health, eligibility, or public legitimacy, the designer must make the human layer explicit. The checklist from A6 is not ceremonial. It is the guardrail against letting convenience replace care.

08 · Value & measurement

Value creation & measurement

The case for AI-native service design is not only productivity. It is productivity, quality, and ethical licence to operate together. That is the important lesson from the series. Article 03 linked design quality to business value in claims. Article 20 linked recovery fairness to retention. Article 21 showed scaling requires governance. Article 22 showed quality needs a standing operating system. AI-native practice turns those findings inward onto the discipline itself.

Metric 2020 baseline 2027 AI-native practice Effect
Human hours per engagement 406 162 More senior attention available for framing and fieldwork
AI-assisted hours per engagement 58 230 Mechanical work shifts to monitored automation
Average quality score 75 91 Better evidence coverage and fewer dropped exception paths
Phase-average cycle time days 20.3 8.3 Faster iteration without skipping gates
Blueprint freshness 42 days 1 day Operations and design stay connected
High-risk changes reviewed before release 18% 100% Ethical risk moves upstream
Evidence coverage on critical artifacts 61% 97% Less argument from opinion
Rework caused by missing traceability 19% 6% Lower delivery waste
Stakeholder confidence score 1-5 3.1 4.5 Service owners trust the review cadence

The causal logic follows the service-profit-chain style reasoning even though the object here is a practice rather than a contact center. Better internal service quality for the design function — reusable standards, clearer ownership, lower clerical drag — increases the practice's capacity to deliver better external service quality. Better external service quality improves trust, adoption, fairness, and retention in the services being designed. In regulated sectors, that then creates a third value stream beyond productivity and CX: ethical licence to operate. The organization can show not only that it moved faster, but that it can explain and defend how it changed services.

A simple KPI tree for the practice:

Layer Core metrics
Internal practice health Human hours, reuse rate, standards freshness, review-cycle time
Service-design quality Evidence coverage, blueprint freshness, traceability, ethics pass rate
Service outcomes Failure demand, effort, resolution time, fairness, trust
Business and institutional outcomes Retention, cost-to-serve, complaint risk, audit confidence, public legitimacy
09 · AI tool playbook

AI tool playbook

Claude Code

Role on this engagement. Orchestrator and primary artifact author. Claude Code is strongest where service design is already methodical: running narrow subagents for synthesis, assembling evidence-bound artifacts, and keeping long-form narrative connected to operational detail.

Concrete usage. Capability audits, journey and blueprint authoring, review-pack generation, issue drafting, and ongoing standards updates.

Illustrative Claude Code host command — emitted pack; live case execution unverified.

NOTE
claude "Read the current service charter, evidence register, and telemetry digest.
Refresh the journey health summary, list only evidence-backed changes, and draft
the review pack for the service owner."

What it excelled at. Cross-file integration, structured writing, reusable SOP execution, and maintaining traceability through long documents.

What it failed at. It can overfit to the existing framing, especially when the practice needs a true second opinion or when evidence is thin.

When the human took over. Reframing the problem, resolving stakeholder conflict, ethics adjudication, and deciding what not to automate.

OpenCode

Role on this engagement. Mechanical build engine for data joins, dashboards, sample datasets, and static operating artifacts.

Concrete usage. Telemetry pipelines, dashboard scaffolds, schema-based report generation, and service-as-code folder setup.

Illustrative OpenCode host command — emitted pack; live case execution unverified.

TERMINAL
opencode run --agent measurer "Join telemetry, evidence freshness, and standards
metadata. Emit a per-service operating pack and fail if any required field is
missing or stale."

What it excelled at. Repeatable pipeline work, file generation, and transforming structured design intent into runnable artifact machinery.

What it failed at. It can generate technically neat outputs that ignore political or emotional context if the brief is underspecified.

When the human took over. Any time a workflow touched vulnerable users, blame, apology, or organizational politics.

Codex

Role on this engagement. Adversarial reviewer and defect finder.

Concrete usage. Challenge passes on capability models, ethics rules, fairness language, and business-case claims.

Illustrative Codex host command — emitted pack; live case execution unverified.

TERMINAL
codex exec --skip-git-repo-check "Attack the proposed service change. Find where
it assumes clean data, hides a human burden, or claims more evidence than the
source files support."

What it excelled at. Finding missing exception paths, weak causal claims, and governance gaps.

What it failed at. It is useful as a critic, not as the final decision-maker; it can be too destructive if used without a clear brief.

When the human took over. Deciding which critiques matter and balancing rigor with service momentum.

GitHub

Role on this engagement. System of record and quality-gate platform.

Concrete usage. Version control for journeys, blueprints, standards, datasets, issue flows, review templates, and automated checks.

Illustrative GitHub CLI command — synthetic repository workflow; live execution unverified.

TERMINAL
gh issue create --title "Ethics review required" \
  --body "High-risk service logic change detected. Attach evidence links,
  explanation text, contest path, override owner, and rollback plan."

What it excelled at. Turning service design into a reviewable operational system rather than a slide-driven craft.

What it failed at. Repository discipline alone does not create ownership; without a named service owner it becomes organized drift.

When the human took over. Prioritization, escalation, and deciding what counts as a blocked release.

10 · Lessons learned

Lessons learned & pitfalls

  • The biggest gain is not faster mapping. It is shorter distance between evidence, decision, and change.
  • AI-native practice fails when teams automate outputs before they define ownership.
  • The most dangerous artifact is the plausible but weakly evidenced service recommendation. It looks finished and travels fast.
  • Ethics gates only work when they are operational triggers, not principles posters.
  • Living blueprints create better services only if operations trusts them enough to use them in real reviews.
  • Designers who stop doing fieldwork because AI can summarize transcripts will lose the very judgment they now most need.
  • Platform thinking matters inside the practice: reusable schemas, templates, and review rituals create more leverage than ever-larger prompt libraries.
  • The discipline's future advantage is not making more artifacts than product teams. It is governing the service layer product teams usually cannot see.
