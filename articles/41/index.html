41 — Copperline Chicken: Blueprinting a 2,000-Store Operation in BUZZ

00 · Provenance note

Provenance note

Copperline Chicken is a fictional composite. The COO, the flagship GM, the crew member, the franchisee, the courier, every metric and every store count are synthetic sentences and numbers written for practice. No real company, store, or person is described — the standard house boundary, stated first in every one of the fourteen artifacts this article indexes.

The second boundary is about the channel itself. Article 39 ran a BUZZ lane live on the tm community relay; this engagement's transcript, artifacts/buzz-transcript.html, is a simulated channel log. It follows the real Buzz relay conventions faithfully — one channel per engagement, role runtimes as members replying under their own identities, owner-posted scoped mentions, and human phase-gate receipts as the only phase authorization — but no relay was contacted and no runtime state was mutated to produce it. Where article 39 proved the loop, this article demonstrates the loop's shape at engagement scale.

Third, scope honesty: this engagement stops at delivery of reports and proposals. Prototyping was not executed, and no artifact claims a pilot. The two coding-agent advisors attached to the channel posted zero messages; no reply is invented for them. Where the simulated log's shorthand diverges from the artifact set (it once says "26 insights" and "18 slides"; the deliverables record 24 insights and 16 slides), the artifacts win — the same rule the project canon imposes on everything: canon over artifact, artifact over chat.

01 · Executive summary

Executive summary

"Copperline 2000" is a full-operation service blueprint and field study for a fictional 2,000-store charcoal-grilled chicken chain, run as a single BUZZ channel — sd/copperline-2000 on the tm community relay — for six weeks under three human phase gates. The owner dispatched work through scoped mentions; eight ASD role runtimes replied under their own identities; the critic (sd_review) issued a verdict on every artifact before its gate; and the channel Canvas indexed the result without ever being treated as the system of record.

What the engagement produced: 14 artifacts — from the engagement brief through the research pack (interviews, surveys, channel extracts) to a full-operation service blueprint, an analysis report, ten proposals and a current-state deck — carrying 560 numbered notes (N001–N560) in one continuous sequence that is referenced, never renumbered, across the whole set. The blueprint itself is the centre of gravity: six operating lanes, twelve journey phases, five store-format variants, seven named systems, and 220 evidence-cited annotations (N001–N220, N551).

The point of the exercise is not the fictional chicken. It is that a multi-agent service design engagement at this scale can be run so that every number in the final deck resolves to a note ID, every note ID resolves to an evidence record, and no runtime — including the orchestrator — could advance a phase without a human receipt.

02 · The client framing

Why blueprint a whole fast-food operation

The fictional sponsor, COO Priya Nair, framed the commission in one sentence the owner repeated in the channel kickoff: "We have 2,000 stores running 2,000 different operations." Copperline had run customer-journey projects, menu projects and app projects. It had never described the machine that makes the customer's hour — the rostering, the grill line, the handover rack, the compliance logging — as one system, end to end, across all five store formats (N542).

The framing question the scaffold selected at gate 1: How might Copperline run one coherent store operation — visible end-to-end from customer order to courier handover — that crew can work in and managers can manage, across all five store formats? (N545). The tension that made the question real: the operation's metrics disagree with each other. Customer NPS sits at +31 while crew eNPS sits at −8 and turnover runs at 94% annualised against an industry median of 78% — an asymmetry the deck opens with, because which one leads the other is the entire strategy question (Service Profit Chain logic, ref 20; blueprint notes N117, N156).

Three boundaries keep this article honest, in the house pattern:

03 · The brief

The engagement brief

The kind 01 design-brief (artifacts/brief.html, notes N501–N540) was staged by the orchestrator in week 1 and reviewed by the critic before gate 1. It fixes the canon the whole set obeys: 2,000 stores in five formats (68 flagship · 1,214 standard · 389 food-court · 251 drive-thru express · 78 travel-hub kiosk), revenue mix dine-in 36% / drive-thru 38% / aggregators 26%, peak windows 11:30–13:30 and 17:30–19:30 carrying 62% of daily volume (N504–N506), and the seven named systems — CopperPOS v7, ShiftOrbit, StockBeak, Q-Pulse, VoiceBoard, Flamebook, Route56 — that the blueprint must show in their lanes.

Five measurable objectives (N509–N513): one evidence-backed full-operation blueprint covering all formats and systems; a minimum of twelve quantified failure-point insights; ten directions for change priced against canon baselines; a re-runnable measurement plan so the ops council can re-baseline quarterly; and completion in six weeks with zero PII and read-only systems access. Scope exclusions are named, not buried: menu R&D, pricing, marketing creative, legal, DC network redesign (N515). The brief also carries the method plan, team RACI, risks, and the artifact index — so that every later artifact answers to a document that was itself reviewed.

04 · Setting up

Setting up the BUZZ channel

One engagement, one channel: sd/copperline-2000 (id 5f9c2b7e-3d64-4a18-9f0e-7b2c8d1a4e56), private, on the tm community relay — because one channel is one evidence boundary: everything cited in the final package must resolve inside the engagement's own log and ledger (N541). The owner added the eight ASD role runtimes as members — orchestrator, researcher, synthesizer, mapper, ideator, measurer, presenter, critic — plus two coding-agent advisors (codex-cli, kimi-cli) as read-only observers attached for the article 42 lane. A persona definition is not a runtime identity; mention routing needs the audited, identity-backed member.

"Kicking off Copperline 2000 — full-operation service blueprint & field study for Copperline Chicken, 2,000 stores. 05 · Running research

Running research through the channel

Research was dispatched as scoped mentions — @role plus a bounded instruction plus a named artifact target — and the runtimes answered with evidence counts, never essays (N546). Three mentions opened weeks 2–3:

"@researcher run the flagship interview set per plan §h — all 8 participants across Harbourgate Flagship #14 and Northline Flagship #3. Depth protocol 01/04. Verbatims to the evidence ledger, no PII." (N546)

— mehran (owner), 2026-07-20T08:12:44Z

The researcher's completion reply is the pattern every runtime followed — counts first, signals second, artifact link third:

"Flagship interviews complete — 8/8 · 412 minutes recorded · 148 verbatims · 62 evidence IDs (INT-001…INT-062). Headline signals: Grace Mwangi has never seen a roster more than 3 days out; Aisha Rahman rebuilds the swap sheet by hand every Thursday; Jack Doyle waits at an unnamed rack that 'changes depending on who's closing'; Raj Patel's team fields recovery calls for failures made 40 minutes earlier at the window." (N547)

— Researcher (sd-researcher · Liam), 2026-07-21T15:03:58Z

The same dispatch pattern produced the survey pack (n=412 managers of 2,000; n=1,893 crew of 41,300, stratified to the format table with food-court and kiosk oversamples) and — via @measurer — the channel extracts: VoiceBoard (18,406 feedback events, sampled 1,240), a 120-store POS sample (3.2M order rows, sampled 41,200), 60 mystery-shopper reports, three aggregator exports (Dashly, Wheelio, Pedalr), and two weeks of call-centre logs (5,840 calls, sampled 980) — all on one shared 90-day window so sentiment, volume, accuracy and recovery could be triangulated week-by-week rather than averaged blindly.

Then the critic earned its keep. Interviews passed 62/62 evidence-resolving. Surveys did not:

"VERDICT: surveys — REVISION. Defect: raw manager sample over-represents flagship (7.3% of n vs 3.4% of store base); headline claims unweighted. Post-stratify to the format mix before any cross-format number is reported." (N548)

— Critic (sd_review · Marcus), 2026-07-23T12:06:18Z

"Revision applied. Format weights published alongside the raw tables; post-stratified deltas move headline metrics by <2pp; no directional finding flips. surveys.html v2 re-persisted, same ID lineage."

— Researcher (Liam), 2026-07-23T13:30:42Z

The weighting fix mattered less as arithmetic than as record: the raw/weighted distinction is now on the ledger, and the gate-2 rationale quotes the reconciliation totals verbatim — 8/8 interviews, 412 + 1,893 post-stratified, 5/5 extracts, 214 evidence IDs (62 + 58 + 94), zero PII, read-only pulls (N549–N550). A research phase that cannot be reconciled numerically cannot safely feed synthesis; an uncited claim entering synthesis would surface in the blueprint wearing the credibility of the whole pack (N550).

06 · Synthesis at scale

Synthesis at scale: the blueprint build

Synthesis opened with the insight register (kind 07, N366–N385): 24 evidence-cited insights across five clusters — roster precarity, handover breakage, compliance theatre, format drift, recovery lag. The register's strongest claim set the engagement's thesis: the 94% turnover loop is a roster-publishing problem before it is a hiring problem, because 41% of stores publish rosters less than 72 hours out and 31% of swap requests need manager rework (INS-01, citing N244 and N278). Personas (six, construction notes N386–N400), the ecosystem map (18 value flows, N346–N365) and the journey pair — a customer's drive-thru run and a crew member's closing shift in one frame, "because the second is the machinery of the first" (N401–N420) — followed.

Then the mapper built the artifact the engagement was named for, per the house alignment-diagram method (refs 09/05, 01/07):

"THE blueprint is up. Full-operation service blueprint: 6 lanes (customer actions · frontstage · backstage · support processes · data & systems · physical evidence), 5 format variants, 7 systems, 118 steps. Annotation range N001–N220 — 220 numbered notes, cross-referenced by every downstream artifact, never renumbered." (N551)

— Mapper (sd-mapper · Kai), 2026-08-07T17:26:19Z

The note-ID discipline is what makes a 180-kilobyte artifact navigable. The 220 notes are banded by lane — N001–N020 evidence, N021–N055 customer actions, N056–N100 frontstage, N101–N145 backstage, N146–N185 support processes, N186–N220 data & systems — and banded by role across the whole set: research N221–N320 (interviews N221–N270, surveys N271–N300, channels N301–N320), then synthesis and delivery artifacts N321–N500, then the brief N501–N540 and the channel log N541–N560. Downstream artifacts cite upward — the analysis report (N421–N455) reads the blueprint note by note; the proposals (N456–N490) inherit its baselines; the deck (N491–N500) cites note IDs on every metric slide. Nothing is ever renumbered, so a citation made in week 4 still resolves at delivery.

The critic reviewed the flagship artifact like any other, and blocked it:

"VERDICT: blueprint — REVISION (2 defects). 1. Defect, high, field-non-empty: crew→courier handover lane is unnamed — handover ownership cannot be reviewed. Name the lane and its evidence. 2. Defect, medium, evidence-resolving: drive-thru lane cites the 246s median but no channel evidence ID is attached at the window step." (N552)

— Critic (sd_review · Marcus), 2026-08-07T18:01:44Z

The mapper remediated in place, same artifact ID: the lane was named "Route56 handover rack" with courier and crew verbatims (INT-019…INT-023) plus the 0.7%-breakage channel record (CH-040), and the window step now carries CH-011 (POS 120-store sample) and CH-052…CH-057 (mystery-shopper reports). The critic's PASS closed it: "Both defects remediated; 220/220 annotations resolve… this verdict, not the mapper's claim, is what the gate reads" (N553). The remaining synthesis artifacts — analysis report (9 failure points scored against the fifteen principles, gaps model: 2 knowledge, 3 standards, 2 delivery, 1 communications, 1 perception; N554), ten proposals locked to canon titles with baseline→target arithmetic (N555), and the 16-slide current-state deck — each took the same verdict path, including one more revision when the deck showed targets without baselines on 2 of 9 metric slides ("an ops council will read those as invented goals", N556).

07 · Governance

Phase gates and the Canvas

No runtime may advance a phase. A phase exists only as the owner's receipt, recorded in the ASD store first and announced in-channel second; the Canvas mirrors receipts but authorizes nothing. The three gates, as the transcript and Canvas record them:

Gate From → to Approved by At (ISO) Rationale (recorded)
Phase gate 1 scaffold → research mehran 2026-07-17T16:40:00.000Z Charter + data boundary posted; brief (kind 01) staged with scope IN/OUT named and 5 measurable objectives; baseline metric frame locked; framing question selected; no-PII / read-only boundary set; union notification sent (N545)
Phase gate 2 research → synthesis mehran 2026-07-31T15:20:00.000Z 8/8 interviews; n=412 managers and n=1,893 crew post-stratified after critic-flagged weighting revision; 5 extracts on one 90-day window; 214 evidence IDs; critic PASS on all four research artifacts; zero PII (N550)
Phase gate 3 synthesis → delivery mehran 2026-08-21T14:05:00.000Z Insight register, personas, ecosystem map, journeys, blueprint N001–N220 (PASS after 2 revisions), analysis, ten proposals, deck (PASS after 1 revision). Delivery = package assembly + handover only; prototyping NOT executed (N557)

The channel Canvas is the collaboration index over all of this — charter and boundary, phase and gate state, participant roster derived from durable records only, the 14-row artifact index with owner and review status, and an integrity block. In the house language it stays a navigation aid, not proof: a row on the Canvas is navigation, not persistence; persistence is proven only by ASD receipts, and authorization only by a human phase-gate receipt. The roster section makes that concrete — the two advisor observers appear exactly as "did not post; no durable record cites this advisor", and participation is attributed from artifact ownership and typed handoffs, never from channel presence.

08 · Tooling

The MCP store in the loop

The role runtimes did not work from memory or from the channel scrollback. Every method citation and template shape they used resolves through the pack-local MCP store — the shared ASD Persona Pack store exposing 29 sd_* tools, 225 resources and 10 prompts, the same pack article 39 exercised live. Two resource families did the work here:

  • The book library (asd://books/…): every NN/CH citation in the channel and the artifacts — blueprinting per 01/07, journeys per 09/04, the fifteen-principles audit lens 05/02–05, the experience-audit rubric 13/02–05, the gaps model 19, the service-profit-chain reading 20/05, research instruments per 18/05 — resolves to a chapter resource, so a runtime cannot cite a book it cannot read.
  • The template catalog (asd://templates/…): each artifact was built against its registered kind — 01 design-brief, 03/04 research (interviews, surveys, channel annex), 05 stakeholder-map, 06 ecosystem-map, 07 insight-register, 09 persona-set, 11 journey-map, 12 service-blueprint, 26 channel-log, 28 experience-audit lens, 31 opportunity-matrix, 32 executive-report, 33 presentation-deck — and the delivery completeness check (N558) verified that every kind the engagement used maps to a file with a final record and a critic verdict.

The division of labour that kept this honest: the MCP store is read-only reference; the ASD workspace is the system of record; BUZZ is the conversation surface; the Canvas is the index. Article 39's strongest moment was an ideator refusing to fabricate when it could see the Canvas but not the workspace. The same discipline is designed in here: nothing in the channel mutates an artifact, and every mutation re-renders the Canvas from the workspace so the index cannot drift from the record.

11 · Lessons learned

Lessons and pitfalls

  • Note-ID ranges per role scale better than note-per-message. Banding notes by artifact and lane (research N221–N320, analysis N421–N455) let five runtimes write into one sequence for six weeks without a collision, and made the fact-check above mechanical.
  • The critic revision cycle is a credibility control, not friction. Four defect rows across three artifacts — survey weighting, an unnamed blueprint lane, an uncited 246s figure, deck targets without baselines — are exactly the errors an executive audience punishes. Every one was caught before a gate, by design (N548, N552, N556).
  • Reconciliation before gate, in numbers. The orchestrator's totals — 8/8, 412 + 1,893, 5/5, 214 IDs — are what the owner approved against; the gate rationale quotes them verbatim (N549). Narrative readiness claims don't gate.
  • Prototyping was out of scope, honestly. Not descope under pressure: the brief names it, gate 3 names it, the close-out repeats it so the strongest possible version of this engagement cannot be misread as a pilot (N557, N559). A pilot needs its own engagement, charter and gates.
  • Silence is a state. The two advisor observers posted zero messages and the Canvas roster says exactly that; no reply is invented for them (N544).
  • Chat is not the record. Where the simulated log's counts drifted from the deliverables, the artifacts won. The channel is where work is coordinated; the artifact set is where it is true.
  • A sponsor is not a channel operator. Priya Nair frames and receives; the owner runs the channel and holds gate authority (N542). Mixing those roles is how governance gets theatrical.
