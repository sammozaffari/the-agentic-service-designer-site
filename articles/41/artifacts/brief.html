01 · Client & context

Client & context

Copperline Chicken (colloquially "Copperline") is a fictional charcoal-grilled chicken fast-food chain operating 2,000 stores in a single country, with 41,300 crew and 2,140 salaried managers. Ownership is split: 1,220 company-owned stores and 780 franchised stores across 62 franchise partners. The Port Meridian Support Centre (fictional) houses 340 head-office staff; supply flows from four regional distribution centres — Northline, Eastcliff, Harbourgate, Southmere — which enter scope only at their store-facing interface.N503

02 · Challenge statement

Challenge statement

"We have 2,000 stores running 2,000 different operations. Our crew turnover is 94% and my best managers are writing rosters on Sunday night for a Monday they cannot predict. I need one Copperline on paper — so we can fix the same problem once instead of two thousand times." — Priya Nair, Chief Operating Officer (sponsor). Synthetic quote written for this fictional engagement.N507

Framing question for the study: How might Copperline run one coherent store operation — visible end-to-end from customer order to courier handover — that crew can work in and managers can manage, across all five store formats?N508

The engagement is a full-operation service blueprint and field study: a 6-week, evidence-bound reconstruction of how Copperline's store operations actually run today, followed by ten directions for change. It stops at delivery of reports and proposals; prototyping is not executed in this engagement — stated honestly, not as an afterthought.

03 · Objectives

Objectives

  1. Produce one evidence-backed full-operation service blueprint covering all five store formats and all seven named systems, with every lane evidence-cited — delivered by end of week 5.N509
  2. Identify and quantify the top operational failure points across the crew journey, management loops and delivery handovers — minimum 12 insights, each carrying its own evidence count from the research pack.N510
  3. Deliver ten directions for change with projected metric impact, each traceable insight → evidence, each priced against a canon baseline (e.g., drive-thru median 246s → 180s; rosters <72h out in 41% of stores → ≤10%).N511
  4. Leave behind a re-runnable measurement plan so the ops council can re-baseline the blueprint quarterly (proposal 10, Blueprint-as-Living-Document) without re-running the field study.N512
  5. Complete within the 6-week window with zero PII collected and read-only systems access — compliance verifiable from the channel log and evidence ledger.N513
04 · Scope

Scope

The boundary is drawn around the store operation as a system — everything between a customer's intent to order and the moment the food is in their hands (or a courier's).N514 Exclusions are named honestly below, not buried in an appendix.N515

IN — in scopeOUT — out of scope, named honestly
  • Store operations end-to-end: order capture → grill line → assembly → handover (counter, table, drive-thru window, courier)
  • Rostering & labour: ShiftOrbit workflows, shift swaps, time-and-attendance, roster publication cadence
  • Management loops: opening/closing routines, Q-Pulse checks, waste logging in StockBeak, weekly manager reporting
  • Franchise operations interface: Flamebook manual versions, compliance notices, franchisee–HQ interactions (opt-in basis)N516
  • Delivery handover: crew → courier handover across Route56's three aggregators (Dashly, Wheelio, Pedalr)
  • Support functions as they touch the store: call-centre recovery, DC inbound → store receiving (interface only)
  • Menu R&D — product development, recipes, LTO pipeline design
  • Pricing — price architecture, promotions economics
  • Marketing creative — campaigns, brand, advertising
  • Legal — contracts, employment-law interpretation, franchise agreements
  • DC network redesign — Northline, Eastcliff, Harbourgate, Southmere configuration is taken as given; only the store-side receiving interface is studied

06 · Constraints & guardrails

Constraints & guardrails

  1. Six-week window. Field study weeks 1–6, then reporting. The design consequence: breadth-first instrumentation (surveys + system extracts) with depth reserved for the 8 flagship interviews.N526
  2. No PII. Crew, customer and franchisee data is collected and reported only in aggregate; interview verbatims use the named (fictional) participant list with no contact data; survey responses are anonymous by design.N527
  3. Read-only systems access. All extracts (CopperPOS v7, ShiftOrbit, StockBeak, Q-Pulse, VoiceBoard, Route56 exports) are pulls, never writes. No live operation is modified by the study.N528
  4. Franchisee opt-in. Of 62 franchise partners, only opt-in franchisees are interviewed or surveyed; findings from franchised stores are reported as the opt-in subset, never extrapolated silently to all 780.N529
  5. Union notification. Because rostering and time-and-attendance data are in scope, the crew union is notified before week-2 fieldwork; no individual attendance records are requested.N530
  6. Scope ceiling. The engagement stops at delivery of reports + proposals; prototyping is NOT executed. Any prototype claim would be outside this engagement's authority — the brief says so now, not later.N531
07 · Method & data-collection plan

Method & data-collection plan

Mixed-method, evidence-ledger-first: every quote, telemetry row and document gets an evidence ID at ingest, and every downstream claim cites those IDs. Method selection follows the research-plan pattern (17/03) with interview technique governed by 01/04.

1 · Flagship interviews (8)

Eight depth interviews at Harbourgate Flagship #14 and Northline Flagship #3, spanning the full cast of the operation:N532

  • Aisha Rahman — Flagship GM, Harbourgate Flagship #14
  • Bo Chen — shift leader
  • Grace Mwangi — crew, 8 months tenure
  • Elena Petrova — franchisee, 6 stores
  • Jack Doyle — aggregator courier
  • Mei Lin — regular customer, office worker
  • Raj Patel — call-centre team lead
  • Oscar Villanueva — DC inbound supervisor

2 · Surveys

  • 412 store managers (of 2,000) — rostering practice, swap handling, Q-Pulse routines, VoiceBoard follow-up
  • 1,893 crew (of 41,300) — shift experience, handover clarity, eNPS items, tenure bands

Sampling logic: stratified by store format proportional to the format table, with deliberate oversamples in food-court (389) and travel-hub kiosk (78) formats so the smallest formats are not drowned out by the 1,214 standard stores; the crew sample is balanced across tenure bands because tenure is the strongest predictor of swap-rework exposure. Results are post-stratified to the format mix before any cross-format claim is made.N533

3 · System & channel extracts (5)

  1. VoiceBoard — 90-day customer feedback extract (app reviews, receipts QR, aggregator ratings)
  2. CopperPOS v7 — order-level sample, 120 stores
  3. Mystery-shopper program — 60 reports
  4. Aggregator rating exports — 3 (Dashly, Wheelio, Pedalr)
  5. Call-centre logs — 2 weeks

Extract windows share one 90-day calendar so the five channels can be triangulated against each other — VoiceBoard sentiment read against POS volume for the same weeks — rather than averaged blindly.N534
