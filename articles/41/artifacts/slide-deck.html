Why the full operation

"We fix the customer's hour. We have never studied the machine that makes it."

The sponsor's framing: Copperline has run journey projects, menu projects, app projects. It has never run a blueprint across all six lanes of all 2,000 stores at once.

  • The customer hour is carried by the whole machine. A recommendation story — "the line moved" — is process reliability, traceable from roster to grill to handover; every backstage lane is customer-facing in effect.
  • The metrics disagree with each other. Customer NPS +31 while crew eNPS is −8 and turnover runs at 94% annualised; the asymmetry is the engagement's central question.
  • Improvements decay. Past programmes faded within a quarter of the final report; nobody owned the metric after handover day — so this engagement ends in a living-document proposal, not a binder.
  • The seams are unowned. Seven systems, 19 point-to-point interfaces, several ownerless — the GM's summary of her own store: "The system is me."

Speaker notes — This slide sets the mandate. Priya's phrase (paraphrased in the headline, synthetic like all quotes here) reframes the study from "customer experience audit" to "operational truth-telling". Land the asymmetry early: the brand is loved and the machine is leaking — both facts are true, and only a full-operation view can hold them together.

The blueprint on one slide

Six lanes, 220 notes, one metronome

The full blueprint maps five journey phases across six lanes. Every claim in this deck resolves to a note ID. N492

LaneCustomer actions — arrive, choose channel, order, pay, wait, verify, recover. Channel choice made before any evidence is seen; waits felt, not clocked.
LaneFrontstage — greeters, scripts, the pass, the window, the delivery shelf. Scripts degrade deterministically at peak; the expeditor is load-bearing.
LaneBackstage — roster, swaps, grill cadence, holds, prep, close-down. Fragility is social (phone trees, Excel shadows); the grill sets the beat.
LaneSupport — 4 DCs, replenishment, maintenance, franchise spine, hiring. Manual steps are the integrations; the calendar ignores the field.
LaneData & systems — 7 systems, 19 interfaces, 3 ownerless. Truth exists in exports; rubrics score completion, not honesty.
LanePhysical evidence — fascia, receipt, buzzer, seal, badge, voucher. Evidence betrays gaps: fading paper, improvised vouchers, badges fed by falsified timing.

Speaker notes — Do not walk the blueprint grid here; point at lanes as a cast list for slides 5–15. The one takeaway: waits, risks and handoffs concentrate where lanes meet — every High-risk handoff in the analysis sits at a lane boundary.

Franchise drift

780 stores run on yesterday's truth

Flamebook carries 214 live documents with independent version numbers and no view of the current whole. Safety-relevant parameters change silently.

  • Version truth is fragmented — "the ops manual is 9.2, but the travel-hub addendum is 9.0, and the grill section was reissued as 9.3"; one kiosk store followed a superseded allergen wipe-down for five weeks — no alert, no attestation.
  • Confidence is low where it matters — only 31% of franchise managers are confident they always run the current version; acknowledgement of compliance notices runs 71% franchised vs 89% company, clustered on Friday-afternoon notices nobody sees.
  • QBRs review a stranger — partners meet their own business via a deck that arrives 24 hours before the meeting.
  • The strongest survey ask is a person — "franchisees do not want another portal; they want scheduled access to Daniel's team."
  • Blended reporting hides the drift — company and franchise cuts must be separated "or 780 stores end up invisible."

Speaker notes — Franchise is 39% of the estate and effectively a second estate operating the same brand on older truth. Note the register's minority flag: two long-tenure partners run the oldest POS hardware under renewal leverage — the drift has a commercial root, not just a content one. Proposal 7 answers with playbooks plus office hours, not a platform.
