ML

Mei Lin

Lunch-peak office customer · 32 · administrative team lead, Port Meridian business district · dine-in regular, 2–3×/week

Behavioural patterns

  • Arrives 12:10–12:40 on Tuesdays and Thursdays with 2–4 colleagues; treats the 11:30–13:30 peak as a fixed social slot (N228)
  • Orders the same two grill combos; abandons the queue and goes upstairs to the food-court competitor when the line exceeds ~7 people (N297)
  • Scans the receipt QR to complain exactly once — when the app coupon failed — and found the form longer than her lunch break (N308)

Goals

  • In and out in 25 minutes, table for the group, food that matches the picture
  • Predictable favourites — novelty is for Fridays, not Tuesdays

Pains

  • Peak queue length decides her venue before the menu does (INS-19)
  • Table hunt after ordering is the worst minute of the visit
  • Coupon friction at the kiosk with a queue behind her
"I have fifty minutes. The grill is worth it only if the line isn't."— Mei Lin, segment workshop (canon interviewee)
Service implications: peak-flow design and kiosk coupon handling (P8), plus honest wait signalling — her walk-away threshold is measurable, so P2/P5 peak forecasting protects the dine-in 36% revenue line.
DC

Devon Carter

Drive-thru commuter parent · 38 · drives the Thursday 17:40 school-run window · drive-thru express, 1–2×/week

Behavioural patterns

  • Time-boxes the whole stop: if the lane looks longer than ~4 cars at 17:40 he diverts to the burger chain on the same junction (N305)
  • Orders a family combo from memory at the speaker; confirms nothing because the kids are loud — discovers misses a mile down the road (N312)
  • Never enters the store; the window and the bag are the entire brand for him (N314)

Goals

  • Under six minutes, bumper to road; hot food; complete order — in that order
  • One app coupon applied without a conversation at the window

Pains

  • Median 246s window time vs a 180s target means his six-minute box fails on the median run (INS-04)
  • Speaker mishears with children in the car; error found too late to fix (INS-05)
  • No way to flag a missing item without parking and queuing inside
"By the time you find the missing dip, you're three exits past going back."— Devon Carter, drive-thru segment workshop
Service implications: the flagship journey for P2 Drive-Thru 180; order confirmation at the speaker plus a no-park recovery path (P9) directly reprice his threshold.
FA

Fatima Al-Sayed

Delivery-app-first customer · 27 · junior architect, works late · orders via aggregators 3–4×/month, rarely visits stores

Behavioural patterns

  • Chooses restaurant by app rating and delivery promise first, menu second; Copperline's 4.3/5 keeps it in her shortlist, barely (N316)
  • Orders at 19:45–20:30 — after the peak, when kitchen attention is lowest and handovers are rushed (N309)
  • Reports problems in the app, never by phone; expects resolution in-chat within the evening (N239)

Goals

  • Food that survives the ride; accurate orders; one-tap recovery when either fails
  • Consistency across Dashly, Wheelio and Pedalr — she uses whichever has a promo

Pains

  • 0.7% of delivery orders break at the crew→courier handoff; hers did — cold chips, missing drink (INS-06)
  • Three aggregators means three different complaint outcomes for the same failure (INS-16)
  • Below-average satisfaction of the whole customer base, hidden inside the +31 NPS average (INS-21)
"The restaurant can't see me. I'm a pin on somebody else's map."— Fatima Al-Sayed, delivery-first segment workshop
Service implications: P3 One Copperline Handover standardises what reaches her door; P9 Recovery Ladder gives the app channel the same compensation logic as POS and call-centre.
GM

Grace Mwangi

Crew member · 24 · 8 months tenure, Harbourgate Flagship #14 · grill and pack stations, closes Tuesdays and alternate Saturdays

Behavioural patterns

  • Checks ShiftOrbit on Sunday night to learn her week; roster still unpublished 40% of the time, so she plans around a guess (N232)
  • Trades shifts by group chat because the in-app swap flow "takes longer than the shift" — 11 requests/week across her store, a third bounced back (N280)
  • Logs Q-Pulse checks in one batch at close "so the screen goes green" — she has never been shown why timing matters (N288)

Goals

  • A week she can plan around: childcare swap with her sister, college day release fixed
  • Get competent fast — she wants Bo's line-check job by month twelve

Pains

  • Breaks slip during the 17:30–19:30 peak; she trades them for coverage and logs nothing (INS-22)
  • 94% annualised turnover means her floor is permanently half-new — she trains people she will outlast (INS-01)
  • Her feedback has no channel; eNPS −8 is news to nobody and actionable to no one (INS-18)
"Nobody asks me anything until I hand in my notice. Then everyone asks why."— Grace Mwangi, crew interview (canon interviewee)
Service implications: primary beneficiary of P1 Roster Stability (≥7-day notice, swap marketplace), P4 Crew Voice Loop, and P6 honest logging that finally explains the "why" behind checklists. Her full shift lifecycle is mapped in journey-map.html.
AR

Aisha Rahman

Flagship GM · 41 · Harbourgate Flagship #14 · 18 crew per peak shift, 1,850 avg daily orders, 6 years with Copperline

Behavioural patterns

  • Rebuilds the ShiftOrbit roster by hand every Thursday to absorb swaps — her largest unpaid-thinking block of the week (N224)
  • Runs "fire-drill coaching": 90-second corrections during service because there is no training slack between peaks (N273)
  • Overrides StockBeak proposals after LTO weeks because the system "doesn't know this mall's rhythm" (N301)

Goals

  • A flagship that hits window, waste and audit targets in the same week, not different weeks
  • Keep her best crew long enough to grow shift leaders internally

Pains

  • LTO launches arrive with marketing fanfare and no forecast help; she eats the stockout and the waste (INS-10)
  • 22% of her Q-Pulse logging is bulk-at-close — she knows it is wrong and cannot staff it right (INS-08)
  • Her escalations to HQ circle between three departments before landing (INS-17)
"I can hit any two of the three targets any week you like. Ask me for all three and I need a roster that stops surprising people."— Aisha Rahman, GM interview (canon interviewee)
Service implications: P1 removes her Thursday rebuild; P5 makes StockBeak proposals worth trusting; P6 re-times compliance into staffed moments; her store is the flagship pilot site in proposals.html.
EP

Elena Petrova

Franchisee · 49 · 6 stores (2 standard, 2 food-court, 1 drive-thru express, 1 travel-hub kiosk) · 11 years franchising with Copperline

Behavioural patterns

  • Runs each format differently because the playbooks she downloaded span three Flamebook versions — "my standard store and my kiosk don't even agree on napkins" (N247)
  • Calls Daniel's team directly rather than reading notices; a phone answer beats a portal login every time (N276)
  • Delays kiosk changes until the landlord approves hardware, which pushes accessibility fixes past every deadline so far (N299)

Goals

  • Protect margin across 6 stores while Copperline standards keep shifting under her
  • Hand a working operation to her daughter in five years

Pains

  • Playbook drift: she is compliant with a version nobody enforces and out of step with the current one (INS-12)
  • Food-court crew ratio — 7 crew against 940 avg daily orders — leaves no slack for new standards (INS-20)
  • LTO compliance costs land on her P&L while the marketing benefit accrues to the brand
"Send me a version number and a person. I will read one and call the other."— Elena Petrova, franchisee interview (canon interviewee)
Service implications: P7 Franchise Ops Bridge (versioned Flamebook + office hours) is her proposal; P8 kiosk pass needs her landlord workflow; her stores are first franchise pilots.

3. Reading notes

Three customers, three channels. Mei (dine-in 36%), Devon (drive-thru 38%), Fatima (app + aggregators 26%) cover the canon revenue mix exactly — every proposal with a customer face can name whose evening it changes.

Three roles, one operating reality. Grace, Aisha and Elena experience the same instability at three altitudes: Grace cannot plan her week, Aisha cannot staff her peaks, Elena cannot keep six stores on one standard. P1 serves all three, which is why it leads the proposal set.
