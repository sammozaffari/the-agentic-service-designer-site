Roster Stability Program

Publish rosters ≥7 days ahead; standing swap marketplace in ShiftOrbit. Owner: Sofia Reyes (Director of Labour & Rostering), sponsored by Priya Nair.
Impact 5 / 5Effort 3 / 5Wave 2 (experiment Wave 1)Kind process + system

Problem (evidence)

41% of stores publish rosters less than 72 hours before week start; crew request swaps at 11 per store per week and 31% need manager rework; turnover runs at 94% annualised against an industry median of 78%. The register grades the flywheel — instability → attrition → instability — as its single High-confidence causal loop, verified across three independent data cuts. The GM's own words: the roster is late because the forecast changes Thursday, the swaps land Friday, and the week starts Monday.

N194 · N118 · N117 · N222 · N223 · N230 · N234 · N235 · N281 · N285 · N289 · INS-01–03

Proposal

Publish at T−7, by decree and by mechanism. The decree: Sofia Reyes sets the roster lock at seven days and makes the timestamp the single reported number in Tom Whelan's regional reviews — objective, comparable, and already measured by ShiftOrbit. The mechanism: move the forecast inputs earlier (LTO lift curves and campaign flight dates feed the demand model automatically instead of living in other teams' calendars), because a lock without earlier inputs just moves the panic to Wednesday.

Rebuild the swap marketplace where the swaps actually happen. Today swaps occur by chat message and die in a ledger that can show two active versions of the same swap ("the ghost swap"), which is why 47% of GMs keep shadow spreadsheets. The proposal adds standing swap offers — crew pre-declare exchangeable shifts within hard constraints ShiftOrbit already knows but doesn't enforce: certifications, hours caps, break rules. The GM's role collapses from phone-tree broker to exception-approver, and the 15% of crew who currently abandon swaps (61% of whom then log an unplanned absence) get an answer inside a day instead of 3.1.

Give last night's reality a system field. The closing manager's paper prep-handover sheet becomes a ShiftOrbit structure read aloud at the morning roster check — the smallest change in this proposal and the one GMs ask for first.

Expected benefit (estimates) N457

Swap rework falls from 31% to under 15% of requests as constraints are enforced pre-match rather than repaired post-hoc (estimate; the 15-min rework job mostly disappears, returning ≈25 min/store/week to GMs). Roster notice: from 59% of stores at ≥72 h to 90% at ≥7 days by end of year one (estimate). Turnover: from 94% annualised toward 85% over four quarters (estimate) — grounded in the exit pattern that names schedule visibility, not pay, as the leaving reason. Secondary estimates: swap volume itself drops 11 → ~8 per store per week; manager admin drops ~2 of its 9.5 weekly hours; crew eNPS moves first and furthest in the 6–18-month tenure band where the churn is manufactured.

Drive-Thru 180

Kitchen-to-window sub-process redesign targeting a 180 s median. Owner: Marcus Adeyemi (VP Store Operations).
Impact 4 / 5Effort 4 / 5Wave 3Kind process + kitchen system

Problem (evidence)

Window time medians 246 s against a 180 s target — a 37% miss at the median, with a heavy tail (P90 411 s) and a best-decile that already beats target (P10 168 s): the gap is load- and process-shaped, not capability-shaped. The register's time-budget decomposition puts grill wait, not customer transaction, as the dominant share of the 66-second gap. And the target itself is contested honestly: 58% of drive-thru managers say 180 s is not achievable at current staffing; the validated store ran it with eleven crew, most run eight.

N111 · N215 · N306 · N378 · N280 · N282 · N243 · N073 · INS-04–05

Proposal

Redesign the lane as a physics problem, honestly staffed. First, publish the target's assumptions: 180 s is achievable with the validated crew model or with a re-designed flow — never by pausing the beacon (9% of measured windows carry a pause; the pause button becomes an audited exception, not a tool). Second, attack the decomposition: grill wait dominates, so the pre-peak cook plan gets a launch-style curve per daypart, the KDS gains the half-cadence mode it lacks (today one grill down means tickets still arrive at full rate), and grill calibration drift gets a daily automated read.

Recover the seconds the interface throws away. The confirm display's 5-second auto-dismiss becomes 10 seconds (customers need 7–9 s to verify a basket); the item repeat-back that cuts mis-hears by 44% is re-sequenced into the grill wait rather than added onto it; the second-window decision rule (open at 5+ stack) becomes explicit with a 90-second setup choreography; and the missing-item re-fire decision gets a standard — remake-in-place under 90 s of remaining wait, send-and-re-deliver beyond it — replacing the ad-hoc judgement that currently produces two very different customer outcomes.

Then, and only then, staff to the honest number. Where the redesigned flow still misses 180 s at peak with eight crew, the proposal says so with data and either funds the ninth hand at the formats that can carry it or re-baselines the format's promise. Moving the target by fiat is explicitly out of scope (see §Honesty).

Expected benefit (estimates) N459

Median window time 246 s → 210 s within two quarters of Wave 3 start, → 180 s at the formats that adopt the full crew model (estimates; sequencing reflects grill-wait dominance). P90 411 s → ~330 s (estimate) — the tail carries the walk-aways and reviews. Speaker mis-hears, the drive-thru's leading accuracy leak, fall materially as repeat-back lands inside the flow (44% reduction is measured; adoption estimate 60%+). Beacon pause rate 9% → <2% (estimate) once pauses are audited.

One Copperline Handover

A single crew→courier handover standard across aggregators. Owners: Hana Kim (Head of Digital & Delivery) + Marcus Adeyemi.
Impact 4 / 5Effort 2 / 5Wave 1Kind process + workspace

Problem (evidence)

Breakage in crew→courier handoffs runs at 0.7% of delivery orders — about one broken handover per store per week, each costing a re-make, a re-dispatch and typically a 4.3→2-star rating swing. Three aggregators impose three pickup rituals on one physical counter; courier wait-at-counter medians 6.4 minutes; the pickup verification scan runs at 93% because scans are skipped when couriers stack; and the shelf's discipline correlates perfectly with whether one named veteran owns it. Couriers are queued in customer lobbies at 88% of stores, generating complaints about "loitering men with stacks of bags" — a design failure displaced onto people.

N085 · N316 · N249 · N250 · N251 · N246 · N247 · N248 · N080 · N081 · N082 · N115 · N012 · N086 · N315 · INS-06–07

Proposal

One ritual, one shelf, one owner. The standard: sealed bag, name-facing label, scan-at-handover, staged in customer view — identical whether the tablet says Dashly, Wheelio or Pedalr. The printed shelf kit from Flamebook replaces hand-taped initials at every site; a named shelf owner is rostered at peak (the field's own positive deviance — "the good stores have one human whose whole job at peak is the shelf" — becomes a role, not an accident). The aggregator tablet moves from the till to the shelf so the "ready" acknowledgement stops lagging the bag by 90 seconds.

Negotiate the variance away. Hana Kim's team takes the unified standard to the weekly incident calls per aggregator and pushes the divergent proof-of-pickup rituals toward the common denominator; what cannot be harmonised commercially gets harmonised physically — the courier sees one counter regardless of whose app pinged. A marked courier waiting area (12% of stores have any) rolls out to the top-quartile delivery-volume sites, taking couriers out of the diner's lobby and the diner's complaint log.

Make the scan trustworthy again. Scan-at-handover returns to 100% target with a shelf-side prompt and a dispute-resolution payoff: every skipped scan is exactly what makes later missing-item disputes unresolvable. The courier's 92–140-second navigation from check-in to shelf is cut by signage that assumes a first-time visitor, not district memory.

Expected benefit (estimates) N461

Breakage 0.7% → under 0.3% of delivery orders (estimate; the 9-of-22 disciplined stores already wait 3.8 vs 8.0 minutes, so the practice gap is measured). Courier wait-at-counter 6.4 → ~4.0 minutes median estate-wide (estimate, from the labelled-shelf cohort). Pickup scan 93% → 99%+ (estimate). "Was my bag opened?" mentions (41 in 90 days) fall below 10 as seal application moves on-stage everywhere (estimate). Refund-code calls attributable to handover failure fall correspondingly (~1/store/week → under 1 per fortnight, estimate).

Crew Voice Loop

A weekly 2-minute crew pulse wired to Roster Stability metrics. Owners: Sofia Reyes + Marcus Adeyemi.
Impact 3 / 5Effort 1 / 5Wave 1Kind instrument

Problem (evidence)

Crew eNPS is −8 while customer NPS is +31 — the engagement's defining asymmetry. The quarterly pulse reaches a 54% response rate skewed to the engaged; the disengaged median crew member (8 months tenure) does not answer the survey that would describe their exit. Crew concerns surface in exit interviews but never in a standing channel anyone answers; the strongest stated condition for feedback participation is visible response — "close the loop. Show me 'you said, we changed'."

N213 · N233 · N257 · N270 · N294 · N295 · N296–N300 · INS-18, INS-22

Proposal

Two minutes, weekly, in the tool crew already open. The pulse lives in the ShiftOrbit crew app beside the roster: three fixed questions (notice adequacy, break reality, one free word) plus a rotating item. Fixed questions are chosen because they wire directly to P1's metrics — the loop can show crew that roster stability is moving because they said it mattered, which is the mechanism the quarterly survey lacks entirely.

Cut by tenure band, answered in public. The 6–18-month band (where eNPS runs −11 and intent-to-stay dips) gets its own read-out, because the estate's churn is manufactured there. Each week one "you said, we changed" item posts to the same app — small, true, verifiable. The 12% of crew who admit logging checks that didn't happen and the 24% who skip breaks are telling the truth into a survey; the pulse gives that truth somewhere to land before it becomes a leaver interview.

Expected benefit (estimates) N463

Pulse response 54% (quarterly) → 75%+ weekly (estimate) once loop-closure is visible. eNPS −8 → −2 within three quarters (estimate), moving first in the 6–18-month band; the pulse itself becomes the early-warning instrument for P1 (roster notice perceived adequacy leading the publish-timestamp by a week). The crew's own top-five asks — earlier schedules, peak cover, real breaks, simpler StockBeak, fairer hours — become a tracked backlog, not a survey annex.

Waste-to-Order Forecasting

StockBeak LTO-aware demand curves to cut chicken waste to 2.8%. Owners: Marcus Adeyemi + Oscar Villanueva (DC inbound, Northline) supply-side.
Impact 4 / 5Effort 3 / 5Wave 3Kind data + process

Problem (evidence)

Chicken waste runs at 3.8% of chicken weight against a 2.5% target, and it correlates with forecast error, not portioning error. The mechanism is documented end-to-end: auto-replenishment ignores LTO promo lift until marketing manually attaches a curve (missed for two of the last five LTOs); week-two overstock then anchors re-orders to the launch spike while demand has already decayed; the cook plan's ±12% residual maps directly onto hold-time waste in the last 20 minutes of every peak; and reason code 7 "other" absorbs 31% of waste entries, so the analytics start from partly-fictional categories. Managers trust their eyes over the curves and override routinely.

N104 · N105 · N126 · N148 · N200 · N226 · N238 · N267 · N268 · N199 · N193 · N217 · INS-10–11, INS-23

Proposal

Model the launch, not just the baseline. LTO-aware demand curves — promo lift, day-of-launch decay, cannibalisation per item — feed both replenishment proposals and cook plans from one engine in StockBeak, replacing the manual lift-attachment step that currently is the integration between marketing and supply. The curve's forecast window aligns to the physical clock that actually binds the estate: the 12-hour brine means Wednesday's peak was decided Tuesday at 23:30.

Close the loop downward. Stores have never received a StockBeak insight about their own waste; the proposal makes the curve two-way — the store's hold-expiry and run-out data re-trains its own next-week curves, and waste reason codes are rebuilt so "other" collapses from 31% toward irrelevance (estimate: under 10%). Recipe-master staleness (23 recipes, 19 quietly wrong) is fixed first, because depletion math downstream of a stale recipe is fiction with decimals.

Keep the human override, but log it against outcomes. The register is explicit that managers overrule the system; the proposal's answer is not to remove the override but to score it — every override is paired with the week's actual, so the curves and the GMs converge on evidence instead of standing on dignity.

Expected benefit (estimates) N465

Chicken waste 3.8% → 2.8% (the proposal's pilot maths; 2.5% remains the long target) — driven mostly by the last-20-minutes-of-peak hold waste, so the curve's accuracy is the binding constraint. Cook-plan residual ±12% → ±7% (estimate). LTO week-one stockouts: two of the last five LTOs → zero of the next five (estimate). Reason-code "other" 31% → under 10% (estimate). At flagship volume (1,850 orders/day) a one-point waste reduction is material weekly chicken weight; estate-scale value is deliberately not claimed here beyond direction (see cost envelope, N486).

Q-Pulse Honest Logging

Timestamp-enforced checklists; kill "bulk-at-close". Owners: Priya Nair (sponsor) + Tom Whelan (regional enforcement), DC scope with Oscar Villanueva.
Impact 3 / 5Effort 1 / 5Wave 1Kind system setting + culture

Problem (evidence)

96.2% of required checks are done, but 22% are logged in bulk at close — timestamps synthetic even when readings are real; the estate's most normalised falsification. The telemetry to see it has existed for years ("a 14-hour day appearing within a 4-minute entry window"), but the audit rubric scores completion, not honesty of timing, and nobody at HQ wanted the answer enough to query it. Managers frame it honestly: "ask me to log in the moment with the crew I have? Or ask me to be green by close? Pick one." The DC tier reports the same pattern isomorphically.

N121 · N202 · N203 · N204 · N014 · N125 · N224 · N244 · N265 · N266 · N273 · N274 · N292 · INS-08–09

Proposal

Turn on the enforcement that already exists, with a justice window. The Q-Pulse engine supports timestamp enforcement — a check cannot be entered outside its window — today, off by default. The proposal: enable it estate-wide, announce it two weeks ahead, and run a one-month amnesty in which bulk-logged history is forgiven and re-baselined. No retroactive discipline; the target is the system's honesty, not the crew's record. Wording follows the register's agreed rule: falsified timing is a system-design outcome, never individual dishonesty.

Re-score what the audit scores. The closure-audit rubric gains a timing-honesty dimension (the export already preserves entry timestamps; the report card merely could not fail what the export plainly shows). Stores that cannot honestly complete checks inside their window are flagged for a labour conversation, not a compliance one — the kiosk manager who logs breaks she did not take is telling the truth about staffing, and the report card will now agree with her.

Extend honesty to the sibling processes and the DC. The log-before-bin rule in waste staging and the DC's chilled-bay tablet UX are in scope: enforcement without usable logging surfaces just relocates the fiction. Mid-shift line checks — the one loop crews run honestly because it protects them — become the template: timing windows sized to the real shift, entry where the work happens.

Expected benefit (estimates) N467

Bulk-at-close 22% → under 5% of checks within one quarter of enforcement (estimate; the 4-minute-entry-window signature becomes an audited anomaly). Audit packs stop carrying synthetic timestamps (immediate, by construction). Near-miss incident capture, currently ~30% captured, is estimated to double toward 60% as the amnesty establishes that logging truth is safe — the report's failure-demand analysis prices today's honesty debt at ~9 min/store/day of trust-repair work. Manager Q-Pulse time (mean 41 min/day) is expected to fall ~10% as in-the-moment entry replaces end-of-day reconstruction (estimate).

Franchise Ops Bridge

Flamebook playbook versioning + Daniel Okafor's team office hours. Owner: Daniel Okafor (Head of Franchise Relations).
Impact 3 / 5Effort 2 / 5Wave 2Kind content model + cadence

Problem (evidence)

Flamebook runs 214 live documents with independent version numbers and no "current state of the whole" view; safety-relevant parameters change silently (a marinade hold-time change arrived with no flag on the old version; one franchisee's kiosk store followed a superseded allergen wipe-down for five weeks — no alert, no attestation). Only 31% of franchise managers are confident they always operate from the current version. Compliance-notice acknowledgement runs 89% company vs 71% franchised. And the strongest survey request from partners is not another portal — it is scheduled access to a person.

N152 · N211 · N225 · N240 · N241 · N242 · N154 · N245 · N280 · INS-12–13

Proposal

Change the unit of content: pages → playbooks. Flamebook's task-spans-five-documents problem is structural; the Ops Bridge assembles versioned playbooks — one shelf standard, one LTO launch, one allergen change — where a change to any component bumps the playbook version and the reader sees one number. Supersession becomes loud: a read-attestation loop on safety-relevant changes, with the attestation list visible in the next QBR (Elena Petrova's "deck 24 hours before the meeting" problem is fixed by the same mechanism — partners review from a live playbook, not a freshly-met document).

Office hours, because the ask was a person. Daniel's team runs standing weekly office hours by format cluster (multi-unit partners run 12.6 stores on average; the onboarding assumes a hands-on single-store owner, so half gets skipped informally). Friday-afternoon compliance notices are banned by policy — the unacknowledged 29% cluster there, below the weekend crush, genuinely never seen.

Stratify the reporting. Company and franchise cuts are separated in every ops scorecard (the franchisee asked for exactly this: "which is how 780 stores end up invisible"), so the bridge's effect is measurable in its own right.

Expected benefit (estimates) N469

Version currency confidence 31% → 80% of franchise managers within two quarters (estimate). Compliance acknowledgement 71% → 85%+ (estimate) with the Friday ban plus attestation loop. Superseded-version dwell (today median 17 days on allergen cards) → under 3 days on safety-relevant items (estimate). 214 documents consolidate to an estimated 40–50 playbooks (estimate). Kiosk Accessibility Pass

Travel-hub + food-court kiosk WCAG 2.2 AA remediation (467 kiosks). Owner: Hana Kim (Head of Digital & Delivery).
Impact 2 / 5 (direct) · high per affected userEffort 2 / 5Wave 2Kind hardware + software

Problem (evidence)

467 kiosks (78 travel-hub + 389 food-court) fail WCAG 2.2 AA basics: reach-height card readers, contrast on sun-struck outdoor units, 90-second timeout resets, no audio path. In the travel-hub sample, 7 of 12 shopper reports noted at least one barrier; a wheelchair user completed an order only with a standing companion's help — recorded by the system as a normal 96-second session, i.e. the failure is invisible to every metric. Kiosk abandonment clusters at payment in food courts, where a restart is socially expensive; luggage pushes travel-hub sessions from 96 s to 141 s and nobody has redesigned the flow for a suitcase.

N190 · N312 · N025 · N031 · INS-14–15

Proposal

Remediate in three passes: software now, hardware next, flow last. Pass one is configuration and firmware: timeout extension and timeout-warning, contrast themes for outdoor units, screen-reader path on the audio jack. Pass two is the hardware cycle: reachable card readers and operable-height controls as units are refurbished (the estate already refurbishes on a cycle; the pass rides it rather than accelerating capex). Pass three is flow: a luggage-aware travel-hub sequence (fewer confirmations, one-hand operable) and a restart-resume that makes an abandoned food-court session recoverable without shame.

Instrument the invisibility. Every remediated kiosk logs assisted-session and restart telemetry the current estate cannot see — the wheelchair user's 96-second "normal" session is the design brief for what to measure. Success is defined per affected user, not only at estate average: the proposal's impact score is low on breadth and deliberately high on depth.

Expected benefit (estimates) N471

All 467 kiosks at WCAG 2.2 AA within two hardware cycles (estimate: software pass in weeks, hardware pass ~12 months). Travel-hub sessions with luggage 141 s → ~110 s (estimate). Food-court payment-step abandonment (currently clustering, unmeasured) becomes measured and is estimated to halve with restart-resume. Barrier observations in mystery-shopper kiosk reports (7 of 12) → 0–1 of 12 (estimate). Legal-exposure reduction is noted qualitatively, not claimed numerically.

Recovery Ladder

Standardised service-recovery bands at POS, courier and call-centre. Owners: Hana Kim + Raj Patel (call-centre team lead), Marcus Adeyemi store-side.
Impact 3 / 5Effort 2 / 5Wave 2Kind policy + tooling

Problem (evidence)

Service recovery is three different ladders: POS crews refund ad hoc within a real-but-capped authority; couriers follow each aggregator's policy; call-centre agents apply their own bands up to £6 with £11 held by a supervisor — and identical failures recover at Band-C rates of 24% / 9% / 17% by platform. The customer experiences the seams directly: "nineteen hours and one human for a small side. The refund was fine. The pilgrimage was the insult." Agents under-recover to protect QA scores; 4% of cases (Band X, ~128 customers in two weeks) fall through the policy map entirely; escalations run 12% of which 7 points are authority-band edges — policy artefacts, not severity.

N019 · N075 · N098 · N169 · N256 · N258 · N260 · N261 · N262 · N263 · N317–N320 · N049 · N052 · INS-16–17, INS-21

Proposal

One ladder, four rungs, every door. The £2 / £6 / £11 bands already exist in the tills and roughly in the contact centre; the proposal unifies them across POS, courier-dispatch and call-centre, adds the fourth rung explicitly (today's Band X becomes a named "recovery desk" outcome with a 24-hour clock), and gives every rung a no-questions script. Full £11 authority moves to front-line agents with post-hoc sampling instead of pre-approval — the QA rule that punishes free-text justification is redesigned so that under-recovery is scored as a defect alongside over-recovery.

Make the customer's path one path. Today the app help flow is circular for 61% of callers and the store phone rings out at peak; the ladder gets a single entry point that routes to whichever tier can close it fastest, with the aggregator portal re-keying (median 96 s of handle time on 69% of refund calls) automated behind Route56 rather than performed by agents. The recovery voucher's physical failure mode is fixed in the same stroke: printable/digital vouchers that reconcile in the POS recovery code, ending the 31% improvisation rate.

Wire recovery to learning. Every banded recovery posts its root cause to the store's VoiceBoard view the same day — recovery data currently flows to refund codes, not to the Tuesday huddle; "my calls are VoiceBoard with a pulse" is the integration the operators themselves asked for.

Expected benefit (estimates) N473

Band X 4% → under 1% of contacts (estimate). AHT 4m51s → ~4m05s (estimate) as portal re-keying automates; structural escalation 12% → ~5% (estimate) as authority edges disappear; first-contact resolution 61% → ~75% (estimate). Platform Band-C divergence (24/9/17) converges to a single policy band within tolerance (estimate). Recovery-to-repeat economics improve as on-the-spot execution (already proven to convert complainers at a rate the call path never matches) becomes the default path: redeemers already out-repeat baseline 61% vs 47% — the estimate is that unified bands raise the share of recoveries executed within 2 minutes from today's minority to a majority.

Honesty

What we deliberately did NOT propose

The register's minority-flag rule applies to us: these exclusions are kept and stated, not smoothed away. N483

  1. A CopperPOS v8 rebuild. Every blueprint seam is a v7 seam and the v8 roadmap is unfunded — but a platform rebuild while the roster flywheel spins would burn the change capacity Waves 1–2 need. Not proposed; named as debt.
  2. Renegotiating aggregator commercial terms or dropping a platform. The 25-minute SLA drives queue-jacking and the rating regimes collide — a commercial negotiation, not a service-design artifact. We propose the physical harmonisation (P3, P9) and leave the contracts to Hana Kim's baton.
  3. Reducing the LTO cadence. Six LTOs a year is the marketing engine; the field's "five-day ramps are too short" feedback has been ignored in three retros and that is a governance finding, not ours to overrule. P5 makes the cadence survivable instead.
  4. Pay rises as the retention lever. The evidence says schedule visibility outbids pay in exit comparisons — and compensation is a board decision we have no synthetic mandate to model. P1 attacks what the evidence supports.
  5. A blanket crew-headcount increase. Food-court and drive-thru ratios are genuinely tight, but blanket hiring feeds the flywheel it means to fix. Targeted peak staffing is tested inside P1/P2 experiments with data, not asserted here.
  6. A loyalty-program rebuild. The fraud-freeze loop is real and cruel, but it sits behind a 14-month-old backlog ticket in digital's roadmap; we surface it (P9's single-path routing relieves the symptom) rather than propose a program we cannot evidence.
  7. New-format strategy or estate redesign. Growth capital favouring drive-thru express — the tightest physics — is flagged as a finding (analysis §09); it is an investment decision above this engagement's remit.
  8. Moving the 180 s target instead of meeting it. The target may genuinely be infeasible at some formats' staffing — but the answer is to publish the assumptions and re-baseline honestly (P2), never to quietly stretch the number a beacon pause at a time.
