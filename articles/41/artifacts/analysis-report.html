01 · Methodology recap

Six weeks in the field, one continuous evidence ledger

Copperline 2000 was commissioned by Priya Nair (COO) as a full-operation service blueprint: not a journey of the customer alone, but of the machine that serves 2,000 stores at once. The engagement ran as a 6-week field study followed by reporting; per the canon, it stops at delivery of reports and proposals — prototyping was not executed, and every proposal's first experiment in proposals.html is framed as the next step, not a completed one. N421

Evidence was collected under a simulation protocol executed by the dispatched ASD roles (Researcher, Synthesizer, Mapper, Measurer, with the Critic running sd_review): 8 flagship interviews at Harbourgate Flagship #14 and Northline Flagship #3 (N221), 412 store-manager surveys of 2,000 and 1,893 crew surveys of 41,300, and 5 channel datasets — a 90-day VoiceBoard extract, a 120-store POS sample (3.41M orders, N305), 60 mystery-shopper reports, 3 aggregator rating exports, and 2 weeks of call-centre logs. Claims were graded High only when two independent methods agreed, per the insight register's triangulation rule (N368–N369); minority evidence was kept, never smoothed (N370). This report adds no new field data — it reads the existing ledger and quantifies what it implies. N422

02 · Findings by lane

Thirty findings across six lanes

Each lane of service-blueprint.html is summarised as five findings (F-01…F-30) citing blueprint note IDs (N001–N220) and research note IDs (N221–N320). Lane colour follows the canon code.

Customer actions — how demand actually arrives and how it copes with failure N423

F-01 · CUSTOMERChannel choice is made before any Copperline evidence is seen — and it swings with weather.

Walk 36% · drive 38% · app + aggregators 26%. The decision is a genuine node driven mostly by transport mode that day; weather flips 6–9 points between walk and drive, a signal the demand model only partially ingests.

N021 · N024 · journey-map.html

F-02 · CUSTOMERThe pre-order promise leaks exactly at its payoff moment.

The arrival-scan mat mis-reads 4% of drive pre-orders, forcing re-statement into speaker noise; click-and-collect orders are "not started" on two arrivals in three. Order-ahead is sold as waiting removed, and is experienced as waiting relocated.

N028 · N253 · N254

F-03 · CUSTOMERPerceived wait is governed by the grill, not the clock.

Stores that walled off the charcoal grill saw perceived waits rise 22%; customers describing 8-minute waits use the word "forgotten". The open grill "theatre" is the estate's cheapest wait-management asset and it is currently a flagship accident, not a design decision.

N013 · N036 · N065

F-04 · CUSTOMERVerification dwell is the cheapest quality gate Copperline owns — and the interface truncates it.

Customers need 7–9 s to verify a 4-item basket; confirmation displays auto-dismiss at 5 s, feeding the 8.6% error tail. At the window, the 31 s customer verification dwell converts kitchen errors into 10-second fixes instead of 190-second re-fires.

N006 · N041 · N072

F-05 · CUSTOMERA measurable slice of failure is silent churn no metric sees.

With accuracy at 91.4%, roughly 1 in 12 orders has something wrong; field sampling suggests 15% of those errors are never noticed by anyone — invisible in NPS, VoiceBoard and refund codes alike. The feedback funnel also over-samples recovery cases (6.1% scan rate among wronged customers vs 2.9% overall) and structurally under-samples drive-thru cash customers.

N047 · N050 · N020

Frontstage — where scripts meet peaks N424

F-06 · FRONTSTAGEScripts degrade deterministically with load, and HQ reads the degradation as effort.

LTO upsell verbatim delivery collapses from 61% off-peak to 22% at 12:30; allergen confirmation's 12% counter gap concentrates in the last hour of peak. Marketing concludes crews "are not trying"; crews are triaging a queue.

N059 · N060 · N310

F-07 · FRONTSTAGEQueue-busting migrates the queue into the kitchen.

Mobile POS cuts order-capture 30% but pulls its operator off assembly, so the wait reappears at the buzzer stage. Any frontstage fix that doesn't touch kitchen capacity is a wait relocation, not a wait reduction.

N057 · N036 · N109

F-08 · FRONTSTAGEThe expeditor is the load-bearing human of the peak.

"The hardest 90 minutes in the store" (Bo Chen) — sequencing the rail, juggling courier SLAs against buzzers, calling re-fires. On thin rosters the expeditor is also the grill hand; queue-jacking then silently taxes dine-in customers who watch later arrivals collect first.

N109 · N140 · N139

F-09 · FRONTSTAGEWindow-time pressure is structural, and its coping mechanism is fragile.

Median 246 s against a 180 s target; crews respond by front-loading assembly before payment completes — which works until a void (2.4% of lines, supervisor 3 minutes away) or a re-fire (3.1% of tickets) lands mid-stream. The re-fire decision — make them wait vs send and re-deliver — is made ad hoc, with no standard, by whoever is at the window.

N073 · N061 · N110 · N074

F-10 · FRONTSTAGEThe delivery shelf is the most error-prone surface on the floor, and its discipline is a person, not a role.

Wrong-shelf bags drive most of the 0.7% crew→courier breakage; rush-hour shelf discipline correlates perfectly with whether one named veteran owns the shelf — a positive-deviance pattern (four such stores named in one district) that the org chart does not contain.

N115 · N085 · N247 · N248

Backstage — the roster, the grill, and the paperwork between them N425

F-11 · BACKSTAGERoster fragility is social, not algorithmic.

Cover for the median store's churn (one crew member lost every 9 days) is found by phone tree — a personal network ShiftOrbit does not model. 47% of GMs maintain shadow spreadsheets; in two sampled stores the spreadsheet, not the system, runs the store. The system of record is not the system of use.

N117 · N197 · N288

F-12 · BACKSTAGEGrill cadence is the operational metronome; its failure modes cascade unmodelled.

Every timing in the estate is downstream of whether the grill kept its beat — yet one-grill-down means "cook half-cadence" while the KDS keeps sending tickets at full rate, 4.2 faults per store per quarter, and a 15 °C calibration drift reads as "slow grill" for days.

N103 · N123 · N142

F-13 · BACKSTAGEThe 3.8% waste figure is a forecasting artefact, not carelessness.

Chicken holds 20 minutes at temperature before mandatory waste; the pre-peak cook plan's forecast residual (±12%) maps directly onto hold-time waste in the last 20 minutes of every peak. Crew extend holds quietly; Q-Pulse catches temperature drift, not timing lies.

N104 · N126 · N226 · N267

F-14 · BACKSTAGEBreaks are structurally unfunded, and the log lies about them.

The break exists on the roster "as an abstract idea" while the floor runs one short; rotation compresses into 30 minutes on short days. 56% of crew "always/usually" get their full 30-minute peak break; a kiosk manager logs breaks they did not take. Break debt is paid in accuracy errors and in the eNPS of −8.

N231 · N119 · N290 · N286

F-15 · BACKSTAGETraining-as-designed and training-as-exist differ by a roster constraint, and last night's reality travels on paper.

The 11-shift shadow ladder runs at a median 6 (54% compliance); the closing manager's prep handover — thaw status, par gaps, equipment quirks — is a paper sheet because ShiftOrbit has no field for it. Exactly the information tomorrow's roster check needs cannot be seen by it.

N132 · N157 · N145 · N116

Support processes — supply, franchise, maintenance, HR N426

F-16 · SUPPORTReplenishment misses LTO lifts because a manual step is the integration.

Auto-replenishment proposals ignore promo lift until marketing manually attaches a curve; the step was missed for two of the last five LTOs — a spreadsheet omission that became a store-level stockout experienced frontstage as an apology at the window. Miss the 14:00 approval and the drop slips a day.

N148 · N127 · N200 · N113 · N268

F-17 · SUPPORTMaintenance friction converts planned work into reactive work.

14% of planned vendor visits get bumped by trading; reactive SLA is met 68% for grill-down. The de facto urgent channel is a regional WhatsApp network that beats official dispatch — effective, unscalable, and invisible to governance.

N161 · N162 · N180 · N176

F-18 · SUPPORTThe franchise spine is a versioning failure.

214 live Flamebook documents with independent version numbers and no whole-view; safety-relevant parameters change silently and old versions persist (median 17 days for allergen cards). Compliance-notice acknowledgement: 89% company, 71% franchised.

N152 · N225 · N240 · N241 · N154

F-19 · SUPPORTLTO and campaign calendars broadcast into stores with zero roster notice.

The LTO calendar is set two quarters ahead; the field's feedback that five-day ramps are too short has been noted in three retros and changed zero times. National campaigns add 9–14% launch-week lift while campaign dates and ShiftOrbit forecasts live in different teams.

N153 · N015 · N164

F-20 · SUPPORTThe hiring funnel leaks where roster instability makes the offer worthless.

To hold 41,300 crew against 94% annualised turnover, the network re-hires its whole workforce roughly every 12.8 months; interview-to-start conversion is 38%, dragged by two-week roster-gap start delays. Exit comparisons name schedule visibility, not pay, as the leaving reason.

Data & systems — seven systems, nineteen seams N427

F-21 · DATAIntegration debt is people-shaped.

Seven named systems (CopperPOS v7, ShiftOrbit, StockBeak, Q-Pulse, VoiceBoard, Flamebook, Route56) joined by 19 point-to-point interfaces; three interfaces have had no owner since a 2024 reorganisation. The stack works because specific people care, which is not an architecture.

N218 · N186

F-22 · DATAThe truth about timing exists and is not scored.

Q-Pulse's export preserves entry timestamps — the 22% bulk-at-close pattern has been queryable for years — but the audit rubric scores completion, not honesty of timing, so the report card cannot fail what the export plainly shows.

N203 · N204 · N121 · N274

F-23 · DATAData flows up and never comes back down.

No store has ever received a StockBeak insight about its own waste pattern; VoiceBoard themes reach stores monthly and summarised, when the actionable shelf-life of "couriers stacked in lobby" is one shift; a quarter of negative feedback cannot be routed to a store at all because the aggregator store-ID field is populated in only 61% of rows.

N199 · N205 · N206 · N257

F-24 · DATAThe estate measures the visible 40% and pauses what it doesn't like.

Mystery shoppers cannot see backstage, so the program optimises the visible slice; 9% of measured drive-thru windows carry at least one beacon pause; top-up requests (the most common minor defect) have no POS code; voids are counted but never classified by cause.

N177 · N111 · N215 · N097 · N188

F-25 · DATAMaster data rots quietly and every downstream number inherits the drift.

23 recipes carry stale yield/portion data maintained by a half-FTE post vacant March–July; the aggregated 4.3/5 rating is quoted network-wide while its per-platform composition (and each platform's policy fingerprint on which failure customers notice) hides inside the average.

Physical evidence — what the customer keeps, and what it betrays N428

F-26 · EVIDENCEOwnership gaps are visible in the fascia.

214 sites run faded fascia vinyl past its 5-year replacement cycle, flagged to maintenance, not branding — nobody owns it. The brand's most repeated physical signal decays without an owner while six LTO poster kits ship on time to only ~89% of stores.

N001 · N015

F-27 · EVIDENCEThe receipt expires before the right to complain does.

Thermal receipt paper fades in ~11 weeks; recovery claims referencing old receipts arrive unverifiable and agents are told to accept claims within 30 days only — the evidence artifact has a shorter life than the dispute window it serves.

N007 · N088

F-28 · EVIDENCETrust signals contradict each other at the seam.

Tamper seals are applied in customer view at food-court sites but behind the window at drive-thru express — generating 41 "was my bag opened?" mentions in 90 days irrespective of actual integrity. Hygiene badges display "last temp check" timestamps fed by a system in which 22% of checks are logged in bulk at close: the badge sometimes displays a drumbeat that was actually entered at 21:40 in one sitting.

N010 · N083 · N014 · N121

F-29 · EVIDENCEHanded evidence carries hygiene and cost problems nobody budgets for.

Buzzers are handed out visibly unwiped at 44% of observed handovers and 3 per store per month are lost, ordered as a "front-of-house consumable" competing with packaging budget. The foil bucket, by contrast, is the packaging program's unambiguous win.

N063 · N008 · N009 · N185

F-30 · EVIDENCEThe recovery ladder's physical token is its weakest link.

Voucher cards sit under every till float in £2/£6/£11 bands and redemption works (58% redeemed within 14 days; redeemers repeat at 61% vs 47% baseline) — but 31% of surveyed managers report improvising hand-written vouchers that do not reconcile in the POS recovery code. The mechanism works; the evidence of it does not.

07 · Experience-economy snapshot

Four realms, one strong quadrant

Pine & Gilmore's four realms of experience (13/02–05), read against the evidence. The estate over-delivers in one realm by accident (the grill) and under-delivers in three by omission — and the under-delivery clusters in exactly the formats (food-court, travel-hub) where the product alone must carry the price. N440

Entertainment passive participation · absorption

Strong, flagship-led. The open charcoal grill is "the theatre", visible from 78% of counters; grill call-outs are read as competence; the pass is kept photogenic on protocol. Walled-in stores lost 22% perceived-wait tolerance — the entertainment realm is literally load-bearing (N013, N064, N065).

Educational active participation · absorption

Weakest realm. The customer who wants to learn (allergen matrix, provenance, why the char matters) meets a version-9 card lagging 17 days and verbal confirmation as the only reliable defence. The double-question ritual of allergen-anxious customers is self-taught knowledge, not a designed path (N016, N039, N174).

Escapist active participation · immersion

Nearly absent beyond the flagship. Peak experience runs the other way: queue anxiety (7-minute walk-away threshold), bag-check mutual suspicion ("which the staff visibly find rude"), and verification-as-labour at the window. The escapist realm currently exists only as its inverse — the 19-minute flagship dwell (N026, N255, N041, N044).

Esthetic passive participation · immersion

Format-dependent. Flagship grill-houses at 92% occupancy deliver it; food-courts bleed the queue into mall walkways, drive-thru customers get a bumper-to-bumper corridor with no evidence but the car in front, and the packaging program's one unambiguous win (the foil bucket) is doing esthetic work the stores' interiors do not (N043, N004, N037, N009).

Implication (estimate): the experience premium currently earned by 68 flagships is unavailable to the 1,214 standard stores not by cost but by design omission — the grill is visible in 78% of counters, yet nothing else in the standard-store journey is asked to carry any realm at all.

09 · Balance of the ledger

What is working, what inaction costs

N444 Do not break these. Customer NPS +31 against an industry-shaped headwind; order accuracy 91.4%; the flagship grill-house standard (18 crew peak, the engagement's reference experience); the foil-bucket packaging win; the flash P&L — "the rare Copperline report that both stores and HQ trust"; Route56, "the quiet success story of the stack"; the recovery voucher ladder, whose redeemers out-repeat the baseline (61% vs 47%). Every proposal in the companion document is sequenced to protect these assets first.

N445 Cost of inaction (estimates). Holding the current state means, per week, roughly: 1,700 GM-hours of swap rework; ~2,000 broken courier handovers each costing a 21-minute re-make plus a refund contact; 22% of food-safety telemetry with synthetic timestamps; 62% of contact-centre volume arriving as failure recovery; and the flywheel compounding — the median crew member leaves inside 12.8 months, taking shorthand, shelf discipline and the expeditor's judgement with them. All figures are estimates from §03; the trend they describe, not their precision, is the finding.

N446 One cut the blended numbers hide. Company stores and the 780 franchised stores should not be read together (per the franchisee's own request): acknowledgement rates split 89%/71%, playbook currency confidence sits at 31% among franchise managers, and two long-tenure partners run the estate's oldest POS hardware under renewal leverage. Wherever this report says "the estate", the franchise cut is more volatile — and it is 39% of the estate.

N447 Peak physics in one line. 62% of daily volume lands in four hours; the labour curve rises 1.9× against a 2.7× order-rate curve, so every peak runs a structural deficit that the current format mix then concentrates hardest in drive-thru express — the format growth capital currently favours (1,310 orders/day on 8 crew). The estate is investing into its tightest physics.

N448 The flywheel, quantified. Late rosters (<72 h, 41% of stores) → swaps (11/week) → rework (31%) → GM time poverty (9.5 h/week admin) → thinner peaks → breaks not taken → crew exit (94% annualised) → new crew median 8 months → shadow training cut (6 of 11 shifts) → errors at peak → worse peaks. Every arrow in that chain is evidence-cited in §02; the loop is the single most expensive structure in the estate.

10 · Confidence & limitations

What this analysis cannot claim

N451 Evidence coverage is uneven by lane: Customer and Frontstage findings rest on three methods each (interviews + surveys + channel extracts), while Support-lane claims (DC operations, maintenance contracts) rest largely on single interviews (N264–N269) and should be treated as directionally strong but thinly triangulated. The 15-principle scores and the issue-matrix placements are structured judgements, not measurements. N454 Prototyping was not executed in this engagement: every quantified benefit in proposals.html is a hypothesis to be tested by that document's 30-day experiments, and this report's failure-demand roll-up is an estimate bounded by stated assumptions rather than a measured baseline. The canon's own boundary applies: this is a fictional composite engagement; every person, quote, figure and outcome is synthetic.

N450 Executive headline. Copperline runs a genuinely loved product (NPS +31) on top of a machine that manufactures its own demand: a third of swap labour, a fifth of compliance telemetry, two-thirds of call volume and every broken handover are self-inflicted. The machine's load-bearing parts are social (a phone tree, a named shelf veteran, a WhatsApp channel) and therefore do not scale — but they are also, precisely for that reason, the cheapest things in the estate to support deliberately. N422Evidence base composition (verbatim from canon §5): 8 flagship interviews; 412 store-manager surveys (of 2,000) and 1,893 crew surveys (of 41,300); 90-day VoiceBoard extract; POS sample (120 stores); 60 mystery-shopper reports; 3 aggregator rating exports; 2 weeks of call-centre logs. N423Customer-lane synthesis: demand arrives pre-decided by transport mode; the service's own promise-leaks (scan mat, click-and-collect) re-create the waiting they were sold to remove; perception is governed by the visible grill; and the most dangerous failures are the 15% nobody notices.
N424Frontstage-lane synthesis: every scripted behaviour degrades deterministically with load, and HQ misreads the degradation as effort; the expeditor is a single load-bearing human; and the delivery shelf — the most error-prone surface — is owned by personality, not role.
N425Backstage-lane synthesis: the roster's fragility is social (phone-tree cover, Excel shadows), the grill is the metronome whose failures cascade unmodelled, waste is a forecasting artefact, and both training and handover run on paper because the systems have no field for reality.
N426Support-lane synthesis: replenishment's integration with marketing is a manual step that fails silently (2 of 5 LTOs); maintenance converts planned to reactive; the franchise spine is a versioning failure; and the hiring funnel leaks at the exact point roster instability makes the offer worthless.
N427Data-lane synthesis: 7 systems, 19 point-to-point interfaces, 3 ownerless since 2024; the truth about timing exists in exports but is not scored by rubrics; data flows up and never returns as an operational prompt; and the estate measures the visible 40% while pausing what it dislikes.
N428Evidence-lane synthesis: physical evidence betrays ownership gaps (faded fascias), expired dispute rights (fading thermal paper), contradictory trust signals (seals, badges fed by falsified timing), and a recovery ladder whose physical token is its weakest link (31% improvised vouchers).
N429Failure-demand model — definition and method: demand that would not exist if the service had worked first time (re-work, re-contact, re-make, audit repair). Copperline does not measure it; this section estimates the four largest streams from canon metrics plus ledger-derived assumptions, each stated inline. Estimates are conservative (no double counting between streams).
N430Swap-rework failure demand: 11 requests/store/week × 31% rework = ~3.4 reworks; × ~15 min GM admin each (interview estimate) ≈ 51 min/store/week ≈ 1,700 GM-hours/week estate-wide. Secondary stream: 15% of swap requesters give up; 61% of those then log unplanned absence — failure demand converting into failure supply.
N431Bulk-logging failure demand: 22% of checks carry synthetic timestamps; the demand materialises as audit-repair (re-validation) and unquantified food-safety exposure. Arithmetic: 22% × 41 min/day ≈ 9 min/store/day ≈ 2,100 estate hours/day of trust-repair work if honesty were enforced and backfilled. Marked estimate; the 41-minute figure is measured, the multiplication is ours.
N432Handover-breakage failure demand: 0.7% of delivery orders ≈ 1 broken handover/store/week (channels reconciliation); each costs a median 21-minute re-make + re-dispatch plus a refund contact arriving within a median 40 minutes. The counterfactual is measured, not estimated: stores with shelf + label discipline wait 3.8 vs 8.0 minutes.
N433Recovery-call failure demand: refunds 38% + status 24% = 62% of 3,206 calls/fortnight ≈ 161 hours of handle time per fortnight at AHT 4m51s. Structural escalation (7 of 12 points) is policy artefact, recoverable by design; Band X (4%) is pure policy-map failure.
N434Roll-up estimate: ~15–20% of salaried-manager labour-hours and ~two-thirds of voice-channel contact is failure demand. Stated as a range deliberately: the streams share root causes (a late roster causes both a swap rework and a missed check), so summation would double-count. This single number is the report's most quotable estimate and its least precise — both facts are the point.
