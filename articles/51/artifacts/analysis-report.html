Executive read

Over 100,000 guest comments, read as one dataset, showed the complaints were never about speed: roughly two-thirds of service complaints involve execution competence, while speed appears in just over half. The cross-mention pair supplies the causal direction — execution failures read as slowness; slowness rarely masks an execution failure — so incompetence causes slowness, not the reverse. The strategy redirects from throughput targets and labour allocation to competence-first training, one memorable values framework, and failure prevention: one negative experience erases roughly four positive interactions.

Definitions were frozen before coding: speed is raw clock time from order to receipt (quantitative); execution is the competency and capability to execute processes correctly, handle complexity, and coordinate effectively (qualitative). Fixing execution could resolve roughly two-thirds of service complaints; focusing on speed alone addresses the smallest slice.

The trial is the case study in one table: satisfaction and accuracy rose while speed barely moved — the competence-first thesis confirmed live. Guests did not experience the programme as slower; what they noticed was accuracy and care. A second natural experiment corroborated it: stores focused on customer experience out-scored the system average on the chain's balanced scorecard.

Open items

Scorecard alignment. The verified-only hospitality metric is deliberately kept off the balanced scorecard to preserve honesty, while the scorecard is what actually drives restaurant behaviour — the gap is named, not closed. Attribution. Most comments still lack channel attribution; the coding rules shrink the gap but the blind spot is reported every week. Capacity. The rollout was rephased once for field capacity; later phases carry explicit proceed-or-pause gates.
