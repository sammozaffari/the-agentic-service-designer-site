One framework instead of four

Four competing customer-experience frameworks coexisted in the field, none focused enough on hospitality — teams cannot execute a value they cannot name. The proposal consolidates them into one memorable framework with three pillars, each with behavioural standards, per-station decomposition, and example scripts. The acronym debate settled by the data: operations argued for putting speed back in, and the decomposition kept it out.

Hospitality
Be proactive, be safe, be you

Standard. Acknowledge every guest within seconds; never leave a guest unanswered at the till or the window; own the interaction from greeting to goodbye.

Script. A verbal confirmation at the speaker: "that's two boxes and two drinks — correct?" and a recovery line for a frustrated guest: "we heard you — here is what happens next."

Ownership. the hospitality programme; per-station standards for order taker, pay window, tap-and-go, pickup, kiosk, delivery, and parked cars

Accuracy
Know it all, make sure the guest gets it all

Standard. Pack-check at handoff; visual checklists at drive-thru and delivery pickup; fast refund and remake script for missed items.

Script. "Here is everything — boxes, sides, sauces." On a miss: remake and refund without friction, and confirm the replacement before the guest leaves.

Ownership. operations; accuracy is the largest complaint cluster and the highest-churn accuracy theme

Taste
Looks good, tastes good

Standard. Presentation check before handoff; temperature and build check at the pack screen; remake standard for anything that does not pass.

Script. Kitchen line: "re-fire that box — it does not pass the look check." Handoff: "fresh from the kitchen — enjoy."

Ownership. operations; taste drives a large share of formal complaints and must be protected at pack

Surprise physical awards for top performers were chosen deliberately to avoid gaming the numbers; the "what's in it for me" logic was written for both managers (easier restaurant, retention, bonuses, career advancement) and crew.

The rollout was rephased once when competing system rollouts absorbed the same field capacity — the plan was rephased, not rushed. Each gate carried explicit options and status, enforced by tooling, not memory.

Measurement design

Success is tracked on verified guest feedback only — responses attributable to a real transaction — deliberately kept off the chain's balanced scorecard, with that alignment gap named as an open issue rather than hidden. Capability checks run on digital observation forms (five-minute checks) feeding a real-time dashboard with automated weekly reports to area leaders; high-scoring crew earn recognition, and managers sustaining high team averages receive small bonuses. A manual observation instrument managers distrusted was killed and redesigned into this form rather than defended.
