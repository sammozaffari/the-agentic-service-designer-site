Six insights, each graded

I-01 Competence, not friendliness, is the hospitality gap — team-member complaints are overwhelmingly about capability, not attitude
Root cause: the frontline gap is execution capability — confusion, disorganisation, failed coordination · Evidence: complaint mix; theme coding; team-member comments dominate the corpus · Implication: train for competence first, speed second; scripted standards beat friendliness campaigns
I-02 The system optimises speed, but the data says fix execution — execution appears in roughly two-thirds of service complaints vs speed in just over half
Root cause: the dominant metric rewards clock time while the real failure mode is capability · Evidence: decomposition exhibit 33/46/21; cross-mention asymmetry · Implication: reframe the fix from labour allocation to competence; "doing it right the first time" is the counter-argument to institutionalised speed
I-03 Guests forgive slow competence but not fast chaos — speed complaints carry far less churn than execution complaints
Root cause: perceived competence is the trust currency; clock time matters far less to loyalty than orderly, capable execution · Evidence: churn rates by theme; leadership agreement · Implication: optimise perceived competence — clarity, ownership, coordination — ahead of throughput
I-04 Service experience is asymmetric: bad hurts more than good helps — one negative experience erases roughly four positive interactions
Root cause: negative service events compound into defection while positive ones are table stakes · Evidence: churn and loyalty driver analysis · Implication: prioritise failure prevention and recovery over delight moments
I-05 Accuracy failures are high-frequency, high-churn, low-loyalty events — missing items dominate, and refund friction amplifies the damage
Root cause: handoff and pack processes fail under complexity · Evidence: theme volumes; complaint categorisation; churn rates · Implication: make accuracy a named value pillar with operations ownership; pack-check at handoff; fast refund and remake script
I-06 A fragmented values stack cannot change frontline behaviour — four competing frameworks dilute execution — teams cannot execute a value they cannot name
Root cause: multiple competing value languages split attention and training effort · Evidence: framework audit; leadership interviews · Implication: consolidate into one memorable framework with per-station behavioural standards and example scripts

Every insight must change what gets designed — an insight that does not alter a decision is a theme, not an insight. Each card carries its evidence and its design implication, and every implication traces into the proposals and the rollout plan (see the proposals artifact).

Grading note

An adversarial review pass attacked each card against the coded evidence register: one candidate fell on a weak quote, and two required stronger evidence before they could ship. The two load-bearing numbers — execution in ~2/3 of service complaints and the cross-mention asymmetry — were recomputed from source data, never remembered.
