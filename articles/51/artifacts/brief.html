The brief

A franchisee group inside a global quick-service restaurant chain had spent years optimizing one number: speed. A successful drive-thru hospitality initiative — scripting, station-level training, recognition — was to expand to the whole restaurant, restoring the balance between technology-driven efficiency and meaningful human interaction at every touchpoint. The brief also asked for a refreshed customer-experience values framework so the system could deliver it.

The evidence reframed the ask. Hospitality was simultaneously the leading cause of churn and the largest loyalty driver; team-service satisfaction sat at barely half of guests system-wide; and over half of all complaints were service-related. Yet the operating culture had institutionalised a different number — speed has been the metric for so long — and the reward systems followed it. Three intertwined problems followed: a mis-calibrated operating metric, a competence gap disguised as an attitude problem, and a values language too scattered to change behaviour.

Success means: competence-first training, one memorable values framework, and measurement that cannot be gamed.

The decomposition (see the analysis report) split service complaints into speed, execution, or both. The cross-mention pair shows the direction of causation: incompetence causes slowness, not the reverse.

Constraints

  • Speed is institutionalised. The dominant metric rewards clock time; teams may read a competence-first programme as "slowing us down" — the mitigation is built in: doing it right the first time is faster overall.
  • Field capacity. Multiple system-wide rollouts were already competing for the same restaurant managers and coaches; the programme had to be phased and gated, not launched.
  • Measurement honesty. Success is tracked only on verified guest feedback, with that exclusion named rather than hidden.
  • Data gaps. A large share of comments could not be attributed to a channel; the team had to name the blind spot and build rules to shrink it.
  • Global alignment. A franchisee-level framework must stay compatible with the chain's global hospitality standards still being defined.
