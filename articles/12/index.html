01 · Executive summary

Executive summary

Northgate Health had solved the front door problem described in article 02: telehealth onboarding was no longer leaking badly. The next constraint was the ecosystem beyond the login. Members still carried paper referrals between GPs and specialists, pharmacists still lacked timely care-plan context after discharge, employers wanted wellness reporting without becoming accidental health-data custodians, and Northgate itself could see only the fragments it owned. Over a 14-week programme, we used service ecosystem mapping (See: 12-service-dominant-logic/04-service-ecosystems.md; 06-the-service-innovation-handbook/03-understanding-the-service-ecosystem.md) and the Orchestration Canvas (See: 08-orchestrating-experiences/03-the-orchestration-canvas.md) to redesign the system around a new coordinating layer: the Northgate Gateway, a care-coordination service combining a unified member record, referral orchestration, provider API hub, and member-controlled care-plan toggle. The result was a future-state operating model that reduced referral admin cycle time from 6.0 to 1.2 days, increased referral loop closure from 54% to 88%, cut provider admin time per referral from 22 to 7 minutes, and improved care-gap closure from 58% to 81% in the partner pilot.

02 · The brief

The brief

Client portrait

Northgate Health is the same fictional mutual insurer introduced in article 02: roughly 40,000 members across Belgium and the Netherlands, older-skewing (31% over 65), with a successful but still bounded telehealth partnership with Meridia Telehealth, a 60-clinician platform. The onboarding redesign from article 02 improved the first mile of access, but it also made a more structural problem visible. Members could now enter telehealth more easily, yet the larger care journey remained fragmented across over 520 GP practices where Northgate members were registered, 210 preferred-network GP practices carrying 68% of referral volume, specialist clinics, pharmacies, diagnostics networks, home-care providers, employer wellness programmes, and national health-data utilities.

The challenge

The symptom Northgate named was "slow referrals." The underlying issue was wider: the member was still acting as the unpaid systems integrator. A GP printed a referral; the specialist owned the booking; diagnostics held results in a separate portal; pharmacy saw prescriptions but not care intent; home care saw discharge tasks but not medication changes; Northgate saw claims after the fact; Meridia saw telehealth episodes but not the whole pathway. Provider portal data showed only 14% use of the referral module and 38% weekly active use across affiliated GP practices. In 24 months of claims and encounter data, OpenCode graph pipelines reconstructed 47,800 referral events and 11,900 multi-actor care chains; only 54% of referral outcomes made it back to the referrer within 30 days, and 11% of diagnostics were duplicated within 90 days because results did not follow the member.

Northgate's executive team asked a more ambitious question than "Can we make referrals digital?" They asked who should orchestrate the system and under what rules. That is an ecosystem design question, not a channel redesign question (See: 09-mapping-experiences/06-other-alignment-diagrams.md; 08-orchestrating-experiences/05-channels-and-ecosystems.md).

Constraints

Budget €240,000; 14 weeks to produce a partner-pilot blueprint and governance pack before 2027 budget cycles; GDPR and national special-category-data rules; no appetite among partners for a Northgate-owned monopoly platform; and a politically sensitive reality that GPs, specialists, pharmacists, and home-care providers each held legitimate professional autonomy. The data-sharing model therefore had to be consensual, reversible, and explicitly non-lock-in. Article 02 proved the front door could improve; article 12 had to prove Northgate could coordinate without overreaching.

Why service design

A linear process map would have hidden the problem by making one organization appear central. Service ecosystem mapping makes the relationships, exchanges, and institutional constraints visible (See: 12-service-dominant-logic/04-service-ecosystems.md; 06-the-service-innovation-handbook/03-understanding-the-service-ecosystem.md). The Orchestration Canvas then translates that network view into an operating surface: channels, actors, signals, responses, owners, and metrics aligned to a real journey (See: 08-orchestrating-experiences/03-the-orchestration-canvas.md). This was also a service-design-in-organizations problem: Northgate needed a federated governance model with shared metrics and partner council decision rights, not another project team and a PDF (See: 01-this-is-service-design-doing/09-service-design-in-organizations.md).

03 · Approach

Approach: the AI-first service design process

We treated the programme as a lighthouse ecosystem project for Northgate's next maturity step: from fixing one touchpoint to orchestrating a cross-silo, cross-partner journey (See: 01-this-is-service-design-doing/09-service-design-in-organizations.md). Structurally it still followed Discover → Define → Develop → Deliver, but we explicitly used that spine as the TISDD loop in practice and as a bridge across Kumar's modes: sense intent, know context, know people, frame insights, explore concepts, frame solutions, and realize offerings. The core methods were therefore different from article 02: ecosystem fieldwork, value-exchange mapping, orchestration-canvas design, governance specification, and partner pilot preparation.

04 · The work, phase by phase

Phase-by-phase narrative

Phase 1 — Discover (weeks 1–4)

Goal. Understand the ecosystem from the outside in: who creates value, who absorbs risk, and where members are forced to bridge seams themselves.

Method & SOP. The research spine combined chronological journey inquiry with ecosystem fieldwork (See: 08-orchestrating-experiences/04-understanding-the-journey.md; 06-the-service-innovation-handbook/03-understanding-the-service-ecosystem.md). We ran 120 stakeholder interviews: 34 GP practice staff, 22 specialists and clinic managers, 18 pharmacists, 14 home-care staff, 12 diagnostics and discharge staff, 8 employer programme leads, 6 health-tech vendors, 4 payer and regulator stakeholders, and 2 professional-body representatives. Interviews were anchored in critical incidents: the last failed referral, the last missing result, the last discharge with medication confusion. In parallel, a value-exchange survey captured what each actor believed they gave and received in the ecosystem. OpenCode then mined 24 months of claims and encounter data into a relationship graph to identify chain length, duplicated tests, and missing loop closures.

AI execution. Claude Code ran the transcript-processing fan-out: one subagent extracted actor, exchange, channel, and governance evidence; one validated every quoted incident against the transcript line range; one normalized actor names into a canonical ecosystem dictionary. OpenCode handled the graph pipeline:

Human designer judgment. The designer chose where neutrality mattered most. Northgate's first instinct was to center the insurer in every interview and canvas. The fieldwork instead centered the member outcome and the care episode, consistent with the A2A ecosystem lens: every participant is a resource-integrating actor, not a subordinate in Northgate's org chart (See: 12-service-dominant-logic/04-service-ecosystems.md).

Artifact produced. Actor inventory, coded critical incidents, graph metrics, and a research-backed first pass of the ecosystem map. The most important early finding: 23% of referral coordination still happened by phone chains that Northgate's owned systems could not see at all.

Phase 2 — Define (weeks 4–6)

Goal. Convert evidence into a systemic diagnosis: which exchanges are broken, one-sided, or ownerless, and which institutional constraints are real versus assumed.

Method & SOP. We built two alignment artifacts together: an ecosystem map and a value-exchange model (See: 09-mapping-experiences/06-other-alignment-diagrams.md; 06-the-service-innovation-handbook/03-understanding-the-service-ecosystem.md). Each link was labeled not just by channel but by what actually flowed: information, money, legal authority, care, labor, or trust. Then we overlaid institutional arrangements — GDPR consent rules, professional autonomy norms, and taken-for-granted beliefs such as "the GP should always remain the primary coordinator" (See: 12-service-dominant-logic/04-service-ecosystems.md).

AI execution. Claude Code drafted the first value-exchange model from the evidence register and graph output, then generated a gap-first reading: unsupported phases, orphaned signals, unowned seams, and one-sided exchanges. Codex was used as an adversarial reviewer, specifically to prevent Northgate's perspective from dominating the model:

TERMINAL
codex exec --skip-git-repo-check "Read articles/12/README.md draft sections 2 to 5,

Codex's most useful challenge was simple: our first map treated provider API adoption as obviously desirable. Interview evidence did not support that. Practices wanted fewer handoffs and less re-keying; they did not want another portal. That distinction forced the design principle Never re-key and the architecture choice of API-first plus structured fallback, rather than a portal-first rollout.

Human designer judgment. The designer rewrote the insight set from Northgate language into ecosystem language. The headline was not "Northgate lacks visibility." It was "The system closes cost loops faster than care loops." That reframing shifted the conversation from reporting to coordination.

Artifact produced. Six ecosystem insights and six design principles:

ID Insight Design implication
E-01 Members still carry referral context across actors because the seam has no persistent state Orchestrate the seam, not the touchpoint
E-02 Outcome information returns to payers and referrers too late or not at all Every referral returns
E-03 Results do not reliably follow the member across providers Shared summary and result push
E-04 Practices spend admin labor re-keying the same data into multiple systems API-first and pre-filled payloads
E-05 Discharge is treated as an event, though recovery is a cross-actor pathway Care-plan orchestration across home care and pharmacy
E-06 Actors will share data only when value and control are reciprocal Consent is a product feature

Phase 3 — Develop (weeks 7–10)

Goal. Translate the diagnosis into an operating design: channel roles, sensing, responding, ownership, governance, and a concrete orchestrating service.

Method & SOP. This phase used the Orchestration Canvas as the master boundary object (See: 08-orchestrating-experiences/03-the-orchestration-canvas.md). The validated journey spine sat across the top; the rows below captured channels, ecosystem actors, signals, responses, ownership, and metrics. We ran a gap-first diagnostic after the first pass: empty cells, duplicate channels, unsupported high-stakes moments, and ownerless handoffs. The design outcome was the Northgate Gateway: a care-coordination service, not a new primary-care platform. It included four bounded capabilities: (1) a unified member record assembled by consented summaries rather than full data takeover; (2) referral orchestration; (3) a provider API hub using open standards; and (4) a member-facing care-plan toggle controlling which actors could see which summaries.

AI execution. Claude Code was strong here because the artifact was both structural and textual. The canvas was versioned as markdown tables; partner comments came in as pull-request reviews, not photographed workshop walls. One subagent generated channel-role options from evidence; another checked every row for the sense-and-respond loop; a third generated the governance implications. OpenCode simulated partner states and portal traffic to test whether proposed signals were actually available. The build prompt for the canvas was explicit:

NOTE
You are ORCHESTRATION-CANVAS-BUILDER.
Input: validated journey phases, actor dictionary, exchange map, portal usage data.
For each journey phase, output rows for channel, actor, purpose, signal,
response, technology and owner.
Rules:
- assign one primary channel per phase
- every signal must trigger a response or be deleted
- every handoff must have an owner and fallback route
- if a response depends on partner data Northgate does not control, mark it as
  negotiated not assumed
- bias check: no more than 40 percent of primary responses may sit in owned channels
  unless evidence justifies it

Human designer judgment. The designer made the most politically sensitive call of the programme: Northgate would orchestrate the seam but would not attempt to become the clinical source of truth. That kept GPs and specialists in their professional roles, reduced partner resistance, and prevented the Gateway from becoming an insurer-owned lock-in layer disguised as convenience.

Artifact produced. A complete Orchestration Canvas, the Gateway concept, and a governance model specifying partner council seats, veto rights on data-use changes, and portability requirements.

Phase 4 — Deliver (weeks 11–14)

Goal. Prepare a live partner pilot proving that orchestration can improve outcomes without centralizing every function inside Northgate.

Method & SOP. We translated the canvas into a future-state service blueprint, pilot scope, integration SOPs, and governance pack. The pilot included 12 GP practices, 2 pharmacy chains (38 branches), 1 diagnostics network, 2 home-care providers, 1 hospital discharge unit, and Meridia Telehealth, together covering about 3,900 Northgate members attached to those practices. This is the organizationalization move described in 01-this-is-service-design-doing/09-service-design-in-organizations.md: a lighthouse project with explicit operating-model consequences.

AI execution. GitHub became the system of record. Every seam in the blueprint produced a work item with the blueprint step ID, partner owner, and legal gate. OpenCode generated sandbox payloads for referral, diagnostics, discharge, and pharmacy events so partners could test the API hub without exposing production data. Codex reviewed the data-sharing agreement shell and flagged a re-identification risk in employer reporting; the design adopted a k-anonymity floor of 25 and aggregate-only reporting for employer programmes.

Human designer judgment. The designer protected the governance boundary. Northgate could fund and convene the Gateway, but scale depended on trust. That meant writing non-technical artifacts with equal care: council charters, exit clauses, partner-facing SOPs, and member consent copy.

Artifact produced. Future-state service blueprint, governance templates, integration SOPs, partner pilot pack, and measurement model.

14 Easy to get human assistance Partial Pass Gateway navigator for complex or stalled pathways
15 Requires the right knowledge Fail Pass Members stop acting as human couriers of clinical context

06 · Use cases

Use cases

Use case A — GP-to-specialist digital handoff

Scenario. Els Vandenberg, 58, is referred by Dr. Peeters for cardiology follow-up after an abnormal ECG. In the old pathway, Els left with a letter and no tracking. In the new pathway, Dr. Peeters sends a structured referral from the GP system; the Gateway acknowledges it within seconds, offers Els live slots once the clinic triages, and returns the specialist outcome to the GP automatically.

What was designed. The core design is the handoff protocol: identity, referral reason, recent diagnostics, urgency, and member consent travel with the referral. The specialist can triage without phoning the practice. If no triage occurs within four business hours, the Gateway chases the case. If Els is inactive for 48 hours, a navigator calls.

AI working session. Claude Code drafted the handoff protocol and status copy; OpenCode generated sample payloads and stale-case simulations; Codex reviewed whether the insurer was assuming authority it did not have. The design was tightened so Northgate coordinates but never edits clinical triage.

Outcome (illustrative). Median admin cycle for this pathway fell from 6.4 days to 1.0 day; loop closure reached 100% in the bounded pilot cohort; referral-journey CES for similar members fell from 3.8 to 2.0.

Use case B — Post-discharge care plan across home care and pharmacy

Scenario. This use case extends article 02's discharge onboarding pattern. A member leaves hospital after a routine orthopaedic stay. Before this programme, home care, the pharmacy, and the GP received partial information on different days. Medication changes were often confirmed by phone after the member was already home.

What was designed. The Gateway treats discharge as the start of a care plan, not the end of an episode. With consent captured at discharge, the hospital sends a summary; home care receives the visit schedule; the pharmacy sees the medication list and interaction risks; the GP sees all three updates in one summary thread.

AI working session. OpenCode built the synthetic event stream for discharge, home-care, and dispensing updates. Claude Code turned the event logic into a partner-readable SOP. A Codex challenge pass flagged one over-broad field in the draft pharmacy payload; only medication-relevant fields remained.

Outcome (illustrative). Post-discharge 7-day follow-up completion rose from 61% to 92%; medication-reconciliation exceptions fell 64%; duplicate follow-up calls into member services dropped 43% in pilot weeks 2–4.

Use case C — Employer wellness programme data exchange

Scenario. Northgate wanted employer programmes to move from generic benefit promotion to more targeted prevention support, but employers did not want raw health data and employees did not trust them with it.

What was designed. The Gateway uses a strict aggregate-only reporting model. Members can opt into a prevention programme and optionally share wearable-derived activity summaries with Northgate for incentive calculations; employers receive only cohort-level reporting above a threshold, never individual participation or health status.

AI working session. Claude Code drafted the care-plan toggle and reporting rules as policy text; Codex reviewed the re-identification risk and forced a k-anonymity floor of 25 plus suppression of small cohorts; GitHub PR review included legal and HR reviewers, not just product and engineering.

Outcome (illustrative). Participation in the pilot employer programme rose from 24% to 41%; there were zero individual data disclosures to employers; member trust scores on the prevention consent flow improved from 61% comprehension in round 1 testing to 88% in round 3.

07 · SOPs for AI

SOPs for AI — how service design SOPs are best used by agents

Encoded SOPs. Three SOPs carried most of the work.

  1. Ecosystem-mapping SOP. Pattern: actor inventory → exchanges → institutions → governance. Output schema: actor list, edge list, one-sided exchange flags, institutional constraints, and governance questions. Acceptance: every actor has a role and incentive; every edge has a value type; every high-risk seam has an owner. Prompt template:
NOTE
You are ECOSYSTEM-MAPPER.
Input: interview evidence, graph summary, actor dictionary.
Produce:
1. actor inventory grouped by direct, indirect and institutional roles
2. exchange list with from, to, value type, channel, volume and fragility
3. governance questions for each fragile seam
Rules:
- center the beneficiary and outcome, not the client organization
- if an actor receives work without visible value back, flag it as one-sided
- map regulative, normative and cultural-cognitive constraints separately
- do not recommend a client-owned platform unless evidence shows partner benefit,
  portability and consent are already resolved

  1. Graph-mining prompt pipeline. OpenCode was used for evidence that workshops rarely produce: chain length, loop closure, duplicate diagnostics, and hidden phone reliance. Acceptance: every metric must reconcile to the source tables; every synthetic sample must preserve the headline proportions.

  2. Orchestration-canvas build SOP. Journey spine → channels → actors → signals → responses → owners → metrics → gap-first audit. Acceptance: one primary channel per phase, every signal paired with a response, every handoff owned, every fallback explicit.

Meta-SOP. Research → model the ecosystem → build the journey spine → specify orchestration → blueprint the future state → encode governance → test with partners. The gates matter. We did not allow the Gateway concept to solidify until the value-exchange model showed reciprocal benefit for partners. We did not allow partner reporting to progress until legal review cleared the aggregate-only pattern.

Guardrails.

  • Data-sharing consent. No cross-partner sharing without explicit, member-visible consent or another lawful basis clearly documented. Care-plan toggles were treated as a product feature, not a footnote.
  • No vendor lock-in bias. Agents were instructed to prefer open standards, equal partner terms, exportability, and structured fallback. The first draft of the architecture over-weighted a proprietary portal; the neutrality guardrail blocked it.
  • Evidence discipline. Quote verification, sample-volume reconciliation, and partner review were mandatory.
  • Human sign-off. Governance, lawful basis, employer reporting, and anything that changed professional roles stayed human-led.

Failure modes observed. The main AI failure mode was over-focusing on owned channels. The first canvas draft pushed too many primary responses into Northgate or Meridia touchpoints because those were the systems with the richest data. The mitigation was a neutral value-exchange lens plus a hard prompt rule: if a partner-owned channel is the natural primary, Northgate must support it rather than replace it. A second failure mode was graph-mining overconfidence: duplicate diagnostics can look like waste when they are clinically necessary repeats. Those cases were validated with clinicians before using the metric.

Causal logic. The value creation is both operational and experiential. Admin minutes, duplicate tests, and manual channels are direct efficiency gains. Following the service-profit-chain logic used in article 05, the operational improvements drive reduced failure demand and improved trust; following the SDL lens, the value is realized in use across actors, not captured in one interface (See: 12-service-dominant-logic/04-service-ecosystems.md). Measurement plan. North-star metric: members whose referral closes as a visible care loop, not a sent document. Driver metrics: send-to-booked cycle time, loop closure, stale-case age, admin minutes per referral, duplicate diagnostics, and care-gap closure. Governance metrics: partner SLA adherence, consent revocation rate, and portability request turnaround.

09 · AI tool playbook

AI tool playbook

Claude Code. Role: orchestration, synthesis, artifact authoring, and governance drafting. It ran the interview fan-out, generated the ecosystem and value-exchange models, drafted the Orchestration Canvas, and maintained consistency between metrics, diagrams, and templates. It excelled at turning messy multi-actor evidence into structured artifacts. It failed when left to infer political legitimacy from data alone; governance still needed human judgment.

OpenCode. Role: graph pipelines, synthetic datasets, payload simulation, and portal-usage analysis. It reconstructed referral chains from claims and encounter logs, created the sample edge list, and generated sandbox event streams for partners. It excelled at multi-file mechanical work and realistic mock data. It failed once by treating all repeated diagnostics as waste; that was corrected through clinician review.

Codex. Role: adversarial reviewer. It was pointed at specific questions: where the insurer view was over-centered, whether a payload over-collected data, whether employer reporting created re-identification risk, and whether a governance clause hid vendor lock-in. It excelled at attacking comfortable assumptions. It failed when it suggested centralizing more authority in Northgate than the ecosystem would tolerate; every recommendation was verified, never averaged in.

GitHub. Role: system of record and gatekeeper. Issues tracked seams, owners, and legal dependencies. PRs carried diagram, data, copy, and governance changes. Actions-style checks were used for Mermaid validity, CSV shape, and quote traceability. Partner review was easier because comments sat on concrete artifacts, not workshop photos. For an ecosystem project, that mattered: the repository became a neutral working surface.

10 · Lessons learned

Lessons learned & pitfalls

  • An ecosystem map without exchange labels collapses into an org chart. The first draft was visually tidy and strategically useless until every edge named what actually flowed.
  • Governance is part of the service, not post-design paperwork. The Gateway became credible only when partners gained veto rights on data-use changes and a contractual exit path.
  • The owned-channel bias is real. Agents naturally over-invest in the channels that emit the cleanest telemetry. The neutral value-exchange lens is a necessary counterweight.
  • Graph mining revealed the shadow journey. Phone chasing and informal staff workarounds explained more pain than portal analytics did.
  • Consent copy needed three rounds. Members accepted the care-plan toggle only once the wording made the exchange reciprocal and revocable.
  • Portals are not orchestration. Practices wanted fewer screens, not a better insurer portal; the winning move was API-first with structured fallback.
  • Done differently next time: bring the regulator liaison into week 2 rather than week 7, and run one time-and-motion study in a GP practice to baseline admin effort more precisely than self-reporting allowed.
