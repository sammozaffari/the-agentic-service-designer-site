Four personas, fourteen composites

Fourteen evidence-based personas were built from the survey segments and interview voices; the four representative cards below anchor the journey and blueprint. Each is a composite of its source segment, not a single individual.

DADana, 19
Crew member · ~89 survey responses

Student working evenings and weekends; phone-first — checks shifts and hours in the mobile app; photos the printed roster and runs breaks off the whiteboard above the time clock.

Goals
  • See shifts, punch times, and expected pay in the app
  • A break countdown instead of guesswork
  • Keep access across phone changes
Pains
  • Being paid wrong and not noticing
  • Losing access after a phone change or re-registration
  • Break timing left to the whiteboard
Composite: crew survey segment (~89 responses) + crew interviews
PRPriya, 27
Rostering manager · ~28 survey responses

Builds the roster copy-week from a template; manages breaks, drops, and swaps; prints the roster daily and keeps a custom spreadsheet for the readable version.

Goals
  • A roster that reads at a glance
  • Cost estimates that hold at scale
  • Fewer hand-fixed breaks
Pains
  • Per-rate errors across hundreds of rostered hours
  • The five-hour auto-break misfires
  • No live labour figures to staff against
Composite: rostering-manager survey segment (~28 responses) + manager interviews
MAMarcus, 41
Area leader · cluster of restaurants

Covers several restaurants; weekly labour reporting and compliance spot-checks; compiles labour figures per store by hand.

Goals
  • Figures that agree across systems
  • Exceptions visible without manual arithmetic
  • Fewer hours on compilation
Pains
  • “The figures I get are not the figures I see in the system”
  • Manual per-store reporting
  • Exception reports need hand division
Composite: area-leader interviews + payroll reconciliation evidence
ELElena, 45
Payroll specialist · equity and franchise

Runs the pay cycle to a fixed cut-off; handles corrections, leave balances, and special pay runs; pay periods are effectively never reopened.

Goals
  • Corrections without ripple effects
  • Balances that are right at approval time
  • A reopen path that is not heroic
Pains
  • Corrections arrive as phantom shifts or emails
  • Flow-on effects for tax, superannuation, and benefits
  • The compliant path is slower than the workaround
Composite: payroll interviews (equity + franchise) + ticket themes

Design response

The personas are not marketing archetypes — each maps to a blueprint lane and a set of measures. Dana defines the crew-visibility spine (D4); Priya defines the rostering and labour-data lanes (D3); Marcus defines the reporting and exception lanes (D3); Elena defines the correction and leave lanes (D2). The design response is read off the pains, not the goals.
