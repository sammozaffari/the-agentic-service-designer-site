Line of visibility

Crew and restaurant-manager rows are frontstage; IT support, payroll, and systems sit below the line. Every fail point carries an owner — the blueprint is a set of accountabilities, not a description. The standardisation boundary is drawn deliberately: the platform layer and the enterprise-agreement interpretation are uniform, while the payroll tail stays variant by design.

Reading the fail points

F-01…F-08 are the same markers the journeys use. F-04 is the negotiated compromise: a visible warning with an audit record instead of a hard stop, protecting 100,000+ rostered shifts at the five-hour boundary. F-06 is the workaround pair — phantom-shift corrections and a reopen path that is effectively refused — and it is the clearest case in the whole blueprint of a rule creating its own violation.
