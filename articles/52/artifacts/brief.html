The brief

A global quick-service restaurant chain runs its rostering, timekeeping, breaks, leave, compliance, and payroll feed on a third-party time & attendance platform. The visible ask was a system-improvement exercise: prioritise and implement critical improvements, fix rostering and labour-cost inefficiencies, mitigate compliance risk, reduce staff frustration, and standardise processes across equity-owned and franchised restaurants. The analysis refused to treat that list as the problem. The problem was alignment: everyone used the platform, and nobody agreed on what to fix first. The core issue lay not in the system’s current limitations, but in the lack of shared priorities for addressing them — the misalignment made vendor collaboration difficult and blocked every strategic decision about enhancements.

Scope

IN: the end-to-end time & attendance service, from restaurant floor to payroll — onboarding and access, rostering, timekeeping, breaks, leave, compliance, and the payroll feed, across seven lifecycle stages and four lanes (frontstage, backstage, support processes, physical evidence). OUT: the payroll engine’s internals beyond the feed, pay architecture itself, and any single feature configuration imposed on franchise groups — the payroll tail stays variant by design.

Constraints

  • The platform is not ours. The time & attendance platform is a software-as-a-service product the chain does not own; the vendor releases features to all clients on its own schedule, and the chain negotiates rather than commands.
  • Global dependencies. Several services the platform depends on cannot be modified locally; the global parent controls the forecast feed — three weeks of forecast are sent when managers need four, and the transfer carries a two-hour delay.
  • Franchise autonomy. Payroll cycles, pay-period reopening policies, leave handling, and feature configurations differ by franchise group; a single feature configuration cannot be imposed top-down.
  • Regulatory layer. Enterprise-agreement interpretation, visa and minor-hour caps, the five-hour break boundary, roster-notice rules, consent requirements, and record-retention obligations all bind the design.
  • No education platform. Roughly a quarter of the pain points are solvable with education and communication, and the business has no engagement platform to deliver them.
  • Integration latency. Role changes take up to two weeks to reach the platform; training completions sync late; reports are capped at a 30-day window; the payroll run closes on a Tuesday-morning cut-off.

Decision requested

Four decisions at the governance gate: (1) the blueprint scope named above; (2) the success measures it must move; (3) the triage taxonomy (solution type × backlog status) as the standing classification; and (4) the Top-10 scoring model — a defensible, repeated, documented instrument to replace the stakeholder-weighted model that was discarded during implementation.
