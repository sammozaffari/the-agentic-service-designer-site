Staging Experiences: From Gym Membership to Transformation at Meridian Clubs

0. Confidentiality & provenance note

Meridian Clubs is a fictional composite client, synthesized from recurring patterns across European premium fitness operators. All clubs, people, figures, datasets, and quotes in this article are illustrative and anonymized; no real client data appears anywhere. The article shares the process, artifacts, and AI-first working method, not client specifics. The engagement itself was run AI-first: Claude Code, OpenCode, Codex, and GitHub were the primary working tools, with the human service designer retaining framing, ethics, and client decisions.

1. Executive summary

Meridian Clubs — a fictional premium fitness group operating 24 clubs across the DACH region with roughly 90,000 members — had built an excellent product (equipment, classes, facilities) and an indifferent experience. Members bought access, used it anonymously, plateaued silently, and churned at renewal. The engagement reframed the business from selling access (goods-dominant logic) to staging guided transformation journeys (experience- and transformation-dominant logic, per Pine & Gilmore), redesigning the member lifecycle as a 12-month narrative arc with staged flagship rituals: the day-1 Member Briefing, the 90-day Transformation Checkpoint, and coach-led milestone ceremonies. A two-club pilot over six months produced, on illustrative figures, +3.2pp twelve-month retention, +21 NPS points, and +18% revenue per member on the staged population.

2. The brief

Client portrait

Meridian Clubs positions itself at the premium end of the DACH fitness market: 24 urban clubs (15 Germany, 6 Switzerland, 3 Austria), approximately 90,000 members, a blended monthly fee of €84 (tiers from €69 to €129), and strong physical assets — new equipment cycles, spa zones in flagship clubs, a competent class schedule, and a functional member app (check-in, class booking, workout tracking) supplied by an external vendor. Digital maturity is middling: the app is a utility, the CRM holds sign-up data but no journey state, and coaching notes live in spreadsheets.

The challenge

The numbers described a commodity trap (See: 13-the-experience-economy/01-welcome-to-the-experience-economy.md):

  • 12-month retention: 68.0% — a third of each join cohort gone within a year, most of it silently, between months 2 and 6.
  • First-90-day churn: 19% — the critical habit-formation window was operationally unstaffed: after a 15-minute induction, new members were alone.
  • NPS: 24 — satisfaction without advocacy; members rated clubs "fine" and left for a €10-cheaper competitor, the classic mercenary pattern (See: 20-the-service-profit-chain/04-customer-loyalty-and-profit.md).
  • Referrals: 14% of new joins — far below what a genuinely memorable service produces.
  • Personal-best attainment: 11% — barely one in nine members logged a verified, celebrated improvement in their first year. The product promised transformation; the service delivered a room full of machines.

The strategic context sharpened the problem: low-cost chains compress the market from below (€25/month, good equipment, no staff), and boutique studios compress it from the side (community, narrative, identity). Meridian's price point was indefensible as access. It was only defensible as outcome.

Success criteria (agreed with the executive board)

  1. +3pp twelve-month retention on the pilot cohort within the engagement window (measured at month 6, modeled to 12).
  2. +15 NPS points on the staged cohort.
  3. First-90-day churn below 14%.
  4. Checkpoint redemption (members completing the 90-day Transformation Checkpoint) above 60%.
  5. A rollout decision — with economics — for the remaining 22 clubs.

Constraints

  • Budget: €240k for the engagement and pilot (no capital refit of clubs; staging had to work with scripts, rituals, and light set changes).
  • The member app vendor's release cadence (quarterly) limited digital surface changes.
  • Labor law and works-council consultation in Germany constrained how "performance" expectations could be formalized for staff.
  • No body-metrics-shaming culture could be created: the transformation narrative had to be aspiration-led, inclusive, and coach-gated.

Why service design

Meridian's problem was not a feature gap but an offering-category error: it priced and operated like a goods/access business while its members bought — and churned against — an unmet aspiration. The Experience Economy progression (commodity → good → service → experience → transformation) gives both the diagnosis and the destination (See: 13-the-experience-economy/01-welcome-to-the-experience-economy.md, 06-transformations.md). Narrative structure supplies the arc (See: 10-the-users-journey/01-why-story.md). The Service Profit Chain supplies the causal measurement logic from internal quality to loyalty to profit (See: 20-the-service-profit-chain/04-customer-loyalty-and-profit.md). This is precisely the class of problem — cross-channel, human-delivered, emotionally sequenced — that service design exists for.

4. Phase-by-phase narrative

Phase 1 — Discover: diagnosing the commodity trap

Goal. Establish, with evidence, what members actually buy and where the current experience collapses.

Method & SOP. Three methods ran in parallel. (1) The Economic Offering Diagnostic (See: 13-the-experience-economy/01-welcome-to-the-experience-economy.md): classify what the buyer pays for, find forced compromises, identify the stage-up opportunity. (2) The Systematic Cue Audit (See: 13-the-experience-economy/03-staging-experiences.md): walk three clubs as a first-time guest, catalog every sensory cue, classify positive/negative/noise. (3) Laddering interviews (See: 10-the-users-journey/02-the-user-as-hero.md): 24 members across segments, asking "why is that important to you" until wants surfaced as needs.

AI execution.

  • OpenCode agent mode built the baseline data pipeline: pulled 14 months of anonymized check-in, churn, and NPS extracts from the CRM export, normalized them, and produced the cohort tables that anchored every later number. Representative command:
opencode run "Build scripts/cohort_baseline.py: read data/raw/crm_export_*.csv,
compute monthly retention, avg visits/member, NPS by join cohort (2024-07..2025-09),
write data/baseline_cohorts.csv and a summary table to stdout. No PII: hash member_id."
  • Claude Code dispatched four parallel analysis agents over the 24 interview transcripts (one agent per segment hypothesis), each returning verbatim evidence quotes with transcript line references — the hallucination-control pattern used throughout the engagement. Representative prompt:
You are a service design research analyst. Read transcripts/T-07.md .. T-12.md.
Extract: (1) the member's stated want, (2) the underlying need surfaced by laddering,
(3) antagonists/obstacles named, (4) any moment of delight or humiliation.
Return a table; every cell must cite a verbatim quote with line number.
If the evidence is absent, write "NO EVIDENCE" — never infer.
  • Codex ran deep research on DACH premium fitness competitive dynamics and, later, an adversarial review of the diagnosis:
codex exec --skip-git-repo-check "Read docs/discover/diagnosis_draft.md. Challenge the
claim that Meridian's churn is experience-driven rather than price-driven. Use only the
evidence in data/baseline_cohorts.csv and transcripts/. Return the three strongest
counter-arguments with citations, then a verdict."

Codex's strongest counter — "price sensitivity is real in the €69 tier" — was incorporated: the redesign explicitly kept a goods-tier access product and staged experiences at the €89+ tiers (multi-tier portfolio, See: 13-the-experience-economy/07-customers-and-marketing-implications.md).

  • GitHub carried the work items: meridian-sd repo, one issue per research question, transcripts and the cue audit as PRs reviewed by the client's Head of Member Experience before synthesis was allowed to start (evidence gate).

Human designer judgment. The designer chose the laddering protocol over a survey refresh, decided the cue audit would include the arrival smell and the sound of the induction, and held the line that coach interviews happen on the gym floor, not in a meeting room. Agents cannot smell a lobby.

Artifact produced. The current-state journey map (A1, below) with its emotion line, the cue audit matrix (excerpt in A6), and the motivation segmentation (pie chart in section 5). The diagnosis in one sentence: Meridian sells access to an esthetic environment plus educational equipment — a passive, narrative-free commodity experience with zero escapist engagement and no follow-through.

Phase 2 — Define: four-realms positioning and the transformation arc

Goal. Convert the diagnosis into a designed destination: where the experience must sit in the Four Realms, and what story the member is living.

Method & SOP. The 4E Experience Audit (See: 13-the-experience-economy/02-the-four-realms.md) plotted every touchpoint on the participation × connection axes. The User-as-Hero canvas (See: 10-the-users-journey/02-the-user-as-hero.md) cast the member as protagonist with explicit wants, needs, stakes, and a dramatic question. The three-tier story architecture (See: 10-the-users-journey/03-structuring-your-product.md) nested the 12-month concept story, the day-1 origin story, and the per-visit usage stories.

AI execution. Claude Code ran the arc-drafting loop: given the evidence pack, it generated three candidate concept arcs; the designer selected and merged. Representative prompt:

Using the User-as-Hero canvas and Freytag's six beats, draft a 12-month concept-story
arc for persona "Results-driven busy professional". Constraints:
- beats: Call to adventure (week 1), Trials (months 1-3), Mentor (3-9), Reward/Return (9-12);
- each beat: member action, coach action, emotional target (1-5), proof artifact;
- no body-shaming framing; aspiration language only;
- cite the transcript evidence that justifies each beat.
Output: markdown table + a mermaid flowchart with 4 subgraphs.

The merge produced the canonical arc: Onboarding (week 1) → Foundations (months 1–3) → Momentum (months 3–6) → Mastery (months 6–9) → Coach-within (months 9–12) — the member progressively becomes the kind of person who can guide others, which is the transformation endpoint (the aspirant becomes the elicitor's evidence; See: 13-the-experience-economy/06-transformations.md).

GitHub issues captured the two contested decisions as explicit decision records: (1) keep the €69 access tier unstaged; (2) replace the 12-month lock-in with an outcome-anchored Journey Membership (see below). Both were resolved in a half-day client workshop, minuted in the issue, and closed by the client sponsor — not by the designer.

Human designer judgment. Choosing the dramatic question is a taste call: "Will a person who has failed at fitness three times become someone for whom training is identity, not chore — if the club walks beside them for twelve months?" Agents drafted; the designer committed.

Artifact produced. The four-realms positioning (section 5), the future-state journey map with story beats (A1 companion), personas (A5), and the membership model redesign: the Journey Membership — monthly rolling, priced €99/month, anchored to the arc: the Member Briefing at day 1, a Transformation Checkpoint at day 90, milestone ceremonies at months 6 and 9, and a "coach-within" pathway at month 12. Outcome anchoring replaces the lock-in: complete the checkpoint and hold an agreed attendance floor and month 4 onward bills at a loyalty rate (€89); miss milestones and the response is a coach conversation, never a penalty. Charging for the journey, not the room, is the admission-fee acid test applied to membership (See: 13-the-experience-economy/07-customers-and-marketing-implications.md).

Phase 3 — Develop: staging the flagship experiences

Goal. Design and rehearse the three flagship staged experiences: the Member Briefing (day-1 ritual), the 90-day Transformation Checkpoint, and coach-led milestone ceremonies — plus the cast infrastructure to perform them 24-club reliably.

Method & SOP. The five staging principles (THEME: theme, harmonize positive cues, eliminate negative cues, memorabilia, engage five senses — See: 13-the-experience-economy/03-staging-experiences.md) governed every design decision. The theme: "Your Year of Becoming" — the club as the setting where an ordinary person completes a hero's year. Work-is-Theatre supplied the cast model: role scripts, frontstage/backstage demarcation, rehearsal cadence (See: 13-the-experience-economy/04-work-is-theatre.md). The dramatic arc blueprint (anticipation → arrival → rising action → climax → departure → memory extension, See: 13-the-experience-economy/05-experience-design-in-practice.md) structured each ritual.

AI execution.

  • Claude Code authored the full script pack — 9 role scripts, 3 ritual scripts, the staging plan template, and the transformation checkpoint SOP — as versioned markdown in the repo, iterated through PR review with the Head Coach Council. The scripts were generated against the evidence pack and the guardrail file (no body-shaming lexicon, inclusivity wordlist, escalation phrases), so the first draft was already compliant; coaches edited for voice, not for safety.
  • OpenCode built the member-app experience prototypes (briefing pre-fill, journey timeline, checkpoint scheduling screens) as a static clickable prototype the vendor could cost, and generated the mock member-lifecycle dataset used for analytics rehearsal:
opencode run "Generate data/members_lifecycle.csv: cohorts legacy-2025Q1 (n=1840),
pilot-control-A (n=620), pilot-staged-B (n=640); months 0-12 (legacy) and 0-6 (pilots);
columns cohort,month,members_active,retention_pct,avg_visits_per_member,nps,
checkpoint_redemption_pct. Staged cohort must show +3.2pp at m12 vs legacy 68.0%,
NPS rising to 45, checkpoint redemption 74% at m3. Make month-to-month noise realistic."
  • Codex ran the adversarial economics review: it attacked the revenue-per-member uplift assumption (+18%), correctly forcing the team to separate price mix (Journey Membership at €99) from behavioral uplift (retained members visiting more, buying more PT sessions) — the final model attributes 11pp to mix and 7pp to behavior, a materially more defensible claim.

Human designer judgment. The designer directed the two dress rehearsals at Munich-Schwabing: coaches performed the Member Briefing against three "difficult guest" archetypes played by staff (the skeptic, the oversharer, the time-pressed executive). Directorial notes — pacing, when to stop talking, how to handle a member who names a medical condition — were human calls, later encoded into the script's improv-boundary sections.

Artifact produced. The A2 experience staging blueprint for the Member Briefing (section 5), the experience script template and staging plan template (A6), the transformation checkpoint SOP-as-code (A6), and the app prototype handed to the vendor.

Phase 4 — Deliver: pilot, measure, decide

Goal. Run the staged experience live at two clubs for six months, measure against control, and produce the rollout economics.

Method & SOP. Live rehearsal prototyping at pilot scale (See: 13-the-experience-economy/05-experience-design-in-practice.md) with the three-phase transformation protocol — diagnose, stage interventions, follow through (See: 13-the-experience-economy/06-transformations.md). Measurement followed the Service Profit Chain logic: internal quality (coach rehearsal completion, script adherence) → external value (checkpoint redemption, visits) → loyalty (retention, NPS, referrals) → growth (revenue/member) (See: 20-the-service-profit-chain/04-customer-loyalty-and-profit.md).

AI execution. GitHub tracked the pilot as two epics (pilot-munich-schwabing, pilot-zurich-enge) with weekly issues per ritual; an Actions workflow linted every artifact PR (mermaid parse check, markdown lint, schema validation of CSVs) so the evidence trail was always buildable. OpenCode ran the weekly cohort analytics pipeline and published the retention/NPS tables into the repo as PRs — the client reviewed numbers the same way it reviewed copy. Codex performed the final results challenge before the G3 gate, testing survivorship bias and cohort mix effects; it flagged that the staged cohort skewed 4pp younger, which the analysis then controlled for.

Human designer judgment. The G3 recommendation — roll out to all 24 clubs over two quarters, with the coach rehearsal cadence as a non-negotiable gate — was the designer's call, made against the board's pressure to cut the ceremony staffing. The data said the ceremony was the retention peak; the designer said cutting it converts the program back into a commodity. The board agreed.

Artifact produced. The pilot dataset (A7), retention and revenue charts (A8), the traceability table (A9), and the club rollout playbook template (A6).

6. Use cases

Use case 1 — New-member first 90 days across the two-club pilot

Scenario. Every Journey Membership join at Munich-Schwabing and Zurich-Enge between weeks 1–13 of the pilot entered the staged arc: briefing at day 1, foundations plan, checkpoint at day 90.

What was designed. The mini-journey: join → app pre-fills briefing slot → Member Briefing (blueprint A2) → weekly coach micro-touch (30 seconds, scripted) → day-87 dossier prep → 90-day Transformation Checkpoint → loyalty-rate eligibility letter.

AI working session summary. Claude Code drafted the micro-touch script variants; OpenCode generated 500 synthetic member journeys to rehearse the analytics pipeline before real data existed; GitHub Actions validated the journey-record schema on every app-vendor export. Sample prompt: "Draft 6 variants of the 30-second week-2 coach micro-touch for persona Jonas; each must reference his aspiration statement, contain no body metrics, and end with one question."

Outcome (illustrative). 640 staged members; briefing attendance 96%; day-90 checkpoint redemption 74%; first-90-day churn 12% vs 19% control; m3 NPS 40 vs 24.

Use case 2 — Post-injury return-to-fitness journey

Scenario. Petra (persona 2): cleared for training 14 months after knee surgery, frightened of the gym floor.

What was designed. A foundations variant of the arc: briefing conducted seated, no movement screen on day 1 (replaced by a confidence conversation); a 6-week "trust the joint" quest co-designed with the physio protocol; a month-6 milestone ceremony framed as public re-entry ("Returner" ceremony, small group, coach-narrated).

AI working session summary. The designer and Claude Code red-teamed the copy: an agent was explicitly instructed to attack every script line for pity-framing, ableism, and false reassurance; 11 lines were rewritten. Codex reviewed the physio-referral boundary conditions against the script's escalation phrases.

Outcome (illustrative). Returners segment in the staged cohort (n=118): m6 retention 79% vs 66% control; zero injury escalations attributable to programming; segment NPS 47 — the highest of any segment, because the emotional stakes were highest.

Use case 3 — Long-tenured member to "coach-within" transformation

Scenario. Members at month 9+ who complete Mastery are invited onto the coach-within path: they mentor one newcomer through weeks 1–4, with the coach supervising.

What was designed. The reward/return beat made structural: mentor training (2 hours, improv-theatre format), a mentee assignment ritual, and the anniversary celebration where mentors are named. This converts the most loyal members into the elicitor's stage — the deepest possible transformation evidence (See: 13-the-experience-economy/06-transformations.md) and a referral engine (See: 20-the-service-profit-chain/04-customer-loyalty-and-profit.md).

AI working session summary. Claude Code generated the mentor micro-training script from the coach role scripts (consistency by construction); GitHub issue epic/coach-within tracked consent, safeguarding rules, and works-council sign-off as checklist items.

Outcome (illustrative). 41 members entered the path in the pilot window; mentor-member 12-month retention modeled at 92%; referred joins from mentor introductions accounted for 6pp of the 14%→23% referral shift.

7. SOPs for AI — how the staging SOPs were encoded

Encoding pattern. Every SOP in this engagement followed the same encoding: method (book reference) → numbered step list → structured output schema (YAML/markdown table) → acceptance criteria (numeric thresholds) → review gate (named human + PR). The transformation checkpoint SOP-as-code in A6 is the canonical example: an agent can execute sop/transformation-checkpoint.yaml end-to-end — assemble the dossier, verify script compliance, write the journey record — and a coach's judgment gates the two branches.

The meta-SOP (research → synthesize → map → ideate → prototype → measure → deliver) ran as GitHub milestones with gate issues: G1 (diagnosis signed by client), G2 (scripts signed by Head Coach Council), G3 (rollout decision by board). No phase could open PRs into the next phase's directory until the gate issue was closed by the named human.

Story-arc generation prompt template (the hero's-journey applied pattern, See: 10-the-users-journey/01-why-story.md, 02-the-user-as-hero.md):

You are the narrative designer for a transformation service. Inputs: persona card,
evidence pack (transcript quotes), arc skeleton [CALL, TRIALS, MENTOR, RETURN].
Produce for each beat: member action / coach action / emotional target (1-5) /
proof artifact / supporting transcript quote.
Hard constraints: aspiration language only; the member is the hero, the club is
the mentor — never the reverse; no beat may exist without cited evidence.
Reject your own output if any beat cites "NO EVIDENCE".

Rollout checklist as GitHub issue template (.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/club_rollout.md): the five playbook gates (cast, set, systems, dry run, hypercare) as checkbox items with owners; the epic closes only when every gate checkbox is ticked by the named accountable human, and Actions verifies the linked cue-audit artifact parses.

Guardrails. (1) No body-shaming copy: a lexicon file (guardrails/lexicon.md) of banned framings was checked by an Actions script on every script PR. (2) Inclusivity audit: a dedicated adversarial agent pass on all member-facing text (used live in use case 2). (3) Coach judgment gates: agents prepare dossiers and drafts; coaches make every member-facing decision — the SOP-as-code's branches route to humans, not automations. (4) Evidence quotes: every research claim carries a verbatim citation or "NO EVIDENCE".

Failure modes observed. (a) Claude Code's first script draft over-celebrated: three exclamation marks per page — fixed by a tone rule in the prompt contract ("practitioner voice, no exclamation marks"). (b) OpenCode's first synthetic dataset was too smooth (retention monotonic to the decimal) — fixed by demanding realistic noise and cross-checking against legacy volatility. (c) Codex, given the whole repo, produced a generic CX review — fixed by scoping prompts to specific files and a specific challenge question.

Claude Code — orchestration, synthesis, artifact authoring. Ran the interview-analysis agent team, drafted all scripts/maps/templates in the repo, and managed the arc-drafting loop. OpenCode — agent-mode pipelines and prototypes. Built the cohort baseline pipeline, the synthetic lifecycle dataset, the weekly pilot analytics PRs, and the static app prototype handed to the vendor. Excelled at: multi-step file/data work against a spec in one opencode run session. Failed at: synthetic-data realism on the first pass (too smooth). Human took over for: validating that synthetic noise matched legacy volatility before analytics rehearsal.

Codex — deep research and adversarial review. Competitor research (DACH premium fitness), the diagnosis challenge in Discover, the economics attack in Develop (forcing the 11pp-mix / 7pp-behavior split), and the results challenge at G3 (catching the 4pp age skew). Always scoped: codex exec --skip-git-repo-check with named files and one explicit challenge question. Failed at: anything repo-wide — generic output. Human reconciled every challenge against the dataset rather than averaging positions.

GitHub — the operating system of the engagement. Issues as work items and decision records; PRs as review gates for scripts, data, and numbers (the client reviewed analytics as PRs); Actions linting mermaid, markdown, CSV schema, and the guardrail lexicon on every PR; project board mirroring the gantt; epics per club rollout. Nothing shipped member-facing without a human-named approval on the PR.

10. Lessons learned & pitfalls

  • The diagnosis is the deliverable. The four-realms plot and the commodity-trap framing did more to move the board than any metric; executives recognized their own business in the esthetic/education quadrant within minutes. Budget time for the framing conversation, not just the analysis.
  • Agents draft scripts; casts make them true. The first table read revealed 20% of script lines were unperformable ("nobody talks like that at 06:30"). Rehearsal feedback loops are not optional in work-is-theatre design.
  • Adversarial review pays for itself on economics. Codex's challenge halved the defensible revenue claim — and the smaller number survived the board's CFO. Unchallenged optimism would have cost credibility at G3.
  • Guardrails must be files, not intentions. The banned-lexicon check in CI caught three violations that every human reviewer missed, all in "encouraging" copy.
  • The ceremony is the product. Pressure to cut milestone ceremonies for staffing cost was the single biggest rollout risk; the retention curve's peak sits exactly on the ceremony months. Protect the climax of the arc.
  • Synthetic data before real data. Rehearsing the analytics pipeline on OpenCode-generated mock data surfaced two schema bugs before a single real member record was touched.
  • Keep an unstaged tier. Forcing transformation staging onto the €69 access tier would have failed both segments; the multi-tier portfolio logic kept the mercenaries served and the aspirants transformed.
  • Mermaid discipline: subgraph-lane blueprints with simple chains survived every renderer; one early diagram with edge labels containing parentheses broke CI — keep node text ASCII-simple.
