The Knowledge Funnel: Design Thinking in a Legacy Corporation

0. Confidentiality & provenance note

Titan Insurance is a fictional composite of large European multiline insurers; every name, metric, dataset, and organizational detail in this article is illustrative and anonymized. The case is designed to teach method, governance, and AI-enabled practice rather than report a real client. The engagement was run AI-first: Claude Code, OpenCode, Codex, and GitHub acted as the working environment for inquiry, experiment design, evidence control, and artifact production, while the human service designer held responsibility for framing, politics, ethics, and final decisions.

1. Executive summary

Titan Insurance had a renewal problem it could price, but could not explain. The insurer's actuarial models were excellent at predicting revenue impact from tariff change, tenure, claims frequency, and channel cost, yet they still failed on the central mystery: why did two households facing the same premium move make opposite renewal decisions? In a 45-year-old corporation optimized for reliability, that unexplained variance was treated as noise. We reframed it as a service-design opportunity. Over 12 months, Titan built a knowledge funnel for renewals: 27 mystery variables were logged from service, claims, broker, and behavioral evidence; abductive hypothesis sprints reduced them to a seven-factor relationship-renewal heuristic; and validated patterns were then codified into a deployable, governed algorithm. The work did not stop at the model. It changed the operating system around renewals: direct and broker journeys were redesigned, experiment governance was separated from pricing governance, and an internal innovation-company model gave exploratory work enough air cover to survive in a risk-averse insurer. In the first year, in-scope renewal improved from 79.4% to 81.2%, mystery-to-algorithm cycle time fell from 26 to 10 weeks, and Titan finally had a credible answer to a question it had previously tried to price away.

2. The brief

Client portrait. Titan Insurance is a fictional composite insurer headquartered in the EU, writing 5.2 million active policies across motor, home, pet, travel, and SME package lines. This case focuses on 1.9 million annual personal-lines renewals in motor and home plus the supporting broker and adviser journeys. The company grew through acquisition, carries a 45-year-old policy core, and runs renewals through a highly mature pricing factory: nightly demand curves, segment elasticity tables, and strict model-risk review. Digital maturity is uneven. The direct channel has good payment and self-service capability, but poor explanation. The broker channel has strong local relationships, but weak shared telemetry. Claims, servicing, pricing, distribution, and digital each see part of the customer, and none owns the renewal relationship end to end.

The challenge. Titan's board believed the renewal problem was mostly a pricing problem. Yet the data kept violating that story. In the prior year, 14.7% of non-renewals happened in policies where price movement was between -1% and +1%. At the same time, 22% of renewed policies accepted increases above 6% with no contact at all. Claims memory, unresolved complaints, broker confidence, payment friction, and household life events were visible in fragments across the organization, but excluded from the dominant decision frame. The insurer had also tried innovation theatre twice: a design think tank in 2022 and a digital lab in 2024. Both were defunded after leaders demanded algorithm-grade ROI before the teams had even validated the mystery. The brief therefore became two problems at once: improve renewal performance, and prove that design thinking can be made operational inside a reliability-driven corporation.

Constraints. No core-platform replacement; all production changes had to work through existing renewal engines and CRM layers. Solvency II governance and model-risk review remained mandatory. GDPR rules limited household-level data joins and forced explicit minimization on life-event signals. Broker councils had veto power over channel changes that shifted work to intermediaries without compensation. Budget was capped at €2.4M for year one, of which only €650k was available before any algorithm entered production. The chief actuary agreed to sponsor the work, but only if exploratory hypotheses never bypassed actuarial control when they became operational.

Why service design. Titan did not need another retention dashboard. It needed a way to convert unexplained customer behavior into disciplined learning, then into operational change. That is exactly the knowledge-funnel problem Roger Martin describes: corporations become excellent at exploiting algorithms and poor at exploring mysteries. See: 16-the-design-of-business/01-the-knowledge-funnel.md. Renewal is also a service moment, not a static tariff event. Claims memory, reassurance, channel fit, trust, and household context all shape whether the offer feels acceptable. Design thinking gave Titan a way to pursue validity before demanding reliability, while service-design evaluation frameworks ensured the work stayed measurable, auditable, and defensible. See: 16-the-design-of-business/02-reliability-versus-validity.md; 07-designing-for-service-key-issues/07-evaluating-and-measuring-service-design.md.

4. Phase-by-phase narrative

4.1 Discover the renewal mystery

Goal. Turn unexplained renewal variance into a disciplined mystery backlog instead of dismissing it as noise.

Method & SOP. The team started with a knowledge-funnel assessment and evaluation baseline. We inventoried what Titan already treated as algorithmic truth, then mapped what sat outside the model: service contacts, claim memory, payment friction, broker notes, household changes, and unresolved reassurance needs. The SOP was strict. First, identify surprising observations. Second, attach evidence from service, claims, or broker behavior. Third, phrase each item as a mystery, not a solution. Fourth, capture baseline metrics before touching the live journey. See: 16-the-design-of-business/01-the-knowledge-funnel.md; 07-designing-for-service-key-issues/07-evaluating-and-measuring-service-design.md.

AI execution. OpenCode joined pricing, renewal, claims, complaint, and contact-center extracts into one pseudonymized analysis set. Claude Code then ran parallel synthesis agents: one identified price-parity anomalies, one mined complaint and broker notes, and one built the first mystery register. GitHub issues were opened as mystery/* objects so every anomaly had evidence, owner, and next test. Codex challenged the baseline math and forced the team to separate correlation from mystery.

opencode run --agent data "Read renewals.csv, claims.csv, contacts.csv, broker_notes.csv.
Pseudonymize household_id and policy_id. Join by stable tokens. Output mystery_base.csv
with price_delta, renewal_outcome, claims_memory_flag, contact_count, broker_channel,
payment_change, and life_event_proxy fields. Refuse to emit direct identifiers."

Human designer judgment. The designer made the critical framing move: Titan would not ask, "How do we defend our price?" until it first asked, "What makes the renewal feel defensible to the customer?" That changed who mattered in the room. Servicing and broker teams stopped being data suppliers to pricing and became co-owners of the mystery.

Artifact produced. A 27-item mystery log, current-state renewal journey, baseline measurement matrix, and a shortlist of three urgent paradoxes: identical price different renewal, life-event-triggered shopping behavior, and broker-versus-direct asymmetry.

4.2 Define abductive hypotheses and stage-appropriate evidence

Goal. Convert the mystery backlog into a small set of human-legible heuristics worth testing.

Method & SOP. Titan used abductive reasoning exactly as Roger Martin describes it: surprising fact, novel explanatory hypothesis, deductive implications, then inductive testing. The team also adopted a WWHTBT review so mystery-stage ideas were never judged with algorithm-stage proof. See: 16-the-design-of-business/03-abduction-and-design-thinking.md; 16-the-design-of-business/02-reliability-versus-validity.md. Each hypothesis card had five fields: mystery statement, existing heuristic that fails, abductive leap, falsification test, and if-true operational consequence.

AI execution. Claude Code generated first-pass hypotheses from the mystery backlog, grouped by cluster. Codex then acted as structured skeptic: it proposed rival explanations, flagged hidden confounders, and wrote falsification tests. GitHub pull requests enforced a rule: no hypothesis moved to experiment without one service reviewer and one actuarial reviewer agreeing that the test matched the funnel stage.

You are ABDUCTION-WORKSHOP-AGENT.
Input: mystery rows with evidence quotes.
Output for each row:
1. surprising fact
2. existing heuristic that fails
3. what might be true instead
4. three falsification tests
5. minimum sample size for a cheap first test
Rules:
- generate 5 distinct hypotheses, not variants of price sensitivity
- include at least one service explanation and one channel explanation
- never claim causation from retrospective data alone
- mark fairness or GDPR risks explicitly

Human designer judgment. The designer rejected seven hypotheses that sounded clever but were politically or ethically weak, including one that would have inferred household stress from late-night web behavior. The final shortlist kept seven factors that staff could explain and act on: trust recovery, service memory, channel fit, broker confidence, billing clarity, household life-event relevance, and proactive reassurance timing. That became the relationship-renewal heuristic.

Artifact produced. Hypothesis portfolio, reliability-versus-validity gate map, stakeholder alignment pack, and the first version of the relationship-renewal score heuristic.

4.3 Develop experiments and the future-state renewal service

Goal. Test whether the heuristic improves renewal behavior when translated into real touchpoints and decision rules.

Method & SOP. We combined formative evaluation, developmental evaluation, and contribution analysis. Each experiment asked both a behavioral question and a systems question: does the touchpoint change renewal behavior, and under what operational conditions does it keep working? See: 07-designing-for-service-key-issues/07-evaluating-and-measuring-service-design.md. The future-state service was then blueprinted in parallel so experiments informed the service architecture, not just the score.

AI execution. OpenCode built the experiment runner and holdout logic for wave-based testing. Claude Code drafted the future-state blueprint, broker scripts, direct-channel renewal explanations, and callback playbooks. Codex reviewed every wave for overfit risk, especially when an uplift looked impressive on a small subgroup. GitHub stored every test pack under experiments/, linked to mystery and heuristic ids.

codex exec --skip-git-repo-check "Review experiment wave 2. Check whether uplift claims
could be explained by seasonality, campaign overlap, or broker-mix distortion. Flag any
cell where sample size is too small for a production recommendation. Return only defects
that change the keep pivot kill decision."

Human designer judgment. The designer made two essential service calls. First, Titan split the future-state journey into distinct direct and broker plays rather than forcing a false omnichannel average. Second, the team protected high-friction, high-value households from pure automation: when the score indicated low trust and recent service pain, a named human callback outperformed more email.

Artifact produced. A future-state blueprint, experiment dataset, channel-specific playbooks, and a charted evidence trail from mystery to tested uplift.

4.4 Deliver the algorithm and the innovation company muscle

Goal. Codify validated heuristics into a deployable scoring algorithm while institutionalizing a dual-track operating model that keeps new mysteries alive.

Method & SOP. Only heuristics that survived repeated tests moved into codification. Titan documented the decision sequence, boundary conditions, exception routes, fairness checks, and human override rules before release. In parallel, the organization adopted a dual-track model: Track 1 for pricing and operational reliability, Track 2 for mystery-to-heuristic exploration. See: 16-the-design-of-business/06-building-the-design-organization.md; 15-change-by-design/09-the-innovation-company.md.

AI execution. OpenCode packaged the scoring pipeline and feature registry. Claude Code wrote the model card, SOPs, and handover pack from exploration board to pricing board. Codex performed adversarial review on fairness, model-risk language, and traceability. GitHub Actions enforced required files for release: model card, experiment evidence, fairness notes, and service blueprint links.

gh issue create --title "Promote RRS v2 to production candidate" \
  --body-file governance/rrs_v2_gate_pack.md \
  --label heuristic --label model-risk --label renewal

Human designer judgment. The designer refused a tempting shortcut: Titan could have shipped the score as a silent actuarial feature and claimed success. Instead, the score remained explainable to service teams and brokers because the real point was capability, not a black box. The chief actuary sponsored the release only after the Renewal Venture Board and Pricing Board agreed on explicit handover criteria.

Artifact produced. Production-candidate relationship-renewal algorithm, dual-track operating charter, innovation-company operating model, and A9 traceability from mystery through blueprint to KPI.

6. Use cases / case studies

Use case 1 — Same price, different decision

Scenario. Two households received almost identical motor-renewal offers: +0.3% and +0.2%. One renewed immediately. The other switched within three days. The difference was not price. The switcher had a recent windscreen claim that was operationally resolved but emotionally unresolved; the claimant had made three status-chase calls and never received an apology for delay.

What was designed. Titan created a trust-recovery path inside the renewal blueprint. Low-trust, recent-claim households received a plain-language explanation of the offer plus a named callback option within 48 hours. The intervention was triggered by the relationship-renewal heuristic, not by claim severity.

AI working session summary. Claude Code synthesized the qualifying pattern from claims and contact logs. OpenCode assembled the live test cohort and holdout set. Codex reviewed whether uplift could simply be a proxy for claim severity. Representative prompt:

Read mystery rows tagged claims_memory and contact_burden.
Generate a callback script that acknowledges prior friction without admitting liability,
explains the renewal in plain language, and offers one corrective action.
Return direct and broker variants.

Outcome. In the low-trust recent-claim cohort, renewal improved by 2.4 percentage points once the EXP-05 trust-recovery insight was scaled through the EXP-10 named callback rule, and status-chase contacts in the 30 days before renewal fell by 19%. The act of acknowledging service memory mattered more than discounting.

Use case 2 — Household life-event detection and cross-sell

Scenario. Titan suspected that some shopping behavior was not dissatisfaction but household transition. Customers who changed address, added a second driver, or altered payment timing often compared quotes because their life context had changed faster than Titan's product framing.

What was designed. The team tested a life-event hypothesis: if movers received a bundled home-plus-motor explanation and one-click cover review during renewal, both retention and cross-sell would improve. The intervention stayed within GDPR-safe operational signals already available to Titan; no inferred sensitive status was used.

AI working session summary. OpenCode built the cohort logic and suppression rules. Claude Code drafted offer copy and experiment briefs. Codex wrote rival explanations, including campaign contamination and seasonal home-move bias. GitHub stored the experiment as EXP-06 with explicit privacy guardrails.

Outcome. The cohort produced a 1.3-point renewal lift and a cross-sell attach increase from 4.1% to 7.2%, but operational effort was high and some segments showed no gain. Titan kept the hypothesis, narrowed the target, and marked it pivot rather than scaling immediately.

Use case 3 — Broker versus direct funnel difference

Scenario. Titan's early models treated direct and broker books as one renewal system with different cost structures. Broker interviews showed the opposite. In broker-led renewals, adviser confidence and clarity of rationale mattered more than digital self-service convenience.

What was designed. Titan split the funnel. Direct customers received self-serve explanation and callback logic. Brokers received a rationale pack, a simple service-memory summary, and a save-playbook that clarified which cases merited escalation.

AI working session summary. Claude Code generated direct and broker playbooks from the same heuristic core. OpenCode created the performance dashboard by channel. Codex reviewed whether broker uplift was merely volume mix. Representative command:

opencode run --agent build "Read experiment outcomes by channel and produce a split dashboard
for direct and broker plays. Include renewal lift, contact reduction, broker query rate,
and confidence intervals. Export markdown and csv summaries."

Outcome. Separating the funnel increased broker-book renewal by 1.6 points in targeted cohorts and reduced manual broker clarification contacts by 28%. The key insight was organizational, not technical: channel logic is part of the mystery, not noise around it.

7. SOPs for AI — how service design SOPs are best used by agents

The most useful AI encoding in this engagement was the knowledge-funnel SOP itself. Titan did not ask agents to "be creative." It gave them a stage, a method, an output schema, acceptance criteria, and a review gate.

Knowledge-funnel SOP

META SOP KNOWLEDGE FUNNEL
Stage 1 Mystery
- ingest evidence from pricing, service, claims, broker, and digital telemetry
- log surprising observations only
- attach evidence quotes or row references
- prohibit solution language
Gate: mystery pack reviewed by service plus actuarial owner

Stage 2 Abduction
- generate multiple hypotheses per mystery
- include rival explanations and falsification tests
- write WWHTBT conditions
Gate: hypothesis card accepted only with stage-appropriate evidence ask

Stage 3 Experiment
- define cohort, exclusion rules, primary metric, minimum sample size
- run cheap reversible test first
- record keep pivot kill decision
Gate: contribution analysis and fairness review

Stage 4 Algorithm
- codify only repeated winners
- document inputs, boundaries, exceptions, and human override
- route anomalies back to mystery log
Gate: model card, blueprint link, audit pack, production sign-off

Abduction prompt used in workshops

You are RENEWAL-ABDUCTION-REVIEWER.
Given a mystery statement, generate 5 hypotheses and rank them by:
1. explanatory power
2. reversibility of the first experiment
3. operational plausibility
4. fairness risk
5. likely value if true
For the top 2, provide:
- what would falsify the idea
- what a direct-channel test looks like
- what a broker-channel test looks like
- what service script or blueprint change would be required

Causal logic. Titan's logic model was simple and explicit. Better explanation, trust recovery, and channel fit reduced avoidable clarification effort. Lower effort and higher reassurance increased acceptance of equivalent or slightly higher prices. Better retention then preserved premium and spread fixed service cost over a larger base. In-scope lift of 1.8 points across 1.9 million annual offers implies roughly 34,200 additional renewals. At an illustrative average annual premium of €690, that is about €23.6M gross written premium retained. Titan did not book all of that as programme benefit. It used a conservative contribution narrative: approximately €3.3M annual contribution margin plus €1.1M operational savings from lower avoidable contact volume.

9. AI tool playbook

Claude Code

Role on this engagement. Research and synthesis engine, artifact author, promptable design operations layer.

What it excelled at. Parallel reading of renewal evidence, drafting hypothesis cards, generating service blueprints, and maintaining the mystery-to-heuristic traceability chain.

Create a renewal mystery register from evidence files.
Rules: one mystery per row, no solution language, include evidence reference,

Where human took over. Framing the mystery, rejecting ethically weak hypotheses, mediating between pricing and service, and deciding which explanatory model deserved executive protection.

OpenCode

Role on this engagement. Data pipeline builder, cohort generator, chart and dashboard producer, experiment runner.

What it excelled at. Joining messy operational sources quickly, generating experiment cohorts, and producing repeatable CSV and markdown outputs for governance.

Concrete usage.

opencode run --agent data "Build rrs_candidate_features.csv from approved fields only.

Where human took over. Final decisions about which fields were legitimate service signals versus unacceptable proxies.

Codex

Role on this engagement. Abductive challenger, adversarial reviewer, small-n skeptic, model-risk ally.

What it excelled at. Attacking overly confident uplift claims, surfacing rival explanations, and strengthening falsification design.

Concrete usage.

Where human took over. Deciding whether a criticized idea was genuinely flawed or merely unfamiliar to a reliability-trained reviewer.

GitHub

Role on this engagement. Knowledge-funnel repository: mysteries, hypotheses, experiments, model cards, PR review, and release gate.

What it excelled at. Making the learning system visible. Every mystery had an issue, every hypothesis a pull request, every algorithm candidate a gate pack.

  --body "Links mystery log, heuristic evidence, fairness note, blueprint deltas,

Where human took over. Political negotiation, executive storytelling, and deciding when a governance artifact had enough credibility to go to the board.

10. Lessons learned & pitfalls

  • Renewal behavior that looks irrational from pricing often becomes legible once service memory and channel context are visible.
  • The reliability trap is real: Titan's prior innovation teams were not defunded because the ideas were wrong, but because the organization asked algorithm questions too early.
  • A heuristic must stay explainable long enough for operations to trust it. Black-box success would have weakened organizational learning.
  • Broker and direct journeys should not be averaged into one renewal play. The channel is part of the service logic.
  • AI made the funnel faster, but it also made weak thinking easier to scale. Guardrails and adversarial review mattered more, not less.
  • Contribution analysis was more credible than grand causal claims. The board accepted the programme faster when the team showed plausible contribution plus rival-explanation checks.
  • Executive sponsorship must be active. A passive sponsor cannot shield validity work from budget, legal, and risk antibodies.
