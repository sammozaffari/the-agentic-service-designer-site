Design brief

Pilot 2 tests one question: does a surprise moment layered on the hospitality foundation lift satisfaction and word of mouth without breaking the guiding principles? The design compresses the intervention to its smallest honest unit — one pre-selected hour per day (a pre-selected low-volume hour), one reporting period only, one token with every receipt in the hour — and promotes it only by word of mouth, which doubles as a free uptake detector: customers asking about the hour at other times are organic proof the surprise is spreading.

Why one hour, why a pre-selected low-volume hour

The window was chosen by operational economics, not convenience. a pre-selected low-volume hour carries lower transaction volume — less gift stock, less cost, less storage — lower staffing stress, and a higher baseline satisfaction. The bounded hour also made the hype exclusive: “one hour, every receipt, maximum effort — not required to sustain over long periods,” which sharply reduced the training burden. The hour is fixed and pre-registered; stock is sized from historical transaction data with an uplift adjustment.

Safeguards

  • Every concept passed through the six guiding principles as a decision filter; anything that failed one was dead on arrival.
  • Rejected: food and voucher giveaways (register-management codes and tracking friction); numbered-customer targeting (complexity, gimmick risk); the standalone 10% discount (needed commercial sign-off, so it folded into the giveaway arm).
  • Apology-only gestures are banned by principle: the gesture must never be a reaction to a negative event.
  • An adversarial design review flagged the automation-rollout variable as a confound — the two arms were balanced across automation-era and non-automation stores deliberately.
  • No margin erosion: the giveaway arm’s cost is absorbed by the low-traffic window; the note arm costs nothing.

Crew-first mechanic

Surprise the crew before the customers. For one week before the customer hour launched, managers were given tokens to surprise their own crew — the intent being that crew “experience being surprised and delighted and then reciprocate this to customers.” On top ran a gifting licence: one crew member per shift may gift a free upsize, item or discount at their own judgment, capped at one gesture per shift by the margin review. The internal gesture is a training mechanic that feels like a perk.
