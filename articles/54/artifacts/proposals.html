Recommendation

The evidence supports two decisions. First: pilot 1’s four-touchpoint hospitality behaviours become the foundation of the drive-thru operating standard, rolled out nationally through the annual operating calendar — the training system already proved it can scale (~200 crew across 10 restaurants, ~50 by day one). Second: pilot 2’s delight hour is held as a periodic engagement driver, not a daily standard. Sustained delight every day is expensive and exhausting; one bounded hour is not — the constraints that shaped the pilot become the features of the run-state design.

The delight hour as a periodic driver

  • One pre-selected hour per day for a bounded run (one reporting period), every receipt touched.
  • Run in low-traffic windows (a pre-selected low-volume hour) so gift cost and storage stay small; stock sized from historical transactions with an uplift adjustment.
  • Hype the exclusivity: “one hour, every receipt, maximum effort — not required to sustain over long periods.”
  • Promote only by word of mouth; customer questions at other times double as uptake measurement.
  • Guardrails hold in run-state: no apology-only gestures, a per-shift cap on the free-item licence, and a margin check on every gesture.

Decision gate

The gate sits on the annual operating calendar; the recommendation is adopted pending promo-calendar alignment. The change-and-comms plan — updated training content, gamification, support materials, what’s-in-it-for-me framing, and leadership meetings for restaurant managers — is ready to build. Expansion to front counter, delivery and self-service runs on the same human-connection metrics, so the drive-thru win stays comparable across channels.
