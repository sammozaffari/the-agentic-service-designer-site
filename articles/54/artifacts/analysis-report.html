Executive read

Pilot 1’s four-touchpoint hospitality behaviours become the foundation of the drive-thru operating standard, rolled out nationally through the annual operating calendar. Pilot 2’s delight hour becomes a periodic engagement driver rather than a daily standard. The headline is not a gadget story: the tokens never appeared in customer comments — the interaction was the gift. Expansion is proposed to the front counter, delivery and self-service channels with consistent human-connection metrics.

Restricted to the intervention hour only. The defensive effect deserves its own line: negative-experience share fell overall (−~4 all channels, −~3 drive-thru), and the programme’s value is at least as much about preventing dissatisfaction as about delight.

The honest null finding

Across both arms, the giveaway tokens and handwritten notes appeared in almost no customer comments. The gift sat below the threshold of what customers mention; the interaction around the token is what moved feedback — the ritual, the line, the surprise moment at the window. The giveaway arm edged the note arm on average, leaving a real cost-vs-outcome tension for scaling: the notes cost nothing and trailed slightly; the merchandise cost a little and led. The team kept the null finding in the after-action review instead of burying it — the review gate protected it — and it became the programme’s most useful lesson.

The interaction was the gift. — the programme’s honest null finding

Crew confidence

The week-1 crew survey rated the training materials useful, and customer-interaction confidence lifted from the ~4.1/5 baseline toward the ≥ 4.5 target. Managers reported visibly higher willingness to engage at the windows. The crew-first mechanic — managers surprising crew with tokens for a week before the customer hour — acted as a training mechanic that feels like a perk: crew experienced the gesture before they delivered it.

Attribution honesty

  • Period-over-period cuts separate pilot 1’s effect from pilot 2’s; test-vs-national comparisons are restricted to the intervention hour.
  • Hourly-level reporting was requested from the feedback platform and never fully delivered; most stores returned thin afternoon samples, so the gap is reported openly.
  • The voice-AI experience question drew zero responses — the automation’s direct effect was never measured; the team measured around it.
  • The tokens’ absence from comments is reported as a null finding, not dressed as a win; the after-action review says “metrics to be refined” where that is true.

Recommendation

  1. Adopt pilot 1’s hospitality behaviours as the foundation of the drive-thru operating standard — national rollout through the annual operating calendar.
  2. Hold pilot 2’s delight hour as a periodic engagement driver, not a daily standard.
  3. Extend consistent human-connection metrics to front counter, delivery and self-service channels.
  4. Pre-register hourly-level reporting before the next pilot; score future baselines against the good-services principles from week 1.

Decision: adopted at the week-14 gate, pending promo-calendar alignment.
