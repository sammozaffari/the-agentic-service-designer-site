The brief

A global quick-service restaurant chain had rolled a voice-AI order taker into its drive-thrus: it takes the spoken order, shows it on a confirmation screen, and alerts crew by headset when a customer needs help. Efficiency went up; the personal touch went down — and the chain’s own data said so first: drive-thru experiences “often lack personal touch and meaningful interactions.”

The stated brief, in the client’s own words: “We are implementing multiple new technologies to enhance our drive-thrus. While this enhances efficiency, it risks diminishing the personal touch that has been central to our brand. We need to balance technological advancement with meaningful human interactions.”

The team’s reframe was blunt: this is not a technology problem — it is a role-and-behaviour problem. The voice-AI did not need removing; the crew needed to know where they are now the experience, and how to be it. The direction-setting question carried the engagement: “Ultimately what is our goal? What do we want to be famous for? Is it that we want to be world class in customer experience and hospitality?”

Constraints

  • Low-to-no technology requirements. The human layer must not need new hardware, software or integrations.
  • No negative impact on speed. A drive-thru second is a commercial fact, not a feeling.
  • No increase in base labour. No new staffing model, no extra hours; the ritual runs inside existing rosters.
  • Low complexity. Briefable in a pre-shift huddle, not a multi-week change programme.
  • No margin erosion. Gestures must not decrease overall margins — and they must never be only an apology or a reaction to a negative event.
  • Four-week implementation horizon. Each pilot must run inside a single reporting period.

Decision requested

  1. The four-touchpoint scope and the pilot-1 training system (touchpoint cards, poor-vs-good videos, leaderboard).
  2. The success criteria and the measurement stack above.
  3. The pilot-2 two-arm design and the one pre-selected intervention hour.
  4. The 10-restaurant pilot sample (5 + 5 arms, plus control) and data access: feedback-platform exports and hourly telemetry.
