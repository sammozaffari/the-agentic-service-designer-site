33 — The Virtual Ward at Capacity: Scaling Telehealth with the ASD Critic on Every Gate

0. Confidentiality & provenance note

Halo Care is a fictional composite telehealth provider; no real patient, clinician, or outcome data appears. This article is a runlog of the Agentic Service Designer (ASD) in Claude Code. Plugin surface and behavior are the real platform; clinical-safety governance is implemented as gate decisions the human signs (kind 37) and critic verdicts (kind 38) — not as an engine feature, because there is no engine feature — and the article says so plainly.

1. Executive summary

A telehealth provider scaled from 400k to 1.4M patients and broke at the seams: 22-minute waits, 18% did-not-attend, 11 concurrent cases per clinician with no context handover, first-visit resolution 61%. One designer rebuilt the care journey in 20 weeks on the ASD plugin, with the Critic gating every review-gated artifact and clinical governance co-signing gate decisions. Virtual wait 22→6 min, DNA 18%→7%, first-visit resolution 61%→78%, clinician eNPS −10→+25.

Key fact Value

What you will learn

  • How safety-critical gates run on a platform whose engine has no "governance config": co-signing is a gate artifact (kind 37) + critic rows (kind 38), enforced by the human and the critic — and that honesty is the contract.
  • The same twelve skills, applied to demand/capacity framing rather than field service.
  • Where clinician experience (books 19/20) becomes KPI rows with baselines.

2. The brief

Halo Care won region after region on speed, then hit structural failure: the service was designed for a boutique model, and scaling meant either doubling clinical cost or redesigning demand. Leadership chose redesign with safety non-negotiable.

Success criteria (illustrative): virtual wait ≤ 8 min at peak; DNA ≤ 10%; first-visit resolution ≥ 75%; clinician eNPS ≥ +15; patient NPS ≥ +50; no safety-event trend increase. Constraints: clinical-governance sign-off on every route change; data protection; clinician scheduling protections; a hard budget cap on clinical hours; every redesign reversible per-region. Why service design: healthcare is a high-stakes service system (book 19); clinicians determine external quality (book 20); at scale the good/broken difference shows in hours (book 03).

3. The plugin, at a glance

Same surface as article 31 §3: install (build + symlink asd-mcp with the asd-mcp --help validation step, copy .claude/, optional .claude/settings.local.json), ten agents, twelve skills, eleven tools, kind-37/38 gates, draft→final status, fact-check. Two things differ for healthcare: (1) the designer configured the engagement's tier and governance as conventions — gate decisions (kind 37) require a signed governance artifact id, and review-gated artifacts get a critic verdict — recorded as project artifacts, not engine settings; (2) the honest boundary (article 31 §3.6) matters more here: demand models, wait-time statistics, and capacity analysis are the team's analysis, marked, not the engine's.

Roster (invoke by @name) — the same ten agents as article 31:

Eleven engine tools (available in Claude Code via asd-mcp, or on the CLI as asd <tool>): sd_project_list/create/open, sd_evidence_add/query, sd_artifact_write (and sd_artifact_list/get), sd_method_list/run, sd_factcheck. Draft→final status; gates are kind-37 decisions and kind-38 critic rows; sd_factcheck re-runs integrity + acceptance over every artifact.

4. Phase-by-phase session log (Claude Code + ASD)

4.1 Session 1 — Discover (weeks 1–6)

Drive.

/asd
@researcher run contextual-inquiry for halo-care: 41 patient interviews,
4 clinician shadow days, a clinician workload diary, plus the 18-month
appointment dataset mined via transcript-mining. Register every row.

What the plugin did. The register reached 1,983 content-addressed rows. Rows carried verbatim quotes and sources; the platform gave the team a single store to argue from.

Team analysis (outside the engine, marked). The demand model — including the correction that the median hid a 2.9x Monday-morning spike and the region "onboarding cliff" — was the designer's analysis of the register and raw data, adversarially checked. It changed the delivery plan from "volume" to "capacity."

Gate G1. Register in place; critic over the research plan: no veto; one MEDIUM proposal (night-shift sample) adopted.

Human judgment. The designer pushed clinician shadow days over convenience interviews, framed burnout as a safety metric (books 19/20) in front of the clinical lead, and rejected two praise-only transcripts (selection for insight).

4.2 Session 2 — Define (weeks 7–9)

Drive.

@mapper run journey-from-transcripts -> three journeys (urgent, routine,
chronic), each evidence-gated. Then draft the triage ladder as a
concept-brief object; hand it to @critic, then to governance for the gate.

What the plugin did. Three journey-map artifacts wrote through the acceptance gate (a first "urgent" node with no evidence was rejected: min-evidence-per-node — re-derived clean). The triage ladder — four demand classes with four access promises — came back from the critic with a veto:

{ "kind": "review-veto-log", "role": "critic", "target": "triage-ladder",
  "rows": [
    { "type": "defect", "rule": "evidence-sufficiency",
      "message": "symptom class 3 routed as routine contradicts register rows
      clin-112 and clin-113; unsafe. Move to urgent-tracked or drop.",
      "evidence": ["clin-112", "clin-113"] }
  ], "verdict": "veto" }

Governance upheld the veto; the ladder was re-gated, and the phase-gate decision (kind 37) carried the signed governance artifact id.

Human judgment. The designer's frame — "we do not schedule visits; we deliver access matched to acuity" — became the reference every later artifact cited.

4.3 Session 3 — Develop (weeks 10–16)

Drive.

@mapper run orchestration-canvas (kind 15 — the Mapper owns it) and
service-blueprinting, then evidence-gated-blueprint for the care journeys.
@prototyper storyboard warm handoff, the clinician context service, and the
escalation path as storyboard-walkthrough objects (kind 23).
@critic review each before governance co-signs the gate.

What the plugin did. The blueprint's five lanes and the evidence-gated gate (min 5 nodes, no placeholder lanes, fail points trace to symptoms) held the operational design honest: a route that silently reverted to the old 5-day queue was rejected by the gate and corrected. The warm-handoff prototype went through the storyboard discipline: assumption, fidelity, subjects, pass/fail criteria.

Team analysis (outside the engine, marked). The handoff timing check — the 30-second protocol that would run 90 seconds at scale — came from the designer's scheduling analysis (with the clinicians) and the 60-second decision-tree protocol was the team's fix. The platform did not simulate.

Human judgment. The designer made the 8-minute wait promise stick against marketing's "as fast as possible," insisted the context service respect consent, and chaired the governance reviews where clinical and design language met.

4.4 Session 4 — Deliver (weeks 17–20)

Drive.

@measurer build the kpi-set (kind 26): wait, DNA, resolution, eNPS, NPS,
each with driver + baseline + source.
@presenter assemble the steering pack from final artifacts.
@librarian produce the rollout handbook.
/asd phase-gate <G4>; asd factcheck --projectId <id>

What the plugin did. The kpi-set wired each success criterion to its driver and source. Deterministic fact-check across 26 artifacts (per-artifact integrity + acceptance replay) with two governance-signed gate decisions: { "pass": true, "rows": […] }.

Team analysis (outside the engine, marked). The rollout rule — expand a region only when it holds its target four consecutive weeks — was the designer's governance convention, executed by the humans; when pilot region 2 showed a week-3 DNA anomaly (9.4%, under target), the designer investigated and found a local event, not a regression.

Human judgment. The designer ran the incident review (a mis-triage claim that proved unfounded), set the stop rule with the clinical lead, and chose two contrasting pilot regions.

6. Use cases (routed to the right agents)

6.1 The 6:40 am spike. Mapper session: triage ladder + capacity blueprint from the register; Measurer session: kpi rows for wait/DNA. Peak wait 22→8, DNA −8pts. 6.2 The locum's first call. Researcher's workload-diary evidence → the context-card spec (Prototyper storyboard); the 60s-vs-90s correction was the team's scheduling analysis (marked). Locum-led resolution 61→76%. 6.3 The escalation path. Mapper's evidence-gated-blueprint runs; critic's veto of the over-broadened threshold that would have flooded urgent slots. 30-day virtual-ward readmission 12→9%.

7. SOPs for AI — skills as the encoded method

Same role contract and skills as article 31 §7. For safety-critical work the load-bearing parts: referential integrity is absolute (every cited row must resolve — a clinical route hinges on it); status is draft→final (nothing ships without its gate); the critic's veto demotes to draft with rows (a veto without a fix suggestion is incomplete). Governance is a human gate expressed as kind-37 decisions — the platform never pretends to be a clinical authority. Failure modes observed: (1) the median-only demand reading was wrong — analyst-corrected; (2) a persona cited a nonexistent row — integrity rejected; (3) an over-broad escalation threshold — critic vetoed; (4) a below-target good-services score with no cited evidence — method's critic step vetoed it.

9. AI tool playbook

Claude Code + ASD plugin — the engagement; the critic gate was the starring mechanic. Failed at: safety judgment — relational risk stays with the clinical lead. asd CLI / MCP — deterministic cross-checks and fact-check. GitHub — PR artifact gates. Other hosts ship the same tree (OpenCode, Grok, OMP/Pi, Codex, AGY).

10. Lessons learned & pitfalls

  1. Median reporting hid the spike; model the distribution, not its center.
  2. Clinician burnout is a clinical-safety metric (books 19/20); that framing unlocked redesign.
  3. Governance on the path beats governance after it; co-sign artifacts (kind 37) were the mechanism.
  4. Prototype timing with load; the 60s handoff survived because it was stress-checked.
  5. A rollout rule the humans run beats a steering-committee promise; it caught and then explained the anomaly.
  6. Consent boundaries are design constraints; the context service is smaller because of them, and better.

13. Tutorial — reproduce this engagement (20 minutes)

Same lifecycle as article 31 §13, this case. Runs verbatim.

export ASD_WORKSPACE=$HOME/consult-halo-care
asd() { platform/packs/cli/asd "$@"; }
OUT=$(asd project-create --name halo-care --client "Fictional composite" --sector healthcare --service "telehealth and virtual ward")
ID=$(echo "$OUT" | grep -oE 'halo-care-[a-f0-9]{8}' | head -1)
E1=$(asd evidence-add --projectId "$ID" --source patient-041 --kind quote --body "I rebooked twice before giving up" | grep -oE '"[a-f0-9]{64}"' | tr -d '"' | head -1)
E2=$(asd evidence-add --projectId "$ID" --source locum-07 --kind quote --body "No context from the last shift" | grep -oE '"[a-f0-9]{64}"' | tr -d '"' | head -1)
NODES=$(printf '[{"id":"request","label":"Request","kind":"step","fields":{"emotion":"3","touchpoint":"app"},"evidence":[{"evidenceId":"%s"},{"evidenceId":"%s"}]},{"id":"queue","label":"Triage queue","kind":"step","fields":{"emotion":"2","touchpoint":"app"},"evidence":[{"evidenceId":"%s"}]},{"id":"handoff","label":"Retell story","kind":"pain","fields":{"emotion":"1","touchpoint":"call"},"evidence":[{"evidenceId":"%s"},{"evidenceId":"%s"}]}]' "$E1" "$E2" "$E1" "$E1" "$E2")
asd artifact-write --projectId "$ID" --methodId journey-from-transcripts --title "Urgent journey" --nodes "$NODES"
asd method-run --methodId good-services-audit --projectId "$ID"
asd factcheck --projectId "$ID"

What you should see: {"accepted": true}; the good-services contract; factcheck → {"pass": true}. In your real engagement, add the governance convention: before passing a gate, require a signed kind-37 decision and a kind-38 critic verdict for every review-gated artifact — that is the safety gate, human-run, artifact-tracked.

Try-it checklist. (1) Install the plugin (§3); (2) @researcher → two evidence rows; (3) write the journey and make the gate reject a zero-evidence node; (4) propose a triage rule that contradicts your own evidence and watch the critic veto it; (5) run asd factcheck at the gate and read the per-artifact rows.
