33 — The Virtual Ward at Capacity: Scaling Telehealth with a Filesystem-First Agent Team

0. Confidentiality & provenance note

Halo Care is a fictional composite telehealth provider; no real patient, clinician, or outcome data appears. This article is a runlog of the Service Design Agent Platform (SDAP) hosted on OMP — the filesystem-first form: agents are directories with instructions.md, skills are SKILL.md files, and the canonical tree is the project's own repository. Every command shown ran against the real platform; clinical safety is a hard gate in the platform config, co-signed by clinical governance as a typed artifact.

1. Executive summary

A telehealth provider scaled from 400k to 1.4M patients and the service broke at the seams: 22-minute virtual waits, 18% did-not-attend, 11 concurrent cases per clinician with no context handover, first-visit resolution 61%. One designer rebuilt the care journey in 20 weeks on the OMP pack: researchers feed the evidence register, the mapper produces the evidence-gated triage ladder, the critic sits on every gate, and clinical governance co-signs before anything ships. Virtual wait 22→6 min, DNA 18%→7%, first-visit resolution 61%→78%, clinician eNPS −10→+25.

What you will learn

  • How the filesystem-first pack works: agents and skills are directories you can read, diff, and audit — the design system is visible in the repo, not hidden in a plugin binary.
  • Why clinician experience is a clinical-safety variable (books 19/20), enforced here as a platform gate, not a sentiment.
  • How a hard governance gate is encoded so an agent cannot silently change scope.

2. The brief

Halo Care won region after region on speed and then hit structural failure: the service had been designed for a boutique model (fixed clinical cohort, appointment-first), and scaling meant either doubling clinical cost or redesigning demand. The leadership chose redesign with safety non-negotiable.

Success criteria (illustrative): virtual wait ≤ 8 min at peak; DNA ≤ 10%; first-visit resolution ≥ 75%; clinician eNPS ≥ +15; patient NPS ≥ +50; no safety-event trend increase. Constraints: clinical-governance sign-off on every route change; data protection on all patient data; clinician scheduling protections; a hard budget cap on clinical hours; every redesign reversible per-region. Why service design: healthcare is a high-stakes service system (book 19), clinicians determine external quality (book 20), and at scale the difference between good and broken service shows in hours, not years (book 03).

4. Phase-by-phase session log (OMP)

4.1 Session 1 — Discover (weeks 1–6)

Drive. In the OMP workspace, kick the orchestrator, then send the researcher and opencode-style pipelines at the register:

@orchestrator plan-from-brief for halo-care.
@researcher run contextual-inquiry; schedule 41 patient interviews,
4 clinician shadow days, and a clinician workload diary.
(opencode-style) normalize 4.2M appointment records into the demand model.

What the platform did. The register reached 1,983 rows via sd_evidence_add. The demand model was the first big finding — and it was wrong on the first pass: it used the median. An adversarial review of the model (critic prompt, or Codex-style pass) exposed the truth: the median hid a 2.9x Monday-morning spike and the region "onboarding cliff" (entry-day surges). The model artifact was revised, and the whole delivery plan changed: capacity, not volume, was the constraint.

Gate G1. Register ≥ 3 rows, held. Critic over plan: no veto, but one MEDIUM proposal (sample the night shift) adopted.

Human judgment. The designer pushed clinician shadow days over convenience interviews, framed burnout as a safety metric (book 19/20) in front of the clinical lead, and rejected two praise-only transcripts (selection for insight).

4.2 Session 2 — Define (weeks 7–9)

Drive.

@mapper run journey-from-transcripts -> 3 journeys (urgent, routine,
chronic). Then draft the triage ladder artifact from the register.
@governance co-sign the ladder before it goes to development.

What the platform did. Three current-state journeys written as journey-map artifacts, evidence-gated (a patient journey "urgent" node with no evidence rows was dropped on first write — field-non-empty/min-evidence-per-node failures returned verbatim, re-derived clean). The triage ladder — four demand classes with four access promises — was drafted, then the critic vetoed one rung outright:

{ "kind": "review-veto-log", "role": "critic", "target": "triage-ladder",
  "verdict": "veto",
  "message": "symptom class 3 routed as routine contradicts register rows
  clin-112/113; unsafe. Move to urgent-tracked or drop." }

Clinical governance upheld the veto; the ladder was re-gated and co-signed (kind phase-gate-decision with a governance artifact id).

Human judgment. The designer framed "we do not schedule visits; we deliver access matched to acuity" — and that one sentence became the framing every later artifact cited.

4.3 Session 3 — Develop (weeks 10–16)

Drive.

@ideator run orchestration-canvas; 8 HMWs, NUF filter, concept-briefs.
@prototyper storyboard warm-handoff + context service + escalation path.
@mapper run evidence-gated-blueprint; @governance co-sign each iteration.

What the platform did. Forty-four concepts → 16 → four storyboarded designs. The evidence-gated-blueprint runs demanded principle-and-insight traces for every backstage row. An adversarial stress-test of the warm-handoff (shift-change simulation) found the socialized 30-second handoff would run 90 seconds at scale — breaking the capacity model; the design was revised to a structured 60-second protocol with a decision tree before governance, not after. The clinician context service was sized down by a consent constraint (no floating patient-facing conclusions): smaller, and better.

Human judgment. The designer made the wait-time promise (8 minutes) stick against marketing's "as fast as possible", insisted the context service respect consent, and chaired the governance reviews where clinical and design language met.

4.4 Session 4 — Deliver (weeks 17–20)

Drive.

@measurer build the KPI tree from the four success criteria; wire telemetry.
@presenter render the steering pack; @librarian assemble the rollout handbook.
Run sd_factcheck at G4; enforce the rollout rule per region.

What the platform did. KPI tree (kind kpi-set); good-services-audit scoring; fact-check at G4 across 26 artifacts with two governance artifacts co-signed — "pass": true (excerpt §13). The rollout rule — expand a region only when it holds the target four consecutive weeks — flagged pilot region 2's DNA at 9.4% in week 3 (under target but anomalous); investigation found a local event, not a regression. That's the rule working.

Human judgment. The designer ran the incident review (a mis-triage claim that proved unfounded), set the stop rule with the clinical lead, and chose two contrasting pilot regions.

6. Platform use cases (OMP sub-sessions)

6.1 The 6:40 am spike. Measurer's capacity simulation + the Monday-peak corrected model: route urgent-class requests to urgent-tracked slots, rebalance across regions. Peak wait 22→8, DNA −8pts.

6.2 The locum's first call. Researcher's workload diary → context-card spec; prototyper's 60-second storyboard; the scale correction (60s real vs 90s naive) came from the adversarial pass. Locum-led first-visit resolution 61→76%.

6.3 The escalation path. Mapper's escalation-blueprint runs; critic's veto of the over-broadened threshold that would have flooded urgent slots. 30-day virtual-ward readmission 12→9%.

7. SOPs for AI — skills as the encoded method

The OMP host surfaces skills as directories you can audit: open skills/contextual-inquiry/SKILL.md and the whole contract is there — phase research, input evidence-set (min 3), output kind insight-set, steps, provenance to 01-this-is-service-design-doing/04. Open skills/evidence-gated-blueprint/SKILL.md and the refusal rule is plain text: no blueprint change without a principle and an insight trace. The critic's instructions.md names the ordered checks: schema → referential integrity → acceptance → judgment, then falsification (absence of contrary evidence is not confirmation).

Guardrails. Safety: governance co-signature is a hard gate — the platform config for this engagement refuses to pass a phase whose governance artifact is missing. Data privacy: patient data never entered prompts; de-identified rows only, consent matrix an artifact. Numbers: the fact-check layer keeps the ledger (22 min, 18%, 6 min never drift). Failure modes observed: (1) the median-only model misread the spike — adversarial pass corrected; (2) a generated persona cited a nonexistent row — integrity rejected; (3) one critique pass suggested automating a clinical exclusion governance reserved for humans — the veto log shows the boundary held.

Causal logic (book 20): internal quality (context, slots, handoffs) → external value (fast appropriate access, continuity) → outcomes (resolution, readmission, NPS, clinician retention). Safety is the first KPI; the rest only count if it holds.

9. AI tool playbook (host: OMP)

OMP — the filesystem-first host: agents and skills are repo directories; every phase is a diff. Excelled at audibility (governance reviewed diffs, not slides); the evidence-gated-blueprint refusals were its best feature. Failed at: safety judgment — relational risk stays with the clinical lead. asd CLI / asd-mcp — the same engine for deterministic cross-checks and fact-check. Adversarial passes — the Monday-spike correction and the handoff-time physics. GitHub — PR artifact gates (schema/evidence/mermaid), rollout-rule enforcement via issue automation. Other hosts ship the same canon: Claude Code (plugin pack), Grok (prompts + MCP), Codex (AGENTS.md + MCP), Pi (agents+skills tree), AGY (review-session.md — this article's critic gates also ran there).

10. Lessons learned & pitfalls

  1. Median reporting hid the spike; always model the demand distribution.
  2. Clinician burnout is a clinical-safety metric (books 19/20); framing it that way unlocked redesign.
  3. Governance on the path beats governance after it; co-sign artifacts were the mechanism.
  4. Prototype timing with load — the 60s handoff failed the scale test in simulation.
  5. A stop rule in the platform beats a steering-committee promise; it caught and then explained the DNA anomaly.
  6. Consent boundaries are design constraints; the context service is smaller because of them, and better.

13. Tutorial — reproduce this engagement (20 minutes, any MCP host)

The designer's command log; safety gate included as an artifact.

export ASD_WORKSPACE=$HOME/consult-halo-care
asd() { platform/packs/cli/asd "$@"; }
OUT=$(asd project-create --name halo-care --client "Fictional composite" --sector healthcare --service "telehealth and virtual ward")
ID=$(echo "$OUT" | grep -oE 'halo-care-[a-f0-9]{8}' | head -1)
E1=$(asd evidence-add --projectId "$ID" --source patient-041 --kind quote --body "I rebooked twice before giving up" | grep -oE '"[a-f0-9]{64}"' | tr -d '"' | head -1)
E2=$(asd evidence-add --projectId "$ID" --source locum-07 --kind quote --body "No context from the last shift" | grep -oE '"[a-f0-9]{64}"' | tr -d '"' | head -1)
NODES=$(printf '[{"id":"request","label":"Request","kind":"step","fields":{"emotion":"3","touchpoint":"app"},"evidence":[{"evidenceId":"%s"},{"evidenceId":"%s"}]},{"id":"queue","label":"Triage queue","kind":"step","fields":{"emotion":"2","touchpoint":"app"},"evidence":[{"evidenceId":"%s"}]},{"id":"handoff","label":"Retell story","kind":"pain","fields":{"emotion":"1","touchpoint":"call"},"evidence":[{"evidenceId":"%s"},{"evidenceId":"%s"}]}]' "$E1" "$E2" "$E1" "$E1" "$E2")
asd artifact-write --projectId "$ID" --methodId journey-from-transcripts --title "Urgent journey" --nodes "$NODES"
asd method-run --methodId good-services-audit --projectId "$ID"
asd factcheck --projectId "$ID"

What you should see. "accepted": true; the good-services contract; factcheck → "pass": true. Gate mechanics, honestly: sd_factcheck takes exactly --projectId — it re-runs integrity and the producing methods' acceptance rules over every artifact. Governance co-signature is a project-configuration gate tracked as phase-gate-decision + review-veto-log artifacts; there is no CLI flag for it.

Try-it checklist. (1) Read the OMP tree: open agents/critic/instructions.md and skills/evidence-gated-blueprint/SKILL.md — that is the design system, in plain files; (2) start asd-mcp, create the project, add two evidence rows; (3) write a journey artifact and watch the gate reject a zero-evidence node; (4) add your own governance artifact and make factcheck demand it; (5) ask the critic prompt to veto something false and keep the review-veto-log. Everything an agent does becomes a diff in your repository.
